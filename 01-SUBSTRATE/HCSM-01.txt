ABSTRACT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The Hodge Complex Standard Model (HCSM) derives the gauge structure,
matter content, particle spectrum, and mixing matrices of the Standard
Model from a single discrete substrate: the periodic 8x8 torus
Lambda = Z_8 x Z_8. This paper establishes the substrate itself.

The substrate-level disclosed inputs are three: (S1) the substrate is a
periodic square lattice with D_4 point-group symmetry; (S2) the operators
of interest are nearest-neighbour and positive-semidefinite with kernel
equal to the constant functions; (S3) the emergent spacetime dimension
equals dim ker D of the substrate's Hodge-Dirac operator.

Given these, the paper derives:

  1. The 5-point Laplacian is the unique such operator on Lambda (Section 2.2).
  2. The emergent spacetime dimension is d = 4, via the Hodge-Dirac kernel
     on the 2-torus (Section 3.3, citing HCSM-03 for the signature).
  3. The framework relation N = d*2^d is a consistency identity at d=4,
     giving N = 64 = 8^2, hence L = 8 (Sections 4.1-4.2).
  4. The substrate size L = 8 is independently selected by two structurally
     distinct derivations: equal entropy spacing on the torus Laplacian
     (Section 4.3) and maximal cancellation of the winding generator
     (Section 4.4, HCSM-14.b).
  5. The multiplicity at the self-paired eigenvalue lambda = 4 is
     2(L-1) = 14 (Section 4.5).

The paper closes with an explicit disclosed-input ledger (Section 5) that
separates substrate-level disclosures from physics-level disclosures
(identification thesis, charge table, mass labels) that live in downstream
papers. All numerical claims are verified at machine precision.

2. THE SUBSTRATE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

2.1 The periodic LxL torus
--------------------------

The substrate is the periodic square lattice

    Lambda_L = Z_L x Z_L,    N = L^2,

of N sites arranged in an L x L grid with periodic boundary conditions.
Site (x_1, x_2) is identified with (x_1 + L, x_2) and with
(x_1, x_2 + L); topologically, Lambda_L is the torus T^2 = S^1 x S^1.
The D_4 point group of the square acts by the rotations and reflections
of the lattice.

2.2 The 5-point Laplacian is forced
-----------------------------------

THEOREM 2.2.1 (Laplacian uniqueness). Let Delta : C^N -> C^N be a linear
operator on the substrate Lambda_L = Z_L x Z_L satisfying:

  (1) D_4-invariance: [R, Delta] = 0 for every R in D_4.
  (2) Nearest-neighbour locality: (Delta f)(x) depends only on f(x) and
      f at the four nearest neighbours of x.
  (3) Positivity and kernel: Delta is positive semidefinite and ker Delta
      is exactly the set of constant functions.

Then Delta is uniquely determined up to overall positive scale, and equals
the 5-point Laplacian

    (Delta f)(x_1, x_2)
      = 4 f(x_1, x_2)
      - f(x_1+1, x_2) - f(x_1-1, x_2)
      - f(x_1, x_2+1) - f(x_1, x_2-1).                            (2.2.1)

Proof. Condition (2) writes Delta f(x) = a_0 f(x) + sum_mu a_mu f(x+mu).
Condition (1) forces a_mu = a for all four nearest-neighbour directions.
So Delta f(x) = a_0 f(x) + a sum_mu f(x+mu). Condition (3) forces
Delta 1 = 0, hence a_0 = -4a. Normalize a=1. QED.

Remark. The 5-point Laplacian is annihilated by the constant functions by
construction; its eigenvalue on the plane wave
phi_{n_1,n_2}(x) = exp(2 pi i (n_1 x_1 + n_2 x_2)/L) is

    lambda(n_1, n_2) = 4 - 2 cos(2 pi n_1 / L) - 2 cos(2 pi n_2 / L),
    n_1, n_2 in {0, 1, ..., L-1}.                                  (2.2.2)

The spectrum is mirror-symmetric under lambda <-> 8 - lambda.

2.3 The Hodge complex
---------------------

The Hodge complex of differential forms on the 2-torus Lambda_L has three
graded pieces:

    Omega^0 = R^N,   Omega^1 = R^{2N},   Omega^2 = R^N,            (2.3.1)

with total dimension N + 2N + N = 4N. On Lambda = Z_8 x Z_8 this gives
total dimension 4*64 = 256.

By HCSM-02, the Hodge-Dirac operator D = d + d* satisfies
D^2|_{Omega^k} = Delta exactly on each form degree, where Delta is the
5-point Laplacian (2.2.1).

3. THE EMERGENT DIMENSION AND ITS SIGNATURE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

3.1 The kernel of the Hodge-Dirac operator
------------------------------------------

THEOREM 3.1.1 (Kernel dimension). On the 2-torus Lambda = Z_8 x Z_8, the
Hodge-Dirac operator D = d + d* has kernel isomorphic to the de Rham
cohomology of the torus:

    ker D = H^0(T^2; R) + H^1(T^2; R) + H^2(T^2; R),

with dimension dim ker D = b_0 + b_1 + b_2 = 1 + 2 + 1 = 4.

Proof. By the discrete Hodge theorem (Dodziuk 1976), ker D on a discrete
torus is isomorphic to the de Rham cohomology. The Betti numbers of T^2
are (b_0, b_1, b_2) = (1, 2, 1). QED.

The four harmonic forms of ker D are explicit: the constant 0-form v_0
(spanning H^0); the two independent harmonic 1-forms v_1^A, v_1^B
(spanning H^1); the constant 2-form v_2 (spanning H^2).

3.2 The Lorentzian signature
----------------------------

By disclosure (S3), the emergent spacetime dimension equals dim ker D = 4.
The signature of the emergent metric is derived in HCSM-03 from the Hodge
star restricted to ker D. We summarize that result.

THEOREM 3.2.1 (Lorentzian signature, HCSM-03). The Hodge star * restricted
to ker D has eigenvalues {+1, -1, +i, -i}: the +-1 eigenvalues come from
the (H^0 + H^2) sector, the +-i eigenvalues from the H^1 sector. The
intersection form Q(alpha, beta) = int alpha ^ beta on the even-degree
sector and the Hodge inner product <alpha, beta> = int alpha ^ *beta on
the odd-degree sector combine into a canonical metric of signature (3,1)
on ker D. The timelike direction is (v_0 + v_2)/sqrt(2).

Together, Theorems 3.1.1 and 3.2.1 give d = 4 with signature (-,+,+,+),
both derived from the substrate and the Hodge star, with no external input
and no Wick rotation.

REMARK 3.2.2 (Two independent routes to d=4). The number d=4 admits two
complementary derivations:

  (i)  The internal Hodge-star argument (Theorem 3.2.1): the Hodge star
       on ker D has eigenvalues {+1, -1, +i, -i}; the intersection form
       and Hodge inner product force signature (3,1).

  (ii) The dimension-counting argument: dim ker D = 4 is the fixed point
       of the torus Betti-number structure under Hodge duality. The
       identity b_0 = b_2 and b_1 = 2 are the only values compatible
       with Hodge duality on a connected closed surface without boundary.

The two routes agree, and both are derived from the substrate.

4. THE SUBSTRATE SIZE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

4.1 N = d*2^d as a consistency identity
---------------------------------------

The HCSM paper stack uses the relation

    N = d * 2^d                                                     (4.1.1)

with N the number of substrate sites and d the emergent spacetime
dimension. We present (4.1.1) as a consistency identity, not a derivation.
The reason is structural: on the 2-torus the Hodge complex has dimension
4N = 256, while the abstract exterior algebra on R^d has dimension
2^d = 16. These are different mathematical objects; a tensor-product
argument identifying them is a plausibility statement, not a theorem.

What (4.1.1) does is state that at d=4 the numbers 4 and 2^4 multiply to
give the substrate size N = 64. This is consistent with the substrate
being Lambda_L = Z_L x Z_L with L=8:

CONSISTENCY 4.1.1 (Substrate size at d=4). At d=4, the framework
relation (4.1.1) gives N = 4 * 16 = 64. Since Lambda_L = Z_L x Z_L
requires N = L^2, this forces L = sqrt(64) = 8.

The physical selection of the substrate size is therefore not (4.1.1)
alone. It is the two independent derivations of L=8 below.

4.2 The perfect-square observation
----------------------------------

At d=4, N = 64 = 8^2 is a perfect square, so the substrate is a square
lattice. We note that d * 2^d = L^2 is not unique to d=4: the integer
solutions form the infinite family d in {4k^2 : k >= 1}, with
d = 16, 36, 64, ... also giving perfect squares. The physical selection
of d=4 is therefore not from the perfect-square condition; it is from
the emergent-dimension argument of Section 3.

4.3 First derivation of L=8: equal entropy spacing
--------------------------------------------------

On the L x L torus, the standing-wave modes fall into discrete Shannon
entropy classes. The companion equal-entropy-spacing paper proves the
following.

THEOREM 4.3.1 (Equal entropy spacing selects L=8). Among all integers
4 <= L <= 10^4, the only L for which the L x L torus has exactly three
equally spaced Shannon entropy classes is L = 8. At L=8 the three
classes are 6.0 bits, 5.5 bits, 5.0 bits, with uniform spacing 0.5 bits.

This gives L=8 independent of the Standard Model particle count.

4.4 Second derivation of L=8: maximal winding cancellation
----------------------------------------------------------

The winding generator of HCSM-14 generalizes to any even L as
H_wind(L) = i sin(pi M / L) on the self-paired mode set of Lambda_L.
HCSM-14.b proves the following.

THEOREM 4.4.1 (Maximal cancellation selects L=8, HCSM-14.b). Let G_L be
the parity block graph of H_wind(L) on the five nodes
{A_odd, A_even, B_odd, B_even, AB}. The number of block couplings that
vanish identically is

    # zeros(G_L) = 4    if L = 2 mod 4,
                = 7    if L = 0 mod 4, L >= 12,
                = 10   if L in {4, 8}.

Among the two maximally-cancelling sizes, only L=8 carries the 14-mode
multiplet. At L=8 the surviving five block couplings form a unicyclic
graph on d+1=5 nodes with rank-1 edges; the winding kernel has dimension
d(d+1)/2 = 10; and the kernel-diagonal values are {2/d, (d-1)/d}.

The two derivations (Sections 4.3 and 4.4) are structurally independent:
the first uses only the torus Laplacian's entropy classes, the second uses
only the winding generator's parity block structure. Their agreement
selects L=8.

4.5 The 14-mode count
---------------------

THEOREM 4.5.1 (Midpoint multiplicity). On the L x L torus with L even,
the multiplicity of the self-paired eigenvalue lambda = 4 is

    mult_{lambda=4}(L) = 2(L-1).

At L=8 this gives 14.

Proof. The self-paired eigenvalue is the fixed point of the mirror
lambda <-> 8 - lambda. Its eigenspace is the set of modes (n_1, n_2) with
cos(2 pi n_1 / L) + cos(2 pi n_2 / L) = 0, i.e., n_1 +- n_2 = L/2 (mod L).
Counting the solutions gives 2(L-1). QED.

The 14 modes at lambda = 4 on Lambda = Z_8 x Z_8 are the subject of
HCSM-04 and are identified with one Standard Model generation in HCSM-23.
