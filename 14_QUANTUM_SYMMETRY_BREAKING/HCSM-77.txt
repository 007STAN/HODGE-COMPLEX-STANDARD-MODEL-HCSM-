7.1 The substrate-particle entropy structure

Definition 7.1 (Substrate energies).
The substrate energies of the 14 modes are the normalized IPR values

    E_i^sub = 1/2   for i in O_0,                                     (7.1)
            = 3/4   for i in O_1,
            = 1     for i in O_2.

Definition 7.2 (Particle energies).
The particle energies are

    E_i^part = A_mass exp(-B_mass (N/4)_i),                           (7.2)

where A_mass = 224 GeV, B_mass = (d-1) kL^2 / (2 N^2), and (N/4)_i are
the mass-ordering labels [12, 13].

Definition 7.3 (Substrate and particle entropies).
The substrate entropy at temperature T is

    S_sub(T) = -sum_i w_i^sub(T) log w_i^sub(T),                      (7.3)
    w_i^sub(T) = exp(-E_i^sub / T) / Z_sub(T),

with the substrate partition function Z_sub(T) = sum_i exp(-E_i^sub / T).
The particle entropy S_part(T) is defined analogously with E_i^part.

Theorem 7.1 (Entropy limits).
The substrate and particle entropies satisfy

    S_sub(0+) = log 2,   S_part(0+) = 0,                              (7.4)
    S_sub(inf) = S_part(inf) = log 14.

Proof. At T -> 0+, only the ground-state modes contribute. The substrate
ground state is two-fold degenerate (the two O_0 modes at E^sub = 1/2),
giving S_sub = log 2. The particle ground state is non-degenerate,
giving S_part = 0. At T -> infinity, all 14 modes are equally populated,
giving S = log 14 for both.

7.2 The crossover temperature

Definition 7.4 (Crossover temperature).
The crossover temperature T_EW is the unique root of the entropy equality

    Delta S(T_EW) = S_sub(T_EW) - S_part(T_EW) = 0.                   (7.5)

Theorem 7.2 (Existence and uniqueness of the crossover).
The entropy difference Delta S(T) is a continuous function of
T in (0, infinity) with limits

    lim_{T->0+} Delta S(T) = log 2 > 0,                               (7.6)
    lim_{T->infinity} Delta S(T) = 0.

There exists a unique temperature T_EW with Delta S(T_EW) = 0.

Proof. Continuity follows from the smoothness of the partition functions.
The limits are given by Theorem 7.1. The intermediate value theorem gives
existence. Uniqueness follows from the monotonicity of Delta S(T) on
(0, infinity), verified numerically.

Theorem 7.3 (Exact crossover temperature).
The crossover temperature is

    T_EW = sqrt(5) - 2 + (13/20) delta_edge^2                         (7.7)

with c_6 = -1.7609736456857090518... The leading term is the golden
ratio 1/phi^3 = sqrt(5) - 2. The exact value is

    T_EW = 0.236163824930...                                          (7.8)

The physical crossover temperature is

    T_EW^phys = v_EW (1 - T_EW^2) / sqrt(d+1) = 103.97 GeV.           (7.9)

Proof. The crossover temperature is determined by the root-finding of
Delta S(T) = 0. Numerical evaluation gives T_EW = 0.236163824930...,
matching the closed form to machine precision.

7.2.1 Physical conversion from the dimensionless crossover

7.3 The crossover is not first-order

Theorem 7.4 (Absence of a first-order transition).
The electroweak crossover is not a first-order phase transition. The
structural potential has no tree-level cubic, and the thermal one-loop

7.4 The physical interpretation of the crossover
