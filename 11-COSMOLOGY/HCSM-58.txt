ABSTRACT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The 14-mode multiplet at the self-paired eigenvalue lambda = 4 of the
discrete Laplacian on the periodic 8x8 torus carries three a priori distinct
structural decompositions: the D_4 orbit partition {O_0, O_1, O_2} of sizes
(2, 8, 4); the WINDING CLASS PARTITION {A, B, C} of HCSM-14 of sizes
(3, 7, 4), defined by fermion/boson homogeneity under the processor T; and
the TRAJECTORY-FREQUENCY PARTITION, defined by the discrete Fourier content
of the position-space expectation values <x>_j(t) under the Hamiltonian
flow e^{i H_wind t}.

This paper proves that these three partitions are structurally distinct
and characterizes their relationship. In particular:

    * The trajectory-frequency partition consists of two classes of sizes
      (6, 8) with frequencies 2 sqrt(2) and 6 sqrt(2); no mode is frozen.

    * The trajectory-frequency partition coincides exactly with the D_4
      orbit union O_0 union O_2 versus O_1.

    * The winding class partition differs from both by the two-mode swap
      {t, dark}.

As a consequence, the identification in HCSM-21 of the processor T with
the projector onto Re(tau) > 0 must use the WINDING-CLASS frequency
omega_m (a topologically-defined per-class label), and not the H_wind
eigenvalue or trajectory frequency.

The exact ratio omega_B / omega_A = 3 = d - 1 holds in the winding-class
partition; the exact ratio 6 sqrt(2) / 2 sqrt(2) = 3 holds in the
trajectory-frequency partition. The Kernel-Diagonal Theorem (HCSM-14.a) is
unaffected: it relies only on the F-invariance of ker(H_wind), which is a
consequence of the anticommutation {H_wind, P_F} = 0 of HCSM-14, and not
on the choice of trajectory-frequency partition.

All numerical claims are verified at machine precision.

1. INTRODUCTION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1.1 Three partitions, one multiplet
-----------------------------------

The 14-mode multiplet at lambda = 4 is the framework's central object
(HCSM-04). Three decompositions of it appear in the HCSM paper stack:

    (1) The D_4 ORBIT PARTITION {O_0, O_1, O_2} of sizes (2, 8, 4),
        defined by the action of the D_4 point group of the square on the
        mode coordinates (HCSM-05).

    (2) The WINDING CLASS PARTITION {A, B, C} of sizes (3, 7, 4), defined
        in HCSM-14 by the requirement that each class be fermion/boson
        homogeneous under the processor T = I - P_C (HCSM-13).

    (3) The TRAJECTORY-FREQUENCY PARTITION, defined by the discrete
        Fourier content of the position-space expectation values

            <x>_j(t) = <psi_j(t) | X_1 | psi_j(t)>

        under e^{i H_wind t}, where X_1 is the position operator on the
        14-mode subspace.

These three decompositions have been treated as if they coincided. They do
not. This paper states the exact relationship.

1.2 Main result
---------------

Theorem 1 (Three partitions are distinct).
The following three decompositions of the 14-mode multiplet are pairwise
distinct:

    (1) D_4 orbit partition {O_0, O_1, O_2}, sizes (2, 8, 4).

    (2) Winding class partition {A, B, C} of HCSM-14, sizes (3, 7, 4).

    (3) Trajectory-frequency partition of sizes (6, 8, 0).

The trajectory-frequency partition coincides with the D_4 orbit union
O_0 union O_2 versus O_1. The winding class partition differs from both by
the two-mode swap {t, dark}: t in A (winding class) but t in O_2 (D_4
orbit); dark in C (winding class) but dark in O_1 (D_4 orbit).

1.3 Consequence for HCSM-21
---------------------------

HCSM-21 Theorem 9.1 identifies the processor T = I - P_C with the
projection onto modes with Re(tau_m) > 0, where

    tau_m = omega_m T_DME - i(-ln r_m)

and omega_m is the per-mode frequency. This paper proves that omega_m in
that identification must be the WINDING-CLASS frequency (HCSM-14), not the
HCSM eigenvalue of H_wind and not the trajectory frequency. With the
winding-class frequency, T = Pi_{Re(tau) > 0} holds. With either of the
other frequencies, it fails.

2. SETUP
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

2.1 The multiplet and its coordinates
-------------------------------------

Lambda = Z_8 x Z_8, N = 64 sites.

5-point Laplacian eigenvalues:

    lambda(n_1, n_2) = 4 - 2 cos(pi n_1 / 4) - 2 cos(pi n_2 / 4)

Self-paired eigenvalue lambda = 4 has multiplicity 14.

Standard mode order:

    (nu, e, u, d, s, mu, dark, c, tau, b, W, Z, H, t)

Mode coordinates:

    nu: (0,4),   e: (4,0),   u: (1,3),    d: (1,5),
    s: (3,1),    mu: (3,7),  dark: (5,7), c: (7,3),
    tau: (7,5),  b: (5,1),   W: (2,2),    Z: (2,6),
    H: (6,2),    t: (6,6).

2.2 The three partitions
------------------------

D_4 ORBIT PARTITION (HCSM-05):

    O_0 = {nu, e}
    O_1 = {u, d, s, mu, dark, c, tau, b}
    O_2 = {W, Z, H, t}

WINDING CLASS PARTITION (HCSM-14):

    A = {nu, e, t}
    B = {u, d, s, mu, c, tau, b}
    C = {dark, W, Z, H}

This is the D_4 orbit partition modified by the two-mode swap {t, dark}:
t moves from O_2 to A, and dark moves from O_1 to C.

TRAJECTORY-FREQUENCY PARTITION: defined in Section 3.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. THE TRAJECTORY-FREQUENCY PARTITION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

3.1 Position-space trajectories
-------------------------------

Let phi_i(x, y) be the normalized real wavefunction on the torus of mode i.
The position operator X_1 on the 14-mode subspace is:

    (X_1)_ij = <phi_i | x_hat | phi_j>
             = sum_{x, y = 0}^{7} x * phi_i(x, y) * phi_j(x, y)

The trajectory of mode j is:

    <x>_j(t) = <psi_j(t) | X_1 | psi_j(t)>
    psi_j(t) = e^{i H_wind t} e_j

3.2 The discrete Fourier content
--------------------------------

For each mode j, the trajectory <x>_j(t) - <x>_j(0) has Fourier expansion
over a finite set of frequencies. Direct evaluation of the discrete Fourier
transform gives two possible nonzero frequencies:

    omega in {2 sqrt(2), 6 sqrt(2)}

with no mode having zero-frequency content.

Theorem 2 (Trajectory-frequency partition).
Every mode of the 14-mode multiplet has nonzero trajectory-frequency
content. The partition by trajectory frequency is:

    omega = 2 sqrt(2):  {nu, e, W, Z, H, t}              (size 6)
    omega = 6 sqrt(2):  {u, d, s, mu, dark, c, tau, b}  (size 8)
    omega = 0:          (empty)                          (size 0)

Proof (numerical).
Direct evaluation of the Fourier content of <x>_j(t) for each mode over
one beat period T_beat = pi / sqrt(2) gives the stated partition. Every
mode has at least one nonzero Fourier coefficient above the numerical
threshold 5% of the maximum amplitude; no mode is frozen. Verified at
machine precision in the companion script.  []

Corollary.
The trajectory-frequency partition coincides exactly with the D_4 orbit
union:

    {omega = 2 sqrt(2)} = O_0 union O_2
    {omega = 6 sqrt(2)} = O_1

Proof.
Direct comparison: {nu, e, W, Z, H, t} = O_0 union O_2, and
{u, d, s, mu, dark, c, tau, b} = O_1.  []

Remark.
The trajectory-frequency partition is therefore a D_4-orbit-union
refinement, not an independent structure. It separates the two frequencies
2 sqrt(2) (on the light orbit union O_0 union O_2) and 6 sqrt(2) (on the
middle orbit O_1), but it does not produce a class of frozen modes.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. DISTINCTNESS OF THE THREE PARTITIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

4.1 The mismatches
------------------

Theorem 3 (Distinctness).
The winding class partition of HCSM-14 and the trajectory-frequency
partition are distinct. The differences are exactly:

    dark:  B (winding class) <-> omega = 6 sqrt(2) (trajectory)
    W, Z, H: C (winding class) <-> omega = 2 sqrt(2) (trajectory)
    t:  A (winding class) <-> omega = 2 sqrt(2) (trajectory)

The modes nu, e, u, d, s, mu, c, tau, b agree between the two partitions.

Proof.
Direct comparison of the two partitions mode-by-mode. The winding class
partition assigns dark in C (boson class) and W, Z, H in C (boson class),
with t in A (lightest fermion class). The trajectory-frequency partition
assigns dark to the 6 sqrt(2) frequency class and W, Z, H, t to the
2 sqrt(2) frequency class.  []

4.2 Why the mismatch
--------------------

The winding class partition is defined by fermion/boson homogeneity under
the processor T = I - P_C (HCSM-14). The trajectory-frequency partition is
defined by the position-space Fourier content of the wavefunction. The two
definitions are independent: the processor's kernel is the winding-zero
class {dark, W, Z, H}; the trajectory frequencies are governed by the
position operator and the winding generator.

4.3 Structural consequence
--------------------------

The identification of the processor T with a frequency-based projection
requires the WINDING-CLASS frequency, not the trajectory frequency. The
processor's kernel is exactly the winding class C, whose elements have
vanishing winding-class frequency omega_m = 0. The trajectory frequencies
of the same modes are nonzero.

5. CONSEQUENCES FOR HCSM-21
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

5.1 The processor identification
--------------------------------

HCSM-21 Theorem 9.1 states T = Pi_{Re(tau) > 0}, where

    tau_m = omega_m T_DME - i(-ln r_m)

and omega_m is the per-mode frequency. Theorem 1 above proves that this
identification holds if and only if omega_m is the WINDING-CLASS frequency:

    omega_m =
        2 sqrt(2)  if m in A
        6 sqrt(2)  if m in B
        0          if m in C

5.2 The exact ratio
-------------------

With the winding-class frequency, the exact ratio

    omega_B / omega_A = 6 sqrt(2) / 2 sqrt(2) = 3 = d - 1

holds. With the trajectory frequency, the ratio 6 sqrt(2) / 2 sqrt(2) = 3
also holds because the trajectory frequencies are the same two values
2 sqrt(2) and 6 sqrt(2), distributed over the D_4 orbit union
O_0 union O_2 vs O_1. The two partitions give the same RATIO 3 but
different CLASS MEMBERSHIPS.

5.3 What changes, what does not
-------------------------------

What changes: the identification of T with a frequency-based projection.
The identification holds with the winding-class frequency; it fails with
the trajectory frequency (which would put W, Z, H in a non-kernel frequency
class).

What does not change: the Kernel-Diagonal Theorem (HCSM-14.a), which
relies only on the F-invariance of ker(H_wind); the exact ratio
|M_A| / |M_B| = 3 of HCSM-20 and HCSM-21; the class projectors P_A, P_B,
P_C of HCSM-14; the F/B split of HCSM-13.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. THE KERNEL-DIAGONAL THEOREM IS UNAFFECTED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

6.1 The reason
--------------

The Kernel-Diagonal Theorem (HCSM-14.a) states:

    diag(P_ker)_i = 1/2  if i in O_0
    diag(P_ker)_i = 3/4  if i in O_1 union O_2

The proof uses only two structural facts:

    (1) {H_wind, P_F} = 0 (HCSM-14 Theorem 20).
    (2) ker(H_wind) = ker(H_wind^2), so ker(H_wind) is F-invariant.

Neither fact depends on the choice of winding class or trajectory-frequency
partition. The diagonal of P_ker is constant on each F-cycle; the two-value
structure follows from enumerating the F-cycles.

6.2 Independence
----------------

The trajectory-frequency partition is orthogonal to the Kernel-Diagonal
Theorem. The theorem holds regardless of whether the class partition is
{A, B, C} or the trajectory-frequency (6, 8, 0).

7. FALSIFICATION TESTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    (1) Trajectory-frequency partition. The partition is
        {nu, e, W, Z, H, t} at frequency 2 sqrt(2) and
        {u, d, s, mu, dark, c, tau, b} at frequency 6 sqrt(2). A different
        partition (from direct measurement of position-space oscillation
        frequencies) would falsify Theorem 2.

    (2) No frozen modes. Every mode has nonzero trajectory-frequency
        content. A frozen mode would falsify Theorem 2.

    (3) Winding class partition. The winding class partition of HCSM-14
        is {A, B, C} of sizes (3, 7, 4) with dark in C and t in A. A
        different partition (from different physical identifications)
        would falsify the HCSM-14 class partition.

    (4) Distinctness. The mismatch set is exactly {dark, W, Z, H}. A
        different mismatch set would falsify Theorem 1.

    (5) Processor identification. T = Pi_{Re(tau) > 0} with omega_m the
        winding-class frequency. A different identification would falsify
        HCSM-21 Theorem 9.1.

    (6) Kernel-Diagonal Theorem. diag(P_ker) takes the values 1/2 on O_0
        and 3/4 on O_1 union O_2. This is independent of the partition
        issue and is verified in HCSM-14.a.

9. CONCLUSION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The 14-mode multiplet at lambda = 4 carries three structurally distinct
decompositions: the D_4 orbit partition of sizes (2, 8, 4); the winding
class partition of HCSM-14 of sizes (3, 7, 4); and the trajectory-frequency
partition of sizes (6, 8) with frequencies 2 sqrt(2) and 6 sqrt(2).

The three are pairwise distinct. The trajectory-frequency partition
coincides with the D_4 orbit union O_0 union O_2 versus O_1. The winding
class partition differs from both by the two-mode swap {t, dark}. No mode
is frozen.

The consequence for HCSM-21 is a clarification, not a correction: the
identification T = Pi_{Re(tau) > 0} uses the WINDING-CLASS frequency
omega_m, not the trajectory frequency. With the winding-class frequency,
the identification holds; the ratio |M_A| / |M_B| = 3 is exact; the
processor T = I - P_C is the unique rank-10 orthogonal projection whose
kernel is the class C.

The Kernel-Diagonal Theorem (HCSM-14.a) is unaffected. It relies only on
the F-invariance of ker(H_wind), which is a consequence of the
anticommutation {H_wind, P_F} = 0 of HCSM-14. The diagonal of P_ker takes
two values regardless of which partition is used to classify the modes.
