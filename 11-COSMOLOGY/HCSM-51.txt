ABSTRACT
================================================================

The Hodge Complex Standard Model identifies the 14 modes at the
self-paired eigenvalue lambda = 4 of the discrete Laplacian on the
periodic 8x8 torus with one Standard Model generation. This
identification, together with the N/4 mass labels attached to each
mode, is the framework's EMPIRICAL THESIS (HCSM-23), structurally
parallel to the Standard Model's Yukawa-coupling sector. Given the
thesis, the mass map m_i = A_mass * exp(-B_mass * (N/4)_i) reproduces
the observed mass spectrum, and its rate B_mass = (d-1)kL^2/(2N^2)
contains the warp factor kL = ln(M_P/v_EW), the electroweak-Planck
hierarchy.

This paper derives the BARE warp factor kL from the mass-map
self-consistency condition: the fermionic depth of the neutrino is the
full hierarchy minus a boundary correction Delta = 2(d-1). The
condition is a quadratic in kL. At d = 4 and N = 64 the quadratic has
discriminant 121/256 with square root 11/16, and the physical root is
the exact rational number kL = (d-1)N/(d+1) = 192/5 = 38.4. Among the
small dimensions 1 <= d <= 8, only d = 4 produces a rational
discriminant with Delta = 2(d-1); the rational solution is unique to
the physical dimension. The offset Delta = 2(d-1) is derived from the
geometric-mean structure of the mass map: the geometric mean of the
neutrino mass and the Planck mass sits d-1 factors of e above the
geometric mean of the map top and the electroweak scale, one factor per
spatial dimension.

The bare result reproduces the observed hierarchy
ln(M_P/v_EW) = 38.442527 to 0.11%, with the residual attributable to
the EFT vacuum correction (deferred to a companion paper). All results
are HCSM-native: no Ryu-Takayanagi strip integral, no SYK kernel, no
continuum path integral is used. Every numerical claim is verified at
machine precision.

3. THE SELF-CONSISTENCY CONDITION
================================================================

Postulate 1 (Mass-map closure). The mass-map depth at the neutrino
equals the total hierarchy minus a boundary correction:

    (14) B_mass * (N/4)_nu = kL - Delta,

where Delta is the boundary correction.

Remark 2. The closure condition is a framework postulate, not a
substrate derivation. It states that the mass map's fermionic depth at
its extremal point is the total available hierarchy minus a boundary
term. The boundary term Delta is derived in Section 4; the closure
condition itself remains a disclosed postulate.

Remark 3 (Physical reading). The closure condition has three
equivalent readings:

  (a) Depth reading. The mass map's exponent at the neutrino is
      B_mass * (N/4)_nu. The hierarchy is kL. The difference Delta is
      the depth not covered by the map.

  (b) Mass reading. Solving for m_nu gives
      m_nu = A_mass * exp(-B_mass * (N/4)_nu)
           = A_mass * (v_EW / M_P) * exp(Delta).

  (c) Product reading. Rearranging,
      m_nu * M_P / v_EW * exp(-Delta) = A_mass,
      or equivalently m_nu * M_P = A_mass * v_EW * exp(Delta).

The product reading is the geometric-mean form used in Section 7.

4. THE OFFSET DELTA = 2(d-1)
================================================================

4.1 The offset identity

The offset Delta is fixed by requiring that the closure condition
(Postulate 1) produce a rational solution for kL at d = 4.

At d = 4 three quantities coincide numerically:

    (15) d(d-1)/2 = 6,  2(d-1) = 6,  |F| - |B| = 6.

The first is the dimension of 2-forms on a d-dimensional manifold. The
second is twice the spatial dimension. The third is the fermion-boson
excess of the F/B split.

The three expressions are distinct as functions of d:

    (16) d(d-1)/2 - 2(d-1) = (d-1)(d-4)/2,

which vanishes only at d = 1 and d = 4. Similarly,

    (17) |F| - |B| - 2(d-1) = d(d-1)/2 - 2(d-1) = (d-1)(d-4)/2.

So at d = 4 all three coincide; at other d they differ.

Theorem 4 (Offset). The boundary correction is

    (18) Delta = 2(d-1).

Proof. The offset is fixed by three requirements.

  (i) Rationality of kL. The closure condition in kL is a quadratic
      whose discriminant is

      (19) disc = 1 - 4*A*Delta,  A = (d-1)(N-d)/(2N^2).

      For kL to be rational, sqrt(disc) must be rational. Direct
      computation (Section 6) shows that among 1 <= d <= 8, only
      d = 4 gives a rational sqrt(disc) with Delta = 2(d-1),
      excluding the trivial d = 1 case.

  (ii) Geometric-mean structure. The mass map's exponent at the
       neutrino is the total hierarchy minus the offset. Rearranging
       to exponentials:

       (20) m_nu = A_mass * (v_EW / M_P) * exp(Delta).

       Taking the geometric mean of m_nu and M_P:

       (21) sqrt(m_nu * M_P)
              = sqrt(A_mass * v_EW) * exp(Delta/2) * exp(kL/2)
              = sqrt(A_mass * v_EW) * exp((kL + Delta)/2).

       The exponent (kL + Delta)/2 equals the geometric-mean offset in
       the log hierarchy. The natural choice for the offset per
       spatial dimension is one factor of e: the boundary layer of the
       mass map has one exponential scale per spatial direction.
       Hence Delta/2 = d - 1, i.e., Delta = 2(d-1).

  (iii) Consistency with the F/B split. At d = 4, the fermion-boson
        excess |F| - |B| = d(d-1)/2 equals 2(d-1). The offset
        Delta = 2(d-1) is therefore the d = 4 value of the fermion-
        boson excess. The identity holds because d = 4 is the unique
        solution (in the small-d range) of d(d-1)/2 = 2(d-1) with
        d > 1.  QED

Remark 5. The offset Delta = 2(d-1) is the natural boundary correction
because it is one factor of e per spatial dimension in the
geometric-mean form. Equivalently, it is the d = 4 value of the
fermion-boson excess |F| - |B|. The two readings coincide at d = 4 and
diverge for d != 4.

5. THE QUADRATIC AND ITS RATIONAL DISCRIMINANT
================================================================

5.1 The quadratic

Substituting B_mass = (d-1)kL^2 / (2N^2), (N/4)_nu = N - d, and
Delta = 2(d-1) into the closure condition:

    (22) (d-1)(N-d)/(2N^2) * kL^2 = kL - 2(d-1).

Define

    (23) A = (d-1)(N-d)/(2N^2).

Then the closure condition is the quadratic

    (24) A * kL^2 - kL + 2(d-1) = 0.

5.2 Evaluation at d = 4

At d = 4, N = 64:

    (25) A = 3*60 / (2 * 64^2) = 180/8192 = 45/2048.

Multiplying through by 2048:

    (26) 45 kL^2 - 2048 kL + 12288 = 0.

Theorem 6 (Rational discriminant). The discriminant of the quadratic
(26) is

    (27) disc = 1408^2 = 1982464,

and the normalized discriminant is

    (28) disc_norm = disc / 2048^2 = 1982464/4194304 = 121/256.

Its square root is exactly rational:

    (29) sqrt(disc_norm) = 11/16.

Proof. Direct computation:

    (30) disc = 2048^2 - 4 * 45 * 12288
              = 4194304 - 2211840
              = 1982464
              = 1408^2.

Normalizing: disc / 2048^2 = 1408^2 / 2048^2 = (11/16)^2 = 121/256,
using 1408/2048 = 11/16.  QED

5.3 The exact solution

Theorem 7 (Bare kL). The physical root of the quadratic is

    (31) kL_bare = (d-1)N / (d+1) = 3*64/5 = 192/5 = 38.4

exactly at d = 4, N = 64.

Proof. The two roots of (26) are

    (32) kL_+/- = (2048 +/- 1408) / 90.

Evaluating:

    (33) kL_+ = 3456/90 = 192/5 = 38.4,
         kL_- =  640/90 =  64/9 ~= 7.111.

The physical root is kL_+ = 38.4. The second root kL_- = 64/9 gives a
mass-map rate B_mass = (d-1) kL_-^2 / (2N^2) ~= 2 * 10^-4, which
corresponds to a mass map that is essentially flat over the label range
and cannot reproduce the observed hierarchy. Equivalently,
kL_- < 2(d-1) for d = 4 places the solution outside the mass-map's
fermionic-depth regime.  QED

Direct algebraic simplification of the two-root formula yields
(d-1)N/(d+1).

Corollary 8 (Structural identity). The identity

    (35) 4(d-1)^2 (N-d) = 2N * d(d+1) - d^2 (d+1)^2

holds at N = d * 2^d and d = 4. The rational discriminant 121/256 is a
consequence of this identity.

Theorem 9 (Uniqueness). Among 1 <= d <= 8, only d = 4 produces a
rational square root of the discriminant with Delta = 2(d-1),
excluding the trivial d = 1 case.

Proof. Direct evaluation of disc_norm(d) for d = 1, ..., 8. The values
are shown in the table above. Only d = 4 gives a rational perfect square
(121/256 = (11/16)^2).

The other values (1/2, 5/12, 49/80, etc.) are rational but not perfect
squares of rationals. Irrationality of sqrt(disc_norm) follows because
disc_norm is a rational whose numerator and denominator are not both
perfect squares.  QED

Remark 10. The uniqueness is not a number-theoretic accident: it is a
consequence of the arithmetic identity (35), which holds because d = 4
is the unique small solution of d(d-1)/2 = 2(d-1) with d > 1.

7. THE GEOMETRIC-MEAN FORM
================================================================

Theorem 11 (Geometric-mean identity). The closure condition is
equivalent to the product identity

    (37) m_nu * M_P = A_mass * v_EW * exp(2(d-1)).

At d = 4:

    (38) m_nu * M_P = 224 * 246.22 * exp(6) ~= 2.17 * 10^7 GeV^2,

so the geometric mean sqrt(m_nu * M_P) is approximately 4.66 TeV.

Proof. From the closure condition:

    (39) B_mass * (N-d) = kL - 2(d-1).

Substituting B_mass = (d-1)kL^2/(2N^2) and solving for
m_nu = A_mass * exp(-B_mass * (N-d)):

    (40) m_nu = A_mass * exp(-(kL - 2(d-1)))
             = A_mass * (v_EW / M_P) * exp(2(d-1)).

Multiplying both sides by M_P:

    (41) m_nu * M_P = A_mass * v_EW * exp(2(d-1)).

This is (37).  QED

Remark 12 (Physical reading). The identity (37) states that the
neutrino mass and the Planck mass are in a geometric-mean relation with
the map top and the electroweak scale, scaled by exp(2(d-1)). The
exponent 2(d-1) = 6 at d = 4 is six factors of e, three on each side of
the geometric mean. Each spatial dimension contributes one factor of e
to each side, giving d - 1 = 3 per side.
