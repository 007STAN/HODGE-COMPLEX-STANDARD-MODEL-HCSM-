2.2 The Ryu-Takayanagi strip integral
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The strip integral at parameter alpha is

    I(alpha) = integral_1^infty [ u^{2 alpha - 2} / sqrt(u^{2 alpha} - 1)
                                  - u^{alpha - 2} ] du.              (3)

It has the closed form

    I(alpha) = (1/(2 alpha)) [ B(a, 1/2) - 1/a ],
        a = -1/2 + 1/(2 alpha).                                      (4)

At alpha = 2 (the DME value alpha = D - 2 = 2), this gives a = -1/4 and

    I(2) = (1/4) B(-1/4, 1/2) + 1.                                   (5)

    gamma = c_2 / I(2),   c_2 = 2 I''(2),                            (6)

    gamma = 0.70283946799007245929257819650854315216110316215188...

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

satisfies

    I(2) = 1 - (1/2) AGM(1, sqrt(2))                                 (7)

where AGM denotes the arithmetic-geometric mean. Equivalently,

    I(2) = 1 - sqrt(2) * pi^{3/2} / Gamma(1/4)^2
         = 1 - pi / (2 sqrt(2) K(1/sqrt(2))),                        (8)

with K the complete elliptic integral of the first kind.

Proof (sketch). From (5), I(2) = 1 + B(-1/4,1/2)/4. The Beta function is
evaluated by reflection:

    B(-1/4,1/2) = Gamma(-1/4) Gamma(1/2) / Gamma(1/4)
                = -4 Gamma(3/4) sqrt(pi) / Gamma(1/4).

Using Gamma(1/4) Gamma(3/4) = pi / sin(pi/4) = pi sqrt(2), this gives

    B(-1/4,1/2) = -4 sqrt(2) pi^{3/2} / Gamma(1/4)^2.

The classical identity AGM(1, sqrt(2)) = sqrt(2) pi^{3/2} / Gamma(1/4)^2
then gives the result. The remaining equivalences follow from
K(1/sqrt(2)) = Gamma(1/4)^2 / (4 sqrt(pi)). [Box]

================================================================================

4.1 The Brillouin-zone structure
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The discrete 5-point Laplacian on the L x L torus has eigenvalues

    lambda_L(k_1, k_2) = 4 - 2 cos(2 pi k_1 / L) - 2 cos(2 pi k_2 / L),
        k_1, k_2 in {0, ..., L-1}.                                    (11)

In the continuum limit L -> infinity, this becomes

    lambda(k_1, k_2) = 4 - 2 cos k_1 - 2 cos k_2,
        (k_1, k_2) in [0, 2 pi]^2,                                    (12)

with BZ average

    <f>_BZ = integral_{[0,2pi]^2} f(lambda(k)) d^2 k / (2 pi)^2.     (13)

The BZ average is the continuum limit of the lattice mean
(1/L^2) sum_k f(lambda_L(k)).

4.2 The three crossover points
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The mirror symmetry lambda <-> 8 - lambda has three natural crossover
points in the BZ:

  - lambda = 0 at the corner Gamma = (0,0): the constant mode.
  - lambda = 4 at the two points X = (pi, 0), (0, pi): the self-paired
    eigenvalue, which is the mirror-fixed point and carries the 14-mode
    multiplet on the discrete torus.
  - lambda = 8 at the corner M = (pi, pi): the anti-constant mode.

An integrand is called crossover-vanishing if it vanishes at all three of
these lambda values.

4.3 The crossover moments
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Theorem 4.1 (Crossover-zero moments). The Brillouin-zone moments of the
three crossover-zero integrands on the torus are exactly

    <(lambda-4)^2>_BZ = 4,
    <lambda(8-lambda)>_BZ = 12,
    <(lambda-4)^2 lambda (8-lambda)>_BZ = 28.                        (14)

Proof. Each moment is a polynomial in lambda. Using
<lambda^n>_BZ = integral_{[0,2pi]^2} (4 - 2 cos k_1 - 2 cos k_2)^n
                d^2 k / (2 pi)^2
and the standard moments <lambda>_BZ = 4, <lambda^2>_BZ = 20,
<lambda^3>_BZ = 112, <lambda^4>_BZ = 656, the polynomial expansions give

    <(lambda-4)^2>_BZ = <lambda^2> - 8 <lambda> + 16 = 20 - 32 + 16 = 4,
    <lambda(8-lambda)>_BZ = 8 <lambda> - <lambda^2> = 32 - 20 = 12,
    <(lambda-4)^2 lambda(8-lambda)>_BZ
        = <(20 - 8 lambda + lambda^2)(8 lambda - lambda^2)> = 28.

The polynomial expansions are exact; the moments of lambda follow from
the standard Fourier averages. Verified in Appendix A. [Box]

Corollary 4.2 (Mersenne pattern). The crossover-zero moments satisfy

    <(lambda-4)^2>_BZ = d * (2^1 - 1) = 4,
    <lambda(8-lambda)>_BZ = d * (2^2 - 1) = 12,
    <(lambda-4)^2 lambda(8-lambda)>_BZ = d * (2^3 - 1) = 28,         (15)

with d = 4. The values 4, 12, 28 are C(8,2)-adjacent integers:
28 = C(8,2) is the Hodge lift Omega^1|_{lambda=4} dimension, and 12 = N/2 - 20
appears in the sigma-line charge bookkeeping of HCSM-24.

Corollary 4.3 (Mirror ratio). The ratio of the two lowest crossover-zero
moments is

    <(lambda-4)^2>_BZ / <lambda(8-lambda)>_BZ = 4/12 = 1/3 = 1/(d-1).  (16)

The right-hand side is the reciprocal generation count, 1/(d-1) = 1/3.

Remark 4.4 (Structural significance). The mirror ratio (16) is exact, and
its value 1/(d-1) is a framework constant. This is the first
crossover-level identity that closes exactly. It shows that the
crossover-zero integrand family is not merely a numerical coincidence:
the family's lowest two moments are framework integers whose ratio is the

search over ~80 framework expressions. The HCSM-native derivation of
I(2) from the substrate therefore requires a construction beyond free
Omega^1 BZ moments. This is stated explicitly as an open item.

The present paper follows the HCSM-06/07 pattern: it derives everything
in its own domain that can be derived, discloses the single physical
identification gamma = c_2/I(2) explicitly, and names the specific open
item that a future HCSM-native construction must close. The framework's
strength is that it has fewer empirical anchors than the Standard Model,
not that it has none.
