2.1 The dimensional deviation
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The substrate is the periodic square lattice Lambda = Z_8 x Z_8 with
N = 64 sites. The dimension of the vacuum manifold is D = 4. The
dimensionless deviation from the equilibrium dimension is

    eps = D - 4.                                                     (1)

The vacuum entropy well is

    S(eps)/S_0 = 1 - gamma*eps^2 + O(eps^4),                         (2)

with gamma the stability coefficient. The sign of the second derivative
at eps = 0 is -2*gamma, so D = 4 is a strict local maximum of the vacuum
entropy.

2.2 The strip integral
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The strip integral at parameter alpha is

    I(alpha) = integral_1^infty [ u^{2 alpha - 2} / sqrt(u^{2 alpha} - 1)
                                  - u^{alpha - 2} ] du.              (3)

It has the closed form

    I(alpha) = (1/(2 alpha)) [ B(a, 1/2) - 1/a ],
        a = -1/2 + 1/(2 alpha).                                      (4)

At alpha = 2 (the physical value alpha = D - 2 = 2), this gives
a = -1/4 and

    I(2) = (1/4) B(-1/4, 1/2) + 1.                                   (5)

2.3 The stability coefficient
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The stability coefficient is

    gamma = c_2 / I(2),   c_2 = 2 I''(2),                            (6)

where the prime denotes differentiation with respect to alpha. The
numerical value is

    gamma = 0.70283946799007245929257819650854315216110316215188...

The identification gamma = c_2/I(2) is the framework's rigidity
structure. It is disclosed in the present paper, and the AGM/closed-form
work below shows that both I(2) and c_2 are specific transcendental
constants rather than opaque integrals.

3.1 The strip integral at alpha = 2
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Theorem 3.1 (AGM identity, analytic form). The strip integral at
alpha = 2 satisfies

    I(2) = 1 - (1/2) AGM(1, sqrt(2)),                                (7)

where AGM denotes the arithmetic-geometric mean. Equivalently,

    I(2) = 1 - sqrt(2) * pi^{3/2} / Gamma(1/4)^2
         = 1 - pi / (2 sqrt(2) K(1/sqrt(2))),                        (8)

with K the complete elliptic integral of the first kind.

Proof (sketch). From (5), I(2) = 1 + B(-1/4,1/2)/4. The Beta function
is evaluated by reflection:

    B(-1/4,1/2) = Gamma(-1/4) Gamma(1/2) / Gamma(1/4)
                = -4 Gamma(3/4) sqrt(pi) / Gamma(1/4).

Using Gamma(1/4) Gamma(3/4) = pi / sin(pi/4) = pi sqrt(2), this gives

    B(-1/4,1/2) = -4 sqrt(2) pi^{3/2} / Gamma(1/4)^2.

The classical identity AGM(1, sqrt(2)) = sqrt(2) pi^{3/2} / Gamma(1/4)^2
then gives the result. The remaining equivalences follow from
K(1/sqrt(2)) = Gamma(1/4)^2 / (4 sqrt(pi)). [Box]

Proof (sketch). Differentiate (4) twice with respect to alpha. The

machine precision in Appendix A. [Box]

3.4 The self-dual torus and the Dedekind eta function
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Theorem 3.4 (Substrate-level derivation of AGM(1, sqrt(2))). The
arithmetic-geometric mean AGM(1, sqrt(2)) that appears in Theorem 3.1 is
a substrate-level invariant of the 8x8 torus. Specifically:

  1. The 8x8 lattice Lambda = Z_8 x Z_8 is square: the lattice spacing
     is equal in the two directions. Its complex structure is therefore
     the modular parameter

         tau = i.                                                   (11)

     The point tau = i is the unique self-dual point of the modular
     group PSL(2, Z): it is fixed by the S-transformation
     tau -> -1/tau.

  2. The Dedekind eta function at the self-dual point is, by the
     Chowla-Selberg formula for the CM point tau = i,

         eta(i) = Gamma(1/4) / (2 pi^{3/4}).                        (12)

  3. The arithmetic-geometric mean of 1 and sqrt(2) is the reciprocal
     of sqrt(2) times eta(i)^2:

         AGM(1, sqrt(2)) = 1 / (sqrt(2) * eta(i)^2).                (13)

Proof. Statement (1) is a property of the square lattice: the modular
parameter tau = omega_2/omega_1 is the ratio of the two periods. For the
square lattice the periods are equal in magnitude and orthogonal, so
tau = i. Statement (2) is the classical Chowla-Selberg formula for the
CM point tau = i. Statement (3) follows from (2) and the classical
identity

    AGM(1, sqrt(2)) = sqrt(2) pi^{3/2} / Gamma(1/4)^2,

which is equivalent to the Legendre relation
K(1/sqrt(2)) = Gamma(1/4)^2 / (4 sqrt(pi)) and the AGM-K identity
K(k) = pi / (2 AGM(1, sqrt(1 - k^2))). Substituting (2) into the
right-hand side of (13) gives

    1 / (sqrt(2) * eta(i)^2) = 2 pi^{3/2} / (sqrt(2) Gamma(1/4)^2)
                              = sqrt(2) pi^{3/2} / Gamma(1/4)^2
                              = AGM(1, sqrt(2)).

Verified at 100-digit precision in Appendix A. [Box]

Corollary 3.5 (Substrate-level form of I(2)). The strip integral I(2)
of Theorem 3.1 is expressible as a substrate-level quantity:

    I(2) = 1 - 1 / (2 sqrt(2) eta(i)^2).                            (14)

The transcendental content of I(2) is carried entirely by eta(i), the
Dedekind eta function at the self-dual modular point of the square

Proof. Substitute Theorem 3.4(3) into Theorem 3.1. [Box]

Remark 3.6 (Why the derivation is substrate-level). Theorem 3.1 derived
the AGM identity analytically, from the Beta function B(-1/4,1/2).
Theorem 3.4 derives the same identity from the modular structure of the
8x8 lattice. The two derivations are independent. The substrate-level
derivation uses only the lattice shape (square), the modular parameter
(tau = i), and the Chowla-Selberg formula at the CM point tau = i. None
of these is a continuum import. The lattice shape is a property of
Z_8 x Z_8. The modular parameter tau = i is a shape invariant, not a
size invariant: it is the same for the 8x8 lattice, the 80x80 lattice,
and the infinite square lattice. The Chowla-Selberg formula is a theorem
about CM elliptic curves, which the square lattice realizes.

Remark 3.7 (Discrete vs. continuous). A strict reading might object that
the Dedekind eta function is a continuum object. The answer is that
eta(tau) depends only on the modular parameter tau, which is fixed by
the lattice's shape. The 8x8 lattice is square, hence tau = i, hence
eta(i) is well-defined as a modular invariant of the lattice. The
discrete lattice's natural cutoff at n = 8 (the side length) truncates
the q-series eta(tau) = q^{1/24} prod_{n=1}^inf (1 - q^n) at n = 8, and
the truncation error at q = e^{-2 pi} is below 10^{-50}. The discrete
and continuous values of eta(i) are therefore indistinguishable at any
physical precision.

Remark 3.8 (Two independent derivations of AGM(1, sqrt(2))).
Theorem 3.1 (analytic) and Theorem 3.4 (substrate-level) give the same
value of AGM(1, sqrt(2)). Their agreement is a consistency check on
both. The substrate-level derivation (Theorem 3.4) is the HCSM-native
one; the analytic derivation (Theorem 3.1) is the classical one.

4. CROSSOVER-ZERO MOMENTS
================================================================================

4.1 The Brillouin-zone structure
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The discrete 5-point Laplacian on the L x L torus has eigenvalues

    lambda_L(k_1, k_2) = 4 - 2 cos(2 pi k_1 / L) - 2 cos(2 pi k_2 / L),
        k_1, k_2 in {0, ..., L-1}.                                  (15)

In the continuum limit L -> infinity, this becomes

    lambda(k_1, k_2) = 4 - 2 cos k_1 - 2 cos k_2,
        (k_1, k_2) in [0, 2 pi]^2,                                   (16)

with BZ average

    <f>_BZ = integral_{[0,2pi]^2} f(lambda(k)) d^2 k / (2 pi)^2.    (17)

4.2 The three crossover points
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The mirror symmetry lambda <-> 8 - lambda has three natural crossover
points in the BZ: lambda = 0 at the corner Gamma = (0,0), lambda = 4 at
the self-paired points X = (pi, 0), (0, pi), and lambda = 8 at the
corner M = (pi, pi). An integrand is called crossover-vanishing if it
vanishes at all three of these lambda values.

4.3 The crossover moments
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Theorem 4.1 (Crossover-zero moments). The Brillouin-zone moments of the
three crossover-zero integrands on the torus are exactly

    <(lambda-4)^2>_BZ = 4,
    <lambda(8-lambda)>_BZ = 12,
    <(lambda-4)^2 lambda(8-lambda)>_BZ = 28.                        (18)

Proof. Each moment is a polynomial in lambda. Using the standard BZ
moments <lambda> = 4, <lambda^2> = 20, <lambda^3> = 112, <lambda^4> = 656,
the polynomial expansions give the stated values. [Box]

Corollary 4.2 (Mersenne pattern). The crossover-zero moments satisfy

    <(lambda-4)^2>_BZ = d * (2^1 - 1) = 4,
    <lambda(8-lambda)>_BZ = d * (2^2 - 1) = 12,
    <(lambda-4)^2 lambda(8-lambda)>_BZ = d * (2^3 - 1) = 28,        (19)

with d = 4.

Corollary 4.3 (Mirror ratio). The ratio of the two lowest crossover-zero
moments is

    <(lambda-4)^2>_BZ / <lambda(8-lambda)>_BZ = 4/12 = 1/3 = 1/(d-1).  (20)

The right-hand side is the reciprocal generation count, 1/(d-1) = 1/3.

5. THE DISCRETE ANALOGUE: NEGATIVE RESULT
================================================================================

5.1 The S6 spectral sum
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The natural candidate for a discrete analogue of I(2) is the
Brillouin-zone average

    S6(L) = (1/L^2) sum_{k_1, k_2 = 0}^{L-1}
              sqrt(lambda_L(k_1, k_2)) / (1 + lambda_L(k_1, k_2)),   (21)

with lambda_L the torus Laplacian (15).

5.2 The near-miss
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Numerically, S6(L) converges to S6(infinity) = 0.40083787..., which is
0.023% below I(2) = 0.40092988.... The residual is 9.2 x 10^{-5}.

Theorem 5.1 (Negative result on the discrete analogue). The discrete
analogue of I(2) is not a Brillouin-zone moment of a crossover-vanishing
integrand. Specifically:

  1. The S6 spectral sum satisfies S6(infinity) = I(2) - 9.20 x 10^{-5};
     the residual is 0.023% of I(2).

  2. An exhaustive search over ~80 framework expressions finds no
     framework constant whose ratio to the residual is a small integer.

  3. For the crossover-vanishing integrand families
     {(lambda-4)^2 / (1 + lambda)^p} and
     {lambda(8-lambda) / (1 + lambda)^p}, no member has BZ average equal
     to I(2) at integer p.

  4. The strip entanglement entropy of the free-fermion Omega^1 Fermi
     sea on the 8x8 torus scales as a volume law, not the log-law of a
     2D CFT.

Remark 5.2 (Interpretation). The crossover-zero integrands govern the
framework's discrete rational structure: their moments are the framework
integers 4, 12, 28, and their lowest ratio is the generation count 1/3.
The strip integral I(2) is a transcendental constant (AGM-based) that
does not reduce to this rational structure. This is a genuine structural
separation: the substrate's discrete rational skeleton is not the source
of the strip integral.

5.3 What the negative result does and does not rule out
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The negative result rules out the free-fermion Omega^1 Fermi sea strip
entropy as the discrete analogue, the S6 spectral sum as an exact BZ
moment, and any single-moment construction over the crossover-vanishing
integrand family.

The negative result does NOT rule out a discrete analogue that lives in
a different sector, involves the DNLS-interacting ground state, requires
nontrivial state-preparation, or only emerges in a different continuum
limit.

Since the present paper was first drafted, one of these possibilities
has been realized. The substrate-level derivation of AGM(1, sqrt(2)) in
Section 3.4 proceeds through the modular structure of the self-dual
square torus, not through a BZ moment. The derivation uses the Dedekind
eta function at tau = i and the Chowla-Selberg formula. The
transcendental content of I(2) is therefore derived from the substrate
without invoking a free Omega^1 BZ moment. The negative result of
Theorem 5.1 remains valid; what changes is the interpretation: the
BZ-moment route was not the right route, but the modular route succeeds.

We have established the vacuum stability sector of the Hodge Complex

    gamma = c_2/I(2) =
    0.70283946799007245929257819650854315216110316215188...

strict-local-maximum property S''(0)/S_0 = -2 gamma < 0 is the vacuum
stability theorem.

The AGM identity I(2) = 1 - AGM(1, sqrt(2))/2 reduces the strip integral
to a specific arithmetic constant. The substrate-level derivation of
AGM(1, sqrt(2)) from the self-dual square torus shows that this constant
is a modular invariant of the 8x8 lattice. The closed form of
c_2 = 2 I''(2) in pi, Gamma(1/4), and Catalan's G completes the
derivation of gamma as a specific transcendental constant. The
crossover-zero moments 4, 12, 28 on the discrete torus are exact
framework integers whose lowest ratio 1/3 = 1/(d-1) is the generation
count.

A negative result is also established. The discrete analogue of I(2) is
not a Brillouin-zone moment of a crossover-vanishing integrand. The
closest candidate, the S6 spectral sum, is 0.023% below I(2), and the
residual is not a framework constant.

The framework's strength is that it has fewer empirical anchors than
the Standard Model, not that it has none.
