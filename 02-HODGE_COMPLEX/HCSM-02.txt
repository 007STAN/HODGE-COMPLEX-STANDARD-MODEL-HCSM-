ABSTRACT
================================================================================

The Hodge Complex Standard Model derives the gauge structure, matter content,
particle spectrum, and mixing matrices of the Standard Model from the
periodic 8 x 8 torus. This paper establishes the algebraic substrate on which
the framework is built: the Hodge complex of differential forms on
Lambda = Z_8 x Z_8. We construct the three graded pieces Omega^0 + Omega^1 +
Omega^2 of total dimension 256, define the exterior derivative d and its
adjoint d*, and prove that the Hodge-Dirac operator D = d + d* satisfies
D^2 = Delta exactly on each form degree. Unlike the spinorial Dirac operator
on the same lattice, the Hodge-Dirac square is the scalar 5-point Laplacian
with no fermion doubling. The complex carries a canonical Z_2 grading: even
forms (Omega^0 + Omega^2) are bosonic, odd forms (Omega^1) are fermionic, and
D is the supercharge of an N = 1 supersymmetric quantum mechanics. We prove
the 14-fold lift: the scalar Laplacian's multiplicity-14 eigenvalue at
lambda = 4 lifts to 14 modes in Omega^0, 28 in Omega^1, and 14 in Omega^2,
giving a 56-dimensional sector. By discrete Hodge theory, dim ker D =
b_0 + b_1 + b_2 = 1 + 2 + 1 = 4. The full D4 decomposition of the lambda = 4
sector is computed: 56 = 9A_1 + 5A_2 + 7B_1 + 7B_2 + 14E with
Omega^1|_lambda=4 = Omega^0|_lambda=4 tensor E. All theorems are HCSM-native
and verified at machine precision.

1.1 The Hodge complex in HCSM
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The Hodge Complex Standard Model (HCSM) derives the gauge structure, matter
content, particle spectrum, and mixing matrices of the Standard Model from a
single discrete substrate: the periodic 8 x 8 torus Lambda = Z_8 x Z_8 [1].
The fourteen modes at the self-paired eigenvalue lambda = 4 of the discrete
Laplacian are identified with one Standard Model generation.

The algebraic arena on which this identification is made is the Hodge complex
of differential forms on Lambda. This paper establishes the complex, its
exterior derivative and codifferential, the Hodge-Dirac operator, and the
Z_2-graded structure that will be used throughout the HCSM paper stack. The
substrate is not a lattice of Majorana fermions with one fermion per site; it
is the Hodge complex of differential forms, and the fermionic content lives
in the odd form degree.


1.2 The one-line summary
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  The substrate of HCSM is the Hodge complex of differential forms on the
  8 x 8 torus. Its Hodge-Dirac operator squares exactly to the scalar
  Laplacian, its Z_2 grading pairs bosons and fermions at every eigenvalue,
  and its kernel has dimension 4 -- the four directions of emergent
  spacetime.


1.3 The three central theorems
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The paper is organized around three central theorems.

  1. Hodge-Dirac Square (Section 3). On the discrete torus, the Hodge-Dirac
     operator D = d + d* satisfies D^2|_Omega^k = Delta exactly on each form
     degree k = 0, 1, 2, where Delta is the scalar 5-point Laplacian.

  2. Fourteen-Fold Lift (Section 5). The multiplicity-14 eigenvalue
     lambda = 4 of the scalar Laplacian lifts to the full Hodge complex as
     14 Omega^0 + 28 Omega^1 + 14 Omega^2 = 56 modes.

  3. Kernel Dimension (Section 6). By discrete Hodge theory,
     dim ker D = b_0 + b_1 + b_2 = 1 + 2 + 1 = 4. The four harmonic forms
     are the four emergent spacetime directions (see HCSM-03).

2. SETUP: THE SUBSTRATE AND THE FORM DEGREES
================================================================================


2.1 The substrate
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The substrate is the periodic square lattice

    Lambda = Z_8 x Z_8,                                                  (1)

consisting of N = 64 sites arranged in an 8-by-8 grid with periodic boundary
conditions. Site (x_1, x_2) is identified with (x_1 + 8, x_2) and with
(x_1, x_2 + 8); equivalently, the coordinates x_1 and x_2 are each defined
modulo 8. Topologically, Lambda is the torus T^2 = S^1 x S^1.

Each site has four nearest neighbors: (x_1 +/- 1, x_2) and (x_1, x_2 +/- 1),
with all coordinates taken modulo 8. The scalar 5-point Laplacian is

    (Delta f)(x) = 4 f(x) - Sum_{|e|=1} f(x + e),                        (2)

where the sum runs over the four unit lattice vectors
e = (+/-1, 0), (0, +/-1).


2.2 The three form degrees
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

On the two-dimensional torus Lambda, there are three types of differential
forms:

  - Omega^0: 0-forms (scalar functions on the 64 sites),
    dimension dim Omega^0 = 64, bosonic.

  - Omega^1: 1-forms (fields on the 128 directed edges, two components per
    site), dimension dim Omega^1 = 128, fermionic.

  - Omega^2: 2-forms (fields on the 64 plaquettes),
    dimension dim Omega^2 = 64, bosonic.

The total dimension is

    dim(Omega^0 + Omega^1 + Omega^2) = 64 + 128 + 64 = 256.              (3)

We denote by Omega^k the k-form degree, k = 0, 1, 2, and by
Omega^0 + Omega^1 + Omega^2 the full Hodge complex.


2.3 The exterior derivative and the codifferential
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Let e_1 = (1, 0) and e_2 = (0, 1) be the two oriented lattice directions.
The exterior derivative acts on 0-forms by

    (d_0 f)(x, mu) = f(x + e_mu) - f(x),   mu in {1, 2},                 (4)

and on 1-forms by

    (d_1 omega)(x) = omega_1(x) + omega_2(x + e_1)
                   - omega_1(x + e_2) - omega_2(x).                      (5)

The codifferential d* = d^dagger is the adjoint with respect to the standard
Euclidean inner products on Omega^0, Omega^1, Omega^2. Explicitly,

    (d_0* omega)(x) = Sum_mu [ omega_mu(x) - omega_mu(x - e_mu) ],       (6)
    (d_1* chi)(x, mu) = +/- [ chi(x) - chi(x + e_mu) ],                  (7)

with the sign chosen so that d_0* d_1* = 0.

The exterior derivative is nilpotent:

    d_1 d_0 = 0  exactly,                                                (8)

and the codifferential satisfies d_0* d_1* = 0. This nilpotency is the
algebraic foundation of the whole paper.


================================================================================
3. THE HODGE-DIRAC OPERATOR
================================================================================


3.1 Definition
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Definition 3.1 (Hodge-Dirac operator). The Hodge-Dirac operator is

    D = d + d*,                                                          (9)

acting on the full Hodge complex Omega^0 + Omega^1 + Omega^2.

In block form,

         (  0     d_0*    0   )
    D =  (  d_0    0     d_1* )                                        (10)
         (  0     d_1     0   )

a symmetric 256 x 256 real operator.


3.2 The Hodge-Dirac square
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Theorem 3.2 (Hodge-Dirac square). On the discrete torus Lambda, the identity

    D^2|_Omega^k = Delta                                                 (11)

holds exactly on each form degree k = 0, 1, 2, where Delta is the scalar
5-point Laplacian of (2).

Proof. Direct computation. On Omega^0, D^2 reduces to d_0* d_0. Expanding in
components,

    (d_0* d_0 f)(x)
      = Sum_mu [ f(x) - f(x - e_mu) ]
      - Sum_mu [ f(x + e_mu) - f(x) ]
      = 4 f(x) - Sum_{|e|=1} f(x + e),                                  (12)

which is Delta f exactly. The same computation on Omega^1 gives
d_0 d_0* + d_1* d_1 = Delta * Identity_{Omega^1}, using the nilpotency
d_1 d_0 = 0 to remove cross terms. On Omega^2, the identity reduces to
d_1 d_1* = Delta. All three identities are verified at machine precision in
the companion repository.  [Box]

Remark 3.3 (No fermion doubling). Unlike the spinorial Dirac operator on
the same lattice, which satisfies D_spin^2 = (doubled Laplacian) and suffers
from fermion doubling, the Hodge-Dirac square equals the scalar Laplacian
exactly. This is the structural reason HCSM adopts the Hodge complex as its
substrate: the Hodge-Dirac operator evades the doubling problem by
construction. The lattice proves the Nielsen-Ninomiya evasion directly at
the level of the differential-form complex.

4. THE Z_2 GRADING AND THE SUPERCHARGE STRUCTURE
================================================================================


4.1 The parity operator
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Definition 4.1 (Parity operator). Define the parity operator (-1)^F on the
Hodge complex by

    (-1)^F = { +1  on Omega^0 + Omega^2,
             { -1  on Omega^1.                                          (13)

The parity operator Z_2-grades the complex into a bosonic sector
Omega^0 + Omega^2 (even forms, eigenvalue +1) and a fermionic sector
Omega^1 (odd forms, eigenvalue -1).

Proposition 4.2 (D is odd). The Hodge-Dirac operator anticommutes with the
parity operator:

    D (-1)^F = -(-1)^F D.                                               (14)

Proof. D maps Omega^k to Omega^{k +/- 1}, changing the form degree by +/-1
and hence flipping the parity eigenvalue. Direct inspection of the block
form (10) confirms the anticommutation.  [Box]


4.2 Field quantization and statistics
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Assign canonical commutation relations to even-sector modes a_n and
canonical anticommutation relations to odd-sector modes psi_n:

    [a_n, a_m^dagger] = delta_{nm},   {psi_n, psi_m^dagger} = delta_{nm}. (15)

The Hodge-Dirac operator becomes the supercharge

    D = Sum_n sqrt(lambda_n) ( a_n^dagger psi_n + psi_n^dagger a_n ),    (16)

where lambda_n are the eigenvalues of Delta.

Theorem 4.3 (Form-degree statistics). The three sectors of the Hodge complex
carry the following statistics under field quantization:

  - Omega^0 (0-forms): bosonic;
  - Omega^1 (1-forms): fermionic;
  - Omega^2 (2-forms): bosonic.

Proof. The Z_2 grading (-1)^F defines the bosonic (even) and fermionic (odd)
sectors. Quantization with commutators on the even sector and anticommutators
on the odd sector gives the stated statistics. Fermionic statistics has a
geometric origin: it is the Z_2-odd part of the Hodge complex.  [Box]


4.3 The N = 1 supersymmetric quantum mechanics algebra
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Theorem 4.4 (Supercharge structure). With Q = D and H = D^2 / 2 = Delta / 2,
the operators satisfy

    {Q, Q^dagger} = 2H,   Q^2 = (Q^dagger)^2 = 0.                       (17)

This is the standard N = 1 supersymmetric quantum mechanics algebra.

Proof. Q^2 = (d + d*)^2 = d^2 + {d, d*} + (d*)^2. Since d^2 = 0 and
(d*)^2 = 0 by nilpotency, this reduces to Q^2 = {d, d*}. But {d, d*} on each
form degree is the Laplacian Delta, so Q^2 = Delta * Identity = 2H. The
anticommutator {Q, Q^dagger} = 2Q^2 = 4H differs only by the conventional
normalization; with H = Delta / 2 the standard form (17) follows.  [Box]

Corollary 4.5 (Boson-fermion pairing at every eigenvalue). Every bosonic
mode at energy lambda_n has a fermionic partner at the same energy. In
particular, at lambda = 4:

    28 bosons + 28 fermions at lambda = 4.                              (18)

Proof. N = 1 supersymmetric quantum mechanics pairs every bosonic eigenstate
of H at energy E with a fermionic eigenstate at the same energy, unless the
eigenstate is annihilated by Q or Q^dagger. The lambda = 4 sector is not in
the kernel of Q (it is a nonzero eigenvalue of D^2), so the pairing is
exact.  [Box]

5. SPECTRUM AND THE 14-FOLD LIFT
================================================================================


5.1 The scalar Laplacian spectrum
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The scalar Laplacian (2) on the 8 x 8 torus has eigenvalues

    lambda(n_1, n_2) = 4 - 2 cos(pi n_1 / 4) - 2 cos(pi n_2 / 4),       (19)

with (n_1, n_2) in {0, ..., 7}^2. The spectrum is mirror-symmetric under
lambda <-> 8 - lambda, with 13 distinct eigenvalues. The self-paired
eigenvalue lambda = 4 has multiplicity 14, corresponding to the 14 modes

    { (0,4), (4,0) },
    { (1,3), (1,5), (3,1), (3,7), (5,1), (5,7), (7,3), (7,5) },         (20)
    { (2,2), (2,6), (6,2), (6,6) }.

These decompose under the D4 point group of the square into three orbits of
sizes (2, 8, 4) (see HCSM-04).


5.2 The lift to the Hodge complex
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Theorem 5.1 (Fourteen-fold lift). The 14-fold degeneracy of the scalar
Laplacian at lambda = 4 lifts to the Hodge complex as

    14 modes in Omega^0,   28 modes in Omega^1,   14 modes in Omega^2. (21)

The total dimension of the lambda = 4 sector is

    14 Omega^0 + 28 Omega^1 + 14 Omega^2 = 56.                          (22)

Proof. Since D^2|_Omega^k = Delta by Theorem 3.2, the spectrum of D^2 on
Omega^k is the scalar Laplacian spectrum with the indicated multiplicities.
The Laplacian has multiplicity 14 at lambda = 4 on Omega^0; on Omega^1, each
site has two 1-form components, so the multiplicity doubles to 28; on
Omega^2, the multiplicity is again 14. Summing gives 56.  [Box]

Remark 5.2 (Uniqueness of L = 8). On an L x L torus, the multiplicity at the
self-paired eigenvalue is exactly 2(L - 1). Setting this equal to 14 gives
L = 8. The torus size is therefore selected by the requirement that one
Standard Model generation sits at the self-paired eigenvalue, not chosen by
hand.

6. THE KERNEL OF THE HODGE-DIRAC OPERATOR
================================================================================


6.1 Discrete Hodge theory
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Theorem 6.1 (Kernel dimension). The kernel of the Hodge-Dirac operator is
isomorphic to the de Rham cohomology of the torus:

    ker D  ~=  DirectSum_{k=0,1,2} H^k(T^2; R),                         (23)

with dimension

    dim ker D = b_0 + b_1 + b_2 = 1 + 2 + 1 = 4,                        (24)

where (b_0, b_1, b_2) are the Betti numbers of the torus.

Proof. By the discrete Hodge theorem (Dodziuk 1976 [7]), the kernel of
D = d + d* is isomorphic to the direct sum of the de Rham cohomology groups
H^k(T^2; R) for k = 0, 1, 2. The Betti numbers of the torus are b_0 = 1,
b_1 = 2, b_2 = 1, so dim ker D = 4.  [Box]

Remark 6.2 (The four harmonic forms). The four harmonic forms are explicit:

  - the constant 0-form v_0 (spanning H^0);
  - the two independent harmonic 1-forms v_1^A, v_1^B (spanning H^1);
  - the constant 2-form v_2 (spanning H^2).

These four forms are the four emergent spacetime directions (see HCSM-03
for the Lorentzian-signature derivation).

Corollary 6.3 (Emergent dimension). The dimension of the emergent spacetime
is d = dim ker D = 4. The Hodge complex selects four dimensions
intrinsically.  [Box]

7. THE D4 DECOMPOSITION OF THE LAMBDA = 4 SECTOR
================================================================================


7.1 The D4 action on the Hodge complex
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The dihedral group D4 of order 8 acts on Lambda = Z_8 x Z_8 by the point
group of the square, generated by the rotation

    r : (x_1, x_2) |-> (-x_2, x_1)                                      (25)

and the reflection

    s : (x_1, x_2) |-> (x_1, -x_2),                                     (26)

subject to the presentation r^4 = s^2 = (rs)^2 = e.

The action on the plane-wave basis v_k(x) = exp(i pi k . x / 4) is by
permutation of the momentum labels k in {0, ..., 7}^2. The action on Omega^1
is the vector-twisted version: the components (omega_1, omega_2) transform
as

    r : (omega_1, omega_2) |-> (-omega_2, omega_1),
    s : (omega_1, omega_2) |-> (omega_1, -omega_2).                     (27)

As a D4 representation, Omega^1 = Omega^0 tensor E, where E is the
two-dimensional irreducible representation of D4.


7.2 The D4 decomposition
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Theorem 7.1 (D4 decomposition of the lambda = 4 sector). As D4
representations, the three form degrees at lambda = 4 decompose as

    Omega^0|_lambda=4 = 3A_1 + A_2 + 2B_1 + 2B_2 + 3E,                  (28)
    Omega^1|_lambda=4 = Omega^0|_lambda=4 tensor E
                      = 3A_1 + 3A_2 + 3B_1 + 3B_2 + 8E,                (29)
    Omega^2|_lambda=4 = 3A_1 + A_2 + 2B_1 + 2B_2 + 3E.                  (30)

The full 56-dimensional sector decomposes as

    56 = 9A_1 + 5A_2 + 7B_1 + 7B_2 + 14E.                               (31)

Proof. Direct computation of the traces of the permutation matrices R (for
r) and S (for s) on the lambda = 4 sector, followed by multiplicity
computation via the D4 character table. The tensor-product identity
Omega^1|_lambda=4 = Omega^0|_lambda=4 tensor E follows from the
vector-twisted action (27). All claims are verified at machine precision in
the companion repository.  [Box]

Remark 7.2 (Character of the 14-mode multiplet). The character of
Omega^0|_lambda=4 is

    chi(e) = 14,  chi(r^2) = 2,  chi(r) = 0,  chi(s) = 2,  chi(rs) = 2. (32)

The vanishing of chi(r) is a structural feature: the cyclic rotation r has
no fixed content on the multiplet. This is the geometric reason D4 acts
effectively as Z_2 x Z_2 on the 14 modes (see HCSM-04 for the orbit
interpretation).

Remark 7.3 (The tensor-product structure). The identity
Omega^1|_lambda=4 = Omega^0|_lambda=4 tensor E is a structural consequence
of the Hodge construction: the 1-form components (e_1, e_2) transform in the
vector representation E, so the Hodge complex extends the scalar D4
representation by a single tensor factor of E in odd degree. This tensor
structure is the geometric origin of the boson-fermion pairing.

9. CONCLUSION
================================================================================

This paper has established the Hodge complex of differential forms on the
periodic 8 x 8 torus as the algebraic substrate of the Hodge Complex
Standard Model. The Hodge-Dirac operator D = d + d* satisfies D^2 = Delta
exactly on each form degree, in contrast to the spinorial Dirac operator on
the same lattice. The complex carries a canonical Z_2 grading -- even forms
bosonic, odd forms fermionic -- and realizes an N = 1 supersymmetric quantum
mechanics with D as supercharge.

Three structural theorems anchor the paper. The 14-fold degeneracy at
lambda = 4 lifts to 14 Omega^0 + 28 Omega^1 + 14 Omega^2 = 56 modes. The
kernel of D has dimension b_0 + b_1 + b_2 = 4, giving the four emergent
spacetime directions. The D4 decomposition of the lambda = 4 sector is
56 = 9A_1 + 5A_2 + 7B_1 + 7B_2 + 14E, with
Omega^1|_lambda=4 = Omega^0|_lambda=4 tensor E as a tensor-product identity.

The Hodge complex is the substrate on which every downstream HCSM result is
built. It is D4-symmetric, entropic (see HCSM-12 for its flat thermal
output), and provides the 14-mode scalar multiplet at lambda = 4 that the
FDLM/WIN processor reads out.
