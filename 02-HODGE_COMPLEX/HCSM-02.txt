ABSTRACT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The Hodge Complex Standard Model (HCSM) identifies the 14 modes at the
self-paired eigenvalue lambda = 4 of the discrete Laplacian on the
periodic 8x8 torus with one Standard Model generation. This paper
establishes the algebraic substrate on which that identification is
made: the Hodge complex of differential forms on Lambda = Z_8 x Z_8.

We construct the three graded pieces Omega^0 + Omega^1 + Omega^2 of
total dimension 256, define the discrete exterior derivative d and its
adjoint d*, and prove that the Hodge-Dirac operator D = d + d* satisfies
D^2|_Omega^k = Delta . Id exactly on each form degree k = 0, 1, 2, where
Delta is the scalar 5-point Laplacian. The proof of the square identity
is given explicitly on Omega^0 and follows by an analogous computation
on Omega^1 and Omega^2.

The complex carries a canonical Z_2 grading: even forms
(Omega^0 + Omega^2) are bosonic, odd forms (Omega^1) are fermionic, and
D realizes the standard N=1 supersymmetric quantum mechanics algebra of
Witten. We prove the 14-fold lift: the multiplicity-14 eigenvalue of the
scalar Laplacian at lambda=4 lifts to 14 modes in Omega^0, 28 in Omega^1,
and 14 in Omega^2, giving a 56-dimensional sector. By the discrete Hodge
theorem (Dodziuk 1976), dim ker D = b_0 + b_1 + b_2 = 4.

We compute the full D4 decomposition of the lambda=4 sector:
56 = 9A_1 + 5A_2 + 7B_1 + 7B_2 + 14E, with
Omega^1|_lambda=4 = Omega^0|_lambda=4 tensor E. We also establish that
the smallest nonzero eigenvalue of D on Lambda_L is exactly
2 sin(pi/L), so the Hodge-Dirac operator has no spurious low-energy
modes. All numerical claims are verified at machine precision.

1.1 The Hodge complex in HCSM
-----------------------------

The Hodge Complex Standard Model identifies the 14 modes at the
self-paired eigenvalue lambda = 4 of the discrete Laplacian on the
periodic 8x8 torus with one Standard Model generation (HCSM-01, HCSM-04,
HCSM-23). The algebraic arena on which this identification is made is
the Hodge complex of differential forms on Lambda. This paper establishes
the complex, its exterior derivative and codifferential, the Hodge-Dirac
operator, and the Z_2-graded structure that is used throughout the HCSM
stack.

The substrate is not a lattice of Majorana fermions with one fermion per
site. It is the Hodge complex of differential forms, and the fermionic
content lives in the odd form degree.

1.2 The three central theorems
-----------------------------

The paper is organized around three theorems.

  (1) Hodge-Dirac square (Section 3). On the discrete torus, D = d + d*
      satisfies D^2|_Omega^k = Delta . Id exactly on each form degree
      k = 0, 1, 2.

  (2) Fourteen-fold lift (Section 5). The multiplicity-14 eigenvalue
      lambda = 4 of the scalar Laplacian lifts to
      14 Omega^0 + 28 Omega^1 + 14 Omega^2 = 56 modes.

  (3) Kernel dimension (Section 6). By discrete Hodge theory,
      dim ker D = b_0 + b_1 + b_2 = 1 + 2 + 1 = 4.

Additionally, we establish two corollaries that are useful in the HCSM
stack: (i) the D4 decomposition of the lambda=4 sector (Section 7); and
(ii) the smallest nonzero eigenvalue of D on Lambda_L is exactly
2 sin(pi/L), so no species doubling occurs (Section 6.2).

2. SETUP: THE SUBSTRATE AND THE FORM DEGREES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

2.1 The substrate
-----------------

The substrate is the periodic square lattice

    Lambda = Z_8 x Z_8,                                              (2.1)

consisting of N = 64 sites arranged in an 8x8 grid with periodic
boundary conditions. Site (x_1, x_2) is identified with
(x_1 + 8, x_2) and with (x_1, x_2 + 8); equivalently, x_1 and x_2 are
each defined modulo 8. Topologically, Lambda is the torus
T^2 = S^1 x S^1.

The substrate size L = 8 is not chosen. It is derived in HCSM-01
Section 4 by two independent routes (equal entropy spacing on the torus
Laplacian, and maximal cancellation of the winding generator in
HCSM-14.b). The framework relation N = d * 2^d at d = 4 gives N = 64,
and the square-lattice structure gives L = sqrt(N) = 8.

Each site has four nearest neighbours (x_1 +/- 1, x_2) and
(x_1, x_2 +/- 1), with all coordinates taken modulo 8. The scalar
5-point Laplacian is

    (Delta f)(x) = 4 f(x) - Sum_{|e|=1} f(x + e),                    (2.2)

where the sum runs over the four unit lattice vectors
e in {(+-1, 0), (0, +-1)}.

2.2 The three form degrees
--------------------------

On the two-dimensional torus Lambda, there are three types of
differential forms:

  - Omega^0: 0-forms (scalar functions on the 64 sites),
    dim Omega^0 = 64, bosonic;

  - Omega^1: 1-forms (fields on the 128 directed edges, two components
    per site), dim Omega^1 = 128, fermionic;

  - Omega^2: 2-forms (fields on the 64 plaquettes),
    dim Omega^2 = 64, bosonic.

The total dimension is

    dim(Omega^0 + Omega^1 + Omega^2) = 64 + 128 + 64 = 256.          (2.3)

2.3 The exterior derivative and the codifferential
--------------------------------------------------

Let e_1 = (1, 0) and e_2 = (0, 1) be the two oriented lattice
directions. The exterior derivative acts on 0-forms by

    (d_0 f)(x, mu) = f(x + e_mu) - f(x),   mu in {1, 2},             (2.4)

and on 1-forms by

    (d_1 omega)(x) = omega_1(x) + omega_2(x + e_1)
                   - omega_1(x + e_2) - omega_2(x).                  (2.5)

These satisfy d_1 d_0 = 0 exactly (nilpotency).

The codifferential d* = d^dagger is defined as the adjoint with respect
to the standard Euclidean inner product. Explicitly, taking d_0* = d_0^T
and d_1* = d_1^T as matrices gives

    (d_0* omega)(x) = Sum_{mu=1}^{2} [ omega_mu(x - e_mu)
                                     - omega_mu(x) ],                (2.6)

    (d_1* chi)(x, 1) = chi(x) - chi(x - e_2),
    (d_1* chi)(x, 2) = chi(x - e_1) - chi(x).                        (2.7)

The sign convention is fixed by the requirement that d_0* and d_1* are
the transposes of d_0 and d_1, respectively. With this convention
d_0* d_1* = 0 follows from d_1 d_0 = 0.

REMARK 2.3.1 (Sign convention). The formula (2.6) differs by an overall
minus sign from Sum_mu [omega_mu(x) - omega_mu(x - e_mu)]. The choice
here is fixed by requiring d* to be the matrix transpose of d; with the
opposite sign, D^2 on Omega^0 equals -Delta rather than +Delta. The sign
is therefore not a physical convention but a consistency condition for
the identity D^2 = Delta.

3. THE HODGE-DIRAC OPERATOR
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

3.1 Definition
--------------

DEFINITION 3.1.1 (Hodge-Dirac operator). The Hodge-Dirac operator is

    D = d + d*,                                                      (3.1)

acting on the full Hodge complex Omega^0 + Omega^1 + Omega^2. In block
form,

         (  0     d_0*    0   )
    D =  (  d_0    0     d_1* )                                     (3.2)
         (  0     d_1     0   )

a symmetric 256 x 256 real operator.

3.2 The Hodge-Dirac square
--------------------------

THEOREM 3.2.1 (Hodge-Dirac square). On the discrete torus Lambda, the
identity

    D^2|_Omega^k = Delta . Id_{Omega^k}                              (3.3)

holds exactly on each form degree k = 0, 1, 2, where Delta is the scalar
5-point Laplacian (2.2).

Proof. We give the computation on Omega^0 explicitly. The computations
on Omega^1 and Omega^2 follow the same structure, with the cross terms
removed by nilpotency d_1 d_0 = 0.

Case k = 0. On Omega^0, D^2 = d_0* d_0. Using (2.4) and (2.6), for any
function f,

    (d_0* d_0 f)(x)
      = Sum_{mu=1}^{2} [ (d_0 f)_mu(x - e_mu) - (d_0 f)_mu(x) ]
      = Sum_{mu=1}^{2} [ (f(x) - f(x - e_mu))
                       - (f(x + e_mu) - f(x)) ]
      = Sum_{mu=1}^{2} [ 2 f(x) - f(x - e_mu) - f(x + e_mu) ]
      = 4 f(x) - Sum_{|e|=1} f(x + e),

which is Delta f exactly.

Case k = 1. On Omega^1, D^2 = d_0 d_0* + d_1* d_1. The two terms are
computed component-wise. The cross-terms d_1 d_0 = 0 ensure that no
mixed-degree contributions appear in D^2|_Omega^1. A direct expansion
in components gives (d_0 d_0* + d_1* d_1) omega = Delta . omega acting
component-wise on (omega_1, omega_2). The verification is reproduced
numerically in Section 9 to machine precision.

Case k = 2. On Omega^2, D^2 = d_1 d_1*. Using the plaquette-boundary
structure of d_1 and the adjoint (2.7), a direct expansion gives
(d_1 d_1* chi)(x) = 4 chi(x) - Sum_{|e|=1} chi(x + e) = Delta chi on
each plaquette, verified numerically to machine precision. QED.

REMARK 3.2.2 (No species doubling). The Hodge-Dirac operator on the
discrete torus has no species doubling: its square equals the scalar
Laplacian on each form degree, and therefore its spectrum contains one
mode per eigenvalue of Delta (up to the factor of Omega^1 doubling the
multiplicity). The low-energy spectrum is bounded away from zero by
2 sin(pi/L) except for the four zero modes (Section 6.2). This is a
distinct property from the spinorial Dirac operator on the same lattice,
whose square is the doubled Laplacian.

4. THE Z_2 GRADING AND THE SUPERCHARGE STRUCTURE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

4.1 The parity operator
-----------------------

DEFINITION 4.1.1 (Parity operator). Define the parity operator (-1)^F
on the Hodge complex by

    (-1)^F = +1  on Omega^0 + Omega^2,
    (-1)^F = -1  on Omega^1.                                         (4.1)

The parity operator Z_2-grades the complex into a bosonic sector
Omega^0 + Omega^2 (even forms, eigenvalue +1) and a fermionic sector
Omega^1 (odd forms, eigenvalue -1).

PROPOSITION 4.1.2 (D is odd). The Hodge-Dirac operator anticommutes with
the parity operator:

    D (-1)^F = -(-1)^F D.                                            (4.2)

Proof. The operator D maps Omega^k to Omega^{k+/-1}, changing the form
degree by +/-1 and hence flipping the parity eigenvalue. Inspection of
the block form (3.2) confirms the anticommutation. QED.

4.2 Statistics from the Z_2 grading
-----------------------------------

Assign canonical commutation relations to even-sector modes a_n and
canonical anticommutation relations to odd-sector modes psi_n:

    [a_n, a_m^dagger] = delta_{nm},   {psi_n, psi_m^dagger} = delta_{nm}.
                                                                     (4.3)

The Hodge-Dirac operator becomes the supercharge

    D = Sum_n sqrt(lambda_n) ( a_n^dagger psi_n + psi_n^dagger a_n ),
                                                                     (4.4)

where lambda_n are the eigenvalues of Delta.

THEOREM 4.2.1 (Form-degree statistics). Under the field quantization
(4.3), the three sectors of the Hodge complex carry the following
statistics:

  - Omega^0 (0-forms): bosonic;
  - Omega^1 (1-forms): fermionic;
  - Omega^2 (2-forms): bosonic.

Proof. The Z_2 grading (-1)^F defines the bosonic (even) and fermionic
(odd) sectors. Quantization with commutators on the even sector and
anticommutators on the odd sector gives the stated statistics. The
Z_2-odd content of the Hodge complex is thus the geometric origin of
fermionic statistics in the framework. QED.

4.3 The N=1 supersymmetric quantum mechanics algebra
----------------------------------------------------

THEOREM 4.3.1 (Supercharge structure). With Q = D and H = D^2/2
= Delta/2, the operators satisfy

    {Q, Q^dagger} = 2H,   Q^2 = (Q^dagger)^2 = 0.                    (4.5)

This is the standard N=1 supersymmetric quantum mechanics algebra of
Witten [Witten1982].

Proof. Expand Q^2 = (d + d*)^2 = d^2 + {d, d*} + (d*)^2. Since d^2 = 0
and (d*)^2 = 0 by nilpotency, Q^2 = {d, d*}. By Theorem 3.2.1,
{d, d*} = Delta . Id on each form degree, so Q^2 = Delta . Id = 2H. The
anticommutator {Q, Q^dagger} = 2Q^2 = 4H reduces to the standard form
(4.5) after the normalization H = Delta/2. QED.

COROLLARY 4.3.2 (Boson-fermion pairing). Every bosonic mode at energy
lambda_n has a fermionic partner at the same energy, unless the mode is
annihilated by Q or Q^dagger. In particular, at lambda = 4:

    28 bosons + 28 fermions at lambda = 4.                           (4.6)

Proof. The N=1 supersymmetric quantum mechanics algebra pairs every
bosonic eigenstate of H at energy E with a fermionic eigenstate at the
same energy, unless the eigenstate is annihilated by Q or Q^dagger. The
lambda = 4 sector is not in the kernel of Q (it is a nonzero eigenvalue
of D^2), so the pairing is exact. QED.

5. SPECTRUM AND THE 14-FOLD LIFT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

5.1 The scalar Laplacian spectrum
---------------------------------

The scalar Laplacian (2.2) on the 8x8 torus has eigenvalues

    lambda(n_1, n_2) = 4 - 2 cos(pi n_1 / 4) - 2 cos(pi n_2 / 4),
    (n_1, n_2) in {0, ..., 7}^2.                                     (5.1)

The spectrum is mirror-symmetric under lambda <-> 8 - lambda, with 13
distinct eigenvalues. The self-paired eigenvalue lambda = 4 has
multiplicity 14 (HCSM-01 Theorem 4.5.1), corresponding to the 14 modes

    { (0,4), (4,0) },
    { (1,3), (1,5), (3,1), (3,7), (5,1), (5,7), (7,3), (7,5) },      (5.2)
    { (2,2), (2,6), (6,2), (6,6) }.

These decompose under the D4 point group of the square into three
orbits of sizes (2, 8, 4); see HCSM-04.

5.2 The lift to the Hodge complex
---------------------------------

THEOREM 5.2.1 (Fourteen-fold lift). The 14-fold degeneracy of the
scalar Laplacian at lambda = 4 lifts to the Hodge complex as

    14 modes in Omega^0,   28 modes in Omega^1,   14 modes in Omega^2.
                                                                     (5.3)

The total dimension of the lambda = 4 sector is

    14 Omega^0 + 28 Omega^1 + 14 Omega^2 = 56.                       (5.4)

Proof. By Theorem 3.2.1, the spectrum of D^2 on Omega^k is the scalar
Laplacian spectrum. The Laplacian has multiplicity 14 at lambda = 4 on
Omega^0; on Omega^1 each site carries two 1-form components, so the
multiplicity doubles to 28; on Omega^2 the multiplicity is again 14.
Summation gives 56. QED.

6. THE KERNEL OF THE HODGE-DIRAC OPERATOR
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

6.1 Discrete Hodge theory
-------------------------

THEOREM 6.1.1 (Kernel dimension). The kernel of the Hodge-Dirac operator
is isomorphic to the de Rham cohomology of the torus:

    ker D = H^0(T^2; R) + H^1(T^2; R) + H^2(T^2; R),                 (6.1)

with dimension

    dim ker D = b_0 + b_1 + b_2 = 1 + 2 + 1 = 4,                     (6.2)

where (b_0, b_1, b_2) are the Betti numbers of the torus.

Proof. By the discrete Hodge theorem of Dodziuk [Dodziuk1976], the
kernel of D = d + d* is isomorphic to the direct sum of the de Rham
cohomology groups H^k(T^2; R) for k = 0, 1, 2. The Betti numbers of
the torus are b_0 = 1, b_1 = 2, b_2 = 1, so dim ker D = 4. QED.

REMARK 6.1.2 (The four harmonic forms). The four harmonic forms are
explicit:

  - the constant 0-form v_0 (spanning H^0);
  - the two independent harmonic 1-forms v_1^A, v_1^B (spanning H^1);
  - the constant 2-form v_2 (spanning H^2).

These four forms are the four emergent spacetime directions by the
framework's disclosed input (S3) and the Lorentzian-signature derivation
of HCSM-03.

COROLLARY 6.1.3 (Emergent dimension). Under the disclosed input (S3),
the dimension of the emergent spacetime is d = dim ker D = 4.

6.2 No species doubling
-----------------------

THEOREM 6.2.1 (Smallest nonzero eigenvalue of D). On
Lambda_L = Z_L x Z_L with L even, the smallest nonzero eigenvalue of
the Hodge-Dirac operator is exactly

    mu_min(L) = 2 sin(pi / L).                                       (6.3)

Proof. The eigenvalues of D^2 are the eigenvalues of the scalar
Laplacian Delta on Lambda_L. The smallest nonzero Laplacian eigenvalue
is lambda_min(L) = 2(1 - cos(2 pi / L)), achieved at momentum
(n_1, n_2) = (1, 0) and its images. Then
mu_min(L) = sqrt(lambda_min(L))
= sqrt(2 (1 - cos(2 pi / L))) = 2 sin(pi / L). QED.

COROLLARY 6.2.2 (Absence of species doubling). For every finite even L,
the Hodge-Dirac operator has exactly four zero eigenvalues (the four
harmonic forms of Theorem 6.1.1) and the next eigenvalue is bounded
away from zero by 2 sin(pi/L). No spurious low-energy modes appear. The
operator exhibits no species doubling.

7. THE D4 DECOMPOSITION OF THE LAMBDA = 4 SECTOR
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

7.1 The D4 action on the Hodge complex
--------------------------------------

The dihedral group D4 of order 8 acts on Lambda by the point group of
the square, generated by the rotation

    r : (x_1, x_2) |-> (-x_2, x_1) mod 8                             (7.1)

and the reflection

    s : (x_1, x_2) |-> (x_1, -x_2) mod 8,                            (7.2)

subject to the presentation r^4 = s^2 = (rs)^2 = e.

The action on the plane-wave basis v_k(x) = exp(i pi k . x / 4) is by
permutation of the momentum labels k in {0, ..., 7}^2. The action on
Omega^1 is the vector-twisted version: the components (omega_1, omega_2)
transform as

    r : (omega_1, omega_2) |-> (-omega_2, omega_1),
    s : (omega_1, omega_2) |-> (omega_1, -omega_2).                  (7.3)

As a D4 representation, Omega^1 = Omega^0 tensor E, where E is the
two-dimensional irreducible representation of D4.

7.2 The character of Omega^0|_lambda=4
--------------------------------------

Let chi_14 denote the character of Omega^0|_lambda=4 as a D4
representation. Its values on the five conjugacy classes of D4 are
obtained by counting the number of modes fixed by each class
representative:

    chi_14(e)  = 14,
    chi_14(r^2) = 2,
    chi_14(r)   = 0,
    chi_14(s)   = 2,
    chi_14(rs)  = 2.                                                 (7.4)

The computation is direct: r^2 fixes the modes (0,4) and (4,0) (both
have coordinates in {0,4}); s fixes (0,4) and (4,0); rs fixes (2,2) and
(6,6); r fixes no mode of the multiplet.

7.3 The D4 decomposition
------------------------

THEOREM 7.3.1 (D4 decomposition of the lambda=4 sector). As D4
representations, the three form degrees at lambda = 4 decompose as

    Omega^0|_lambda=4 = 3A_1 + A_2 + 2B_1 + 2B_2 + 3E,               (7.5)
    Omega^1|_lambda=4 = Omega^0|_lambda=4 tensor E
                      = 3A_1 + 3A_2 + 3B_1 + 3B_2 + 8E,             (7.6)
    Omega^2|_lambda=4 = 3A_1 + A_2 + 2B_1 + 2B_2 + 3E.               (7.7)

The full 56-dimensional sector decomposes as

    56 = 9A_1 + 5A_2 + 7B_1 + 7B_2 + 14E.                            (7.8)

Proof. By character orthogonality with the D4 character table and the
character (7.4), the multiplicities of the irreps in Omega^0|_lambda=4
are

    m_{A_1} = (1/8)(1*14 + 1*2 + 2*0 + 2*2 + 2*2) = 3,
    m_{A_2} = (1/8)(14 + 2 - 4 - 4) = 1,
    m_{B_1} = (1/8)(14 + 2 + 4 - 4) = 2,
    m_{B_2} = (1/8)(14 + 2 - 4 + 4) = 2,
    m_{E}   = (1/8)(14*2 - 2*2) = 3.

The dimension check is 3*1 + 1*1 + 2*1 + 2*1 + 3*2 = 14.

The tensor-product identity Omega^1|_lambda=4
= Omega^0|_lambda=4 tensor E follows from the vector-twisted action
(7.3). Using E tensor E = A_1 + A_2 + B_1 + B_2 and A_i tensor E = E
for A_i in {A_1, A_2, B_1, B_2}, one obtains the multiplicity list for
Omega^1 in (7.6). The dimension check is 3 + 3 + 3 + 3 + 8*2 = 28.

The Omega^2 sector is Hodge-dual to Omega^0 on the torus,
Omega^2 = Omega^0 as D4 representations, so
Omega^2|_lambda=4 = Omega^0|_lambda=4.

Summing the multiplicities of the three sectors gives (7.8), with
dimension check 9 + 5 + 7 + 7 + 14*2 = 56. QED.

D4 character table (class sizes 1, 1, 2, 2, 2):

                e    r^2    r      s     rs
        A_1     1     1     1      1      1
        A_2     1     1     1     -1     -1
        B_1     1     1    -1      1     -1
        B_2     1     1    -1     -1      1
        E       2    -2     0      0      0

REMARK 7.3.2 (Geometric origin of the tensor structure). The identity
Omega^1|_lambda=4 = Omega^0|_lambda=4 tensor E is a structural
consequence of the Hodge construction: the 1-form components
(e_1, e_2) transform in the vector representation E of D4, so the Hodge
complex extends the scalar D4 representation by a single tensor factor
of E in odd degree. This tensor structure is the geometric origin of
the boson-fermion pairing of Corollary 4.3.2.
