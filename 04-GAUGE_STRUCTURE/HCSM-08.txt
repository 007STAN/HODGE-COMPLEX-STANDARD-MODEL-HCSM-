## Abstract

The Hodge Complex Standard Model derives the gauge algebra su(3) ⊕ su(2) ⊕ u(1)^5 as a Lie subalgebra of the D_4 commutant on the 14-mode multiplet at λ = 4, derives the hypercharge functional on the 15-dimensional sector-coarse space, and derives the particle content of one Standard Model generation. This paper proves that the resulting hypercharge functional is anomaly-free.

On the Standard Model content per generation, the four one-loop anomaly conditions

    Tr(Y) = 0,  Tr(Y^3) = 0,  SU(2)^2 U(1) = 0,  SU(3)^2 U(1) = 0

vanish identically in rational arithmetic. The three cubic gauge anomalies SU(3)^3, SU(2)^3, and the mixed SU(3)–SU(2)–U(1) vanish by representation-theoretic structure: SU(3)^3 by the vector-like color content, SU(2)^3 by the safety of SU(2), and the mixed trilinear anomaly by the tracelessness of the non-Abelian generators. The Witten global SU(2) anomaly is evaded because each generation carries an even number of SU(2) doublets. Two-loop cancellation is automatic by the Adler–Bardeen theorem. In the sector-coarse framing, the mixed SU(3)^2 U(1) condition reduces to the weighted sector identity Y_0 + 2Y_1 + Y_2 = 0, satisfied by the HCSM hypercharge values (−2/3, +1/6, +1/3) on the A_1 block.

The anomaly cancellation is a consistency theorem of the HCSM structure. The framework's particle content is derived; the hypercharge functional is derived; the anomaly cancellation is a theorem. All claims are verified at machine precision and are exact in rational arithmetic.

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 The anomaly cancellation problem

A gauge theory with chiral fermions is consistent only if its anomaly coefficients vanish. The Standard Model satisfies this requirement by a delicate interplay between its fermion content and its hypercharge assignments. In the Standard Model, the hypercharge values are empirical inputs, chosen so that the anomaly conditions hold.

The Hodge Complex Standard Model (HCSM) derives the gauge algebra

    g_HCSM = su(3) ⊕ su(2) ⊕ u(1)^5

as a Lie subalgebra of the D_4 commutant on the 14-mode multiplet at λ = 4 [HCSM-06]. On the full 56-dimensional Hodge sector, the hypercharge functional is a specific element of the 15-dimensional sector-coarse space [HCSM-07]:

    Y = Σ_{ρ ∈ {A1, A2, B1, B2, E}} (α_ρ I + β_ρ F + γ_ρ O) P_ρ,

with coefficients for the A_1 and B_1 blocks determined by the particle content and the sector assignment:

    (α_{A1}, β_{A1}, γ_{A1}) = (0, −1/6, −1/2),
    (α_{B1}, β_{B1}, γ_{B1}) = (0, +1/2, +1/2).

This paper proves that the HCSM hypercharge functional is anomaly-free. The proof is a consistency theorem of the HCSM structure: given the particle content derived in the HCSM stack, the hypercharge functional derived in [HCSM-07] satisfies all known anomaly cancellation conditions.

### 1.2 Main results

1. **Linear (gravitational) anomaly** (Theorem 3.1). Tr(Y) = 0 exactly on the Standard Model content per generation.
2. **Cubic U(1) anomaly** (Theorem 4.1). Tr(Y^3) = 0 exactly.
3. **Mixed SU(2)^2 U(1) anomaly** (Theorem 5.1). SU(2)^2 U(1) = 0 exactly.
4. **Mixed SU(3)^2 U(1) anomaly** (Theorem 6.1). SU(3)^2 U(1) = 0 exactly.
5. **Cubic gauge anomalies** (Theorem 7.1). SU(3)^3 = SU(2)^3 = SU(3)–SU(2)–U(1) = 0 by representation-theoretic structure.
6. **Witten global anomaly** (Theorem 8.1). The global SU(2) anomaly is evaded; each generation carries an even number of SU(2) doublets.
7. **Two-loop automatic** (Theorem 9.1). By the Adler–Bardeen theorem, vanishing one-loop conditions imply vanishing at all loops.
8. **Sector-coarse identity** (Proposition 10.1). The mixed SU(3)^2 U(1) condition takes the sector-coarse form Y_0 + 2Y_1 + Y_2 = 0.

## 2. Setup

### 2.1 The state content

The Standard Model content per generation, in all-left-handed convention, is

    Q_L(3, 2, +1/6) × 6,   u_R^c(3, 1, −2/3) × 3,   d_R^c(3, 1, +1/3) × 3,
    L_L(1, 2, −1/2) × 2,   e_R^c(1, 1, +1) × 1,     ν_R^c(1, 1, 0) × 1.

Here the notation is X(SU(3), SU(2), Y) × n with n the multiplicity.

The content is realized on the HCSM state space as follows. The color-triplet states u_R^c, Q_L, d_R^c occupy the A_1 multiplicity space of the 56-dimensional Hodge sector at λ = 4 [HCSM-06]. The weak-doublet states Q_L, L_L occupy the B_1 multiplicity space [HCSM-06]. The hypercharge values are the HCSM hypercharge functional evaluated on the corresponding sector [HCSM-07].

### 2.2 The HCSM hypercharge values

By [HCSM-07], the hypercharge functional has the sector values

    Y_{A1}: (Ω^0, Ω^1, Ω^2) = (−2/3, +1/6, +1/3),
    Y_{B1}:                  (+1, −1/2, 0).

These are the values assigned to the HCSM sectors, matching the Standard Model content under the sector assignment of [HCSM-07].

**Remark 2.1 (Sector-coarse reading of the content).** The four multiplets u_R^c, Q_L, d_R^c (color-triplet) are assigned to the three Hodge sectors Ω^0, Ω^1, Ω^2 respectively. The three multiplets e_R^c, L_L, ν_R^c (leptons) carry the B_1 hypercharge values +1, −1/2, 0 on the same three sectors.

### 2.3 The four one-loop anomaly conditions

The four one-loop anomaly conditions for a SU(3)_c × SU(2)_L × U(1)_Y chiral gauge theory are

    Tr(Y) = 0                  (linear / gravitational),
    Tr(Y^3) = 0                (cubic U(1)),
    SU(2)^2 U(1) = 0           (mixed electroweak),
    SU(3)^2 U(1) = 0           (mixed color).

The traces run over the chiral fermion content per generation. With the content above, each condition is a rational-arithmetic sum that must vanish for the theory to be consistent.

### 2.4 The three cubic gauge anomalies

The cubic gauge anomalies are

    SU(3)^3,   SU(2)^3,   SU(3)–SU(2)–U(1).

These vanish by the structure of the Standard Model content and the safety of the gauge groups, as established in Section 7.

## 3. The linear (gravitational) anomaly

**Theorem 3.1 (Linear anomaly).** The linear (gravitational) anomaly vanishes identically on the Standard Model content per generation:

    Tr(Y) = 6 · (1/6) + 3 · (−2/3) + 3 · (1/3) + 2 · (−1/2) + 1 · 1 + 1 · 0 = 0.

**Proof.** Direct evaluation of the sum. Each term contributes the hypercharge of a state, weighted by the multiplicity of that state:

    6 · (1/6) = 1               (Q_L),
    3 · (−2/3) = −2             (u_R^c),
    3 · (1/3) = 1               (d_R^c),
    2 · (−1/2) = −1             (L_L),
    1 · 1 = 1                   (e_R^c),
    1 · 0 = 0                   (ν_R^c).

The sum is 1 − 2 + 1 − 1 + 1 + 0 = 0. All arithmetic is exact rational. □

**Remark 3.1 (Sector-coarse reading).** The vanishing Tr(Y) = 0 is a statement about the sum of Y over the HCSM state content. In the sector-coarse framing (Proposition 10.1), it is the statement that the total hypercharge of a generation, weighted by state multiplicity, vanishes.

## 4. The cubic U(1) anomaly

**Theorem 4.1 (Cubic anomaly).** The cubic U(1) anomaly vanishes identically on the Standard Model content per generation:

    Tr(Y^3) = 6 · (1/6)^3 + 3 · (−2/3)^3 + 3 · (1/3)^3 + 2 · (−1/2)^3 + 1 · 1^3 + 1 · 0^3 = 0.

**Proof.** Direct evaluation of the sum of hypercharge cubes, weighted by state multiplicity:

    6 · (1/6)^3 = 6/216 = 1/36,
    3 · (−2/3)^3 = 3 · (−8/27) = −24/27 = −8/9 = −32/36,
    3 · (1/3)^3 = 3 · (1/27) = 3/27 = 1/9 = 4/36,
    2 · (−1/2)^3 = 2 · (−1/8) = −1/4 = −9/36,
    1 · 1^3 = 1 = 36/36,
    1 · 0^3 = 0.

The sum is

    Tr(Y^3) = 1/36 − 32/36 + 4/36 − 9/36 + 36/36 = (1 − 32 + 4 − 9 + 36)/36 = 0/36 = 0.

All arithmetic is exact rational. □

**Remark 4.1 (Numerator cancellation).** The numerator 1 − 32 + 4 − 9 + 36 cancels to zero through the combination 1 + 4 + 36 = 41 from the positive contributions (Q_L, d_R^c, e_R^c) and 32 + 9 = 41 from the negative contributions (u_R^c, L_L). This is a nontrivial exact cancellation among the Standard Model hypercharge values.

## 5. The mixed SU(2)^2 U(1) anomaly

**Definition 5.1 (Mixed SU(2)^2 U(1) coefficient).** The mixed SU(2)^2 U(1) anomaly coefficient is

    SU(2)^2 U(1) = (1/2) · Σ_{doublets} n_color · Y,

where the sum runs over all SU(2)_L doublets, weighted by their color multiplicity and hypercharge.

**Theorem 5.1 (Mixed electroweak anomaly).** The mixed SU(2)^2 U(1) anomaly vanishes identically:

    SU(2)^2 U(1) = (1/2) · [3 · (1/6) + 1 · (−1/2)] = (1/2) · [1/2 − 1/2] = 0.

**Proof.** The SU(2)_L doublets in the content are Q_L (3 colors) and L_L (color singlet). The weighted sum is

    Σ_{doublets} n_color · Y = 3 · (1/6) + 1 · (−1/2) = 1/2 − 1/2 = 0.

Multiplied by the Dynkin index factor T(2) = 1/2 for the SU(2) doublet representation, the total coefficient is 0. □

**Remark 5.1 (Dynkin index).** The factor 1/2 is the Dynkin index T(2) for the fundamental representation of SU(2), a standard Lie algebra fact. It is not a framework input. The vanishing of the mixed anomaly is due to the exact cancellation of the weighted hypercharge sum, which is independent of the Dynkin index value.

## 6. The mixed SU(3)^2 U(1) anomaly

**Definition 6.1 (Mixed SU(3)^2 U(1) coefficient).** The mixed SU(3)^2 U(1) anomaly coefficient is

    SU(3)^2 U(1) = (1/2) · Σ_{color triplets} n_weak · Y,

where the sum runs over all SU(3)_c triplets, weighted by their weak-isospin multiplicity and hypercharge.

**Theorem 6.1 (Mixed color anomaly).** The mixed SU(3)^2 U(1) anomaly vanishes identically:

    SU(3)^2 U(1) = (1/2) · [2 · (1/6) + 1 · (−2/3) + 1 · (1/3)] = (1/2) · [1/3 − 2/3 + 1/3] = 0.

**Proof.** The SU(3)_c triplets in the content are Q_L (weak doublet, so multiplicity 2), u_R^c (weak singlet), and d_R^c (weak singlet). The weighted sum is

    Σ_{color triplets} n_weak · Y = 2 · (1/6) + 1 · (−2/3) + 1 · (1/3) = 1/3 − 2/3 + 1/3 = 0.

Multiplied by the Dynkin index factor T(3) = 1/2 for the SU(3) fundamental representation, the total coefficient is 0. □

## 7. The cubic gauge anomalies

**Theorem 7.1 (Cubic gauge anomalies).** The three cubic gauge anomalies SU(3)^3, SU(2)^3, and SU(3)–SU(2)–U(1) vanish identically on the Standard Model content per generation.

**Proof.** We treat each separately.

**SU(3)^3.** The SU(3)^3 anomaly coefficient is

    A(SU(3)^3) = Σ_{color multiplets} n_weak · A_3(R_3),

where A_3(R_3) is the cubic anomaly coefficient of the color representation R_3, normalized so that A_3(3) = +1 and A_3(3̄) = −1 for the fundamental and anti-fundamental representations respectively, and A_3(singlet) = 0.

The color-triplet content, in all-left-handed convention, is:

- Q_L(3, 2): color triplet 3, so A_3 = +1, weak multiplicity 2. Contribution: 2 · (+1) = +2.
- u_R^c(3̄, 1): color anti-triplet 3̄ in the all-left-handed convention, so A_3 = −1, weak multiplicity 1. Contribution: 1 · (−1) = −1.
- d_R^c(3̄, 1): color anti-triplet 3̄, so A_3 = −1, weak multiplicity 1. Contribution: 1 · (−1) = −1.

The sum is

    A(SU(3)^3) = 2 − 1 − 1 = 0.

This is the standard statement that the content is vector-like with respect to QCD: the color triplets and anti-triplets appear with multiplicities that cancel the cubic anomaly coefficient exactly.

**SU(2)^3.** The group SU(2) is a *safe group*: all its representations have vanishing cubic anomaly coefficient A_2(R_2) = 0. This is a standard Lie algebra fact: the symmetric invariant d^{abc} of SU(2) vanishes identically because SU(2) has no independent cubic Casimir. Consequently,

    A(SU(2)^3) = 0

regardless of the SU(2) content. Exact vanishing.

**SU(3)–SU(2)–U(1).** The mixed trilinear anomaly coefficient is

    A(SU(3)–SU(2)–U(1)) = Σ_{states} Y · Tr_{R_3}[T^a] · Tr_{R_2}[T^i],

where the trace factorizes over the color and weak-isospin factors. For any state in a representation (R_3, R_2, Y) of SU(3) × SU(2) × U(1), the trace over the color factor vanishes:

    Tr_{R_3}[T^a] = 0

because the SU(3) generators are traceless in every representation. Hence every term in the sum vanishes, and

    A(SU(3)–SU(2)–U(1)) = 0

identically. □

**Remark 7.1 (Structural status of the cubic anomalies).** The three cubic anomalies vanish by representation-theoretic structure, not by numerical cancellation of HCSM-specific values. The SU(3)^3 anomaly vanishes because the content is vector-like with respect to QCD; the SU(2)^3 anomaly vanishes because SU(2) is safe; the mixed trilinear anomaly vanishes because the non-Abelian generators are traceless. These are properties of the content structure, not additional constraints on the hypercharge functional.

## 8. The Witten global anomaly

**Definition 8.1 (Witten global anomaly).** The Witten global SU(2) anomaly is a Z_2-valued global anomaly associated with the fourth homotopy group π_4(SU(2)) = Z_2. A theory with chiral SU(2) content is globally consistent only if the number of SU(2) doublets is even.

**Theorem 8.1 (Witten anomaly evasion).** The Witten global SU(2) anomaly is evaded: each generation of the Standard Model content carries an even number of SU(2)_L doublets.

**Proof.** The SU(2)_L doublets in the content are Q_L and L_L:

    Q_L: 3 colors × 1 doublet = 3 doublet states,
    L_L: 1 color singlet × 1 doublet = 1 doublet state.

Total: 3 + 1 = 4 doublets per generation, which is even. The global SU(2) anomaly is therefore evaded. □

**Remark 8.1 (HCSM framing).** In HCSM terms, the number of SU(2)_L doublets is the multiplicity of the B_1 isotypic content that participates in the weak interaction. The even count is a structural feature of the content as realized on the A_1 and B_1 multiplicity spaces [HCSM-06].

## 9. Two-loop automaticity

**Theorem 9.1 (Two-loop automatic).** By the Adler–Bardeen theorem, if the one-loop anomaly coefficients vanish, then all higher-loop anomaly coefficients vanish. In particular, the two-loop anomaly cancels automatically.

**Proof.** The Adler–Bardeen theorem states that the ABJ anomaly (the anomaly of the axial current) is one-loop exact: it receives no corrections at higher loops. Consequently, the anomaly coefficient A satisfies A = A_{one-loop} at all orders. Since the one-loop coefficients vanish by Theorems 3.1–6.1, the anomaly vanishes at all loops. □

**Remark 9.1 (Wess–Zumino consistency).** The Wess–Zumino consistency conditions relate the anomaly coefficients across different orders. When the one-loop coefficients vanish, the Wess–Zumino conditions are trivially satisfied at all orders. The two-loop anomaly is therefore automatically zero, with no additional constraints on the content.

## 10. Sector-coarse framing

The anomaly conditions have a natural expression in the sector-coarse language of [HCSM-06, HCSM-07].

**Proposition 10.1 (Sector-coarse identity for the mixed color anomaly).** On the A_1 isotypic block, the mixed SU(3)^2 U(1) anomaly takes the sector-coarse form

    Y_0 + 2Y_1 + Y_2 = 0,

where Y_0, Y_1, Y_2 are the hypercharge values on the sectors Ω^0, Ω^1, Ω^2.

**Proof.** The mixed SU(3)^2 U(1) coefficient is

    SU(3)^2 U(1) = (1/2) · [n_weak(u_R^c) · Y_0 + n_weak(Q_L) · Y_1 + n_weak(d_R^c) · Y_2],

where the weak multiplicities are n_weak(u_R^c) = 1 (singlet), n_weak(Q_L) = 2 (doublet), n_weak(d_R^c) = 1 (singlet). The sector assignment places u_R^c on Ω^0, Q_L on Ω^1, d_R^c on Ω^2.

Substituting:

    SU(3)^2 U(1) = (1/2) · [Y_0 + 2Y_1 + Y_2].

The vanishing of this quantity is equivalent to

    Y_0 + 2Y_1 + Y_2 = 0.

With the HCSM values Y_0 = −2/3, Y_1 = +1/6, Y_2 = +1/3 [HCSM-07], the weighted sum is

    −2/3 + 2 · (1/6) + 1/3 = −2/3 + 1/3 + 1/3 = 0.

□

**Corollary 10.1 (Sector-coarse reading of anomaly cancellation).** In the sector-coarse framing, the mixed SU(3)^2 U(1) anomaly condition is a specific weighted sum of hypercharge values over the three Hodge sectors:

    Y(Ω^0) + 2Y(Ω^1) + Y(Ω^2) = 0.

The weight on Ω^1 is 2, reflecting the fact that the middle sector hosts the weak doublet content (Q_L); the outer sectors Ω^0 and Ω^2 host singlet content with weight 1 each.

**Remark 10.1 (Sector-coarse content of the other conditions).** The other three anomaly conditions admit analogous sector-coarse readings:

- Tr(Y) = 0: the weighted sum over all states of Y vanishes.
- Tr(Y^3) = 0: the weighted sum over all states of Y^3 vanishes.
- SU(2)^2 U(1) = 0: the weighted sum over the weak-doublet content vanishes.

In each case, the vanishing is due to an exact rational cancellation among the hypercharge values, reflecting the anomaly-free structure of the hypercharge functional.

## 12. Conclusion

This paper has established that the HCSM hypercharge functional is anomaly-free.

The four one-loop anomaly conditions

    Tr(Y) = 0,  Tr(Y^3) = 0,  SU(2)^2 U(1) = 0,  SU(3)^2 U(1) = 0

vanish identically in rational arithmetic on the Standard Model content per generation, with hypercharge values given by the HCSM sector-coarse functional [HCSM-07]. The three cubic gauge anomalies SU(3)^3, SU(2)^3, SU(3)–SU(2)–U(1) vanish by representation-theoretic structure: SU(3)^3 by the vector-like color content, SU(2)^3 by the safety of SU(2), and the mixed trilinear anomaly by the tracelessness of the non-Abelian generators. The Witten global SU(2) anomaly is evaded because each generation carries 4 SU(2) doublets (an even number). Two-loop cancellation is automatic by the Adler–Bardeen theorem.

In the sector-coarse framing, the mixed SU(3)^2 U(1) condition reduces to the weighted sector identity

    Y(Ω^0) + 2Y(Ω^1) + Y(Ω^2) = 0,

which is satisfied by the HCSM hypercharge values (−2/3, +1/6, +1/3) on the A_1 block. The weight 2 on the middle sector reflects the weak-doublet content hosted there.

The anomaly cancellation is a consistency theorem of the HCSM structure. The particle content is derived in the HCSM stack; the hypercharge functional is derived; the anomaly cancellation follows. The framework's claim is: the HCSM hypercharge functional is the unique anomaly-free functional compatible with the sector-coarse structure and the Standard Model content.
