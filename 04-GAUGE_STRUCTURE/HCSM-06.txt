## Abstract

The Hodge Complex Standard Model assigns the 14 modes at the self-paired eigenvalue λ = 4 of the discrete Laplacian on the periodic 8×8 torus to one Standard Model generation. This paper derives the gauge algebra su(3) ⊕ su(2) ⊕ u(1)^5 as a Lie subalgebra of the D4 commutant on the complexified 14-mode multiplet, through the endomorphism algebra of the D4 isotypic multiplicity spaces. We prove:

(i) the D4 commutant on C^14 decomposes as ⊕_ρ M_{m_ρ}(C) with complex dimension 27;

(ii) su(3) embeds in the A1-block endomorphism algebra M_3(C) as the traceless anti-Hermitian subalgebra;

(iii) **the A1 block is uniquely selected as the physical color algebra by a dimension-and-invariance theorem**: among the two three-dimensional multiplicity spaces M_{A1} and M_E, only M_{A1} is D4-invariant on its isotypic subspace, so only M_{A1} can host the physical color triplet;

(iv) su(2) embeds in the B1-block endomorphism algebra M_2(C);

(v) **the choice of B1 over B2 is a substrate-level labeling convention**, forced by the outer automorphism of D4 and by the D4-equivariance of all HCSM-native operators;

(vi) the five D4 isotypic projectors span an abelian u(1)^5 subalgebra;

(vii) the generators of these three factors close to su(3) ⊕ su(2) ⊕ u(1)^5, a 16-dimensional real Lie subalgebra of the commutant.

On the full 56-dimensional Hodge sector at λ = 4, we derive the sector-coarse correspondence: the space of D4-invariant, sector-block-diagonal, multiplicity-scalar operators is exactly 15-dimensional, spanned by the three sector operators {I, F, O} on each of the five isotypic blocks. All algebraic claims are verified at machine precision.

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 The gauge algebra problem

The Hodge Complex Standard Model identifies the 14 modes at the self-paired eigenvalue λ = 4 of the discrete Laplacian on the periodic 8×8 torus with one Standard Model generation (HCSM-04). On this multiplet the D4 point group of the square acts by permutation, and HCSM-05 established the D4 isotypic decomposition.

The present paper asks: what Lie algebra emerges from the D4 isotypic structure of this multiplet? The answer is the gauge algebra

    g_HCSM = su(3) ⊕ su(2) ⊕ u(1)^5,     (1.1)

realized as a Lie subalgebra of the D4 commutant on the complexified 14-mode multiplet C^14.

### 1.2 The one-line summary

The 14-mode multiplet at λ = 4 decomposes under D4 as 3A1 ⊕ A2 ⊕ 2B1 ⊕ 2B2 ⊕ 3E. The D4 commutant on this multiplet is the endomorphism algebra ⊕_ρ M_{m_ρ}(C), of complex dimension 27. The gauge algebra su(3) ⊕ su(2) ⊕ u(1)^5 embeds in the commutant: su(3) acts on the A1 multiplicity space, su(2) on the B1 multiplicity space, and the five isotypic projectors span the u(1)^5 center. The A1 identification with physical color is forced by dimension matching; the B1 identification with physical weak isospin is a labeling convention fixed by the outer automorphism of D4.

### 1.3 Main results

1. **D4 isotypic decomposition** (Theorem 3.1). V_14 = 3A1 ⊕ A2 ⊕ 2B1 ⊕ 2B2 ⊕ 3E, of complex dimension 14.

2. **Commutant dimension** (Theorem 4.3). By Schur's lemma, dim_C Comm(D4, V_14) = 3² + 1² + 2² + 2² + 3² = 27.

3. **Endomorphism algebra** (Theorem 4.4). Comm(D4, V_14) ≅ M_3(C) ⊕ M_1(C) ⊕ M_2(C) ⊕ M_2(C) ⊕ M_3(C).

4. **su(3) subalgebra** (Theorem 5.3). The traceless anti-Hermitian subalgebra of M_3(C) is a copy of su(3) embedded in the commutant.

5. **A1 uniqueness theorem** (Theorem 5.4). Among the two 3-dimensional multiplicity spaces M_{A1} and M_E, only M_{A1} admits an su(3) action on a D4-trivial 3-dimensional subspace. Therefore physical color su(3)_c is the su(3) subalgebra on M_{A1}.

6. **su(2) subalgebra** (Theorem 5.7). The traceless anti-Hermitian subalgebra of M_2(C) is a copy of su(2) embedded in the commutant.

7. **B1/B2 outer automorphism** (Theorems 5.8, 5.10). The outer automorphism φ: D4 → D4 defined by φ(r) = r, φ(s) = rs swaps B1 ↔ B2 while fixing A1, A2, E. No D4-equivariant operator on V_14 implements φ. Therefore the choice of B1 (rather than B2) as physical weak isospin is a substrate-level labeling convention.

8. **u(1)^5 subalgebra** (Theorem 5.11). The five isotypic projectors span an abelian Lie subalgebra isomorphic to u(1)^5.

9. **Closure** (Theorem 5.12). The generators of the three factors above close to su(3) ⊕ su(2) ⊕ u(1)^5, of real dimension 8 + 3 + 5 = 16.

10. **Sector-coarse correspondence** (Theorem 6.3). On the full 56-dimensional Hodge sector at λ = 4, the space of D4-invariant, sector-block-diagonal, multiplicity-scalar operators is exactly 15-dimensional, spanned by the three sector operators {I, F, O}.

## 2. Setup

### 2.1 The substrate and the mode multiplet

The substrate is the periodic square lattice

    Λ = Z_8 × Z_8,    N = 64,    (2.1)

consisting of N = 64 sites arranged in an 8×8 grid with periodic boundary conditions. The discrete 5-point Laplacian has eigenvalue

    λ(n1, n2) = 4 − 2 cos(π n1 / 4) − 2 cos(π n2 / 4),    (2.2)

for (n1, n2) ∈ {0, ..., 7}². At the self-paired eigenvalue λ = 4, the mode set M has 14 elements:

    O_0 = {(0,4), (4,0)},                                (2.3a)
    O_1 = {(1,3), (1,5), (3,1), (3,7), (5,1), (5,7), (7,3), (7,5)},   (2.3b)
    O_2 = {(2,2), (2,6), (6,2), (6,6)}.                  (2.3c)

We work with the complexified mode space V_14 = C^14 with basis {e_m : m ∈ M}.

### 2.2 The D4 action

The D4 point group of the square acts on Λ by

    r: (n1, n2) ↦ (−n2, n1)  (mod 8),                   (2.4a)
    s: (n1, n2) ↦ (n1, −n2)  (mod 8),                   (2.4b)

subject to r⁴ = s² = (rs)² = e. The permutation action on the 14 modes extends to a representation

    ρ_14 : D4 → GL(V_14),    ρ_14(g) e_m = e_{g·m}.     (2.5)

We denote by R = ρ_14(r) and S = ρ_14(s) the two generators.

### 2.3 The D4 character table

The D4 irreps are five: A1, A2 (1-dim), B1, B2 (1-dim), and E (2-dim). On the conjugacy classes {e}, {r²}, {r, r³}, {s, r²s}, {rs, r³s}, with sizes 1, 1, 2, 2, 2:

| irrep | e | r² | r, r³ | s, r²s | rs, r³s |
|-------|---|----|-------|--------|---------|
| A1    | 1 | 1  | 1     | 1      | 1       |
| A2    | 1 | 1  | 1     | −1     | −1      |
| B1    | 1 | 1  | −1    | 1      | −1      |
| B2    | 1 | 1  | −1    | −1     | 1       |
| E     | 2 | −2 | 0     | 0      | 0       |

## 3. The D4 Isotypic Decomposition

**Theorem 3.1 (D4 isotypic decomposition of V_14).** Under D4, the complexified 14-mode multiplet decomposes as

    V_14 = 3A1 ⊕ A2 ⊕ 2B1 ⊕ 2B2 ⊕ 3E,                    (3.1)

with multiplicities (m_{A1}, m_{A2}, m_{B1}, m_{B2}, m_E) = (3, 1, 2, 2, 3).

**Proof.** Direct decomposition via the character formula. The character of the permutation module on the 14 modes is

    χ_{V_14} = (14, 2, 0, 2, 2),                          (3.2)

on the conjugacy classes {e}, {r²}, {r, r³}, {s, r²s}, {rs, r³s}. Then

    m_ρ = (1/8) Σ_C |C| χ_{V_14}(C) χ_ρ(C)*,             (3.3)

giving (3, 1, 2, 2, 3). Dimension check: 3 + 1 + 2 + 2 + 6 = 14. ∎

**Remark 3.2 (Isotypic decomposition).** The isotypic decomposition of V_14 is

    V_14 = ⊕_ρ V_ρ ⊗ M_ρ,                                (3.4)

where V_ρ is the irreducible representation space of ρ and M_ρ is the multiplicity space. Explicitly:

    dim V_{A1} = 1, dim M_{A1} = 3;    dim V_{A2} = 1, dim M_{A2} = 1;
    dim V_{B1} = 1, dim M_{B1} = 2;    dim V_{B2} = 1, dim M_{B2} = 2;
    dim V_E  = 2, dim M_E  = 3.

**Remark 3.3 (Isotypic dimensions).** The A1 block has isotypic dimension 1 × 3 = 3. The E block has isotypic dimension 2 × 3 = 6. This difference — A1 trivial in V_ρ, E non-trivial — is the structural fact that selects A1 as the color algebra in Section 5.2.

## 4. The D4 Commutant

### 4.1 Definition and Schur's lemma

**Definition 4.1 (D4 commutant).** The D4 commutant on V_14 is the algebra of D4-equivariant endomorphisms,

    Comm(D4, V_14) = { X ∈ End_C(V_14) : [X, R] = [X, S] = 0 }.    (4.1)

**Theorem 4.2 (Schur decomposition of the commutant).** For a D4-representation V = ⊕_ρ V_ρ ⊗ M_ρ with dim M_ρ = m_ρ,

    Comm(D4, V) ≅ ⊕_ρ End_C(M_ρ) = ⊕_ρ M_{m_ρ}(C).       (4.2)

**Proof.** Standard Schur's lemma. Since the V_ρ are pairwise non-isomorphic, every D4-equivariant endomorphism preserves the isotypic decomposition; on each block V_ρ ⊗ M_ρ, an equivariant endomorphism must act as id_{V_ρ} ⊗ A_ρ with A_ρ ∈ End(M_ρ). ∎

**Theorem 4.3 (Commutant dimension).** The D4 commutant on the complexified 14-mode multiplet has complex dimension

    dim_C Comm(D4, V_14) = Σ_ρ m_ρ² = 3² + 1² + 2² + 2² + 3² = 27.    (4.3)

**Proof.** Immediate from Theorem 4.2 and the multiplicities (3, 1, 2, 2, 3). ∎

### 4.2 The endomorphism algebra

**Theorem 4.4 (Endomorphism algebra structure).** The commutant decomposes explicitly as

    Comm(D4, V_14) ≅ M_3(C) ⊕ M_1(C) ⊕ M_2(C) ⊕ M_2(C) ⊕ M_3(C),    (4.4)

with C-dimensions 9 + 1 + 4 + 4 + 9 = 27.

**Proof.** Immediate from Theorems 4.2 and 4.3. The five blocks correspond to the five irreps A1, A2, B1, B2, E in that order. ∎

**Remark 4.5 (Block decomposition and physical sectors).** The five matrix blocks are:

- M_3(C) on the A1 multiplicity space: complex dim 9, real dim 18.
- M_1(C) on the A2 multiplicity space: complex dim 1, real dim 2.
- M_2(C) on the B1 multiplicity space: complex dim 4, real dim 8.
- M_2(C) on the B2 multiplicity space: complex dim 4, real dim 8.
- M_3(C) on the E multiplicity space: complex dim 9, real dim 18.

The gauge algebra su(3) ⊕ su(2) ⊕ u(1)^5 embeds in this algebra.

## 5. The Gauge Algebra

### 5.1 The su(3) subalgebra on the A1 multiplicity space

The A1 multiplicity space M_{A1} ≅ C^3 is the home of the color algebra. Let {u_1, u_2, u_3} be an orthonormal basis of M_{A1}.

**Definition 5.1 (Embedding map).**

    φ_{A1} : M_3(C) → End_C(V_14),    φ_{A1}(X) = Σ_{a,b} u_a X_{ab} u_b†,   (5.1)

extended by zero on the other isotypic blocks.

**Proposition 5.2 (Homomorphism).** φ_{A1} is a unital algebra homomorphism and a Lie algebra homomorphism:

    φ_{A1}(XY) = φ_{A1}(X) φ_{A1}(Y),    φ_{A1}([X, Y]) = [φ_{A1}(X), φ_{A1}(Y)].   (5.2)

**Theorem 5.3 (su(3) subalgebra).** The traceless anti-Hermitian subalgebra of M_3(C),

    su(3) = { X ∈ M_3(C) : X† = −X, tr(X) = 0 },         (5.3)

embeds in Comm(D4, V_14) via φ_{A1} as a real 8-dimensional compact Lie subalgebra.

**Proof.** The image φ_{A1}(su(3)) is contained in the A1 block of the commutant, and φ_{A1} preserves brackets by Proposition 5.2. The Lie algebra su(3) is generated by anti-Hermitian traceless 3×3 matrices, of real dimension 8. Using Gell-Mann matrices λ^a, the embedding is T_c^a = φ_{A1}(λ^a / 2), a = 1, ..., 8, with [T^a, T^b] = i Σ_c f^{abc} T^c. ∎

### 5.2 The A1 uniqueness theorem

The commutant Comm(D4, V_14) contains **two** 3-dimensional matrix blocks: M_{A1} and M_E. Both are candidates for hosting an su(3) subalgebra. The following theorem selects the unique candidate compatible with physical color.

**Theorem 5.4 (A1 uniqueness for physical color).** Let M_{A1} and M_E be the two 3-dimensional multiplicity spaces in Comm(D4, V_14). Then:

(a) V_{A1} ⊗ M_{A1} = C^3 is a **D4-trivial** 3-dimensional subspace of V_14.

(b) V_E ⊗ M_E = C^2 ⊗ C^3 is a **6-dimensional** subspace of V_14, decomposing under D4 as E ⊗ C^3.

(c) Physical color is a 3-dimensional internal index that transforms trivially under the D4 symmetry of the substrate: the structural premise (C1).

(d) The unique such subspace of V_14 is V_{A1} ⊗ M_{A1}.

(e) Consequently, physical color su(3)_c is the su(3) subalgebra of the commutant on M_{A1}.

**Proof.**

(a) V_{A1} is 1-dimensional and carries the trivial rep A1. Hence V_{A1} ⊗ M_{A1} = C^3 with D4 acting trivially.

(b) V_E is 2-dimensional and carries the irrep E. Hence V_E ⊗ M_E = C^2 ⊗ C^3 with D4 acting as E on the first factor. Dimension = 6.

(c) This is premise (C1).

(d) From (a) and (b), the only 3-dimensional subspace of V_14 on which D4 acts trivially is V_{A1} ⊗ M_{A1}.

(e) From (c) and (d), the su(3) subalgebra must be the one on M_{A1}. ∎

**Remark 5.5.** Theorem 5.4 replaces the earlier framework input "A1 ↔ physical color" with a derived statement. The only retained premise is (C1), the D4-triviality of color.

### 5.3 The su(2) subalgebra on the B1 multiplicity space

**Definition 5.6 (Embedding map).** φ_{B1} : M_2(C) → End_C(V_14), extended by zero.

**Theorem 5.7 (su(2) subalgebra).** The traceless anti-Hermitian subalgebra su(2) ⊂ M_2(C) embeds in Comm(D4, V_14) via φ_{B1} as a real 3-dimensional compact Lie subalgebra.

**Proof.** Same structure as Theorem 5.3. The standard physics basis uses Pauli matrices σ^i, with T^i = φ_{B1}(σ^i / 2) satisfying [T^i, T^j] = i ε^{ijk} T^k. ∎

### 5.4 The B1/B2 outer automorphism and the labeling convention

**Theorem 5.8 (Outer automorphism of D4).** The map φ : D4 → D4 defined by φ(r) = r, φ(s) = rs is a group automorphism of D4. It is not inner and it exchanges the two reflection conjugacy classes {s, r²s} ↔ {rs, r³s}, thereby swapping B1 ↔ B2 while fixing A1, A2, E.

**Proof.** Check the relations: φ(r)⁴ = r⁴ = e; φ(s)² = (rs)² = r s r s = r (s r) s = r (r³ s) s = r⁴ s² = e; (φ(r) φ(s))² = (r · rs)² = (r² s)² = r² s r² s = r² (r² s) s = r⁴ s² = e. So φ preserves the D4 presentation. Its effect on the character table: on B1, χ_{B1}(s) = +1, χ_{B1}(rs) = −1; on B2, χ_{B2}(s) = −1, χ_{B2}(rs) = +1. Applying φ exchanges the two reflection classes. ∎

**Theorem 5.10 (B1/B2 labeling convention).** The outer automorphism φ of Theorem 5.8 acts on Comm(D4, V_14) by exchanging the two M_2(C) blocks M_{B1} ↔ M_{B2}. No D4-equivariant operator on V_14 implements φ. Therefore the choice of B1 (rather than B2) as the physical weak-isospin block is a substrate-level labeling convention.

**Proof.** The outer automorphism acts on Rep(D4) by permuting irreps: B1 ↔ B2, with A1, A2, E fixed. Hence on V_14 = 3A1 ⊕ A2 ⊕ 2B1 ⊕ 2B2 ⊕ 3E, the automorphism exchanges the two B-blocks. On the commutant ⊕_ρ M_{m_ρ}(C), it exchanges the corresponding M_2(C) factors.

For the second statement: no D4-equivariant operator on V_14 implements φ, because φ is not an element of D4. The set of D4-equivariant operators is exactly Comm(D4, V_14), generated by R and S, both of which cannot implement an outer automorphism. All HCSM-native operators — the processor T (HCSM-13), the winding generator H_wind (HCSM-14), the zero state s (HCSM-22), the sector operators I, F, O (this paper), the orientation flip σ (HCSM-09) — are D4-equivariant. None distinguishes B1 from B2. ∎

**Corollary 5.11 (Physical content invariance).** The physical content of HCSM — gauge algebra, fermion representation content, anomaly cancellation, hypercharge quantization, sector-coarse correspondence, and all downstream predictions — is invariant under the relabeling B1 ↔ B2.

**Proof.** The gauge algebra su(3) ⊕ su(2) ⊕ u(1)^5 is invariant under φ (it fixes su(3), permutes the two isomorphic su(2) factors, and permutes the associated u(1) projectors). The fermion content is fixed by the isotypic decomposition, invariant under φ. Anomaly cancellation (HCSM-08) is a symmetric sum, invariant under φ. Hypercharge quantization is invariant. ∎

### 5.5 The u(1)^5 subalgebra from the isotypic projectors

**Definition 5.12 (Isotypic projectors).** For each irrep ρ of D4,

    P_ρ = (dim ρ / |D4|) Σ_{g ∈ D4} χ_ρ(g)* ρ_14(g).     (5.12)

The five projectors satisfy P_ρ P_{ρ'} = δ_{ρρ'} P_ρ and Σ_ρ P_ρ = I_14.

**Theorem 5.13 (u(1)^5 subalgebra).** The five isotypic projectors span an abelian Lie subalgebra of Comm(D4, V_14) isomorphic to u(1)^5, of real dimension 5.

### 5.6 Closure of the gauge algebra

**Theorem 5.14 (Closure).** The image φ_{A1}(su(3)) ⊕ φ_{B1}(su(2)) ⊕ span{P_{A1}, P_{A2}, P_{B1}, P_{B2}, P_E} is a Lie subalgebra of Comm(D4, V_14) isomorphic to su(3) ⊕ su(2) ⊕ u(1)^5, of real dimension 8 + 3 + 5 = 16.

**Proof.** We verify the brackets: (1) su(3)-su(3) by homomorphism; (2) su(2)-su(2) by homomorphism; (3) su(3)-su(2) vanishes because they act on orthogonal blocks; (4) su(3)-u(1) vanishes because each P_ρ acts as a scalar on the A1 block; (5) su(2)-u(1) same; (6) u(1)-u(1) abelian. ∎

## 6. Sector-Coarse Correspondence

### 6.1 The full Hodge sector

    V_56 = Ω^0|_{λ=4} ⊕ Ω^1|_{λ=4} ⊕ Ω^2|_{λ=4},          (6.1)

with D4 decomposition V_56 = 9A1 ⊕ 5A2 ⊕ 7B1 ⊕ 7B2 ⊕ 14E. Dimension check: 9 + 5 + 7 + 7 + 28 = 56.

### 6.2 Sector operators

**Definition 6.1.** On V_56:

    I = P_{Ω^0} + P_{Ω^1} + P_{Ω^2},                       (6.2a)
    F = P_{Ω^0} − P_{Ω^1} + P_{Ω^2},                       (6.2b)
    O = P_{Ω^0} − P_{Ω^2}.                                (6.2c)

Sector signatures: I = (1, 1, 1), F = (1, −1, 1), O = (1, 0, −1).

### 6.3 The sector-coarse sub-commutant

**Theorem 6.3 (Sector-coarse correspondence).** The sector-coarse sub-commutant has complex dimension exactly 15 = 5 × 3. It is spanned by the three sector operators {I, F, O} on each isotypic block.

**Proof.** By Schur's lemma, one complex scalar per (ρ, Ω^k) block, of which there are 5 × 3 = 15. On each block, (I, F, O) give the three vectors (1,1,1), (1,−1,1), (1,0,−1), which span C³ (det M = 4 ≠ 0). Over the five irreps, the span has complex dimension 15. ∎

**Corollary 6.4 (Orientation Z2).** Under the sign flip O ↦ −O, the multiset of physical sector values is preserved. Full orientation-Z2 theorem is HCSM-09.

## 9. Conclusion

This paper has established the sector-coarse correspondence of the Hodge Complex Standard Model. The 14-mode multiplet decomposes under D4 as 3A1 ⊕ A2 ⊕ 2B1 ⊕ 2B2 ⊕ 3E. The D4 commutant is the endomorphism algebra of complex dimension 27. The gauge algebra su(3) ⊕ su(2) ⊕ u(1)^5 embeds in the commutant.

**The identification of the A1 factor with physical color is now a theorem** (Theorem 5.4). The A1 block is the unique 3-dimensional D4-invariant subspace of V_14, so it is the unique candidate for physical color.

**The identification of the B1 factor with physical weak isospin is a labeling convention** (Theorem 5.10). The two blocks B1 and B2 are exchanged by the outer automorphism of D4, and no D4-equivariant operator on V_14 implements this automorphism. The physical content of the framework is invariant under the relabeling.

On the full 56-dimensional Hodge sector at λ = 4, the sector-coarse correspondence is that the space of D4-invariant, sector-block-diagonal, multiplicity-scalar operators is exactly 15-dimensional.

The framework derives the algebraic structure that any physically realized gauge theory with the Standard Model content and the D4 structure of the 14-mode multiplet must be compatible with.
