ABSTRACT
================================================================================

The Hodge Complex Standard Model assigns the 14 modes at the self-paired
eigenvalue lambda = 4 of the discrete Laplacian on the periodic 8 x 8 torus
to one Standard Model generation. This paper derives the gauge algebra
su(3) + su(2) + u(1)^5 as a Lie subalgebra of the D4 commutant on the
complexified 14-mode multiplet, through the endomorphism algebra of the D4
isotypic multiplicity spaces. We prove: (i) the D4 commutant on C^14
decomposes as sum over rho of M_{m_rho}(C) with complex dimension 27;
(ii) su(3) embeds in the A_1-block endomorphism algebra M_3(C) as the
traceless anti-Hermitian subalgebra; (iii) su(2) embeds in the B_1-block
endomorphism algebra M_2(C); (iv) the five D4 isotypic projectors span an
abelian u(1)^5 subalgebra; (v) the generators of these three factors close
to su(3) + su(2) + u(1)^5, a 16-dimensional real Lie subalgebra of the
commutant. On the full 56-dimensional Hodge sector at lambda = 4, we
derive the sector-coarse correspondence: the space of D4-invariant,
sector-block-diagonal, multiplicity-scalar operators is exactly
15-dimensional, spanned by the three sector operators {I, F, O} on each
of the five isotypic blocks. The identification of the A_1 factor with
physical color and the B_1 factor with physical weak isospin is the
framework's physical input, not a derivation. All claims are HCSM-native
and verified at machine precision.

1. INTRODUCTION

1.1 The gauge algebra problem
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The Hodge Complex Standard Model (HCSM) identifies the 14 modes at the
self-paired eigenvalue lambda = 4 of the discrete Laplacian on the periodic
8 x 8 torus with one Standard Model generation [HCSM-04]. On this multiplet,
the D4 point group of the square acts by permutation, and the paper HCSM-05
[HCSM-05] established the D4 isotypic decomposition.

The present paper asks: what Lie algebra emerges from the D4 isotypic
structure of this multiplet? The answer is the gauge algebra

    g_HCSM = su(3) + su(2) + u(1)^5,                                   (1)

realized as a Lie subalgebra of the D4 commutant on the complexified
14-mode multiplet C^14.

The derivation proceeds through the sector-coarse correspondence: the D4
isotypic sectors of the mode multiplet correspond to the gauge-algebra
factors through the endomorphism algebra of their multiplicity spaces.
The "sector" structure is the fine D4 isotypic decomposition; the "coarse"
structure is the gauge algebra acting on the multiplicities. The
correspondence between them is the content of this paper.

1.2 The one-line summary
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    The 14-mode multiplet at lambda = 4 decomposes under D4 as
    3A_1 + A_2 + 2B_1 + 2B_2 + 3E. The D4 commutant on this multiplet is
    the endomorphism algebra sum over rho of M_{m_rho}(C), of complex
    dimension 27. The gauge algebra su(3) + su(2) + u(1)^5 embeds in the
    commutant: su(3) acts on the A_1 multiplicity space, su(2) on the
    B_1 multiplicity space, and the five isotypic projectors span the
    u(1)^5 center.

1.3 Main results
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

(1) D4 isotypic decomposition (Theorem 3.1, restated from HCSM-05
    [HCSM-05]). The 14-mode multiplet decomposes as

        V_14 = 3A_1 + A_2 + 2B_1 + 2B_2 + 3E,                          (2)

    of complex dimension 14.

(2) Commutant dimension (Theorem 4.3). By Schur's lemma, the D4 commutant
    on V_14 has complex dimension

        dim_C Comm(D4, V_14) = sum_rho m_rho^2
                            = 3^2 + 1^2 + 2^2 + 2^2 + 3^2 = 27.        (3)

(3) Endomorphism algebra (Theorem 4.4). The commutant is the endomorphism
    algebra

        Comm(D4, V_14) = sum over rho of M_{m_rho}(C),                 (4)

    of complex dimension 27.

(4) su(3) subalgebra (Theorem 5.2). The traceless anti-Hermitian
    subalgebra of the A_1-block factor M_{m_{A_1}}(C) = M_3(C) is a copy
    of su(3), embedded in the commutant.

(5) su(2) subalgebra (Theorem 5.4). The traceless anti-Hermitian
    subalgebra of the B_1-block factor M_{m_{B_1}}(C) = M_2(C) is a copy
    of su(2), embedded in the commutant.

(6) u(1)^5 subalgebra (Theorem 5.6). The five D4 isotypic projectors
    P_{A_1}, P_{A_2}, P_{B_1}, P_{B_2}, P_E span an abelian Lie
    subalgebra of the commutant isomorphic to u(1)^5.

(7) Closure (Theorem 5.7). The generators of the three factors above
    close to a Lie subalgebra of Comm(D4, V_14) isomorphic to
    su(3) + su(2) + u(1)^5, of real dimension 8 + 3 + 5 = 16.

(8) Sector-coarse correspondence (Theorem 6.3). On the full 56-dimensional
    Hodge sector at lambda = 4, the space of D4-invariant,
    sector-block-diagonal, multiplicity-scalar operators is exactly
    15-dimensional, spanned by the three sector operators {I, F, O} on
    each of the five isotypic blocks.

2. SETUP

2.1 The substrate and the mode multiplet
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The substrate is the periodic square lattice Lambda = Z_8 x Z_8 with
N = 64 sites [HCSM-01]. The discrete 5-point Laplacian has eigenvalue

    lambda(n_1, n_2) = 4 - 2 cos(pi n_1 / 4)
                          - 2 cos(pi n_2 / 4),                        (5)

with n_1, n_2 in {0, ..., 7}. At the self-paired eigenvalue lambda = 4,
the mode set M has 14 elements, listed explicitly in HCSM-04 [HCSM-04]:

    O_0 = {(0,4), (4,0)},                                             (6)

    O_1 = {(1,3), (1,5), (3,1), (3,7),
           (5,1), (5,7), (7,3), (7,5)},                               (7)

    O_2 = {(2,2), (2,6), (6,2), (6,6)}.                              (8)

We work with the complexified mode space V_14 = C^14, with basis
{e_m : m in M}. The physical mode vectors on the torus are real, but the
complexification is the natural home of the gauge algebra (as in QFT).

2.2 The D4 action
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The D4 point group of the square acts on Lambda by

    r : (n_1, n_2) -> (-n_2, n_1)  (mod 8),                           (9)

    s : (n_1, n_2) -> (n_1, -n_2)  (mod 8),                          (10)

subject to r^4 = s^2 = (rs)^2 = e. The permutation action of D4 on the
14 modes extends to a representation of D4 on V_14:

    rho_14 : D4 -> GL(V_14),    rho_14(g) e_m = e_{g . m}.           (11)

We denote by R = rho_14(r) and S = rho_14(s) the two generators.

2.3 The D4 character table
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The D4 irreps are five: two 1-dimensional real representations A_1, A_2,
two 1-dimensional real representations B_1, B_2, and one 2-dimensional
real representation E. We use the standard conjugacy classes
{e}, {r^2}, {r, r^3}, {s, r^2 s}, {rs, r^3 s} with sizes 1, 1, 2, 2, 2:

    irrep rho | e  r^2  r, r^3  s, r^2 s  rs, r^3 s
    ----------|------------------------------------------
    A_1       | 1   1     1        1           1
    A_2       | 1   1     1       -1          -1
    B_1       | 1   1    -1        1          -1
    B_2       | 1   1    -1       -1           1
    E         | 2  -2     0        0           0

3. THE D4 ISOTYPIC DECOMPOSITION
================================================================================

The following theorem is stated in HCSM-05 [HCSM-05]. We recall it here
for self-containedness.

Theorem 3.1 (D4 isotypic decomposition of V_14). Under D4, the
complexified 14-mode multiplet decomposes as

    V_14 = 3A_1 + A_2 + 2B_1 + 2B_2 + 3E,                            (12)

with multiplicities (m_{A_1}, m_{A_2}, m_{B_1}, m_{B_2}, m_E) =
(3, 1, 2, 2, 3).

Proof. Direct decomposition via the character formula of HCSM-05. We
record the character of the permutation module on the 14 modes:

    chi_{V_14} = (14, 2, 0, 2, 2)                                    (13)

on conjugacy classes {e}, {r^2}, {r, r^3}, {s, r^2 s}, {rs, r^3 s}.
Then

    m_rho = (1/8) sum_C |C| chi_{V_14}(C) chi_rho(C)^*,             (14)

giving (3, 1, 2, 2, 3). Dimension check: 3 + 1 + 2 + 2 + 6 = 14. [Box]

Remark 3.2 (Isotypic decomposition). The isotypic decomposition of V_14
is

    V_14 = sum over rho of (V_rho tensor M_rho),                     (15)

where V_rho is the irreducible representation space of rho and M_rho is
the multiplicity space of dimension m_rho. Explicitly:

  - V_{A_1} tensor M_{A_1}: dim V_{A_1} = 1, dim M_{A_1} = 3.
  - V_{A_2} tensor M_{A_2}: dim V_{A_2} = 1, dim M_{A_2} = 1.
  - V_{B_1} tensor M_{B_1}: dim V_{B_1} = 1, dim M_{B_1} = 2.
  - V_{B_2} tensor M_{B_2}: dim V_{B_2} = 1, dim M_{B_2} = 2.
  - V_E tensor M_E: dim V_E = 2, dim M_E = 3.

The multiplicity space M_{A_1} of dimension 3 is the natural home of the
color algebra; the multiplicity space M_{B_1} of dimension 2 is the
natural home of the weak-isospin algebra.


================================================================================
4. THE D4 COMMUTANT
================================================================================

4.1 Definition and Schur's lemma
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Definition 4.1 (D4 commutant). The D4 commutant on V_14 is the algebra
of D4-equivariant endomorphisms,

    Comm(D4, V_14) = {X in End_C(V_14) : [X, R] = [X, S] = 0}.       (16)

Theorem 4.2 (Schur decomposition of the commutant). For a D4-
representation V = sum_rho (V_rho tensor M_rho) with dim M_rho = m_rho,
the commutant is

    Comm(D4, V) = sum_rho End_C(M_rho) = sum_rho M_{m_rho}(C).      (17)

Proof. Standard Schur's lemma. Since the V_rho are pairwise non-
isomorphic, every D4-equivariant endomorphism preserves the isotypic
decomposition; on each block V_rho tensor M_rho, an equivariant
endomorphism must act as id_{V_rho} tensor A_rho with A_rho in End(M_rho).
The general element is thus sum_rho (id_{V_rho} tensor A_rho). [Box]

Theorem 4.3 (Commutant dimension). The D4 commutant on the complexified
14-mode multiplet has complex dimension

    dim_C Comm(D4, V_14) = sum_rho m_rho^2
                        = 3^2 + 1^2 + 2^2 + 2^2 + 3^2
                        = 27.                                        (18)

Proof. Immediate from Theorem 4.2 and the multiplicities (3, 1, 2, 2, 3).
[Box]

4.2 The endomorphism algebra
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Theorem 4.4 (Endomorphism algebra structure). The commutant decomposes
explicitly as

    Comm(D4, V_14) = M_3(C) + M_1(C) + M_2(C) + M_2(C) + M_3(C),     (19)

with C-dimensions 9 + 1 + 4 + 4 + 9 = 27.

Proof. Immediate from Theorems 4.2 and 4.3. The five blocks correspond
to the five irreps A_1, A_2, B_1, B_2, E in that order. [Box]

Remark 4.5 (The block decomposition and the physical sectors). The five
matrix blocks are the natural Lie-algebraic factors:

  - M_3(C) on the A_1 multiplicity space: complex dimension 9.
  - M_1(C) on the A_2 multiplicity space: complex dimension 1.
  - M_2(C) on the B_1 multiplicity space: complex dimension 4.
  - M_2(C) on the B_2 multiplicity space: complex dimension 4.
  - M_3(C) on the E multiplicity space: complex dimension 9.

The gauge algebra su(3) + su(2) + u(1)^5 embeds in this algebra, with
su(3) in the A_1 block, su(2) in the B_1 block, and u(1)^5 spanned by
the identity elements of all five blocks.

5. THE GAUGE ALGEBRA
================================================================================

5.1 The su(3) subalgebra on the A_1 multiplicity space
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The A_1 multiplicity space M_{A_1} = C^3 is the home of the color algebra.
Let {u_1, u_2, u_3} be an orthonormal basis of M_{A_1}.

Definition 5.1 (Embedding map). The embedding of the A_1 block
endomorphism algebra is

    phi_{A_1} : M_3(C) -> End_C(V_14),
        phi_{A_1}(X) = sum_{a, b = 1}^{3} u_a X_{ab} u_b^dagger,      (20)

extended by zero on the other isotypic blocks.

Proposition. phi_{A_1} is a unital algebra homomorphism and a Lie algebra
homomorphism:

    phi_{A_1}(XY) = phi_{A_1}(X) phi_{A_1}(Y),
    phi_{A_1}([X, Y]) = [phi_{A_1}(X), phi_{A_1}(Y)].                (21)

Proof. Direct computation. [Box]

Theorem 5.2 (su(3) subalgebra). The traceless anti-Hermitian subalgebra
of M_3(C),

    su(3) = {X in M_3(C) : X^dagger = -X, tr(X) = 0},                (22)

embeds in Comm(D4, V_14) via phi_{A_1} as a real 8-dimensional compact
Lie subalgebra.

Proof. The image phi_{A_1}(su(3)) is contained in the A_1 block of the
commutant, and phi_{A_1} preserves brackets. The Lie algebra su(3) is
generated by the anti-Hermitian traceless 3 x 3 matrices, of real
dimension 8. Its standard physics form uses the Gell-Mann matrices
lambda^a (a = 1, ..., 8) with Hermitian generators T^a = lambda^a / 2
satisfying

    [T^a, T^b] = i sum_c f^{abc} T^c,                                (23)

where f^{abc} are the su(3) structure constants. The embedding in the
commutant is

    T^a_c = phi_{A_1}(lambda^a / 2),   a = 1, ..., 8.                (24)

[Box]

Remark 5.3 (Physical color as input). The identification of this su(3)
factor with the physical color algebra su(3)_c is a physical input of
the framework, not a derivation. The framework derives that a copy of
su(3) acts naturally on the A_1 multiplicity space; the identification
with color is the same identification the Standard Model makes.

5.2 The su(2) subalgebra on the B_1 multiplicity space
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The B_1 multiplicity space M_{B_1} = C^2 is the home of the weak
isospin algebra. Let {v_1, v_2} be an orthonormal basis.

Definition. phi_{B_1} : M_2(C) -> End_C(V_14) is defined by

    phi_{B_1}(X) = sum_{a, b = 1}^{2} v_a X_{ab} v_b^dagger,         (25)

extended by zero on the other isotypic blocks.

Theorem 5.4 (su(2) subalgebra). The traceless anti-Hermitian subalgebra
su(2) of M_2(C) embeds in Comm(D4, V_14) via phi_{B_1} as a real
3-dimensional compact Lie subalgebra.

Proof. Same structure as Theorem 5.2. The Lie algebra su(2) is generated
by the anti-Hermitian traceless 2 x 2 matrices; the standard physics
basis uses the Pauli matrices sigma^i with Hermitian generators
T^i = sigma^i / 2 satisfying [T^i, T^j] = i epsilon^{ijk} T^k. [Box]

Remark 5.5 (Physical weak isospin as input). The identification of this
su(2) factor with the physical weak isospin algebra su(2)_L is a physical
input, not a derivation.

5.3 The u(1)^5 subalgebra from the isotypic projectors
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Definition. For each irrep rho of D4, the isotypic projector is

    P_rho = (dim rho / |D4|)
            sum_{g in D4} chi_rho(g)^* rho_14(g).                    (26)

The five projectors P_{A_1}, P_{A_2}, P_{B_1}, P_{B_2}, P_E satisfy
P_rho P_{rho'} = delta_{rho rho'} P_rho and sum_rho P_rho = I_14.

Theorem 5.6 (u(1)^5 subalgebra). The five isotypic projectors span an
abelian Lie subalgebra of Comm(D4, V_14) isomorphic to u(1)^5, of real
dimension 5.

Proof. Each P_rho commutes with the D4 action by construction, so
P_rho in Comm(D4, V_14). The projectors are mutually orthogonal
(P_rho P_{rho'} = 0 for rho != rho') and sum to I_14, giving a
5-dimensional span. As a Lie algebra with the commutator bracket, this
span is abelian (all brackets vanish), hence isomorphic to u(1)^5. [Box]

5.4 Closure of the gauge algebra
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Theorem 5.7 (Closure). The image

    phi_{A_1}(su(3)) + phi_{B_1}(su(2))
        + span{P_{A_1}, P_{A_2}, P_{B_1}, P_{B_2}, P_E}              (27)

is a Lie subalgebra of Comm(D4, V_14) isomorphic to

    su(3) + su(2) + u(1)^5,                                          (28)

of real dimension 8 + 3 + 5 = 16.

Proof. We verify the brackets.

(1) su(3)-su(3) brackets. phi_{A_1} is a Lie algebra homomorphism, so
    [phi_{A_1}(X), phi_{A_1}(Y)] = phi_{A_1}([X, Y]) in phi_{A_1}(su(3)).

(2) su(2)-su(2) brackets. Same as (1).

(3) su(3)-su(2) brackets. phi_{A_1}(su(3)) is supported only on the A_1
    multiplicity space; phi_{B_1}(su(2)) is supported only on the B_1
    multiplicity space. The two are orthogonal in End_C(V_14), so
    [phi_{A_1}(X), phi_{B_1}(Y)] = 0.

(4) su(3)-u(1)^5 brackets. Each projector P_rho acts as the identity on
    the rho-block and zero elsewhere. On the A_1 block, P_{A_1} acts as
    I_{M_{A_1}}, which commutes with every element of M_{A_1}(C) (in
    particular with phi_{A_1}(su(3))). Similarly for P_{A_2}, P_{B_1},
    P_{B_2}, P_E on the A_1 block (they act as zero). Hence
    [phi_{A_1}(X), P_rho] = 0 for all X in su(3) and all rho.

(5) su(2)-u(1)^5 brackets. Same as (4).

(6) u(1)^5-u(1)^5 brackets. The projectors are mutually commuting, so
    their span is abelian.

The algebra thus closes to su(3) + su(2) + u(1)^5, a direct sum of the
three factors, of real dimension 8 + 3 + 5 = 16. [Box]

Remark 5.8 (Sub-algebra of the commutant). The gauge algebra
su(3) + su(2) + u(1)^5 is a 16-dimensional real Lie subalgebra of the
27-dimensional complex commutant. The commutant has real dimension 54
(as a real vector space), so the gauge algebra is a proper subspace; the
remaining directions correspond to the "extra" structure not used by the
gauge algebra (specifically, the traceless parts of the A_2, B_2, and E
multiplicity blocks, which are also matrix algebras but not part of the
framework's gauge algebra).

6. SECTOR-COARSE CORRESPONDENCE
================================================================================

The derivation above was on the 14-dimensional visible sector
Omega^0|_{lambda=4}. We now extend to the full 56-dimensional Hodge
sector at lambda = 4, establishing the sector-coarse correspondence.

6.1 The full Hodge sector
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The full 56-dimensional sector at lambda = 4 is

    V_56 = Omega^0|_{lambda=4} + Omega^1|_{lambda=4}
           + Omega^2|_{lambda=4},                                    (29)

with D4 decomposition (from HCSM-02 [HCSM-02])

    V_56 = 9A_1 + 5A_2 + 7B_1 + 7B_2 + 14E.                          (30)

Dimension check: 9 + 5 + 7 + 7 + 28 = 56.

6.2 Sector operators
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Definition. On V_56, define the three sector operators

    I = P_{Omega^0} + P_{Omega^1} + P_{Omega^2},                     (31)

    F = P_{Omega^0} - P_{Omega^1} + P_{Omega^2},                     (32)

    O = P_{Omega^0} - P_{Omega^2}.                                   (33)

Restricted to the three sectors (Omega^0, Omega^1, Omega^2), their
signatures are

    I|_sectors = (1, 1, 1),
    F|_sectors = (1, -1, 1),
    O|_sectors = (1, 0, -1).                                         (34)

6.3 The sector-coarse sub-commutant
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Definition 6.1 (Sector-coarse sub-commutant). The sector-coarse
sub-commutant is the space of operators X in End_C(V_56) satisfying:

(i) [X, R] = [X, S] = 0 (D4 invariance);

(ii) X = P_{Omega^0} X P_{Omega^0} + P_{Omega^1} X P_{Omega^1}
    + P_{Omega^2} X P_{Omega^2} (sector block-diagonality);

(iii) X acts as a scalar on each (rho, Omega^k) block, where rho in
    {A_1, A_2, B_1, B_2, E} and k in {0, 1, 2}.

Theorem 6.3 (Sector-coarse correspondence). The sector-coarse
sub-commutant has complex dimension exactly 15 = 5 x 3 (five irreps
times three Hodge sectors). It is spanned by the three sector operators
{I, F, O} acting on each isotypic block.

Proof. By Schur's lemma applied to each (rho, Omega^k) block, a
D4-invariant, sector-block-diagonal, multiplicity-scalar operator is
determined by 15 complex scalar coefficients - one for each of the
5 x 3 = 15 blocks. Hence the space has complex dimension 15.

To see that the span of {I, F, O} on each block has the same dimension:
for each fixed rho, the restrictions of (I, F, O) to the three sectors
give three vectors in C^3:

    I^{(rho)} = (1, 1, 1),
    F^{(rho)} = (1, -1, 1),
    O^{(rho)} = (1, 0, -1).                                          (35)

The matrix with rows (I^{(rho)}, F^{(rho)}, O^{(rho)}) is

    M = [[1, 1, 1],
         [1, -1, 1],
         [1, 0, -1]],                                                (36)

with determinant

    det M = 1(1 - 0) - 1(-1 - 1) + 1(0 + 1) = 1 + 2 + 1 = 4 != 0.    (37)

So the three operators are linearly independent and span C^3 on each
rho-block. Over the five irreps, the span has complex dimension 5 x 3 =
15. The two spaces coincide. [Box]

Corollary 6.4 (Orientation-Z2). A sector-coarse functional Y on V_56 is
a complex linear combination

    Y = sum_rho (alpha_rho I + beta_rho F + gamma_rho O) P_rho,      (38)

of the three sector operators on each isotypic block. Under the sign
flip O -> -O, the multiset of physical sector values is preserved, up
to relabeling of Omega^0 and Omega^2.

Proof. Immediate from Theorem 6.3 and the antisymmetry of O under the
D4 orientation flip Omega^0 <-> Omega^2. The full orientation-Z2 theorem
is the subject of HCSM-09 [HCSM-09]. [Box]

7.1 Derived
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The following results are derived from the substrate and the D4 action
alone:

1. The D4 isotypic decomposition V_14 = 3A_1 + A_2 + 2B_1 + 2B_2 + 3E
   (Theorem 3.1; from HCSM-05).

   dim_C Comm(D4, V_14) = 27 (Theorem 4.3).
3. The endomorphism algebra decomposition
   Comm(D4, V_14) = M_3(C) + M_1(C) + M_2(C) + M_2(C) + M_3(C)
   (Theorem 4.4).
4. The embedding of su(3) in the A_1 block (Theorem 5.2).
5. The embedding of su(2) in the B_1 block (Theorem 5.4).
6. The embedding of u(1)^5 from the isotypic projectors (Theorem 5.6).
7. The closure of the gauge algebra su(3) + su(2) + u(1)^5 (Theorem 5.7).
8. The sector-coarse correspondence on the 56-dimensional Hodge sector:
   15-dimensional sub-commutant (Theorem 6.3).

7.2 Inputs
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The following are physical inputs, not derivations:

Input 1 (physical identification). The identification of the A_1 factor
with the physical color algebra su(3)_c and the B_1 factor with the
physical weak isospin algebra su(2)_L. The framework derives that an
su(3) factor and an su(2) factor act naturally on the multiplicities;
the identification of these factors with the physical gauge groups is
the same identification the Standard Model makes.

Input 2 (physical hypercharge). The physical hypercharge Y is a specific
linear combination of the three sector operators (I, F, O) on the A_1
and B_1 blocks. The specific coefficients are determined by the Standard
Model content and are the subject of HCSM-07 [HCSM-07].

7.3 What is not claimed
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

We do not claim to derive the Standard Model gauge group
SU(3)_c x SU(2)_L x U(1)_Y from first principles. We derive the Lie
algebra su(3) + su(2) + u(1)^5 as a subalgebra of the D4 commutant. The
reduction to the physical gauge algebra involves the physical inputs
above.

The framework's claim is: any physically realized gauge theory with
Standard Model content and the D4 structure of the 14-mode multiplet
must be compatible with a subalgebra su(3) + su(2) + u(1)^5 of the D4
commutant. The physical content of the gauge algebra is then selected by
Input 1 and Input 2.

9. CONCLUSION
================================================================================

This paper has established the sector-coarse correspondence of the Hodge
Complex Standard Model: the D4 isotypic sectors of the 14-mode multiplet
at lambda = 4 correspond to the gauge algebra structure through the
endomorphism algebra of their multiplicity spaces.

The 14-mode multiplet decomposes under D4 as
3A_1 + A_2 + 2B_1 + 2B_2 + 3E. The D4 commutant on the complexified
multiplet is the endomorphism algebra sum over rho of M_{m_rho}(C), of
complex dimension 27. The gauge algebra

    su(3) + su(2) + u(1)^5                                           (39)

embeds in the commutant: su(3) acts on the A_1 multiplicity space
(dim 3), su(2) on the B_1 multiplicity space (dim 2), and the five
isotypic projectors span the u(1)^5 center.

The derivation is HCSM-native: it uses only the D4 action on the 14-mode
multiplet at lambda = 4 and the standard endomorphism algebra structure.
No imported machinery (no path integrals, no SYK kernels, no gauge
fields as fundamental objects) is used.

On the full 56-dimensional Hodge sector at lambda = 4, the sector-coarse
correspondence is that the space of D4-invariant, sector-block-diagonal,
multiplicity-scalar operators is exactly 15-dimensional, spanned by the
sector operators {I, F, O} on each of the five isotypic blocks. This
15-dimensional space is the natural home of the physical hypercharge
functional, whose specific coefficients are determined by the Standard
Model content and are the subject of HCSM-07.

The identification of the derived su(3) and su(2) factors with the
physical color and weak-isospin gauge groups is a physical input, the
same input the Standard Model itself makes. The framework derives the
algebraic structure that any physically realized gauge theory with the
Standard Model content and the D4 structure of the 14-mode multiplet
must be compatible with.
