ABSTRACT
================================================================

The Hodge Complex Standard Model identifies the 14 modes at the
self-paired eigenvalue lambda = 4 of the discrete Laplacian on the
periodic 8x8 torus with one Standard Model generation. The assignment
of each mode to a specific Standard Model particle, together with the
N/4 mass label attached to each, is the framework's EMPIRICAL THESIS
(HCSM-23), structurally parallel to the Standard Model's Yukawa-
coupling sector. The substrate derives the 14 modes, their coordinates,
and the D4 orbit structure; the N/4 labels are EMPIRICAL ANCHORS.

Given the anchor, the mass map
m_i = A_mass * exp(-B_mass * (N/4)_i)
relates the N/4 anchors to the observed particle masses. The map's two
coefficients have framework-constant forms:
A_mass = (2d-1)N/2 = 224
and B_mass = (d-1)kL^2/(2N^2) = 0.541197, where
kL = ln(M_P/v_EW) is the electroweak-Planck hierarchy (HCSM-51).
Applied to the twelve charged particles (electron through top quark,
plus W, Z, and Higgs), the map reproduces the observed masses at
RMS 0.0449 dex -- approximately 11% accuracy -- spanning six orders of
magnitude. The residual does not correlate with N/4 (Spearman
rho = +0.414, p = 0.18), consistent with noise.

The mass map is a CONSISTENCY CHECK on the framework's mass sector: it
verifies that the anchored N/4 values and the framework-constant
coefficients A_mass and B_mass are jointly consistent with the observed
masses at the 11% level. The map also has structural consequences: it
predicts a neutrino mass m_nu = 1.77 meV from the anchor
(N/4)_nu = 60, it relates the neutrino and Planck masses via the
geometric-mean form m_nu * M_P = A_mass * v_EW * exp(2(d-1)) (used in
HCSM-51), and it identifies the map rate B_mass with the DNLS
interaction strength U (HCSM-18). All numerical claims are verified at
machine precision.

1.1 The mass map in HCSM

The Hodge Complex Standard Model (HCSM) identifies the 14 modes at the
self-paired eigenvalue lambda = 4 of the discrete Laplacian on the
periodic 8x8 torus with one Standard Model generation [HCSM-00,
HCSM-04]. The assignment of each mode to a specific Standard Model
particle, together with the N/4 mass label attached to each, is the
framework's EMPIRICAL THESIS (HCSM-23). It is not a derived result. The
N/4 labels are empirical anchors, structurally parallel to the Standard
Model's Yukawa couplings.

The mass map relates the anchored N/4 values to the observed masses
through the exponential relation

    (1)  m_i = A_mass * exp(-B_mass * (N/4)_i),

with two coefficients

    (2)  A_mass = (2d-1)N/2,
         B_mass = (d-1)kL^2 / (2N^2).

The map top A_mass is a framework constant. The rate B_mass is a
framework constant that depends on the warp factor
kL = ln(M_P/v_EW), which is derived in HCSM-51 as kL_bare = 38.4
(matching the observed hierarchy 38.442527 to 0.11%).

Throughout this paper:

  - The 14 modes, their coordinates, and the D4 orbit structure are
    DERIVED from the substrate [HCSM-04].

  - The N/4 mass label attached to each mode is an EMPIRICAL ANCHOR
    (HCSM-23).

  - The coefficients A_mass and B_mass have framework-constant forms.
    Their specific forms were identified by requiring the map to
    reproduce the observed mass spectrum; the origin of the forms is
    disclosed in Section 3.3.

  - The map itself is a CONSISTENCY CHECK on the framework's mass
    sector: it verifies that the anchored N/4 values and the
    framework-constant coefficients are jointly consistent with the
    observed masses.

1.2 The one-line summary

The mass map's exponent spans from 0.27 (top) to 13.0 (electron) at the
two ends of the anchor range. The exponential of this range covers the
six orders of magnitude from the top mass to the electron mass. The map
reproduces the twelve observed masses at RMS 0.0449 dex with
framework-constant coefficients.

      0.11%).
