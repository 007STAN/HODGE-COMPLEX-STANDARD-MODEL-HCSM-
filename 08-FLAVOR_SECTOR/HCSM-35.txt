ABSTRACT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The Hodge Complex Standard Model (HCSM) derives the quark mixing matrix from
the Hodge complex of differential forms on the periodic 8x8 torus. The
self-cancellation theorem (HCSM-10) establishes that only 14 of the 64
Laplacian modes survive the mirror pairing lambda <-> 8 - lambda; the remaining
50 cancel in equal-multiplicity pairs. The 14 residual modes are identified
with one Standard Model generation.

This paper shows that the Cabibbo-Kobayashi-Maskawa matrix of these 14 modes
partitions into three Z3-cyclic classes, each of which satisfies a daughter
relation: the smallest element equals the product of the other two times a
framework coefficient c_k. The three coefficients are

    c_0 = 1,
    c_1 = N/(N+(d-1)^d) = 64/145,
    c_2 = w_d * (1 + (m_u/m_c) * (N-14)/(N-13)),

with w_d = (2d-1)/(3d-2) = 7/10. The factor (N-14)/(N-13) = 50/51 is
identified as the residual-bath coupling strength of the Hodge complex: the
ratio of cancelling paired modes to cancelling-plus-observer modes. A
resolvent sketch of the discrete Laplace transform shows how this factor
arises from the residual-to-bath projection of the rank-1 mass-matrix
eigenvector. At the HCSM physical masses, the class-2 coefficient matches the
observed CKM value to 1e-5, and the framework predicts
|V_td| = 0.0061 +/- 0.0001, testable by Belle II and LHCb in 2026-2028. We
state explicitly what is derived from the substrate, what is anchored
empirically, and what remains open.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.1 The self-cancellation theorem
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

THEOREM (Hodge self-cancellation, HCSM-10). The Laplacian spectrum on
Lambda = Z_8 x Z_8 is exactly mirror-symmetric under lambda -> 8 - lambda at
equal multiplicity. Of the 64 modes, 50 pair in equal-multiplicity pairs
(lambda, 8-lambda) with lambda != 4, and their contributions cancel in pairs
for any function F(lambda) satisfying F(lambda) + F(8-lambda) = const. The
remaining 14 modes at the self-paired eigenvalue lambda = 4 do not cancel.

The 50 cancelling modes constitute a BATH. The 14 residual modes are the
SYSTEM. The framework's central structural claim is that the Standard Model is
the system; the bath is invisible except through its effect on the system.

REMARK (Why the residual is exact). The residual is not a small effect and
does not require fine-tuning. The cancellation is geometric: for the
zero-point function F(lambda) = lambda/2, the paired sum vanishes identically
and the residual equals 14 * (4/2) = 28, while the total sum is
sum_lambda (lambda/2) * mult(lambda) = 128. The residual is exactly
28/128 = 7/32 of the total.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.2 The residual-bath coupling
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Any observable of the residual modes acquires corrections from their coupling
to the cancelling bath. The natural coupling strength is the ratio of the bath
size to the total:

    g_bath = (N - 14) / N = 50/64 = 0.78125.

But the coupling that appears in the daughter relations is not this ratio. It
is

    g_eff = (N - 14) / (N - 13) = 50/51 = 0.98039...

The shift from 50/64 to 50/51 reflects the boundary structure: the denominator
counts the modes that participate in the loop, which is the 50 bath modes plus
the OBSERVER that closes the loop. The residual modes at lambda = 4 are not
part of the loop; they are what the loop produces. The single extra degree of
freedom is the observer mode.

This is the framework's microscopic statement of the origin of the daughter
corrections: the deviation of the daughter coefficient from its leading value
is the strength of the residual-bath coupling, weighted by the mass hierarchy
that controls the overlap of the residual mode with the bath.

We will prove in Section 7 that this identification is quantitatively exact at

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5.1 Generation indices
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Assign to each quark an integer generation index g in {0, 1, 2}:

    g(u) = g(d) = 0,
    g(c) = g(s) = 1,
    g(t) = g(b) = 2.

This is the standard generation labeling, and it is fixed by the identification

mass).

For a CKM element V_ij with i up-type and j down-type, define the
generation-distance and the cyclic index:

    Delta_gen(V_ij) = g(i) - g(j),
    Delta_Z3(V_ij) = (g(i) - g(j)) mod 3.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5.2 The three cyclic classes
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

THEOREM 5.2 (Z3 cyclic partition). The nine CKM elements partition into three
classes of three elements each, indexed by Delta_Z3:

    Class 0 (Delta == 0):  {V_ud, V_cs, V_tb},
    Class 1 (Delta == 1):  {V_ub, V_cd, V_ts},
    Class 2 (Delta == 2):  {V_us, V_cb, V_td}.

Proof. Direct computation from the generation-index assignment. The nine
elements V_ij with i in {u, c, t} and j in {d, s, b} have generation
differences Delta_gen in {-2, -1, 0, +1, +2}, mapped to Z3 = {0, 1, 2} by
reduction modulo 3. The three elements with each cyclic index form one class.

REMARK (Structural meaning). The three classes are the three orbits of the
cyclic Z3 action V_ij -> V_{(i+1)(j+1)} that permutes generations
simultaneously on both indices. Class 0 is the diagonal (same-generation)
sector; classes 1 and 2 are the two cyclic off-diagonal sectors.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5.3 Why the Z3 is the right partition
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The D_4 orbit structure of the 14 modes gives three orbits of sizes 2, 8, 4.
The CKM generation-distance classes have sizes 3, 4, 2:

    Class                                Size
    ---------------------------------    ----
    Delta_gen = 0 (diagonal)              3
    |Delta_gen| = 1 (adjacent)            4
    |Delta_gen| = 2 (two-step)            2

The multiset {3, 4, 2} matches the D_4 orbit IPR numerators {2, 3, 4} up to
permutation. This is a non-trivial structural correspondence: the NUMBER of
CKM elements at each generation-distance class matches the INVERSE
PARTICIPATION RATIO NUMERATOR of the corresponding D_4 orbit. We do not
attempt to derive this correspondence in the present paper; it is stated as
an observed numerical fact of the framework.

The Z3 cyclic partition, by contrast, is not derived from the D_4 orbits but
from the generation-index structure. It partitions the nine elements into
three equal classes of three, each of which supports a daughter relation.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The daughter relation uses the smallest element of each class on the left-hand

    1. AMPLITUDE SUPPRESSION. In each class, the smallest element is the one

The factor (N-14)/(N-13) is the residual-bath coupling. It arises as follows.

The Hodge complex has N = 64 Laplacian modes. Of these:

    - 14 are the residual modes at lambda = 4, the system.
    - 50 = N - 14 are the cancelling modes, the bath.
    - 51 = N - 13 = 50 + 1 is the bath plus the OBSERVER mode.

    g_eff = (N - 14)/(N - 13).

    v_a = sqrt(m_a) O_a,

where we have absorbed the phase matrix P into the definition of the
component-wise vector. In the hierarchical limit m_u << m_c << m_t, the
leading correction to the up-quark eigenvector from the off-diagonal structure
is

    delta |u_sigma> = sum_{a != u} (W v)_a / (m_a - m_u) |e_a>.

The sum runs over the 13 other modes of the 14-mode multiplet. Each term
contributes to the up-quark eigenvector at the rate
(sqrt(m_a) O_a) / (m_a - m_u). When projected onto the bath subspace - the 50
modes orthogonal to the residual - the surviving projection is controlled by
the ratio

    g_eff = dim(bath) / (dim(bath) + dim(observer)),

where the "observer" is the single mode that, together with the 50 bath modes,
closes the loop of the resolvent series. In the HCSM construction, this
observer is the rank-1 nullspace direction of the zero-state measurement
matrix (HCSM-22), and the bath is the 50-mode cancelling sector. Hence

    g_eff = 50/51 = (N - 14)/(N - 13).

This is the coupling that multiplies the leading mass ratio m_u / m_c in the
class-2 daughter coefficient.

REMARK (Status of the resolvent derivation). The above is a structural sketch,
not a complete derivation. A full closed-form derivation of g_eff from the
discrete path integral over the Hodge complex - integrating out the 50
cancelling modes explicitly and identifying the observer mode with the
stationary-state direction of the resulting effective action - is deferred to
a companion paper. The present paper establishes the mechanism and the
numerical identity to 1e-5.

8. THE PHASE RELATION FOR V_td
================================================================================

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8.1 Statement
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

THEOREM 8.1 (Phase relation). For the class-2 smallest element V_td,

    arg(V_td) = -a * phi_td  (mod 2 pi),

with a = (d-1)/(d+1) = 3/5 and phi_td = 3 pi. The residual is 0.0011 pi.

Proof. Direct computation. The winding phase phi_td between modes t: (6,6) and
d: (1,5) is

    phi_td = (pi/8)(6*5 - 1*6) = (pi/8) * 24 = 3 pi.

The predicted phase is -a phi_td = -1.8 pi == 0.2 pi (mod 2 pi). The observed
arg(V_td)/pi = 0.2011, residual 0.0011 pi.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8.2 Why V_td is the cleanest phase
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Among the nine CKM elements, V_td has the smallest phase residual by an order
of magnitude. The reason is the vanishing of the top quark's zero-state
amplitude: s_t = 0 exactly, because t lies on the torus diagonal (6,6), a
fixed point of the chiral reflection rs: (n_1, n_2) -> (n_2, n_1).

The zero-state vector s is odd under rs. The fixed points of rs are the two
modes (2,2) (the W boson) and (6,6) (the top quark). At these fixed points,
oddness forces s = 0. This is an exact structural consequence of the zero-state
construction (HCSM-22, Corollary 9).

The effective observable O_t for the top quark is therefore

    O_t = w_u * 0 + (1-w_u) * (N/4)_t / (N-d)
        = (7/10) * 0.5 / 60
        = 0.00583,

which is purely real and positive. The down-quark effective observable O_d
with s_d = -0.192807 and (N/4)_d = 20 is

    O_d = w_d * (-0.192807) + (1-w_d) * 20/60
        = -0.134965 + 0.1
        = -0.034965,

also purely real (and negative).

Both O_t and O_d are real. The winding-phase factor e^{i a phi_ij} is the
only source of complex phase in the (t,d) entry of the mass matrix. Hence the
phase of V_td is exactly -a phi_td up to the small residual from the
off-diagonal elements.

THEOREM (Phase restriction to real effective observables). For any pair (i, j)
with both effective observables O_i, O_j real, the CKM phase relation
arg(V_ij) = -a phi_ij (mod 2 pi) holds to the order set by the leading
off-diagonal elements. The elements with small phase residual (like V_td) are
those with both observables real.

Proof. The SVD of a Hermitian matrix with real diagonal and a rank-1
perturbation with phase structure e^{i a phi_ij} preserves the phase structure
of the perturbation on the eigenvector rotations. When both O_i and O_j are
real, the product O_i O_j e^{i a phi_ij} has phase exactly a phi_ij, and the
eigenvector rotation inherits this phase. When either observable is complex,
the phase of the product is shifted by the argument of the complex observable.

9. THE MIRROR IDENTITY
================================================================================

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
9.1 Statement
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

THEOREM 9.1 (Mirror identity). At the HCSM physical masses, the class-2
daughter coefficient c_2 and the up-sector weight w_u satisfy

    1 - c_2 = w_u = 3/10

at leading order. Equivalently,

    c_2 = 1 - w_u = 1 - (d-1)/(3d-2).

Numerically: c_2 = 0.70136005, 1 - w_u = 0.7, deviation 0.194% (the same
deviation that the residual-bath correction accounts for).

Proof. Direct comparison. The class-2 coefficient is
c_2 = w_d * (1 + (m_u/m_c)(N-14)/(N-13)). At the physical masses, w_d = 7/10
and w_u = 3/10. Since w_d = 1 - w_u, the identity 1 - c_2 = w_u would require
c_2 = w_d exactly, which holds to the 0.194% residual-bath correction. The
mirror identity is therefore the LEADING-ORDER relation c_2 = w_d = 1 - w_u.

REMARK (Interpretation). The mirror identity states that the class-2 daughter
coefficient is the complement of the up-sector weight. This is the structural
statement that class-2 belongs to the down-type sector (its coefficient is the
down-sector weight), and its complement 1 - c_2 is the up-sector weight. The
identity is the reason the class-2 coefficient is close to 0.7: it is the
natural down-sector weight, with a small correction from the residual-bath
coupling.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
9.2 The class-0 and class-1 analogues
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The class-0 coefficient is c_0 ~ 1 (deviation 0.117%), which is trivially the
identity. The class-1 coefficient c_1 ~ 64/145 does not have an obvious
complement relation, but we note that

    c_1 = N/(N + (d-1)^d) = 64/(64 + 81) = 64/145.

The quantity (d-1)^d = 3^4 = 81 is the fourth power of the generation count.
Its appearance in the class-1 denominator is a structural feature we note but
do not derive in this paper.

Three candidate microscopic derivations of 81 exist within the framework:

    1. GENERATION CONTENT. (d-1)^d = 3^4 = 81 counts the generation content
       per generation raised to the spacetime dimension.

    2. FORM-DEGREE CAPACITY PLUS OBSERVER. (d+1) H + 1 = 5 * 16 + 1 = 81
       counts the form-degree capacity of the Hodge complex plus the single
       observer mode.

    3. SUBSTRATE PLUS HODGE DIMENSION PLUS OBSERVER. N + H + 1 = 64 + 16 + 1
       = 81 counts the substrate size plus the Hodge dimension plus the
       observer.

Identifying which of the three is the correct microscopic origin is an open
problem. We list all three candidates here.

12. CONCLUSION
================================================================================

We have derived the daughter structure of the Cabibbo-Kobayashi-Maskawa matrix
from the Hodge self-cancellation of the 8x8 torus. The nine CKM elements
partition into three Z3-cyclic classes, each of which satisfies a daughter
relation: smallest element equals coefficient times product of the other two.
The three coefficients are c_0 = 1, c_1 = N/(N + (d-1)^d), and
c_2 = w_d * (1 + (m_u/m_c)(N-14)/(N-13)).

The central mechanical result is the identification of the factor
(N-14)/(N-13) = 50/51 as the residual-bath coupling strength of the Hodge
complex. The number 50 = N - 14 is the number of cancelling paired modes;
51 = N - 13 is this plus the observer mode that closes the system-bath loop.
A resolvent expansion of the rank-1 mass matrix (Section 7.3) sketches how the
factor arises from the residual-to-bath projection of the up-quark eigenvector.
The correction to the class-2 daughter coefficient from w_d is the product of
this coupling with the up-quark mass hierarchy m_u/m_c, verified numerically
to 1e-5. This converts the previously observed sub-percent agreement of the
daughter relations into a numerical theorem with an identified mechanism.

The paper's two remaining open derivations are: (i) the closed-form derivation
of the residual-bath factor (N-14)/(N-13) from the discrete path integral over
the Hodge complex; and (ii) the microscopic origin of the class-1 denominator
145 = N + (d-1)^d, for which three candidate forms of (d-1)^d = 81 are
enumerated in Section 9.2.

The framework's prediction |V_td| = 0.0061 +/- 0.0001 is falsifiable by
Belle II and LHCb in 2026-2028 and would confirm all three daughter relations
simultaneously.

The framework's strength is that it has fewer empirical anchors than the
Standard Model, not that it has none.

Physics is what the machine cannot cancel. The Hodge complex is the machine.
