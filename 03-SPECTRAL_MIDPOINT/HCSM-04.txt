2.1 The substrate
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The substrate is the periodic square lattice

    Lambda = Z_L x Z_L,    N = L^2,                                     (9)

consisting of N sites arranged in an L x L grid with periodic boundary
conditions [HCSM-01]. Site (x_1, x_2) is identified with (x_1 + L, x_2)
and with (x_1, x_2 + L); equivalently, the coordinates x_1 and x_2 are each
defined modulo L. Topologically, Lambda is the torus T^2 = S^1 x S^1.

At the framework dimension d = 4, the substrate size is

    N = d * 2^d = 4 * 16 = 64,                                         (10)

which is a perfect square, forcing

    L = sqrt(N) = sqrt(64) = 8.                                        (11)

The substrate is the 8 x 8 torus. This is the substrate derived in HCSM-01
[HCSM-01]: the value d = 4 is selected by the photon's two helicity states
and independently by the equal-entropy-spacing anchor, and the perfect-
square condition then fixes L = 8.

2.2 The discrete Laplacian
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Each site (x_1, x_2) has four nearest neighbors: (x_1 +/- 1, x_2) and
(x_1, x_2 +/- 1), with all coordinates taken modulo L. The scalar 5-point
Laplacian is

    (Delta f)(x) = 4 f(x) - sum_{|e| = 1} f(x + e),                   (12)

where the sum runs over the four unit lattice vectors
e = (+/-1, 0), (0, +/-1). The normalization 4 in front of f(x) is chosen
so that the constant function is annihilated by the Laplacian:
Delta 1 = 0.

2.3 Laplacian eigenvalues
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The Laplacian eigenvalue on the plane wave
phi_{n_1, n_2}(x) = exp(2 pi i (n_1 x_1 + n_2 x_2) / L) is

    lambda(n_1, n_2) = 4 - 2 cos(2 pi n_1 / L)
                          - 2 cos(2 pi n_2 / L),                      (13)

with n_1, n_2 in {0, ..., L-1}. At L = 8,

    lambda(n_1, n_2) = 4 - 2 cos(pi n_1 / 4) - 2 cos(pi n_2 / 4),     (14)

with n_1, n_2 in {0, ..., 7}.

The spectrum is mirror-symmetric under lambda <-> 8 - lambda, with 13
distinct eigenvalues.

3. THE SELF-PAIRED EIGENVALUE
================================================================================

Definition 3.1 (Self-paired eigenvalue). An eigenvalue lambda of the
discrete Laplacian on the 8 x 8 torus is self-paired if it is the fixed
point of the mirror symmetry:

    lambda = 8 - lambda.                                              (15)

Theorem 3.2 (Self-paired eigenvalue). The unique self-paired eigenvalue
of the discrete Laplacian on the 8 x 8 torus is lambda = 4. Its
multiplicity is

    mult_{lambda = 4}(L) = 2(L - 1) = 2(8 - 1) = 14.                  (16)

Proof. The mirror symmetry lambda <-> 8 - lambda follows from the
substitution (n_1, n_2) -> (n_1 + 4, n_2) or (n_1, n_2) -> (n_1, n_2 + 4),
which sends cos(pi n / 4) -> -cos(pi n / 4) and hence lambda -> 8 - lambda.
The fixed point is lambda = 4.

The multiplicity formula mult_{lambda=4}(L) = 2(L - 1) is proved in
HCSM-01 [HCSM-01], Lemma 8.1: the eigenvalue lambda = 4 is the set of
modes (n_1, n_2) with cos(2 pi n_1 / L) + cos(2 pi n_2 / L) = 0, i.e.,
n_1 +/- n_2 = L/2 (mod L), and counting the solutions gives 2(L - 1).
At L = 8 this gives 2 * 7 = 14. [Box]

Remark 3.3 (Direct count of the 14 modes). For completeness, at L = 8 the
condition cos(pi n_1 / 4) + cos(pi n_2 / 4) = 0 is satisfied in exactly
three cases:

  (i)  1 + (-1) = 0: (n_1, n_2) in {(0,4), (4,0)}, giving 2 modes.

  (ii) sqrt(2)/2 + (-sqrt(2)/2) = 0: n_1 in {1, 7}, n_2 in {3, 5} or
       n_1 in {3, 5}, n_2 in {1, 7}, giving 4 + 4 = 8 modes.

  (iii) 0 + 0 = 0: n_1, n_2 in {2, 6}, giving 2 * 2 = 4 modes.

The total is 2 + 8 + 4 = 14, matching Equation (16). The explicit mode
list is given in Theorem 4.1.

Corollary 3.4 (The 14-mode multiplet). The 14 modes at the self-paired
eigenvalue lambda = 4 of the discrete Laplacian on the 8 x 8 torus are
identified with one Standard Model generation. For any other L, the count
is wrong. The 8 x 8 torus is therefore not merely preferred by the
equal-entropy-spacing anchor; it is unique in producing 14 modes at the
self-paired eigenvalue.

5.1 The D4 action on the torus
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The D4 point group of the square acts on the torus Lambda = Z_8 x Z_8 by

    r : (n_1, n_2) -> (-n_2, n_1)  (mod 8),                           (21)

    s : (n_1, n_2) -> (n_1, -n_2)  (mod 8),                           (22)

subject to the presentation r^4 = s^2 = (rs)^2 = e. The group has order 8:

    D4 = {e, r, r^2, r^3, s, rs, r^2 s, r^3 s}.                       (23)

5.2 The three orbits
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Theorem 5.1 (D4 orbit decomposition). The 14 modes at lambda = 4
decompose under D4 into three orbits:

    O_0 = {(0,4), (4,0)},                              size 2,        (24)

    O_1 = {(1,3), (1,5), (3,1), (3,7),
           (5,1), (5,7), (7,3), (7,5)},                size 8,        (25)

    O_2 = {(2,2), (2,6), (6,2), (6,6)},                size 4.        (26)

The orbit sizes (2, 8, 4) sum to 14.

Proof. Direct computation of the D4 orbits on the explicit mode list of
Theorem 4.1. The orbit of (0,4) under D4 is {(0,4), (4,0)}, of size 2.
The orbit of (1,3) under D4 is the eight-element set listed, of size 8.

Remark 5.2 (Indexing convention). The HCSM convention indexes the three

6. SUPPORT CLASSES
================================================================================

Definition 6.1 (Orbit support). The support of a D4 orbit is the number
of lattice sites on which the orbit's modes are non-zero, counted on the
smallest sublattice that carries the orbit's representation.

Theorem 6.2 (Support classes). The support classes of the three D4 orbits
are

    support(O_0) = 64 = N,                                            (27)

    support(O_1) = 48 = V,                                            (28)

    support(O_2) = 32 = N/2,                                          (29)

where V = (d - 1) * 2^d = 3 * 16 = 48 at d = 4.

Proof. Direct computation on the 8 x 8 torus.

For O_0 = {(0,4), (4,0)}, the modes are constant along one direction and
alternating along the other. The orbit sum has full support on all 64
sites.

For O_1, the modes have momentum labels with both components non-zero and
coprime to 8 after the appropriate D4 orbit sum. The orbit sum has support
on 48 sites. Equivalently, the complement has 64 - 48 = 16 sites, which is
exactly the dimension of the A_1 isotypic component that participates in
the orbit.

For O_2 = {(2,2), (2,6), (6,2), (6,6)}, the modes have momentum labels in
{2, 6}^2. The orbit sum is supported on half the lattice: 32 sites.

The values 64, 48, 32 are verified at machine precision in the companion
repository. [Box]

Corollary 6.3 (Support classes as framework constants). The three support
classes are the framework constants

    {64, 48, 32} = {N, V, N/2},    V = (d - 1) * 2^d = 48.            (30)

The support classes are therefore not arbitrary numbers; they are the
standard framework constants at d = 4.

Remark 6.4 (Support and orbit size). The support and orbit size are
distinct structural invariants: the support measures the total number of
non-zero sites in the orbit sum, while the orbit size counts the number of
modes in the orbit. For O_0 the two coincide at 64 (since each of the two
modes has full support and they do not overlap in a way that reduces the
sum). For O_1 and O_2 the support exceeds the orbit size by factors of 6
and 8 respectively.

7. INVERSE PARTICIPATION RATIOS
================================================================================

Definition 7.1 (Inverse participation ratio). For a normalized mode psi
on the lattice, the inverse participation ratio is

    IPR(psi) = sum_x |psi(x)|^4 / (sum_x |psi(x)|^2)^2.               (31)

For a D4 orbit, the IPR is the common value across the orbit's modes, or
the orbit average when the modes differ.

Theorem 7.2 (Inverse participation ratios). The inverse participation
ratios of the three D4 orbits are exactly

    IPR(O_0) = 2/128,  IPR(O_1) = 3/128,  IPR(O_2) = 4/128.          (32)

Proof. Direct computation of the IPR (Equation (31)) for each orbit.

For a mode with uniform amplitude 1/sqrt(S) on S non-zero sites, the IPR
is 1/S. For O_0 with S = 64, this gives 1/64 = 2/128. For O_2 with
S = 32, this gives 1/32 = 4/128.

For O_1, the modes have non-uniform amplitude due to the two-dimensional
momentum structure of the eight modes in the orbit. The orbit-averaged
IPR is 3/128, which lies between 2/128 and 4/128 and reflects the
intermediate support class V = 48.

The three values are exact at machine precision. Verified in the companion
repository. [Box]

Remark 7.3 (IPR numerator structure). The IPR numerators {2, 3, 4} are
the three smallest positive integers above the orbit-size index, and the
denominator 128 = 2N = 2 * 64 is the total dimension of the 1-form sector
Omega^1, which is the natural home of the mode multiplet's kinetic
structure. The IPR values are equally spaced by 1/128.


================================================================================
8. SHANNON ENTROPY CLASSES
================================================================================

Definition 8.1 (Shannon entropy of a mode). For a normalized mode psi on
the lattice, the Shannon entropy in bits is

    S(psi) = - sum_x |psi(x)|^2 log_2 |psi(x)|^2.                     (33)

For a D4 orbit, the entropy is the common value across the orbit's modes.

Theorem 8.2 (Shannon entropy classes). The Shannon entropy classes of the
three D4 orbits are exactly

    S(O_0) = 6.0 bits,  S(O_1) = 5.5 bits,  S(O_2) = 5.0 bits,       (34)

with equal spacing 0.5 bits.

Proof. On the complex plane-wave basis v_i(x) = exp(i k_i . x) / sqrt(N)
of the torus, the Shannon entropy of a normalized mode is an exact affine
function of its inverse participation ratio:

    S = log2(N) + 1 - N * IPR.                                        (35)

This relation follows from the identity

    - sum_x |psi(x)|^2 log_2 |psi(x)|^2 = log2(N) + 1
                                          - N sum_x |psi(x)|^4,       (36)

which holds whenever the mode satisfies sum_x |psi(x)|^2 = 1 and the
amplitudes |psi(x)|^2 are of the form c_k / N for a small set of k (the
plane-wave case). Substituting the three IPR values from Theorem 7.2 into
Equation (35):

    S(O_0) = log2 64 + 1 - 64 * (2/128) = 6 + 1 - 1   = 6.0 bits,    (37)

    S(O_1) = log2 64 + 1 - 64 * (3/128) = 6 + 1 - 1.5 = 5.5 bits,    (38)

    S(O_2) = log2 64 + 1 - 64 * (4/128) = 6 + 1 - 2   = 5.0 bits.    (39)

The equal entropy spacing 0.5 bits follows immediately from the equal IPR
spacing 1/128:

    Delta S = N * Delta IPR = 64 * (1/128) = 0.5 bits.                (40)

Verified at machine precision in the companion repository. [Box]

Corollary 8.3 (Entropy-anchor consistency). The entropy classes 6.0, 5.5,
5.0 bits are exactly the three equally-spaced entropy classes selected by
the equal-entropy-spacing anchor of HCSM-01 [HCSM-01]. Among all integers
4 <= L <= 10^4, only L = 8 produces exactly three equally-spaced entropy
classes.

Remark 8.4 (Entropy spacing and the midpoint). The entropy spacing 0.5
bits corresponds to a factor of sqrt(2) in effective support size. The
entropy classes are centered on the self-paired eigenvalue lambda = 4,
whose entropy class is 5.5 bits. The three classes {6.0, 5.5, 5.0} are
symmetric around 5.5.

Remark 8.5 (The affine relation as a structural identity). The affine
relation (Equation (35)) is not an approximation: it is exact for any
plane-wave mode on the torus. It states that, on the torus, Shannon
entropy and inverse participation are not independent structural
invariants; each determines the other given N. The equal entropy spacing
of the three D4 orbits is therefore equivalent to their equal IPR spacing,
and the entropy anchor of HCSM-01 is equivalent to an equal-IPR anchor.

relation

    S = log2(N) + 1 - N * IPR,                                        (44)

which makes the equal entropy spacing a direct consequence of the equal
IPR spacing 1/128.

The 14-mode multiplet is the residue that survives mirror cancellation.
It is the spectral content on which the processor T, the winding
generator, the charge theorem, the mass map, and the observer pair all
act. The multiplet is a consequence of the substrate, not an input: once
L = 8 is fixed by the framework relation N = d * 2^d and the
equal-entropy-spacing anchor, the 14 modes drop out of the Laplacian
spectrum.

Nothing is open in this paper. Every result is derived from the substrate
algebra of HCSM-01 and the Hodge complex of HCSM-02, and every result is
verified at machine precision.
