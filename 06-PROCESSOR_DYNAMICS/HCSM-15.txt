ABSTRACT
----------------------------------------------------------------

The Hodge Complex Standard Model derives the gauge algebra
g_SM = su(3)_c + su(2)_L + u(1)^5 from the D_4 endomorphism algebra of
the 14-mode multiplet at lambda = 4 (HCSM-06). Two Hermitian operators
act on that multiplet: the loop operator M_loop of the framework
(HCSM-17) and the DNLS Hartree Hamiltonian H_DNLS = 4I + U K whose
kernel has entries K_ab = sum_x |phi_a(x)|^2 |phi_b(x)|^2 built from
the mode wavefunctions on the torus (HCSM-04, HCSM-19).

We determine the intersection of g_SM with the commutant of each
operator on the 14-mode multiplet. We prove:

  (i)   g_SM cap Comm(M_loop) is a three-dimensional real, Hermitian,
        abelian subalgebra of g_SM;
  (ii)  every element of that subalgebra has equal coefficients on the
        isotypic projectors P_{A_2}, P_{B_2}, P_E;
  (iii) the root generators lambda^1, lambda^2, lambda^4, lambda^5,
        lambda^6, lambda^7 of su(3)_c and sigma^1, sigma^2 of su(2)_L
        lie in the kernel of the projection onto the loop commutant;
  (iv)  g_SM cap Comm(H_DNLS) is a ten-dimensional real, Hermitian
        subalgebra of g_SM isomorphic to su(2)_L + u(1)^7;
  (v)   the full su(2)_L factor is preserved exactly, with
        sigma^1, sigma^2, sigma^3 in the nullspace;
  (vi)  su(3)_c is broken to a two-dimensional abelian subalgebra, with
        the root generators lambda^2, lambda^3, lambda^7 orthogonal to
        the DNLS commutant and the Cartan generators lambda^3, lambda^8
        having partial overlap.

The exact su(2)_L preservation is proved by a doubled-momentum pairing
mechanism: the two B_1 basis vectors pair modes with the same doubled
momentum (2n_1, 2n_2) mod 8 and opposite signs, so the B_1-compressed
kernel U_B1^T K U_B1 vanishes identically. We construct the explicit
B_1 basis vectors in Appendix A and verify the density-equality
precondition directly from the HCSM-04 wavefunctions. The closure of
the DNLS coupling to g = q = 33/64 follows from HCSM-64 section 11.5
and is consistent with the Hartree trace Tr(K) = 11/32. All claims are
HCSM-native and verified at machine precision.

1. INTRODUCTION

1.1 Gauge symmetry and the machine

The Hodge Complex Standard Model (HCSM) derives the gauge algebra
g_SM as a subalgebra of the D_4 commutant on the complexified 14-mode
multiplet at the self-paired eigenvalue lambda = 4 of the discrete
Laplacian on the periodic 8x8 torus (HCSM-06). The processor T and the
winding generator H_wind act on the same multiplet (HCSM-13, HCSM-14),
and it is a structural question which part of the gauge algebra
survives each operator's action.

The loop operator M_loop of HCSM-17 and the DNLS Hartree Hamiltonian
H_DNLS of HCSM-18 and HCSM-19 are the two Hermitian operators whose
commutants we determine in this paper. Both commutants are finite-
dimensional real, Hermitian subalgebras of g_SM; their dimensions and
Lie-algebraic structures are the central results.

1.2 The one-line summary

On the 14-mode multiplet, the loop operator preserves only a three-
dimensional abelian subalgebra of g_SM; the DNLS Hartree Hamiltonian
preserves a larger ten-dimensional subalgebra isomorphic to
su(2)_L + u(1)^7. The DNLS Hamiltonian preserves the full weak-isospin
factor exactly via a doubled-momentum pairing mechanism in the B_1
isotypic sector, while reducing the color factor to a two-dimensional
abelian remnant.

1.3 Main results

  * Loop commutant (Theorem 3.1).
    g_SM cap Comm(M_loop) is a 3-dimensional real, Hermitian, abelian
    subalgebra of g_SM.

  * Isotypic projector degeneracy (Theorem 4.1).
    Every element of the loop commutant has equal coefficients on
    P_{A_2}, P_{B_2}, P_E.

  * No root generators (Theorem 4.3).
    The loop commutant has zero overlap with lambda^{1,2,4,5,6,7} and
    sigma^{1,2}.

  * DNLS commutant (Theorem 5.4).
    g_SM cap Comm(H_DNLS) is a 10-dimensional real, Hermitian
    subalgebra of g_SM, isomorphic to su(2)_L + u(1)^7.

  * su(2)_L exact preservation (Corollary 6.3).
    The three generators sigma^1, sigma^2, sigma^3 lie in the DNLS
    commutant, with overlap 1 at machine precision.

  * Doubled-momentum pairing (Theorem 6.2 / A.6).
    The B_1-compressed kernel U_B1^T K U_B1 vanishes identically
    because the two B_1 basis vectors pair modes with the same doubled
    momentum (2n_1, 2n_2) mod 8 and opposite signs.

  * su(3)_c breaking (Theorem 7.1).
    The DNLS commutant contains a 2-dimensional abelian subalgebra of

    the commutant.

2.1 The substrate and the 14-mode multiplet

The substrate is Lambda = Z_8 x Z_8 with N = 64 sites (HCSM-01). The
discrete 5-point Laplacian has eigenvalue

  lambda(n_1,n_2) = 4 - 2 cos(pi n_1 / 4) - 2 cos(pi n_2 / 4),
  (n_1,n_2) in {0,...,7}^2.                                    (2.1)

The self-paired eigenvalue lambda = 4 has multiplicity 14 (HCSM-04).
In the standard mode order

  (nu, e, u, d, s, mu, dark, c, tau, b, W, Z, H, t),           (2.2)

the mode coordinates are

  nu:(0,4)    e:(4,0)    u:(1,3)    d:(1,5)
  s:(3,1)     mu:(3,7)   dark:(5,7) c:(7,3)
  tau:(7,5)   b:(5,1)    W:(2,2)    Z:(2,6)
  H:(6,2)     t:(6,6).                                          (2.3)

2.2 The D_4 action and isotypic decomposition

The D_4 point group acts on Lambda by

  r : (n_1,n_2) -> (-n_2, n_1) mod 8,
  s : (n_1,n_2) -> (n_1, -n_2) mod 8,                           (2.4)

with r^4 = s^2 = (rs)^2 = e.

On the 14-mode multiplet, V_14 = C^14 decomposes as

  V_14 = 3 A_1 + A_2 + 2 B_1 + 2 B_2 + 3 E                      (2.5)

(HCSM-05, HCSM-06). The isotypic projectors are

  P_rho = (dim rho / |D_4|) sum_{g in D_4} chi_rho(g)^* R(g),    (2.6)

where R(g) is the permutation representation of g on C^14 and
chi_rho is the character of the irrep rho. The five projectors satisfy
P_rho P_rho' = delta_{rho rho'} P_rho and sum_rho P_rho = I_14.

2.3 The gauge algebra g_SM on the 14-mode multiplet

By HCSM-06, the gauge algebra is the Lie subalgebra

  g_SM = su(3)_c + su(2)_L + u(1)^5
       subset Comm(D_4, V_14)                                   (2.7)

of the D_4 commutant, of real dimension 8 + 3 + 5 = 16. Concretely:

  * su(3)_c acts on the A_1 multiplicity space M_{A_1} = C^3.
    Generators: T_c^a = U_{A_1} lambda^a U_{A_1}^T for a = 1,...,8,
    where U_{A_1} is an orthonormal basis of the A_1 isotypic sector
    and lambda^a are the Gell-Mann matrices.

  * su(2)_L acts on the B_1 multiplicity space M_{B_1} = C^2.
    Generators: T_L^i = U_B1 sigma^i U_B1^T for i = 1,2,3, where
    U_B1 is an orthonormal basis of the B_1 isotypic sector and
    sigma^i are the Pauli matrices.

  * u(1)^5 is spanned by the five isotypic projectors
    P_{A_1}, P_{A_2}, P_{B_1}, P_{B_2}, P_E.

The ordered list of the 16 gauge generators on C^14 is

  g_SM^(14) = {T_c^1,...,T_c^8, T_L^1, T_L^2, T_L^3,
               P_{A_1}, P_{A_2}, P_{B_1}, P_{B_2}, P_E}.         (2.8)

All 16 generators are Hermitian and D_4-invariant.

2.4 The winding class partition

By HCSM-05 Theorem 7.7 (joint (D,T) class partition) and HCSM-14
Definition 5.1, the 14 modes partition into three winding classes

  A = {nu, e, t},
  B = {u, d, s, mu, c, tau, b},
  C = {dark, W, Z, H}.                                          (2.9)

The class projectors P_A, P_B, P_C are diagonal in the mode basis,
orthogonal, idempotent, and sum to the identity. The processor T of
HCSM-13 satisfies T = I - P_C = P_A + P_B, so the fermion sector is
A + B and the boson sector is C.

3. THE LOOP OPERATOR AND ITS COMMUTANT
================================================================

3.1 Definition of M_loop on the 14-mode multiplet

Definition 3.1 (Loop operator; HCSM-17 Definition 2.1). The loop
operator on the 14-mode multiplet is the diagonal operator

  M_loop = lambda_A P_A + lambda_B P_B + lambda_C P_C,          (3.1)

where P_A, P_B, P_C are the class projectors of Eq. (2.9) and
lambda_A, lambda_B, lambda_C are the pairwise-distinct complex loop
eigenvalues defined in HCSM-17. Their physical magnitudes are

  |lambda_A| = 0.5757, |lambda_B| = 0.1919, |lambda_C| = 0,
  |lambda_A|/|lambda_B| = 3,                                    (3.2)

as given in HCSM-61 section 12.12. For the computation of the loop
commutant we take (lambda_A, lambda_B, lambda_C) = (1, 2, 0) without
loss of generality: any other choice of pairwise-distinct eigenvalues
gives the same commutant.

Remark 3.2 (Winding-generator class values). The winding generator
H_wind of HCSM-14 carries the class values

  omega_A = 2 sqrt(2), omega_B = 6 sqrt(2), omega_C = 0,
  |omega_B|/|omega_A| = 3.                                      (3.3)

These values fix the class partition and hence the projectors
P_A, P_B, P_C. They are not the loop eigenvalues. The ratio
|omega_B|/|omega_A| = 3 is a property of the winding generator, not of
the abstract loop operator; the loop commutant is independent of the
numerical values of lambda_A, lambda_B, lambda_C so long as they are
pairwise distinct.

M_loop is normal (it is diagonal), so M_loop M_loop^dagger =
M_loop^dagger M_loop. It commutes with each class projector:

  [M_loop, P_A] = [M_loop, P_B] = [M_loop, P_C] = 0.            (3.4)

3.2 The loop commutant

Definition 3.3 (Loop commutant). The loop commutant is the
intersection

  g_loop = g_SM cap Comm(M_loop) subset g_SM.                    (3.5)

Theorem 3.4 (Loop commutant dimension). The loop commutant g_loop is
a three-dimensional real, Hermitian, abelian subalgebra of g_SM.

Proof. Form the stacked commutator matrix C in C^{(14)^2 x 16} whose
j-th column is vec([M_loop, g_j]) for g_j in g_SM^(14). The realified
matrix

  C_R = [ Re(C) ; Im(C) ] in R^{2(14)^2 x 16}                    (3.6)

has rank 13 at machine precision, with a singular value gap of
~10^15 between the 13th and 14th singular values. The nullspace
dimension is 16 - 13 = 3.

The three nullspace vectors {v^(1), v^(2), v^(3)} in R^16 span a real
3-dimensional subspace N. Each vector generates a Hermitian operator

  O_i = sum_{j=1}^{16} v_j^(i) g_j in g_SM                      (3.7)

satisfying ||[O_i, M_loop]|| < 10^-15 and ||O_i^dagger - O_i|| = 0.
Since the g_j are Hermitian and the v_j^(i) are real, every O_i is
Hermitian; the span of the O_i is therefore a real 3-dimensional
Hermitian subspace of g_SM.

Cross-commutators [O_i, O_j] vanish at machine precision
(||[O_i, O_j]|| < 10^-15), so the algebra is abelian. Since each O_i
is a real linear combination of the 16 Hermitian generators of g_SM
and the commutator of any two such combinations can be expanded in the
structure constants of g_SM, direct computation gives zero at machine
precision. QED

Remark 3.5. The loop commutant g_loop is a proper 3-dimensional
subalgebra of the 16-dimensional g_SM. The remaining 13 directions of
g_SM are broken by the loop operator: they correspond to generators
that do not commute with M_loop.

4. STRUCTURE OF THE LOOP COMMUTANT
================================================================

4.1 Isotypic projector degeneracy

Theorem 4.1 (Isotypic projector degeneracy). Every element of the
loop commutant has equal coefficients on the isotypic projectors
P_{A_2}, P_{B_2}, P_E.

Proof. Let v in R^16 be any nullspace vector. The components of v
corresponding to the three isotypic projectors P_{A_2}, P_{B_2}, P_E
are the entries v_{A_2}, v_{B_2}, v_E. Direct computation gives

  |v_{A_2} - v_{B_2}| < 10^-15,
  |v_{A_2} - v_E|     < 10^-15,
  |v_{B_2} - v_E|     < 10^-15.                                 (4.1)

The class partition of HCSM-14 is blind to the distinction between the
A_2, B_2, and E isotypic sectors. This is a structural feature of the
loop operator's action on the multiplet. QED

Remark 4.2. The degeneracy of P_{A_2}, P_{B_2}, P_E coefficients
reflects the fact that the loop operator's class partition of HCSM-14
does not distinguish these three sectors. The triple (A_2, B_2, E)
shares the property of being invisible to the winding generator's
class structure.

4.2 No root generators

Theorem 4.3 (No root generators). The loop commutant has zero overlap
with the root generators lambda^1, lambda^2, lambda^4, lambda^5,
lambda^6, lambda^7 of su(3)_c and sigma^1, sigma^2 of su(2)_L. Only
the Cartan generators lambda^3, lambda^8, sigma^3 of g_SM have nonzero
overlap with the loop commutant.

Proof. For each root generator g_j, project the unit coefficient
vector e_j in R^16 onto the nullspace N via the orthogonal projector
P_N:

  ||P_N e_j|| < 10^-15, j in {1,2,4,5,6,7,9,10}.                (4.2)

The vanishing residuals reflect the fact that the loop operator breaks
all off-diagonal (root) directions of su(3)_c and su(2)_L. Only the
diagonal (Cartan) generators lambda^3, lambda^8, sigma^3 have nonzero
overlap with g_loop. QED

Corollary 4.4 (Loop commutant basis). The loop commutant g_loop is
spanned by three Hermitian combinations of {lambda^3, lambda^8,
sigma^3, P_{A_1}, P_{A_2}, P_{B_1}, P_{B_2}, P_E}, in which the
coefficients on P_{A_2}, P_{B_2}, P_E are equal.

5. THE DNLS HARTREE HAMILTONIAN AND ITS COMMUTANT
================================================================

5.1 Definition of H_DNLS on the 14-mode multiplet

Definition 5.1 (DNLS Hartree kernel). For each mode i of the 14-mode
multiplet, let phi_i(x,y) denote the normalized real wavefunction on
the torus (HCSM-04). The Hartree kernel is

  K_ab = sum_{x in Lambda} |phi_a(x)|^2 |phi_b(x)|^2,
  a, b = 1,...,14.                                              (5.1)

By HCSM-04, the diagonal of K equals the inverse participation ratio
IPR_a, taking values (2/128, 3/128, 4/128) on the three D_4 orbits.
The trace is

  Tr(K) = 2*(2/128) + 8*(3/128) + 4*(4/128) = 11/32.           (5.2)

Definition 5.2 (DNLS Hartree Hamiltonian). The DNLS Hartree
Hamiltonian on the 14-mode multiplet is

  H_DNLS = 4 I_14 + U K,                                        (5.3)

with U = B_mass = (d-1) kL^2 / (2 N^2) = 0.5411967342... the
mass-map rate (HCSM-19, HCSM-33).

H_DNLS is a real symmetric 14x14 matrix, hence Hermitian. It commutes
with no particular class projector in general, but as we prove below,
it commutes with the full su(2)_L factor of g_SM.

5.2 The DNLS commutant

Definition 5.3 (DNLS commutant). The DNLS commutant is

  g_DNLS = g_SM cap Comm(H_DNLS).                               (5.4)

Theorem 5.4 (DNLS commutant dimension). The DNLS commutant g_DNLS is
a ten-dimensional real, Hermitian subalgebra of g_SM, isomorphic to
su(2)_L + u(1)^7.

Proof. The realified DNLS commutator matrix has rank 6 and nullity 10
at machine precision, with a singular value gap of ~10^13 between the
6th and 7th singular values.

The nullspace decomposes explicitly as

  g_DNLS = su(2)_L
         + <P_{A_1}, P_{A_2}, P_{B_1}, P_{B_2}, P_E>
         + c,                                                    (5.5)

where c is a two-dimensional abelian subalgebra of su(3)_c. The
dimension count is 3 + 5 + 2 = 10, matching the theorem's statement.
QED

6. EXACT PRESERVATION OF su(2)_L
================================================================

6.1 The doubled-momentum pairing mechanism

Lemma 6.1 (Density equality from HCSM-04). For each paired pair
{m, m'} in v_1 and v_2, the modes satisfy

  |phi_{m'}(x)|^2 = |phi_m(x)|^2 for every x in Lambda.         (6.1)

Proof. For nu = (0,4) and e = (4,0): by HCSM-04 Eq. (7),

  phi_nu(x) = cos(pi x_2)/8,  phi_e(x) = cos(pi x_1)/8,        (6.2)

so |phi_nu(x)|^2 = cos^2(pi x_2)/64 and |phi_e(x)|^2 =
cos^2(pi x_1)/64. Since cos^2(pi x_i) = 1 for every integer x_i, both
equal 1/64 for all x. Hence |phi_nu|^2 = |phi_e|^2 = 1/64 uniformly.

For each of the four O_1 pairs, the modes differ by (4,4) mod 8 or
(4,-4) mod 8. Writing the mode as

  phi_n(x) = cos(pi (n_1 x_1 + n_2 x_2)/4) / sqrt(32),         (6.3)

and using n' = n + (4,4),

  phi_{n'}(x) = cos(pi n.x/4 + pi(x_1+x_2)) / sqrt(32)
              = (-1)^{x_1+x_2} phi_n(x).                        (6.4)

For the pairs (d,b) and (c,mu), the shift is (4,-4), giving
(-1)^{x_1-x_2} instead. In all cases the sign is +-1, so
|phi_{n'}(x)|^2 = |phi_n(x)|^2. QED

Theorem 6.2 (Doubled-momentum pairing). The B_1-compressed Hartree
kernel vanishes identically:

  K_B1 = U_B1^T K U_B1 = 0 in R^{2x2}.                          (6.5)

Proof. See Appendix A, Theorem A.6. QED

Corollary 6.3 (su(2)_L preservation). The three generators sigma^1,
sigma^2, sigma^3 commute with the DNLS Hartree Hamiltonian:

  [sigma^i, K] = 0, [sigma^i, H_DNLS] = 0, i = 1,2,3.           (6.6)

Each of sigma^1, sigma^2, sigma^3 has overlap 1 with the DNLS
commutant at machine precision.

Proof. We first establish a lemma.

Lemma 6.4. [P_B1, K] = 0.

Proof of Lemma 6.4. The Hartree kernel K_ab = sum_x |phi_a(x)|^2
|phi_b(x)|^2 is D_4-invariant: for every g in D_4, [R(g), K] = 0,
since the sum over all sites is D_4-invariant and the mode index
transforms by the permutation representation. The isotypic projector
P_B1 is a polynomial in the R(g)'s (Schur orthogonality), hence
[P_B1, K] = 0. QED

By Lemma 6.4, the commutator decomposes as

  [T_L^i, K] = U_B1 [sigma^i, K_B1] U_B1^T.                     (6.7)

By Theorem 6.2, K_B1 = 0, so [sigma^i, K_B1] = 0 identically. Hence
[T_L^i, K] = 0 for all i. Since [sigma^i, I] = 0 trivially,
[T_L^i, H_DNLS] = 0. QED

Remark 6.5 (DNLS coupling closure). The DNLS coupling closes to

  g = q = (11/32) * (3/2) = 33/64 ~ 0.515625,                   (6.8)

as derived in HCSM-64 section 11.5 from the Hartree trace
Tr(K) = 11/32 and the mass-map coefficient (d-1)/2 = 3/2.

Remark 6.6 (Independent confirmation). The vanishing K_B1 = 0 is
independently confirmed in HCSM-61 section 8.11, where it is cited as
a structural input to the bundle construction over ker D.

7. BREAKING OF su(3)_c
================================================================

7.1 Root generator exclusion

Theorem 7.1 (su(3)_c breaking). The DNLS commutant contains a
two-dimensional abelian subalgebra c of su(3)_c. The root generators
lambda^2, lambda^5, lambda^7 have zero overlap with the DNLS
commutant; the Cartan generators lambda^3, lambda^8 have partial
overlap.

Proof. Project each su(3)_c generator onto the DNLS nullspace
N_DNLS in R^16 via the orthogonal projector P_N_DNLS. Direct
computation gives

  ||P_N_DNLS e_j|| < 10^-10 for j in {2,5,7},                   (7.1)

so lambda^2, lambda^5, lambda^7 are orthogonal to g_DNLS. The
remaining five su(3)_c generators lambda^1, lambda^3, lambda^4,
lambda^6, lambda^8 have partial overlap with N_DNLS; the two-
dimensional abelian subalgebra c is a specific Hermitian combination
of these five generators that commutes with K. Because c has
dimension 2 and is abelian, it is a Cartan-like subalgebra of
su(3)_c. QED

Remark 7.2. The breaking pattern su(3)_c -> su(3)_c^Cartan
(2-dimensional abelian) is the color analog of the electroweak
symmetry breaking pattern, but it occurs at the substrate level (the
mean-field Hartree dynamics) rather than at the electroweak scale. It
reflects the mismatch between the A_1 isotypic sector's mode-momentum
pairing and the B_1 isotypic sector's doubled-momentum pairing.

7.2 Dimension bookkeeping

The DNLS commutant dimension is 3 + 5 + 2 = 10, matching
Theorem 5.4. The ten generators are:

  * sigma^1, sigma^2, sigma^3: full su(2)_L (3 generators,
    non-abelian).
  * P_{A_1}, P_{A_2}, P_{B_1}, P_{B_2}, P_E: full u(1)^5
    (5 generators, abelian).
  * Two generators of the abelian subalgebra c in su(3)_c
    (2 generators, abelian).

Corollary 7.3 (Contrast with the loop commutant). The DNLS commutant
(10-dimensional, su(2)_L + u(1)^7) is strictly larger than the loop
commutant (3-dimensional, abelian). The two commutants share the
u(1)^5 isotypic projector sector but differ on the su(3)_c and su(2)_L
content: the loop operator preserves only the Cartan generators
lambda^3, lambda^8, sigma^3 (three abelian directions), while the DNLS
Hamiltonian preserves the full su(2)_L factor and a two-dimensional
abelian subalgebra of su(3)_c.

8.3 What is not claimed

We do not claim to derive the gauge algebra g_SM from the substrate;
that is the subject of HCSM-06. We do not claim to derive the loop
eigenvalues lambda_A, lambda_B from the substrate; those are the
subject of HCSM-14 and HCSM-17. We do not claim to derive the
mass-map rate U from the substrate; that is the subject of HCSM-19
and HCSM-33. We do not claim to derive the physical interpretation of
the surviving 3-dimensional loop commutant or the 2-dimensional
abelian su(3)_c remnant; these are structural features of the algebra
whose physical meaning requires the framework's gauge-quantum-number
dictionary (HCSM-06, HCSM-07). We do not extend any of the analysis to
the 56-dimensional Hodge sector or the generation-extended space;
these extensions are the subject of HCSM-16.

10. CONCLUSION
================================================================

This paper has determined the gauge symmetry structure of the loop
operator and the DNLS Hartree Hamiltonian on the 14-mode multiplet of
the Hodge Complex Standard Model.

The loop operator M_loop = lambda_A P_A + lambda_B P_B + lambda_C P_C
preserves a three-dimensional real, Hermitian, abelian subalgebra of
g_SM = su(3)_c + su(2)_L + u(1)^5. The surviving generators are three
Hermitian combinations of the Cartan generators lambda^3, lambda^8,
sigma^3 and the isotypic projectors, with equal coefficients on
P_{A_2}, P_{B_2}, P_E. The root generators lambda^{1,2,4,5,6,7} of
su(3)_c and sigma^{1,2} of su(2)_L are broken by the loop operator.

The DNLS Hartree Hamiltonian H_DNLS = 4 I + U K preserves a larger
ten-dimensional subalgebra of g_SM, isomorphic to su(2)_L + u(1)^7.
The full su(2)_L factor is preserved exactly, a fact proved by a
doubled-momentum pairing mechanism: the two B_1 basis vectors pair
modes with the same doubled momentum (2n_1, 2n_2) mod 8 and opposite
signs, forcing the B_1-compressed kernel K_B1 = U_B1^T K U_B1 to
vanish identically. The density equality required for this pairing is
derived directly from the HCSM-04 wavefunctions (Lemma 6.1). The
su(3)_c factor is broken to a two-dimensional abelian subalgebra, with
the root generators lambda^2, lambda^5, lambda^7 orthogonal to the
DNLS commutant.

The contrast between the two commutants (3-dimensional for the loop,
10-dimensional for the DNLS Hamiltonian) reflects the different
symmetry structures of the two operators. The loop operator is a
class-partition projector that selects only the abelian content of the
multiplet; the DNLS Hamiltonian is a mode-density kernel that
preserves the full weak-isospin structure through a doubled-momentum
degeneracy specific to the B_1 isotypic sector.

The exact su(2)_L preservation is the strongest structural statement in
the paper. It establishes that, at the mean-field level, the
framework's weak-interaction algebra is a robust symmetry of the
substrate dynamics. The su(3)_c breaking pattern is equally
structural: it identifies precisely which color directions survive the
mean-field reduction, and it exhibits the difference between the A_1
and B_1 isotypic sectors' pairing mechanisms.

APPENDIX A. EXPLICIT B_1 BASIS VECTORS AND THE
            DOUBLED-MOMENTUM PAIRING THEOREM

A.1 The B_1 isotypic projector

The B_1 isotypic projector on the 14-mode multiplet is

  P_B1 = (1/8) [ R(e) + R(r^2) - R(r) - R(r^3)
                 + R(s) + R(r^2 s) - R(rs) - R(r^3 s) ],       (A.1)

where the D_4 action on mode coordinates is given by Eq. (2.4). The
B_1 character is chi_B1 = (1,1,-1,1,-1) on the conjugacy classes
(e, r^2, r, s, rs).

A.2 Explicit construction of the basis vectors

Proposition A.1 (First B_1 basis vector). Applying P_B1 to the
neutrino mode nu = (0,4) gives, after normalization,

  v_1^(B_1) = (|nu> - |e>)/sqrt(2)
            = (|(0,4)> - |(4,0)>)/sqrt(2).                     (A.2)

Proof. Direct computation:

  P_B1 |nu> = (1/8) [ 4|nu> - 4|e> ]
            = (|nu> - |e>)/2.                                  (A.3)

Normalizing gives (A.2). QED

Proposition A.2 (Second B_1 basis vector). Applying P_B1 to the
up-quark mode u = (1,3) gives, after normalization,

  v_2^(B_1) = (sqrt(2)/4) ( |u> + |tau> - |b> - |mu>
                          + |d> + |c> - |s> - |dark> ).          (A.4)

In coordinate form:

  v_2^(B_1) = (sqrt(2)/4) ( |(1,3)> + |(7,5)> - |(5,1)> - |(3,7)>
                          + |(1,5)> + |(7,3)> - |(3,1)>
                          - |(5,7)> ).                           (A.5)

Proof. Direct computation of P_B1 |u> gives

  P_B1 |u> = (1/8) [ |u> + |tau> - |b> - |mu>
                     + |d> + |c> - |s> - |dark> ].               (A.6)

The eight coefficients are +-1/8 in the standard mode order.
Normalizing: ||P_B1 |u>||^2 = 8 * (1/8)^2 = 1/8, so the normalized
vector is sqrt(8) * P_B1 |u> = (sqrt(2)/4)(...). QED

Proposition A.3 (Orthonormality). The two vectors v_1^(B_1) and
v_2^(B_1) are orthonormal:

  v_1 . v_1 = 1, v_2 . v_2 = 1, v_1 . v_2 = 0.                  (A.7)

Proof. For v_1: ||v_1||^2 = (1/2)(1 + 1) = 1. For v_2:
||v_2||^2 = (2/16) * 8 = 1. For the inner product: the supports of
v_1 and v_2 are disjoint (v_1 is supported on {nu, e} subset O_0,
while v_2 is supported on {u, d, s, mu, dark, c, tau, b} subset O_1),
so the inner product vanishes. QED

A.3 The matrix U_B1

In the standard mode order (nu, e, u, d, s, mu, dark, c, tau, b, W,
Z, H, t):

  U_B1 = [  1/sqrt(2)    0
           -1/sqrt(2)    0
            0            sqrt(2)/4
            0            sqrt(2)/4
            0           -sqrt(2)/4
            0           -sqrt(2)/4
            0           -sqrt(2)/4
            0            sqrt(2)/4
            0            sqrt(2)/4
            0            sqrt(2)/4
            0            0
            0            0
            0            0
            0            0    ].                                 (A.8)

This matrix satisfies U_B1^T U_B1 = I_2.

A.4 Verification of the B_1 transformation properties

Direct computation verifies that both basis vectors transform correctly
under the B_1 character chi_B1 = (1,1,-1,1,-1):

  g        R(g) v_1     R(g) v_2
  ---      --------     --------
  e        + v_1        + v_2
  r^2      + v_1        + v_2
  r        - v_1        - v_2
  r^3      - v_1        - v_2
  s        + v_1        + v_2
  r^2 s    + v_1        + v_2
  rs       - v_1        - v_2
  r^3 s    - v_1        - v_2

Both vectors transform as B_1. This confirms that U_B1 is a valid
isotypic basis.

A.5 The doubled-momentum pairing structure

Proposition A.4 (Pairing structure of v_1). The vector v_1^(B_1)
pairs the two modes nu = (0,4) and e = (4,0), both with doubled
momentum

  (2*0, 2*4) = (2*4, 2*0) = (0,0) mod 8,                        (A.9)

and with opposite signs +1/sqrt(2) and -1/sqrt(2).

Proposition A.5 (Pairing structure of v_2). The vector v_2^(B_1)
contains four pairs of modes, each pair with the same doubled momentum
and opposite signs:

  Pair | Mode 1        | 2n          | Sign | Mode 2         | 2n
  -----|---------------|-------------|------|----------------|-------------
   1   | u = (1,3)     | (2,6)       |  +   | dark = (5,7)   | (2,6)
   2   | tau = (7,5)   | (6,2)       |  +   | s = (3,1)      | (6,2)
   3   | d = (1,5)     | (2,2)       |  +   | b = (5,1)      | (2,2)
   4   | c = (7,3)     | (6,6)       |  +   | mu = (3,7)     | (6,6)

Proof. Direct computation from (A.5). For pair 1: u = (1,3) has
doubled momentum (2,6); dark = (5,7) has doubled momentum
(10,14) = (2,6) mod 8. The coefficients in v_2 are +sqrt(2)/4 and
-sqrt(2)/4 respectively. The other three pairs are verified
analogously. QED

A.6 Proof that K_B1 = 0

Theorem A.6 (B_1-compressed kernel vanishes). The B_1-compressed
Hartree kernel vanishes identically:

  K_B1 = U_B1^T K U_B1 = 0 in R^{2x2}.                          (A.10)

Proof. The compression K_B1 has entries

  (K_B1)_ij = (v_i^(B_1))^T K v_j^(B_1) = sum_x rho_i(x) rho_j(x),  (A.11)

where the density profiles are

  rho_i(x) = sum_a v_i^(a) |phi_a(x)|^2, i = 1,2.               (A.12)

For i = 1: using Proposition A.4,

  rho_1(x) = (1/sqrt(2)) ( |phi_nu(x)|^2 - |phi_e(x)|^2 ).      (A.13)

By Lemma 6.1, |phi_nu(x)|^2 = |phi_e(x)|^2 = 1/64 for all x in
Lambda. Therefore rho_1(x) = 0 for all x in Lambda.

For i = 2: using Proposition A.5,

  rho_2(x) = (sqrt(2)/4) [ |phi_u(x)|^2 - |phi_dark(x)|^2
                          + |phi_tau(x)|^2 - |phi_s(x)|^2
                          + |phi_d(x)|^2 - |phi_b(x)|^2
                          + |phi_c(x)|^2 - |phi_mu(x)|^2 ].      (A.14)

By Lemma 6.1, each of the four pairs satisfies
|phi_{m'}(x)|^2 = |phi_m(x)|^2. Therefore each bracket vanishes
identically, and rho_2(x) = 0 for all x in Lambda.

Since rho_1 = rho_2 = 0 identically, every entry of K_B1 vanishes:

  K_B1 = [ 0  0
           0  0 ].                                              (A.15)
QED

A.7 Corollary: exact su(2)_L preservation

Corollary A.7 (su(2)_L preservation on the 14-mode multiplet). The
three generators of su(2)_L commute with the Hartree kernel and the
DNLS Hamiltonian:

  [T_L^i, K] = 0, [T_L^i, H_DNLS] = 0, i = 1,2,3.               (A.16)

Proof. Since T_L^i = U_B1 sigma^i U_B1^T has support only on the
B_1 isotypic sector, and [P_B1, K] = 0 by Lemma 6.4, we have

  [T_L^i, K] = U_B1 [sigma^i, K_B1] U_B1^T.                     (A.17)

By Theorem A.6, K_B1 = 0, so [sigma^i, K_B1] = 0 identically. Hence
[T_L^i, K] = 0 for all i. Since [sigma^i, I] = 0 trivially,
[T_L^i, H_DNLS] = 0. QED
