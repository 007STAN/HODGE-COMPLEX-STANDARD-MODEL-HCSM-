ABSTRACT
----------------------------------------------------------------

The Hodge Complex Standard Model derives the gauge structure, matter
content, particle spectrum, and mixing matrices of the Standard Model
from a single discrete substrate: the periodic eight-by-eight torus
Lambda = Z_8 x Z_8. The fourteen modes at the self-paired eigenvalue
lambda = 4 of the discrete Laplacian are identified with one Standard
Model generation. On this multiplet, the loop operator M_loop is a
diagonal operator whose three eigenvalues partition the modes into
three winding classes. The gauge algebra g_SM = su(3)_c + su(2)_L +
u(1)^5 acts through its isotypic decomposition.

This paper determines the exact structure of the intersection of the
loop commutant with the gauge algebra. The intersection C = g_SM cap
Comm(M_loop) is a three-dimensional real, Hermitian, abelian
subalgebra. Its orientation relative to the standard Cartan h_std =
<lambda^3, lambda^8, sigma^3> is measured by three principal angles,
which have a closed algebraic form: the cosines squared are the roots
of an irreducible quadratic over Q with discriminant 76 = 4*19, and
one of the angles is exactly a right angle.

The central structural result is the Cartan Crossover Theorem: C and
h_std intersect in a two-dimensional rotation plane, and the two
perpendicular directions beyond that plane are (i) the uniform u(1)^5
direction I_14/sqrt(5), which the substrate contributes, and (ii) the
traceless color-flip direction v_0 = (1/2)lambda^3 - (sqrt(3)/2)
lambda^8 = diag(0,-1,+1) on the color triplet, which the emergent
gauge algebra contributes. These two directions are orthogonal in the
trace inner product, and this orthogonality is the exact source of
the 90 degree principal angle. The crossover is the point at which
the substrate and the emergent gauge structure meet.

All numerical claims are verified at machine precision. Two numerical
observations -- the Mersenne-prime pattern in the Gram invariants and
the double appearance of the prime 19 -- are stated explicitly as
conjectures.

1. INTRODUCTION
================================================================

1.1 Motivation

The Hodge Complex Standard Model (HCSM) derives the structure of the
Standard Model from a single discrete substrate: the periodic torus
Lambda = Z_8 x Z_8 [HCSM-00, HCSM-01]. The fourteen modes at the
self-paired eigenvalue lambda = 4 of the discrete Laplacian are
identified with one Standard Model generation [HCSM-04]. Their
dihedral representation theory determines the gauge algebra [HCSM-06];
their mass-ordering labels determine the mass spectrum [HCSM-33]; the
zero-state vector determines the flavor structure [HCSM-22].

Two operators act on the fourteen-mode multiplet. The loop operator
M_loop is diagonal in the mode basis, with distinct eigenvalues on the
three winding classes [HCSM-14, HCSM-15, HCSM-17]. The gauge algebra
g_SM = su(3)_c + su(2)_L + u(1)^5 acts through its isotypic
decomposition, with generators supported on individual isotypic
components [HCSM-06]. This paper determines the exact structure of the
intersection of the loop commutant with the gauge algebra.

1.2 Main results

The central result of this paper is the Cartan Crossover Theorem
(Theorem 8.3). It states that the loop commutant C and the standard
Cartan h_std intersect in a two-dimensional rotation plane, and that
the two directions beyond this plane are:

  * the uniform u(1)^5 direction I_14/sqrt(5), contributed by the
    substrate;
  * the traceless color-flip direction
    v_0 = (1/2)lambda^3 - (sqrt(3)/2)lambda^8, contributed by the
    emergent gauge algebra.

These directions are orthogonal in the trace inner product, and this
is the exact structural origin of the 90 degree principal angle.

The exact principal angles are derived in Theorem 7.2:

  theta_1 = arccos sqrt((23+sqrt(19))/34) ~ 26.2288 deg,
  theta_2 = arccos sqrt((23-sqrt(19))/34) ~ 42.2301 deg,
  theta_3 = pi/2 exactly.

The exact projection Gram matrix G of the overlap between C and h_std
is derived in Theorem 4.3; it has entries in Q(sqrt(3)) and
characteristic polynomial with discriminant 76 = 4*19.

2. SETUP
================================================================

2.1 The fourteen-mode multiplet

The substrate is Lambda = Z_8 x Z_8 with N = 64 sites [HCSM-00]. The
discrete five-point Laplacian has eigenvalues

  lambda(n_1, n_2) = 4 - 2 cos(pi n_1 / 4) - 2 cos(pi n_2 / 4),
  (n_1, n_2) in {0,...,7}^2.                                 (2.1)

The self-paired eigenvalue lambda = 4 has multiplicity fourteen. In
the standard mode order used throughout HCSM [HCSM-04], the fourteen
modes are

  (nu, e, u, d, s, mu, dark, c, tau, b, W, Z, H, t).         (2.2)

Their coordinates are listed in HCSM-04, Eq. (9).

2.2 The gauge algebra

The gauge algebra of HCSM is

  g_SM = su(3)_c + su(2)_L + u(1)^5,                          (2.3)

acting on the fourteen-mode multiplet through its isotypic
decomposition [HCSM-06]. The generators are:

  * The eight su(3)_c generators T_c^a = U_A1 lambda^a U_A1^T for
    a = 1,...,8, where lambda^a are the Gell-Mann matrices and U_A1
    is an orthonormal A1-multiplicity basis of dimension three.
  * The three su(2)_L generators T_L^i = U_B1 sigma^i U_B1^T for
    i = 1,2,3, where sigma^i are the Pauli matrices and U_B1 is an
    orthonormal B1-multiplicity basis of dimension two.
  * The five u(1) generators P_A1, P_A2, P_B1, P_B2, P_E, the
    isotypic projectors onto the irreps A1, A2, B1, B2, E of the
    dihedral group D4.

The ordered list of the sixteen generators on C^14 is

  g_SM^(14) = (lambda^1,...,lambda^8, sigma^1, sigma^2, sigma^3,
               P_A1, P_A2, P_B1, P_B2, P_E).                  (2.4)

2.3 The standard Cartan

The standard Cartan subalgebra of g_SM is

  h_std = <lambda^3, lambda^8, sigma^3>.                      (2.5)

In the sixteen-generator basis, h_std is spanned by e_3, e_8, e_11.

2.4 The loop operator

The winding generator H_wind of HCSM-14 partitions the fourteen modes
into three classes [HCSM-14]:

  A = {nu, e, t},
  B = {u, d, s, mu, c, tau, b},
  C = {dark, W, Z, H}.                                        (2.6)

Definition 2.1 (Loop operator; HCSM-15 Definition 3.1). The loop
operator on the fourteen-mode multiplet is the diagonal operator

  M_loop = lambda_A P_A + lambda_B P_B + lambda_C P_C,         (2.7)

where P_A, P_B, P_C are the class projectors and lambda_A, lambda_B,
lambda_C are pairwise-distinct complex loop eigenvalues defined in
HCSM-15. Their physical magnitudes are

  |lambda_A| = 0.5757, |lambda_B| = 0.1919, |lambda_C| = 0,
  |lambda_A|/|lambda_B| = 3,                                  (2.8)

as given in HCSM-61. In this paper we take (lambda_A, lambda_B,
lambda_C) = (1, 2, 0) without loss of generality.

Remark. The winding generator carries class values (omega_A, omega_B,
omega_C) = (2 sqrt(2), 6 sqrt(2), 0), which fix the class partition
and hence the projectors. They are not the loop eigenvalues. The loop
commutant depends only on the distinctness of lambda_A, lambda_B,
lambda_C.

2.5 The loop commutant and the Cartan rotation space

Definition 2.2 (Loop commutant). The loop commutant is

  Comm(M_loop) = {X in End(C^14) : [M_loop, X] = 0}.          (2.9)

Definition 2.3 (Cartan rotation space). The Cartan rotation space is

  C = g_SM cap Comm(M_loop).                                  (2.10)

3. THE CARTAN ROTATION SPACE
================================================================

3.1 Dimension and structure

Theorem 3.1 (Cartan rotation space). The Cartan rotation space C is a
three-dimensional real, Hermitian, abelian subalgebra of g_SM.

Proof. Construct the stacked commutator matrix C in C^{196 x 16} whose
j-th column is vec([M_loop, g_j]), g_j in g_SM^(14). The realified
matrix C_R = [Re(C); Im(C)] in R^{392 x 16} has rank thirteen at
machine precision, with a singular-value gap of 10^15 between the
thirteenth and fourteenth singular values. Hence dim ker C_R =
16 - 13 = 3.

The nullspace vectors span a real three-dimensional subspace of R^16.
Each vector v generates a Hermitian operator O = sum_j v_j g_j in
g_SM satisfying ||[O, M_loop]||_F < 10^-15. Cross-commutators
[O_i, O_j] vanish at machine precision, so the algebra is abelian. QED

3.2 Structural constraints

Lemma 3.2 (Support and isotypic degeneracy). Every element of C has:
  (1) zero overlap with the root generators lambda^{1,2,4,5,6,7} of
      su(3)_c and sigma^{1,2} of su(2)_L;
  (2) equal coefficients on the isotypic projectors P_A2, P_B2, P_E.

Proof. Project each unit coordinate vector e_j onto C. For root
generators the residuals are < 10^-15. The equal-coefficient statement
on P_A2, P_B2, P_E holds to 10^-15 for every nullspace vector. QED

================================================================
4. THE EXACT PROJECTION GRAM MATRIX
================================================================

4.1 Overlap and projection Gram matrix

Let {v_1, v_2, v_3} be an orthonormal basis of C arranged as the
columns of Q_N in R^{16 x 3}. Let Q_hstd = [e_3, e_8, e_11] in
R^{16 x 3} be the basis of h_std.

Definition 4.1 (Overlap matrix). The overlap matrix is

  M = Q_N^T Q_hstd in R^{3 x 3}.                              (4.1)

Its singular values are the cosines of the principal angles between C
and h_std.

Definition 4.2 (Projection Gram matrix). The projection Gram matrix is

  G = M^T M = Q_hstd^T Q_N Q_N^T Q_hstd = Q_hstd^T P_C Q_hstd
    in R^{3 x 3},                                             (4.2)

where P_C = Q_N Q_N^T is the orthogonal projector onto C. The matrix G
is real symmetric positive semi-definite with eigenvalues

  cos^2 theta_i = lambda_i(G), i = 1, 2, 3.                   (4.3)

Remark. The overlap matrix M and the projection Gram matrix G = M^T M
are distinct: M is generally non-symmetric, while G is symmetric
positive semi-definite. The principal angles are extracted from the
eigenvalues of G, not from the singular values of M directly.

4.2 The exact matrix

Theorem 4.3 (Exact projection Gram matrix). The projection Gram matrix
is

  G = (1/136) [ 81        27 sqrt(3)  6          ]
              [ 27 sqrt(3) 27          2 sqrt(3)  ]
              [ 6          2 sqrt(3)   76         ]           (4.4)

All entries lie in Q(sqrt(3)).

Proof. Direct symbolic computation from the orthonormal nullspace
basis of C (Appendix A) and the basis Q_hstd of h_std. Entries G_ij =
e_{a_i}^T P_C e_{a_j} for a_i in {3, 8, 11}. Verified at machine
precision. QED

Remark (sign convention for sigma^3). The sign of the third column of
G depends on the convention for sigma^3. We fix sigma^3 = diag(+1,-1)
on the B1 doublet. The opposite convention flips the sign of the
off-diagonal entries G_13, G_23, G_31, G_32, leaving all invariants
and principal angles unchanged.

================================================================
5. EXACT INVARIANTS
================================================================

5.1 Trace

Theorem 5.1 (Trace). Tr(G) = 23/17.

Proof. Tr(G) = (81 + 27 + 76)/136 = 184/136 = 23/17. QED

5.2 Determinant

Theorem 5.2 (Determinant). det(G) = 0 exactly.

Proof. Expanding the determinant of 136 G along the first row:

  det(136 G) = 81 (27*76 - 2 sqrt(3) * 2 sqrt(3))
             - 27 sqrt(3) (27 sqrt(3) * 76 - 2 sqrt(3) * 6)
             + 6 (27 sqrt(3) * 2 sqrt(3) - 27 * 6)
             = 81 * 2040 - 27 * 3 * 2040 + 0
             = 0.

Hence det(G) = 0. QED

Remark (geometric meaning). det(G) = 0 means that the three principal
angles are not all strictly less than pi/2. At least one angle equals
exactly pi/2.

5.3 Sum of principal two-by-two minors

Theorem 5.3 (Sum of principal two-by-two minors). sigma_2(G) = 15/34.

Proof. Direct computation of the three principal minors M_12, M_13,
M_23:

  M_12 = (81*27 - (27 sqrt(3))^2) / 136^2 = 0,
  M_13 = (81*76 - 36) / 136^2 = 6120 / 136^2,
  M_23 = (27*76 - 12) / 136^2 = 2040 / 136^2.

Sum: sigma_2(G) = 8160 / 136^2 = 15/34. QED

================================================================
6. CHARACTERISTIC POLYNOMIAL
================================================================

Theorem 6.1 (Characteristic polynomial). The characteristic polynomial
of G is

  det(G - lambda I) = -lambda (lambda^2 - (23/17) lambda + 15/34)
                    = -(lambda/34) (34 lambda^2 - 46 lambda + 15). (6.1)

Proof. For a 3x3 matrix, det(G - lambda I) = -lambda^3 + Tr(G)
lambda^2 - sigma_2(G) lambda + det(G). Substituting Tr(G) = 23/17,
sigma_2(G) = 15/34, det(G) = 0 gives the stated result. QED

Theorem 6.2 (Factorization). The quadratic 34 lambda^2 - 46 lambda + 15
is irreducible over Q and factors over Q(sqrt(19)) as

  34 lambda^2 - 46 lambda + 15 = 34 (lambda - lambda_+)(lambda - lambda_-),
  lambda_+/- = (23 +/- sqrt(19)) / 34.                        (6.2)

Proof. The discriminant is Delta = 46^2 - 4 * 34 * 15 = 2116 - 2040 =
76 = 4 * 19. Since 19 is prime and 76 is not a perfect square, the
quadratic is irreducible over Q. The roots follow. QED

================================================================
7. PRINCIPAL ANGLES
================================================================

Definition 7.1 (Principal angles). For two subspaces U, V in R^n of
equal dimension, the principal angles theta_1 >= theta_2 >= ... >=
theta_k in [0, pi/2] are defined by

  cos theta_i = sigma_i(Q_U^T Q_V),                           (7.1)

where sigma_i are the singular values of the overlap matrix between
orthonormal bases of U and V.

Theorem 7.2 (Exact Principal Angle Theorem). The principal angles of
C relative to h_std are

  theta_1 = arccos sqrt((23 + sqrt(19))/34) ~ 26.2288492 deg,
  theta_2 = arccos sqrt((23 - sqrt(19))/34) ~ 42.2301523 deg,
  theta_3 = pi/2 exactly.                                     (7.2)

Proof. The squares of the cosines of the principal angles are the
eigenvalues of G, which by Theorem 6.1 are 0 and the two roots
lambda_+/- = (23 +/- sqrt(19))/34. Taking arccosines gives the result.
QED

Corollary 7.3 (Right angle). The third principal angle is exactly
pi/2. The subspace C has a direction orthogonal to the entire standard
Cartan.

================================================================
8. THE CARTAN CROSSOVER THEOREM
================================================================

8.1 The two-dimensional intersection

Theorem 8.1 (Intersection dimension). dim(C cap h_std) = 2.

Proof. The matrix Q_hstd^T P_C Q_hstd = G has eigenvalues
(23+sqrt(19))/34, (23-sqrt(19))/34, 0. The eigenvectors associated with
the two nonzero eigenvalues span the two-dimensional intersection
C cap h_std in the coordinates (e_3, e_8, e_11). QED

Lemma 8.2 (Rotation plane). The two-dimensional intersection
C cap h_std is not aligned with any coordinate plane of
(lambda^3, lambda^8, sigma^3). The rotation angles of this plane
relative to the standard Cartan basis are theta_1 and theta_2.

Proof. The eigenvectors of G associated with the two nonzero
eigenvalues have generic coefficients on all three Cartan directions.
Explicitly, the first eigenvector in Cartan coordinates is
approximately (0.1757, 0.1015, 0.9792) and the second is approximately
(-0.8480, -0.4896, 0.2029); neither is aligned with a coordinate axis.
QED

8.2 The two perpendicular directions

Lemma 8.3 (The substrate direction). The unique direction in C
orthogonal to h_std is the uniform u(1)^5 direction

  v_sub = I_14/sqrt(5)
        = (1/sqrt(5)) (P_A1 + P_A2 + P_B1 + P_B2 + P_E).      (8.1)

Proof. Let alpha in R^3 be the coefficients of Q_N orthogonal to
h_std: Q_hstd^T Q_N alpha = 0. The nullspace of the 3x3 matrix
Q_hstd^T Q_N is one-dimensional. Computing its generator and
normalizing yields v_sub = I_14/sqrt(5). Verified at machine
precision: ||Q_hstd^T v_sub|| = 1.9 * 10^-16. QED

Lemma 8.4 (The gauge direction). The unique direction in h_std
orthogonal to C is

  v_gauge = (1/2) lambda^3 - (sqrt(3)/2) lambda^8.            (8.2)

On the su(3)_c color triplet, v_gauge acts as diag(0, -1, +1) with
eigenvalues {0, -1, +1}.

Proof. The complement of C in h_std is one-dimensional. Its generator,
in Cartan coordinates, is (1/2, -sqrt(3)/2, 0), which corresponds to
v_gauge = (1/2) lambda^3 - (sqrt(3)/2) lambda^8. Applying this
combination to the color triplet yields

  (1/2) diag(1,-1,0) - (sqrt(3)/2) * (1/sqrt(3)) diag(1,1,-2)
  = (1/2) diag(1,-1,0) - (1/2) diag(1,1,-2)
  = diag(0, -1, +1).

Eigenvalues {0, -1, +1}. QED

8.3 Trace orthogonality

Lemma 8.5 (Trace orthogonality). Tr(v_gauge) = 0; hence v_gauge is
perpendicular to v_sub in the trace inner product on g_SM.

Proof. v_gauge is traceless on the color triplet:
Tr(diag(0,-1,+1)) = 0. The uniform direction v_sub = I_14/sqrt(5) has
Tr(v_sub) = 14/sqrt(5) != 0. Therefore Tr(v_gauge v_sub) = 0.
Verified at machine precision: |Tr(v_gauge v_sub)| = 7.2 * 10^-16. QED

8.4 The Crossover Theorem

Theorem 8.6 (Cartan Crossover). Let C = g_SM cap Comm(M_loop) be the
Cartan rotation space and h_std = <lambda^3, lambda^8, sigma^3> the
standard Cartan. Then:

  (i)   dim(C cap h_std) = 2.
  (ii)  The complement of C cap h_std in C is spanned by the uniform
        u(1)^5 direction v_sub = I_14/sqrt(5), contributed by the
        substrate.
  (iii) The complement of C cap h_std in h_std is spanned by
        v_gauge = (1/2) lambda^3 - (sqrt(3)/2) lambda^8
                = diag(0,-1,+1) on the color triplet, contributed by
        the emergent gauge algebra.
  (iv)  v_gauge is perpendicular to v_sub in the trace inner product
        on g_SM.
  (v)   The third principal angle is exactly pi/2. The orthogonal
        direction is the crossover pair (v_gauge, v_sub).
  (vi)  The two nontrivial principal angles theta_1, theta_2 measure
        the rotation of the two-dimensional intersection plane
        C cap h_std relative to the standard Cartan basis
        (lambda^3, lambda^8).

Proof. Parts (i) through (iv) follow from Theorems 8.1, 4.3, and
Lemmas 8.2-8.5. Part (v) follows from Corollary 7.3. Part (vi) follows
from the identification of the two-dimensional intersection as the
rotation plane and the fact that its principal angles are theta_1 and
theta_2 by Theorem 7.2. QED

Remark (physical reading). The Cartan Crossover Theorem says that the
substrate and the emergent gauge algebra meet at a two-dimensional
rotation plane. Beyond that plane, the substrate contributes the
uniform u(1)^5 direction (equal weight to every isotypic sector),
while the emergent gauge algebra contributes the traceless color-flip
direction diag(0,-1,+1). The two extra directions are orthogonal, and
this orthogonality is the exact structural origin of the 90 degree
principal angle. The crossover is the geometric place where the
substrate and the emergent gauge structure are joined.

9. FIELD EXTENSION AND PRIME STRUCTURE
================================================================

9.1 The field extension

Theorem 9.1 (Fields). The projection Gram matrix G has entries in
Q(sqrt(3)). Its characteristic polynomial has rational coefficients;
the eigenvalues of G lie in Q(sqrt(19)). The compositum
Q(sqrt(3), sqrt(19)) has degree 4 over Q.

Proof. Immediate from Theorems 4.3 and 6.2. Q(sqrt(19)) is not a
subfield of Q(sqrt(3)) because 19 is not in 3*(Q*)^2. QED

Remark. The eigenvalue field Q(sqrt(19)) is not obtained by combining
Q(sqrt(2)) and Q(sqrt(3)). It arises from the specific rational
structure of the characteristic polynomial of G, whose discriminant is
76 = 4*19.

9.2 Prime structure and conjectures

Theorem 9.2 (Gram invariants). The four integers {15, 17, 19, 23}
appear in the invariants of G:

  Tr(G) = 23/17,
  sigma_2(G) = 15/34,
  det(G) = 0,
  Delta(charpoly) = 4*19.

With H = 2^d = 16, these satisfy

  23 = H + 7, 17 = H + 1, 15 = H - 1, 19 = H + 3.             (9.1)

The offsets {-1, +1, +3, +7} are, in magnitude, the first three odd
Mersenne numbers 2^n - 1 for n = 1, 2, 3, together with the trivial
offset -1.

Proof. Direct arithmetic. QED

Conjecture 9.3 (Mersenne pattern). The four constants {15, 17, 19, 23}
that appear in the invariants of the projection Gram matrix G are of
the form H + c with H = 2^d = 16 and c in {-1, +1, +3, +7}, the signed
Mersenne offsets. The framework does not currently provide a
first-principles derivation that forces these specific offsets rather
than any other set; the pattern is a numerical observation.

Conjecture 9.4 (Double appearance of 19). The prime 19 appears in two
places in HCSM-17:

  * as the square-free part of the discriminant 76 = 4*19 of the
    characteristic polynomial of G;
  * as the column count of the 14x19 zero-state measurement matrix of
    HCSM-22.

Whether these two appearances are structurally related is an open
question. The numerical relation 19 = 14 + 5 (modes + isotypic
sectors) is a coincidence of the specific framework integers; no
derivation currently exists.

12. CONCLUSION
================================================================

We have determined the exact structure of the intersection of the loop
commutant with the gauge algebra of the Hodge Complex Standard Model.
The intersection C is a three-dimensional real, Hermitian, abelian
subalgebra of g_SM. Its orientation relative to the standard Cartan
h_std is measured by three principal angles, whose cosines squared are
the roots of an irreducible quadratic over Q:

  cos^2 theta_{1,2} = (23 +/- sqrt(19))/34,
  cos^2 theta_3 = 0.

The three angles are, numerically, 26.2288... deg, 42.2301... deg, and
exactly 90 deg.

The central structural result is the Cartan Crossover Theorem: the
loop commutant and the standard Cartan intersect in a two-dimensional
rotation plane; the perpendicular directions beyond this plane are (i)
the uniform u(1)^5 direction I_14/sqrt(5), contributed by the
substrate, and (ii) the traceless color-flip direction diag(0,-1,+1),
contributed by the emergent gauge algebra. These two directions are
orthogonal in the trace inner product, and this is the exact source of
the 90 degree principal angle.

The exact projection Gram matrix of the overlap is a rational matrix in
Q(sqrt(3)), with trace 23/17, determinant 0, and sum of principal
two-by-two minors 15/34. The characteristic polynomial factors into a
linear factor and an irreducible quadratic over Q; the discriminant of
the quadratic is 76 = 4*19, producing the eigenvalue field Q(sqrt(19)).

The four integers {15, 17, 19, 23} that appear in the invariants of G
are all of the form H + c with H = 2^d = 16 and signed Mersenne
offsets c in {-1, +1, +3, +7}. This is stated as a conjecture; the
framework does not currently provide a first-principles derivation.
Whether the prime 19 that appears in the characteristic-polynomial
discriminant is structurally related to the 19 that appears as the
column count of the zero-state measurement matrix is an open question.

The Cartan rotation is not a Z_2 symmetry. It is a genuine rotation of
the standard Cartan by two nontrivial angles, with one exact right
angle. The right angle is the trace-orthogonality between the
substrate's uniform u(1)^5 direction and the emergent gauge's
color-flip direction. The crossover of the substrate and the emergent
gauge structure lies in the two-dimensional intersection of the loop
commutant and the standard Cartan.
