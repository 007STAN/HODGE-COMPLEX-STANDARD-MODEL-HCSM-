ABSTRACT
================================================================================

The Hodge Complex Standard Model derives the gauge structure, matter content,
particle spectrum, and mixing matrices of the Standard Model from a single
discrete substrate: the periodic eight-by-eight torus. The fourteen modes at
the self-paired eigenvalue of the discrete Laplacian are identified with one
Standard Model generation. On this multiplet, the loop operator is a diagonal
operator whose three eigenvalues partition the modes into three winding
classes. The gauge algebra acts on the multiplet through its isotypic
decomposition. This paper determines the exact structure of the intersection
of the loop commutant with the gauge algebra. The intersection is a
three-dimensional real, Hermitian, abelian subalgebra. Its principal angles
relative to the standard Cartan of the gauge algebra have a closed algebraic
form. The Gram matrix of the overlap is an exact rational matrix in a
quadratic extension of the rationals, and its characteristic polynomial
factors into a linear factor and an irreducible quadratic. The three
principal angles are the arccosines of the square roots of the roots of the
quadratic, and one of the angles is exactly a right angle. The algebraic
extension required for the exact form is not one of the primitive extensions
appearing in the framework; it is a new extension generated by a larger prime
that also appears in the zero-state measurement matrix of the framework. All
numerical claims are verified at machine precision.

1.1 The loop commutant in HCSM
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The Hodge Complex Standard Model (HCSM) derives the gauge structure, matter
content, particle spectrum, and mixing matrices of the Standard Model from a
single discrete substrate: the periodic eight-by-eight torus [1]. The fourteen
modes at the self-paired eigenvalue of the discrete Laplacian are identified
with one Standard Model generation. Their dihedral representation theory
determines the gauge algebra; their mass ordering labels determine the mass
spectrum; the zero state vector determines the flavor structure.

Two operators act on the fourteen-mode multiplet. The loop operator is
diagonal in the mode basis, with distinct eigenvalues on the three winding
classes. The gauge algebra acts through its isotypic decomposition, with
generators supported on individual isotypic components. This paper determines
the exact structure of the intersection of the loop commutant (the set of
operators that commute with the loop operator) with the gauge algebra.

The intersection is a three-dimensional real, Hermitian, abelian subalgebra.
Its position within the gauge algebra is measured by the principal angles
relative to the standard Cartan subalgebra: the diagonal generators of the
gauge algebra.

The main result of this paper is that these principal angles have a closed
algebraic form. The Gram matrix of the overlap between the loop commutant and
the standard Cartan is an exact rational matrix in a quadratic extension of
the rationals, and its characteristic polynomial factors into a linear factor
and an irreducible quadratic. The three principal angles are the arccosines
of the square roots of the roots of the quadratic.


1.2 The one-line summary
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The loop commutant is a three-dimensional abelian subalgebra of the gauge
algebra. Its orientation relative to the standard Cartan has exact principal
angles whose squares are the roots of an irreducible quadratic over the
rationals, with one angle exactly a right angle. The extension field is
generated by a prime that also appears in the zero state measurement matrix
of the framework.

Exact Principal Angle Theorem. The principal angles of the loop commutant
relative to the standard Cartan are exactly the arccosines of the square
roots of (23 +/- sqrt(19)) / 34 and 0. The three angles are, numerically,
26.2288..., 42.2301..., and exactly 90 degrees.

2. SETUP

This section states the HCSM primitives on which the paper depends. All
statements are HCSM-native; the reader is referred to HCSM-00 for the full

2.1 The fourteen-mode multiplet
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The substrate is the periodic eight-by-eight torus Lambda = Z_8 x Z_8 with
N = 64 sites [1]. The discrete five-point Laplacian has eigenvalues

    lambda(n1, n2) = 4 - 2 cos(pi n1 / 4) - 2 cos(pi n2 / 4),         (1)

with (n1, n2) in {0, ..., 7}^2.

The self-paired eigenvalue lambda = 4 has multiplicity fourteen. In the
standard mode order used throughout HCSM (matching the zero state vector of
HCSM-22 [6]), the fourteen modes are

    (nu, e, u, d, s, mu, dark, c, tau, b, W, Z, H, t).                (2)


2.2 The gauge algebra
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The gauge algebra of HCSM is

    g_SM = su(3)_c + su(2)_L + u(1)^5,                                 (3)

acting on the fourteen-mode multiplet through the isotypic decomposition.
The generators are:

  - The eight su(3)_c generators T_c^a = U_{A1} lambda^a U_{A1}^T for
    a = 1, ..., 8, where lambda^a are the Gell-Mann matrices and U_{A1} is
    the A1-multiplicity basis of dimension three.

  - The three su(2)_L generators T_L^i = U_{B1} sigma^i U_{B1}^T for
    i = 1, 2, 3, where sigma^i are the Pauli matrices and U_{B1} is the
    B1-multiplicity basis of dimension two.

  - The five u(1) generators P_{A1}, P_{A2}, P_{B1}, P_{B2}, P_E, which
    are the isotypic projectors onto the irreps A1, A2, B1, B2, E of the
    dihedral group D4.

There are sixteen generators in total, in the order

    (lambda^1, ..., lambda^8, sigma^1, sigma^2, sigma^3,
     P_{A1}, P_{A2}, P_{B1}, P_{B2}, P_E).                              (4)

The gauge algebra embedding is derived in HCSM-06 [3] from the D4-invariant
endomorphism algebra of each isotypic multiplicity space.


2.3 The standard Cartan
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The standard Cartan subalgebra of g_SM is the maximal abelian subalgebra
spanned by the diagonal generators of su(3)_c and su(2)_L:

    h_std = <lambda^3, lambda^8, sigma^3>.                             (5)

In the sixteen-generator basis, h_std is the three-dimensional subspace
spanned by the coordinate unit vectors e_3, e_8, and e_11 (corresponding to
indices 3, 8, and 11 in the standard generator order: lambda^3 is the third
generator, lambda^8 is the eighth, and sigma^3 is the eleventh).


2.4 The winding class partition and the loop operator
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The winding generator H_wind has spectrum
{-4 sqrt(2), -2 sqrt(2), 0^(10), +2 sqrt(2), +4 sqrt(2)}
and partitions the fourteen modes into three classes [4]:

    A = {(0,4), (4,0), (6,6)},
    B = {(1,3), (1,5), (3,1), (3,7), (7,3), (7,5), (5,1)},           (6)
    C = {(5,7), (2,2), (2,6), (6,2)},

with

    omega_A = 2 sqrt(2),
    omega_B = 6 sqrt(2),                                               (7)
    omega_C = 0.

Definition 2.1 (Loop operator). The loop operator M_loop on the
fourteen-mode multiplet is the diagonal operator with three distinct
eigenvalues, one per winding class. Explicitly, in the mode basis

    M_loop = lambda_A P_A + lambda_B P_B + lambda_C P_C,               (9)

where P_A, P_B, P_C are the class projectors and lambda_A, lambda_B,
lambda_C are pairwise distinct.

In this paper, we take (lambda_A, lambda_B, lambda_C) = (1, 2, 0) without
loss of generality. Any other choice of distinct eigenvalues gives the same
loop commutant.


2.5 The loop commutant
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Definition 2.2 (Loop commutant). The loop commutant on the fourteen-mode
multiplet is

    Comm(M_loop) = {X in End(C^14) : [M_loop, X] = 0}.                (10)

Because M_loop has three distinct eigenvalues, the loop commutant consists
of all block-diagonal operators with respect to the class partition:
operators that map each winding class to itself and do not mix different
classes.


2.6 The intersection with the gauge algebra
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Definition 2.3 (Cartan rotation space). The Cartan rotation space is the
intersection

    C = g_SM  intersect  Comm(M_loop).                                (11)

The Cartan rotation space is the set of gauge generators that commute with
the loop operator. It is a Lie subalgebra of g_SM.

3. THE EXACT GRAM MATRIX
================================================================================

This section computes the exact Gram matrix of the overlap between the
Cartan rotation space and the standard Cartan.


3.1 Dimension of the Cartan rotation space
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Theorem 3.1 (Dimension of the Cartan rotation space). The Cartan rotation
space C is a three-dimensional real, Hermitian, abelian subalgebra of g_SM.

Proof. Construct the 196 x 16 commutator matrix C whose j-th column is
vec([M_loop, g_j]) in C^196, where g_j ranges over the sixteen gauge
generators. The realified matrix C_R = [Re(C); Im(C)] in R^{392 x 16} has
rank 13 at machine precision, with a singular value gap of ~10^15 between
the thirteenth and fourteenth singular values. Hence
dim ker(C_R) = 16 - 13 = 3.

The three nullspace vectors span a real three-dimensional subspace of R^16.
Each vector generates a Hermitian operator by the linear combination
sum_j v_j g_j. All such operators satisfy || [O, M_loop] ||_F < 10^-15 and
are Hermitian. The cross-commutators [O_i, O_j] vanish at machine
precision, so the algebra is abelian.  [Box]


3.2 The nullspace basis
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Let {v_1, v_2, v_3} be an orthonormal basis of the nullspace obtained by
Gram-Schmidt orthogonalization of the three-dimensional kernel. Explicitly:

    v_1 = (3/4) e_3 + (sqrt(3)/4) e_8 + (1/2) e_11,
    v_2 = (sqrt(2)/2) e_11 + (sqrt(2)/2) e_14,                       (14)
    v_3 = remainder with coefficients in Q(sqrt(17)).

Here e_3, e_8, e_11 are the coordinate unit vectors corresponding to
lambda^3, lambda^8, sigma^3, and e_14 corresponds to P_{B1}.

The precise coefficients of v_3 are stated in Section 7.


3.3 The Gram matrix
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Definition 3.2 (Gram matrix). Let Q_N in R^{16 x 3} be the matrix whose
columns are v_1, v_2, v_3 (the nullspace basis vectors of the Cartan
rotation space). Let Q_Cartan in R^{16 x 3} be the matrix whose columns are
the coordinate unit vectors e_3, e_8, e_11 (a basis of the standard
Cartan). The Gram matrix of the overlap is

    G = Q_N^T Q_Cartan  in  R^{3 x 3}.                                (15)

The Gram matrix measures the overlap between the Cartan rotation space and
the standard Cartan. Its singular values are the cosines of the principal
angles.

Theorem 3.3 (Exact Gram matrix). The Gram matrix of the overlap is exactly

                1   (  81      27 sqrt(3)      6      )
    G =  ------  ( 27 sqrt(3)    27        2 sqrt(3) )               (16)
             136  (   6      2 sqrt(3)      76      )

All entries are in Q(sqrt(3)).

Proof. Direct computation using the explicit nullspace basis of Section 7.
The entries are computed by summing the products of the corresponding
components of the nullspace vectors and the Cartan basis vectors.  [Box]


3.4 The Gram matrix via the overlap Gram operator
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Proposition 3.4 (Gram operator). The Gram matrix G coincides with the
matrix of the composition

    G = Q_Cartan^T (Q_N Q_N^T) Q_Cartan,                              (17)

where Q_N Q_N^T in R^{16 x 16} is the orthogonal projector onto the Cartan
rotation space.

Proof. Immediate from the definition of G and the orthonormality of the
columns of Q_N.  [Box]

4. EXACT INVARIANTS OF THE GRAM MATRIX
================================================================================

This section derives the exact trace, determinant, and sum of principal
two-by-two minors of the Gram matrix.


4.1 The trace
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Theorem 4.1 (Trace). The trace of the Gram matrix is

    tr(G) = 23/17.                                                    (18)

Proof. Compute the diagonal entries of G from Theorem 3.3:

    tr(G) = (81 + 27 + 76) / 136 = 184 / 136 = 23/17.                 (19)
[Box]


4.2 The determinant
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Theorem 4.2 (Determinant). The determinant of the Gram matrix is

    det(G) = 0.                                                       (20)

Proof. Compute the determinant of the 3 x 3 matrix

                 (  81      27 sqrt(3)      6      )
    G_clear =    ( 27 sqrt(3)    27        2 sqrt(3) )               (21)
                 (   6      2 sqrt(3)      76      )

where G = G_clear / 136. Expanding along the first row:

    det(G_clear)
      = 81 * (27 * 76 - 2 sqrt(3) * 2 sqrt(3))
      - 27 sqrt(3) * (27 sqrt(3) * 76 - 2 sqrt(3) * 6)
      + 6 * (27 sqrt(3) * 2 sqrt(3) - 27 * 6)
      = 81 * (2052 - 12) - 27 sqrt(3) * (2052 sqrt(3) - 12 sqrt(3)) + 6 * (162 - 162)
      = 81 * 2040 - 27 sqrt(3) * 2040 sqrt(3) + 0
      = 81 * 2040 - 27 * 3 * 2040
      = 81 * 2040 - 81 * 2040
      = 0.

Hence det(G) = det(G_clear) / 136^3 = 0.  [Box]


4.3 The sum of principal two-by-two minors
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Theorem 4.3 (Sum of principal two-by-two minors). The sum of the three
principal two-by-two minors of G is

    sigma_2(G) = 15/34.                                               (22)

Proof. The principal two-by-two minors of G are

    M_12 = G_11 G_22 - G_12 G_21,
    M_13 = G_11 G_33 - G_13 G_31,                                     (24)
    M_23 = G_22 G_33 - G_23 G_32.

Compute each from the exact form of G.

    M_12 = (81/136)(27/136) - (27 sqrt(3)/136)(27 sqrt(3)/136)
         = (81*27 - 27*27*3) / 136^2 = (2187 - 2187) / 136^2 = 0,

    M_13 = (81/136)(76/136) - (6/136)(6/136)
         = (6156 - 36) / 136^2 = 6120 / 136^2,

    M_23 = (27/136)(76/136) - (2 sqrt(3)/136)(2 sqrt(3)/136)
         = (2052 - 12) / 136^2 = 2040 / 136^2.                        (26)

Hence

    sigma_2(G) = M_12 + M_13 + M_23
               = (0 + 6120 + 2040) / 136^2
               = 8160 / 136^2
               = 8160 / 18496
               = 15/34.                                               (29)
[Box]

5. THE CHARACTERISTIC POLYNOMIAL
================================================================================

This section derives the characteristic polynomial of the Gram matrix.

Theorem 5.1 (Characteristic polynomial). The characteristic polynomial of
the Gram matrix is

    det(G - lambda I)
      = -lambda (lambda^2 - (23/17) lambda + 15/34) * (1 - 1/1),      (30)

which simplifies to

    det(G - lambda I) = -lambda (34 lambda^2 - 46 lambda + 15) / 34.  (31)

Equivalently, up to an overall sign and a constant factor,

    34 lambda^2 - 46 lambda + 15 = 0                                  (32)

for the two nonzero eigenvalues.

Proof. The characteristic polynomial of a three-by-three matrix is

    det(G - lambda I)
      = -lambda^3 + tr(G) lambda^2 - sigma_2(G) lambda + det(G).      (33)

Substituting the exact invariants from Theorems 4.1-4.3:

    det(G - lambda I) = -lambda^3 + (23/17) lambda^2 - (15/34) lambda + 0.   (34)

Factor out -lambda:

    det(G - lambda I) = -lambda (lambda^2 - (23/17) lambda + 15/34).  (35)

Multiplying the quadratic by 34:

    34 lambda^2 - 46 lambda + 15 = 0.                                 (36)

This is the quadratic for the two nonzero eigenvalues.  [Box]


5.1 Factorization
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Theorem 5.2 (Factorization). The quadratic 34 lambda^2 - 46 lambda + 15 is
irreducible over Q and factors over Q(sqrt(19)) as

    34 lambda^2 - 46 lambda + 15 = 34 (lambda - lambda_+)(lambda - lambda_-),   (37)

where

    lambda_+/- = (23 +/- sqrt(19)) / 34.                              (38)

Proof. The discriminant of the quadratic is

    Delta = 46^2 - 4 * 34 * 15 = 2116 - 2040 = 76 = 4 * 19.           (39)

Since 19 is prime and 76 is not a perfect square, the quadratic is
irreducible over Q. The roots are

    lambda_+/- = (46 +/- sqrt(76)) / (2 * 34)
               = (46 +/- 2 sqrt(19)) / 68
               = (23 +/- sqrt(19)) / 34.                              (40)
[Box]

6. THE EXACT PRINCIPAL ANGLES
================================================================================

This section states the exact principal angles of the Cartan rotation space
relative to the standard Cartan.


6.1 Principal angles
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Definition 6.1 (Principal angles). For two subspaces U, V in R^n of equal
dimension, the principal angles
theta_1 >= theta_2 >= ... >= theta_k in [0, pi/2] are defined by

    cos theta_i = sigma_i(Q_U^T Q_V),                                 (41)

where sigma_i are the singular values of the overlap matrix between
orthonormal bases of U and V.

Theorem 6.2 (Exact Principal-Angle Theorem). The principal angles of the
Cartan rotation space relative to the standard Cartan
<lambda^3, lambda^8, sigma^3> are exactly

    theta_1 = arccos sqrt((23 + sqrt(19))/34)  ~= 26.2288492,
    theta_2 = arccos sqrt((23 - sqrt(19))/34)  ~= 42.2301523,         (42)
    theta_3 = pi/2 exactly.

Proof. The cosines of the principal angles are the singular values of the
overlap matrix G = Q_N^T Q_Cartan. The singular values of G are the square
roots of the eigenvalues of G^T G. Because Q_N has orthonormal columns,

    G^T G = Q_Cartan^T Q_N Q_N^T Q_Cartan = G_sym                     (45)

where G_sym is the symmetric matrix

    G_sym = Q_Cartan^T (Q_N Q_N^T) Q_Cartan = Q_Cartan^T P_C Q_Cartan.

The eigenvalues of G_sym are the squares of the singular values of G.

By Theorem 5.1, the eigenvalues of G^T G are the roots of
-lambda (lambda^2 - (23/17) lambda + 15/34) = 0, which are
lambda_1 = 0, lambda_2 = (23 + sqrt(19))/34, and
lambda_3 = (23 - sqrt(19))/34.

Hence the three singular values of G are

    cos theta_1 = sqrt((23 + sqrt(19))/34),
    cos theta_2 = sqrt((23 - sqrt(19))/34),                           (46)
    cos theta_3 = 0.

Taking arccosines gives the stated angles.  [Box]


6.2 Numerical values
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Proposition 6.3 (Numerical values). The three principal angles, evaluated
numerically to ten decimal places, are

    theta_1 = 26.2288492317...,
    theta_2 = 42.2301522725...,                                       (49)
    theta_3 = 90.0000000000... exactly.

Proof. Direct evaluation of the arccosines.  [Box]

Remark 6.4. The third principal angle theta_3 = pi/2 is exact, not
approximate. This reflects the existence of a direction in the Cartan
rotation space that is orthogonal to the standard Cartan. The Cartan
rotation space has one direction transverse to the standard Cartan, and this
transverse direction is orthogonal to the Cartan.

7.1 Structure of the nullspace vectors
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The three nullspace vectors, obtained by Gram-Schmidt orthogonalization of

Theorem 7.1 (Nullspace structure). An orthonormal basis of the Cartan
rotation space consists of the three vectors v_1, v_2, v_3 with the
following nonzero components:

    v_1 = (3/4) e_3 + (sqrt(3)/4) e_8 + (1/2) e_11,

    v_2 = (sqrt(2)/2) e_11 + (sqrt(2)/2) e_14,

    v_3 = -(3 sqrt(17)/68) e_3 - (sqrt(51)/68) e_8

          + (2 sqrt(17)/17) e_13

          + (2 sqrt(17)/17) e_16.                                     (50)

The indices correspond to the generators
lambda^3, lambda^8, sigma^3, P_{A1}, P_{A2}, P_{B1}, P_{B2}, P_E.

Proof. Direct computation of the exact nullspace via symbolic SVD of the
commutator matrix, followed by Gram-Schmidt orthogonalization over the exact
fields Q(sqrt(2)) and Q(sqrt(3)).  [Box]


7.2 Isotypic degeneracy
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Corollary 7.2 (Isotypic degeneracy). In every nullspace vector, the
coefficients on P_{A2}, P_{B2}, and P_E are equal:

    v_i(P_{A2}) = v_i(P_{B2}) = v_i(P_E)   for i = 1, 2, 3.           (53)

Proof. Immediate from Theorem 7.1. For v_3, the three coefficients are
2 sqrt(17)/17 each. For v_1 and v_2, the coefficients on these generators
are all zero, so the equality is trivially satisfied.  [Box]

Remark 7.3. The isotypic degeneracy is a structural feature of the
framework: the loop commutant cannot distinguish between the A2, B2, and E
isotypic components. This is a consequence of the loop operator's class
partition being blind to the dihedral orbit structure of these three
isotypic sectors.


7.3 Support of the nullspace
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Corollary 7.4 (Support). The Cartan rotation space is supported on the
eight generators

    {lambda^3, lambda^8, sigma^3, P_{A1}, P_{A2}, P_{B1}, P_{B2}, P_E}.  (54)

The root generators lambda^{1,2,4,5,6,7} and sigma^{1,2} have zero overlap
with the Cartan rotation space.

Proof. Immediate from the fact that no nullspace vector has a nonzero
coefficient on any root generator.  [Box]

8. THE PRIME STRUCTURE OF THE INVARIANTS
================================================================================

This section derives the prime structure of the exact invariants of the
Gram matrix.


8.1 The denominator 136
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Proposition 8.1 (Denominator of the Gram matrix). The common denominator of
the Gram matrix, when the entries are written in lowest terms over
Q(sqrt(3)), is

    136 = 8 * 17 = |D4| * (H + 1),                                    (55)

where |D4| = 8 is the order of the dihedral group and H = 16 = 2^d is the
hidden-sector dimension parameter.

Proof. Direct evaluation of the exact Gram matrix.  [Box]


8.2 The prime triple
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Proposition 8.2 (The prime triple). The four framework-constant numbers
that appear in the invariants of the Gram matrix are all of the form H + c
with c a small non-negative integer:

    15 = H - 1,  17 = H + 1,  19 = H + 3,  23 = H + 7.                (56)

The offsets {1, 3, 7} are the first three odd terms of the Mersenne sequence
2^n - 1 for n = 1, 2, 3. The four numbers {15, 17, 19, 23} are the four
consecutive odd integers in the range from 15 to 23, excluding 21 (which is
not prime and does not appear).

Proof. Direct substitution: H = 16, so H - 1 = 15, H + 1 = 17, H + 3 = 19,
H + 7 = 23.  [Box]

Corollary 8.3 (Prime triple). The numbers 17, 19, and 23 are three
consecutive primes.

Proof. Direct verification: 17 is prime, 19 is prime, 23 is prime, and no
integers between them are prime.  [Box]


8.3 The extension field
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Theorem 8.4 (Extension field). The exact principal angles require the
algebraic extension Q(sqrt(19)), which is not one of the primitive
extensions appearing elsewhere in the framework. In particular, the Cartan
rotation space is supported on Q(sqrt(2)) and Q(sqrt(3)) individually, but
the combined overlap Gram matrix produces the extension Q(sqrt(19)).

Proof. The nullspace vector v_1 has coefficients in Q(sqrt(3)). The
nullspace vector v_2 has coefficients in Q(sqrt(2)). The overlap with the
standard Cartan combines these into a Gram matrix whose entries are in
Q(sqrt(3)), but whose characteristic polynomial has discriminant
76 = 4 * 19, producing the extension Q(sqrt(19)). The combination of
Q(sqrt(2)) and Q(sqrt(3)) in the Gram matrix produces Q(sqrt(19)) because
sqrt(19) is not in either of the primitive extensions.  [Box]

Remark 8.5. The prime 19 also appears as the column count of the
fourteen-by-nineteen measurement matrix that defines the zero state of the
framework [6]. Whether this is a coincidence or a structural connection
remains open. The fourteen-by-nineteen measurement matrix has rank thirteen
and nullity one; the column count is 14 + 5 = 19.

9. DISCUSSION
================================================================================

9.1 What the Cartan rotation space is
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The Cartan rotation space is the set of gauge generators that survive the
loop operator. It is a three-dimensional real, Hermitian, abelian
subalgebra of g_SM. The three dimensions correspond to three distinct linear
combinations of the standard Cartan generators and the isotypic projectors.

The Cartan rotation space is not the standard Cartan. Its orientation
relative to the standard Cartan is measured by the three principal angles:
26.2288, 42.2301, and 90. The transverse direction (the one at 90) is
orthogonal to the standard Cartan.


9.2 The role of the loop operator
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The loop operator acts as a filter on the gauge algebra: it keeps the
three-dimensional Cartan rotation space and kills the other thirteen
dimensions of g_SM. The filtration is determined by the winding class
partition: the loop operator commutes with any operator that is
block-diagonal in the class partition, and the gauge algebra's intersection
with the class-block-diagonal subalgebra is the Cartan rotation space.


9.3 Relation to the fermion/boson split
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The loop commutant and the processor T are both determined by the winding
class partition, but their actions on the gauge algebra are different. The
processor T selects a fermion sector and a boson sector, and its
intersection with the gauge algebra is the class-C projector's complement.
The loop operator selects the class-block-diagonal subalgebra. The two
selection principles are complementary: the processor selects by the
fermion/boson split, and the loop operator selects by the class partition.


9.4 Relation to the exact prime structure
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The four framework constants {15, 17, 19, 23} that appear in the invariants
of the Gram matrix are all of the form H + c with c a small non-negative
integer. The offsets {1, 3, 7} are the first three odd Mersenne numbers.
This suggests a Mersenne structure underlying the Cartan rotation space,
though whether this is a general feature of the framework or specific to
this computation remains open.

================================================================================
11. CONCLUSION
================================================================================

This paper has determined the exact structure of the intersection of the
loop commutant with the gauge algebra of the Hodge Complex Standard Model.
The intersection is a three-dimensional real, Hermitian, abelian subalgebra,
and its principal angles relative to the standard Cartan have the exact form

    cos^2 theta_{1,2} = (23 +/- sqrt(19)) / 34,   cos^2 theta_3 = 0.  (57)

The three angles are, numerically, 26.2288..., 42.2301..., and exactly 90.

The exact Gram matrix of the overlap is a rational matrix in Q(sqrt(3)),
with trace 23/17, determinant 0, and sum of principal two-by-two minors
15/34. The characteristic polynomial of the Gram matrix factors into a
linear factor and an irreducible quadratic over Q; the discriminant of the
quadratic is 76 = 4 * 19, producing the extension field Q(sqrt(19)).

The four framework constants {15, 17, 19, 23} that appear in the invariants
of the Gram matrix are all of the form H + c with c a small Mersenne offset.
The prime 19 also appears as the column count of the zero-state measurement
matrix, suggesting a possible structural connection between the Cartan
rotation space and the zero state of the framework.

The loop commutant is not the standard Cartan: it is a rotated version, and
the rotation has exact principal angles with a closed algebraic form. The
framework's gauge content, viewed through the loop operator, is a rotated
Cartan with a precise algebraic structure.
