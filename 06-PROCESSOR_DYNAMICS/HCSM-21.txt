================================================================================

We derive time and its arrow in the Hodge Complex Standard Model (HCSM) as
internal structures of the five-layer loop. The loop operator

    M_loop = T . L_EFT . L_Core . L_DME . L_FDLM

acts diagonally on the 14-mode multiplet with polar form
M_loop(m) = r_m exp(i theta_m). The complex time of mode m is

    tau_m = theta_m + i ln r_m = omega_m T_DME - i(-ln r_m),

where omega_m is the winding-generator frequency, T_DME = 1/(2 gamma) is
the DME relaxation time, and r_m is the per-pass amplitude. The real part
theta_m is the clock; the imaginary part -ln r_m > 0 is the arrow
increment. The processor T is exactly the projection onto modes with
Re(tau) > 0.

The paper establishes twelve principal results. First, the prior, during,
and after states at each of the five levels are derived explicitly, with
the transition rules between levels. Second, the complex time is derived
as the logarithm of the loop operator: tau_m = i ln M_loop(m). Third, the
arrow of time is derived from the loop's dissipativity:
|M_loop(m)| < 1. Fourth, the topological floor function is derived as the
winding number of the DME-Core interface. Fifth, dimensional homogeneity
of tau_m is proved. Sixth, the exact ratio |M_A|/|M_B| = 3 = d - 1 is
derived from the closed-form T_DME. Seventh, the loop-pass duration is
selected by the deterministic Landauer bound: T_DME is the unique
dissipative scale in HCSM. Eighth, the spin chirality
Z_2 x Z_2 x Z_2 grading commutes with the loop. Ninth, the time unit is
T_unit = 2 pi / m_s approximately 5.3 days. Tenth, the particle
identification is derived from the Midpoint Sorting Theorem. Eleventh,
continuous time t = k T_DME is derived from the loop count and loop-pass
duration. Twelfth, a philosophical and physical discussion places HCSM's
derived time and motion in contrast with general relativity and the
Standard Model.

All numerical claims are verified at 50-digit precision. The framework has
a single dimensionful anchor M_P, with all dimensionless ratios determined
by the substrate geometry. The elegant theorem is stated: Time is the
loop's logarithm; motion is the loop's composition; the loop is the
machine.

Keywords: Hodge Complex Standard Model; HCSM; Time Theorem; complex time;
arrow of time; Landauer selection; loop-pass duration; floor function;
spin chirality; motion-time unification.

1.1 THE TIME PROBLEM

The Standard Model has no fundamental arrow of time. The Einstein
equations of general relativity are time-reversal symmetric. Neither
framework derives time from deeper principles; both assume a time
coordinate as part of their kinematic structure. The Hodge Complex
Standard Model (HCSM) supplies a closed five-layer loop with a preferred
direction and a dissipative operator. Time is a readout of the loop, not
an input.

The present paper is the twenty-first in the HCSM White Paper Series. It
builds on the substrate foundations (HCSM-00, HCSM-01, HCSM-02), the
14-mode multiplet (HCSM-04), the processor (HCSM-13), the winding
generator (HCSM-14), the motion theorem (HCSM-20), the vacuum stability
sector (HCSM-54), and the vacuum effective field theory (HCSM-56). It
closes the time sector of the framework.
