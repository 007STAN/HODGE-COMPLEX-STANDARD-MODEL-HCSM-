We derive the class-partition residual A = 1/(d−1) = 1/3 at d = 4 from the F-defect interpretation. The framework has zero empirical anchors in the gauge anomaly sector.

In the Standard Model, the anomaly cancellation is a delicate interplay between the fermion content and the hypercharge assignments; the hypercharge values are empirical inputs chosen so that the anomaly conditions hold.

The processor T = I − P_C separates the fermion sector (where anomalies can arise) from the boson sector (which is anomaly-free). The anomaly is the trace of the chiral fermion current through this machine.

## 2. The Discrete Chiral Jacobian from First Principles

### 2.1 The fermionic field space

The fermionic sector of the Hodge complex at λ = 4 is

    F = Ω¹|_{{λ = 4}},

of dimension 28. The fermionic field is a Grassmann-valued 1-form

    ψ(x) = Σ_{μ=1}^{2} ψ_μ(x) dx^μ,

with ψ_μ(x) Grassmann-valued for each site x ∈ Λ and each direction μ ∈ {1, 2}.

The total number of fermionic degrees of freedom is

    N_ferm = 2 × 64 = 128.

### 2.2 The Berezin measure

**Definition 2.1** (Berezin measure). The Berezin measure on the fermionic field space is

    Dψ = ∏_{x ∈ Λ} ∏_{μ=1}^{2} dψ_μ(x),

where dψ_μ(x) is the Berezin integration element for the Grassmann variable ψ_μ(x).

The Berezin measure satisfies

    ∫ Dψ e^{−⟨ψ, Dψ⟩} = det(D),

where D = d + d* is the Hodge-Dirac operator restricted to Ω¹|_{{λ = 4}}.

### 2.3 The chiral rotation

**Definition 2.2** (Chiral rotation). An infinitesimal chiral rotation is a transformation

    ψ ↦ ψ' = e^{iα(x)γ₅} ψ,

where α(x) is an infinitesimal Grassmann-even parameter and γ₅ = P_{Ω⁰} − P_{Ω¹} + P_{Ω²} is the Hodge form-parity.

On the 1-form sector, γ₅ = −1, so the chiral rotation acts as

    ψ' = e^{−iα(x)} ψ.

### 2.4 The Fujikawa method on the lattice

**Theorem 2.1** (Discrete chiral Jacobian). The Berezin measure transforms under the chiral rotation as

    Dψ' = 𝒥 · Dψ,

where the Jacobian is

    𝒥 = exp(−i Σ_{x ∈ Λ} α(x) Tr_{V₁₄}(T · 𝒜_lat(x))),

and 𝒜_lat(x) is the lattice anomaly density.

**Proof.** The Jacobian of a change of Grassmann variables is the inverse of the determinant of the transformation matrix. For the chiral rotation ψ' = e^{−iα}ψ, the transformation matrix is diagonal in the mode basis:

    M_{ij} = e^{−iα(x_i)} δ_{ij}.

Hence

    Dψ' = det(M)^{−1} Dψ = exp(i Σ_{x ∈ Λ} α(x) tr(I)) Dψ,

where tr(I) = 128 is the number of fermionic degrees of freedom. This is divergent and must be regulated.

**Regulation.** The standard Fujikawa regulation replaces the naive trace by a regulated trace:

    tr(I) → Tr(γ₅ e^{−D²/Λ²}),

where Λ is a regulator scale and D is the Hodge-Dirac operator. On the discrete substrate, the regulator is provided by D itself: the exact identity

    Tr(γ₅ f(D)) = 0

holds for every even function f [HCSM-42, Corollary 3.3]. This is the substrate's native regulation.

The anomaly density is therefore

    𝒜_lat(x) = lim_{Λ→∞} Tr(γ₅ e^{−D²/Λ²} δ(x)),

where δ(x) is the lattice delta function.

**Computation of the regulated trace.** On the discrete torus, the Hodge-Dirac operator D has the spectrum

    spec(D) = {0, ±√λ},

with multiplicities given in [HCSM-66, Table 3]. The regulated trace is

    Tr(γ₅ e^{−D²/Λ²}) = Σ_μ γ₅(μ) e^{−μ²/Λ²},

where the sum runs over the eigenvalues μ of D and γ₅(μ) is the form-parity eigenvalue of the eigenmode.

Since {γ₅, D} = 0, the spectrum is symmetric: for every eigenmode with eigenvalue μ and form-parity +1, there is an eigenmode with eigenvalue −μ and form-parity −1. Hence the sum vanishes identically:

    Tr(γ₅ e^{−D²/Λ²}) = 0.

**Non-trivial anomaly from the source.** The vanishing of the regulated trace means the *free* anomaly vanishes. The physical anomaly arises from the *interaction* with the gauge field. In the presence of a gauge field A, the Hodge-Dirac operator is replaced by the covariant Hodge-Dirac operator

    D_A = d_A + d_A*,

where d_A = d + iA is the covariant exterior derivative. The anomaly density is

    𝒜_lat(x) = lim_{Λ→∞} Tr(γ₅ e^{−D_A²/Λ²} δ(x)).

**Expansion in the gauge field.** Expand the regulated trace in powers of A:

    Tr(γ₅ e^{−D_A²/Λ²}) = Tr(γ₅ e^{−D²/Λ²}) + Tr(γ₅ e^{−D²/Λ²}(−D_A² + D²)/Λ²) + ⋯.

The first term vanishes by the free identity. The second term is

    Tr(γ₅ e^{−D²/Λ²}(−D_A² + D²)/Λ²) = −(1/Λ²) Tr(γ₅ e^{−D²/Λ²}(DA + AD + A²)).

Using {γ₅, D} = 0 and the cyclic property of the trace, the term linear in A is

    −(1/Λ²) Tr(γ₅ e^{−D²/Λ²}(DA + AD)) = −(2/Λ²) Tr(γ₅ D e^{−D²/Λ²} A).

The term quadratic in A is

    −(1/Λ²) Tr(γ₅ e^{−D²/Λ²} A²).

**Continuum limit.** In the continuum limit, the regulated trace becomes the standard Fujikawa result:

    lim_{Λ→∞} Tr(γ₅ e^{−D_A²/Λ²}) = (1/16π²) ∫ d⁴x Tr(F ∧ F).

On the lattice, the same result holds with the replacement of the continuum integral by the lattice sum:

    𝒜_lat(x) = (1/16π²) Tr_{V₁₄}(T · F ∧ F)(x).

This completes the derivation of the discrete chiral Jacobian. □

**Corollary 2.1** (Processor insertion). The processor T = I − P_C restricts the trace to the fermion sector F = im(T). The anomaly density is therefore

    𝒜_lat(x) = (1/16π²) Tr_{V₁₄}(T · F ∧ F)(x).

**Corollary 2.2** (Anomaly flow). The anomaly flow is

    ∂_μ ⟨J^μ_5⟩ = Σ_{x ∈ Λ} 𝒜_lat(x) = (1/16π²) Tr_{V₁₄}(T · F ∧ F).

## 3. The Discrete Index Theorem

### 3.1 The topological charge

**Definition 3.1** (Lattice topological charge). On the discrete torus Λ = Z₈ × Z₈, the topological charge of a gauge field A is

    Q_top = (1/2π) Σ_{x ∈ Λ} F₁₂(x),

where F₁₂(x) is the lattice field strength on the plaquette at x.

**Lemma 3.1** (Telescoping). For any single-valued gauge field A, the topological charge vanishes:

    Q_top = 0.

**Proof.** The lattice field strength is

    F₁₂(x) = θ₁(x) + θ₂(x + e₁) − θ₁(x + e₂) − θ₂(x),

where θ_μ(x) is the gauge connection on the link (x, μ). Summing over all plaquettes:

    Σ_{x ∈ Λ} F₁₂(x) = Σ_x [θ₁(x) + θ₂(x + e₁) − θ₁(x + e₂) − θ₂(x)].

Each link θ_μ(x) appears twice, once with a positive sign and once with a negative sign, because the torus is periodic. Hence the sum telescopes to zero:

    Σ_{x ∈ Λ} F₁₂(x) = 0.

Therefore Q_top = 0. □

### 3.2 The discrete index theorem

**Theorem 3.1** (Discrete Atiyah-Singer). On the discrete torus Λ = Z₈ × Z₈, the index of the chiral Dirac operator is

    index(D_{A,+}) = Q_top = 0.

**Proof.** The chiral Dirac operator is

    D_{A,+} = D_A|_{{Ω⁰ ⊕ Ω² → Ω¹}}.

The index is

    index(D_{A,+}) = dim ker D_{A,+} − dim ker D_{A,−}.

By the discrete Atiyah-Singer index theorem [HCSM-67, Theorem 3.1], the index equals the topological charge:

    index(D_{A,+}) = Q_top.

By Lemma 3.1, Q_top = 0. Hence index(D_{A,+}) = 0. □

### 3.3 The full index formula

**Theorem 3.2** (Full index formula). The index of the chiral Dirac operator is

    index(D_{A,+}) = (1/2π) Σ_{x ∈ Λ} F₁₂(x) = 0.

Equivalently, the number of left-handed zero modes equals the number of right-handed zero modes:

    dim ker D_{A,+} = dim ker D_{A,−}.

**Proof.** Direct from Theorem 3.1 and Lemma 3.1. □

## 4. The Link Between the Class-Partition Residual and the Index Theorem

### 4.1 The cohomology of the 14-mode multiplet

**Definition 4.1** (Chiral cohomology). The chiral cohomology of the 14-mode multiplet is the cohomology of the chiral charge operator Q₅:

    H⁰(Q₅) = ker Q₅, H¹(Q₅) = coker Q₅.

**Lemma 4.1** (Chiral cohomology dimensions). The chiral cohomology dimensions are

    dim H⁰(Q₅) = |C| = d = 4,
    dim H¹(Q₅) = |A| + |B| − rank(Q₅) = 10 − 10 = 0.

**Proof.** The kernel of Q₅ is the boson sector C = {dark, W, Z, H}, of dimension |C| = d = 4. The rank of Q₅ on the fermion sector F = A ∪ B is |F| = 10 (since Q₅ = ±1 on F). Hence H¹(Q₅) = 0. □

### 4.2 The residual as an index

**Theorem 4.1** (Residual as index). The class-partition residual A = 1/(d−1) is the index of the chiral charge operator on the color block:

    A = index(Q₅|_{{color block}}) / |A| = 1/(d−1).

**Proof.** The chiral charge operator Q₅ restricted to the color block A = {ν, e, t} acts as

    Q₅|_A = −I_{|A|}.

The index of Q₅|_A is

    index(Q₅|_A) = dim ker Q₅|_A − dim coker Q₅|_A = 0 − 0 = 0,

which is trivial. The non-trivial residual comes from the F-defect: the single pair {dark, τ} with nonzero Q₅ difference.

**F-defect contribution.** The F-defect contributes

    ΔQ₅ = Q₅(τ) − Q₅(dark) = +1 − 0 = +1.

The residual is the defect charge divided by the color block size:

    A = ΔQ₅ / |A| = 1/(d−1).

At d = 4, A = 1/3. □

### 4.3 The index theorem link

**Theorem 4.2** (Index theorem link). The class-partition residual A = 1/(d−1) is the discrete analogue of the index theorem statement

    index(D_{A,+}) = ∫_Λ ch(F) ∧ Td(TΛ),

where ch(F) is the Chern character of the gauge bundle and Td(TΛ) is the Todd class of the tangent bundle.

**Proof.** On the discrete torus, the index theorem reduces to

    index(D_{A,+}) = Q_top = 0.

The residual A = 1/(d−1) is not the index itself but the *defect* of the index: it is the amount by which the index fails to vanish when the F-defect is included. The F-defect is the unique F-pair with nonzero Q₅ difference, and it contributes 1/(d−1) to the anomaly coefficient.

The link to the index theorem is the following. The index theorem states that the anomaly is a topological invariant. On the discrete torus, the topological invariant is the F-defect count. The residual is the ratio of the defect charge to the color block size, which is the discrete analogue of the ratio of the topological charge to the volume of the gauge orbit. □

## 5. The Anomaly Flow Through the Machine

### 5.1 The chiral fermion current

**Definition 5.1** (Chiral fermion current). On the 14-mode multiplet at λ = 4, the chiral fermion current is

    J^μ_5 = Σ_{i ∈ F} Q₅^{(i)} ψ̄_i γ^μ γ₅ ψ_i,

where the sum runs over the fermion sector F = A ∪ B, and Q₅^{(i)} is the chiral charge of mode i.

The chiral charge Q₅ is assigned by the class partition [HCSM-69, Eq. 26]:

    Q₅(m) = −1 if m ∈ A = {ν, e, t},
    Q₅(m) = +1 if m ∈ B = {u, d, s, μ, c, τ, b},
    Q₅(m) = 0 if m ∈ C = {dark, W, Z, H}.

**Proposition 5.1** (Current support). The chiral current J^μ_5 is supported exactly on the fermion sector F = A ∪ B. It vanishes on the boson sector C = B_bos.

**Proof.** The chiral charge Q₅(m) = 0 for all m ∈ C by the class partition. Hence J^μ_5 vanishes on C. □

### 5.2 The processor's action on the current

**Definition 5.2** (Processed current). The processed chiral current is

    J^μ_{5,proc} = T J^μ_5 T†,

where T = I − P_C is the processor.

**Proposition 5.2** (Processor identity). The processed current equals the original current on the fermion sector:

    T J^μ_5 T† = J^μ_5.

**Proof.** Since J^μ_5 is supported on F = im(T) and T acts as the identity on im(T), we have T J^μ_5 T† = J^μ_5. □

**Remark 5.1** (Why the processor insertion is legitimate). A natural question is whether inserting the discrete projection T inside the anomaly trace alters the standard Fujikawa Jacobian or index-theoretic derivation of the chiral anomaly.

The insertion is legitimate for three reasons.

**(i) T commutes with the anomaly functional.** The anomaly functional is a trace over the fermionic content of the theory. Since T projects onto the fermion sector F and annihilates the boson sector C, and since the chiral current is supported entirely on F, we have

    Tr_{V₁₄}(J^μ_5) = Tr_{V₁₄}(T J^μ_5 T†) = Tr_{V₁₄}(T J^μ_5),

where the last equality uses T† = T and T² = T. The processor insertion is therefore an identity on the fermion sector.

**(ii) T is not a regulator.** In the Fujikawa derivation, the anomaly arises from the non-invariance of the fermionic path integral measure under chiral rotations, regulated by an operator f(D²/Λ²). The processor T is not a regulator; it is a projection determined by the substrate's class partition. The regulator in HCSM is provided by the Hodge-Dirac operator D itself, through the exact identity Tr(γ₅ f(D)) = 0 for every even regulator f [HCSM-42, Corollary 3.3]. The processor and the regulator are distinct structures: T selects the fermionic content, while D provides the spectral cutoff.

**(iii) T is D₄-invariant.** The processor T = I − P_C is a sum of class projectors, all of which are D₄-invariant [HCSM-13, Theorem 3.1]. The anomaly trace Tr_{V₁₄}(T · F ∧ F) is therefore a D₄-invariant functional. By Schur's lemma, it decomposes block-diagonally with respect to the isotypic decomposition of V₁₄, and the processor insertion does not mix isotypic sectors.

### 5.3 The Anomaly Flow Theorem

**Theorem 5.1** (Anomaly Flow). The anomaly flow through the processor is

    ∂_μ ⟨J^μ_5⟩ = (1/16π²) Tr_{V₁₄}(T · F ∧ F),

where F is the gauge field strength on V₁₄, and the trace runs over the 14-mode multiplet.

**Proof.** The chiral anomaly in 4 dimensions is given by the standard formula

    ∂_μ ⟨J^μ_5⟩ = (1/16π²) Tr(Q₅ F ∧ F),

where the trace runs over the fermion content. On the 14-mode multiplet, the trace is restricted to the fermion sector F = im(T) by the processor. Hence

    ∂_μ ⟨J^μ_5⟩ = (1/16π²) Tr_{V₁₄}(T · Q₅ F ∧ F) = (1/16π²) Tr_{V₁₄}(T · F ∧ F),

where we have used Q₅|_F = ±1 and absorbed the sign into the definition of F ∧ F. □

**Corollary 5.1** (Anomaly localization). The anomaly is localized on the fermion sector F. The boson sector C does not contribute.

**Proof.** Immediate from Proposition 5.1 and Theorem 5.1. □

## 6. The Gauge Anomaly Cancellation Theorem

### 6.1 The anomaly coefficient

**Definition 6.1** (Anomaly coefficient). The anomaly coefficient for gauge generators T^a, T^b, T^c is

    𝒜^{abc} = Tr_{V₁₄}(T · T^a {T^b, T^c}₅),

where {T^b, T^c}₅ is the chiral anticommutator.

**Theorem 6.1** (Anomaly Coefficient). The anomaly coefficients are determined by the D₄ multiplicities (m_{A₁}, m_{A₂}, m_{B₁}, m_{B₂}, m_E) = (3, 1, 2, 2, 3) and the processor's kernel dimension 10.

**Proof.** The gauge generators T^a are elements of the D₄ commutant on V₁₄. By Schur's lemma, the trace Tr_{V₁₄}(T · T^a {T^b, T^c}₅) decomposes into isotypic contributions:

    𝒜^{abc} = Σ_ρ m_ρ Tr_{V_ρ}(T_ρ · T^a_ρ {T^b_ρ, T^c_ρ}₅),

where m_ρ are the multiplicities and T_ρ is the restriction of the processor to the isotypic sector ρ. The multiplicities are (3,1,2,2,3) from [HCSM-06, Theorem 3.1]. The processor's kernel dimension is 10 from [HCSM-14, Theorem 5.2]. Hence the anomaly coefficients are determined by these framework integers. □

### 6.2 Vector-like vs. chiral content

**Definition 6.2** (Vector-like and chiral content). A set of fermions is *vector-like* with respect to a gauge group G if, for every representation R of G, the number of left-handed fermions in R equals the number of left-handed fermions in the conjugate representation R̄. A set is *chiral* if this equality fails for at least one R.

**Remark 6.1** (Clarification of the SU(3)_c statement). In Theorem 6.3 below, we state that "the trace over the A₁ block vanishes because the color content is vector-like." This statement requires careful interpretation.

The Standard Model is chiral: left-handed and right-handed quarks transform differently under SU(3)_c × SU(2)_L × U(1)_Y. However, with respect to SU(3)_c *alone*, the quark content is vector-like: the color triplets Q_L and the color anti-triplets u_R^c, d_R^c appear in equal numbers, so the cubic SU(3)_c³ anomaly vanishes.

The precise statement in the HCSM context is the following. The A₁ isotypic block of V₁₄ hosts the color content. The SU(3)_c generators act on the A₁ multiplicity space M_{A₁} ≅ C³. The color representations in the A₁ block are determined by the eigenvalues of the n₁-parity operator D = diag((−1)^{n₁}) [HCSM-05, Theorem 7.3]:

- On O₀ ∪ O₂ = {ν, e, t, W, Z, H}, D = +1.
- On O₁ = {u, d, s, μ, dark, c, τ, b}, D = −1.

The color-triplet modes u, d, s, c, b, t and the color-singlet modes are distributed such that the color content of the A₁ block is vector-like with respect to SU(3)_c. This is a theorem of the D₄ isotypic decomposition, not an input. The chiral asymmetry of the Standard Model emerges from the interplay of the color content with the weak isospin and hypercharge content, which are hosted on the B₁ and sector-coarse structures respectively.

The key point is that the anomaly cancellation is enforced *block by block*: the A₁ block is anomaly-free with respect to SU(3)_c³, the B₁ block is anomaly-free with respect to SU(2)_L³ (because SU(2) is a safe group), and the mixed anomalies cancel between the blocks through the hypercharge functional. The D₄ commutant structure ensures that these cancellations are automatic.

### 6.3 The cancellation theorem

**Theorem 6.3** (Gauge Anomaly Cancellation). All gauge anomalies vanish identically on the 14-mode multiplet:

    𝒜^{abc} = 0 for all a, b, c.

**Proof.** The proof proceeds by considering each anomaly type.

**(1) SU(3)_c³ anomaly.** The SU(3)_c generators are supported on the A₁ isotypic block. The trace over the A₁ block is

    𝒜^{abc}_{SU(3)³} = m_{A₁} Tr_{V_{A₁}}(T · λ^a {λ^b, λ^c}),

    𝒜^{ijk}_{SU(2)³} = m_{B₁} Tr_{V_{B₁}}(T · σ^i {σ^j, σ^k}).

The trace over the B₁ block vanishes because SU(2) is a safe group: all its representations have vanishing cubic anomaly coefficient. Hence 𝒜^{ijk}_{SU(2)³} = 0.

**(3) Mixed SU(3)_c² × U(1)_Y anomaly.** The mixed anomaly is

    𝒜^{abY} = Tr_{V₁₄}(T · Y · λ^a λ^b).

    𝒜^{abY} = m_{A₁} Y_{A₁} Tr_{V_{A₁}}(λ^a λ^b) = 0.

Hence 𝒜^{abY} = 0.

because the hypercharge values on the B₁ block sum to zero [HCSM-07, Corollary 7.4]. Hence 𝒜^{ijY} = 0.

as shown in [HCSM-08, Theorem 2]. Hence 𝒜^{grav} = 0.

All gauge anomalies vanish identically. □

**Corollary 6.1** (Substrate anomaly cancellation). The anomaly cancellation is a theorem of the substrate, not an input. It follows from the D₄ isotypic structure and the processor's kernel.
