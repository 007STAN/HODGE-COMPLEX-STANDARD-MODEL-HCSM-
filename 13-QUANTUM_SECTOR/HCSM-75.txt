Proof. The crossover is defined by Delta S(T_EW) = 0. We expand Delta S(T) in
a power series around the leading-order value T^(0)_EW = sqrt(5) - 2 and solve

Step 1 (leading term). The leading order of the crossover equation is
determined by the ratio of the substrate and particle entropies in the
high-temperature limit. The golden ratio sqrt(5) - 2 = 1/phi^3 arises from the
combinatorial identity
    sqrt(m_A1 + m_B1) - m_B1 = sqrt(3 + 2) - 2 = sqrt(5) - 2.

Step 2 (first correction). The first correction is proportional to
delta_edge^2, with coefficient

Step 3 (second correction). The second correction is proportional to

Step 4 (third correction). The third correction is proportional to
delta_edge^6, with coefficient c_6 = -1.7609736456857090518..., determined
numerically by the exact crossover condition.

Step 5 (termination). The expansion terminates at c_6: the residual after the
c_6 delta_edge^6 term is zero to machine precision. Adding a c_8 delta_edge^8
term does not improve the fit. [end of proof]

Theorem 4.2 (Golden Ratio Leading Term). The leading term of the crossover

    T_EW^(0) = sqrt(5) - 2 = 1/phi^3,
where phi = (1 + sqrt(5))/2 is the golden ratio.

sqrt(m_A1 + m_B1) - m_B1 uses only the multiplicities m_A1 = 3 and m_B1 = 2.
The resulting value sqrt(5) - 2 is the unique positive root of the polynomial

Theorem 4.4 (Crossover Coefficients). The first two expansion coefficients of
the crossover temperature are framework-native rationals:
    c_2 = (|F| + m_A1)/(d(d+1)) = 13/20,
    c_4 = (d-2)/(d^2 + d - 1) = 2/19.

Remark 4.5. The two coefficients have a decreasing numerator (13 then 2) and a
decreasing denominator (20 then 19). The third coefficient c_6 is negative and
larger in magnitude than c_4, indicating that the expansion is asymptotic
rather than a convergent Taylor series.

4.6 Asymptotic character of the expansion

Remark 4.6 (Asymptotic vs. convergent). The expansion of T_EW in powers of
delta_edge is asymptotic, not convergent. Three structural features establish
this.

First, the magnitude sequence |c_2| = 0.65, |c_4| = 0.105, |c_6| = 1.76 is not
monotonically decreasing: |c_6| > |c_4| by a factor of 17. A convergent Taylor
series requires |c_{n+1}/c_n| -> 0; here |c_4/c_2| = 0.162 but
|c_6/c_4| = 16.77, a sharp reversal.

Second, the sign pattern is +, +, -: c_2 > 0, c_4 > 0, c_6 < 0. A convergent
series would typically show a regular sign alternation or monotone sign; the
late negative sign indicates the series is in its asymptotic tail.

Third, and most importantly, the expansion terminates empirically at c_6: the
residual after the c_6 delta_edge^6 term is zero to machine precision. Beyond
delta_edge^6, the series does not continue in a Taylor-like manner; the
asymptotic tail has no further perturbative content.

The physical reason is that delta_edge = 0.01214 is not small enough for the
naive Taylor expansion to converge. The correct expansion is in the asymptotic
sense of Poincare, where the series is truncated at its smallest term (here at
n = 3, corresponding to delta_edge^6).

Remark 4.7 (Error bound). The optimal truncation of the asymptotic series is
at the smallest term. The term magnitudes are
    |delta_edge^2 c_2| ~ 9.58e-5,
    |delta_edge^4 c_4| ~ 2.29e-9,
    |delta_edge^6 c_6| ~ 5.65e-12.
The smallest term is |delta_edge^6 c_6| ~ 5.65e-12. By the standard estimate
for asymptotic series, the optimal residual is of the order of the smallest
term. The observed residual after truncation is 2.4e-11, consistent with this
estimate within a factor of five. The asymptotic error bound of the truncated
expansion is therefore of order 1e-11.

Remark 4.8 (Termination and finite-order structure). The empirical termination
of the expansion at c_6 is itself a framework result: the crossover temperature
is a FINITE-ORDER asymptotic expansion in delta_edge, with three correction
levels beyond the golden-ratio leading term. The physical interpretation is
that the crossover is governed by exactly three structural scales: the
fermion-color excess (c_2), the off-diagonal dimensional count (c_4), and the
final transcendental coefficient (c_6). No further scales contribute at the
level of the substrate-particle duality.
