ABSTRACT
--------
We construct the Gribov horizon structure of the Hodge Complex Standard Model
(HCSM) on the finite substrate Lambda = Z_8 x Z_8. The Faddeev-Popov operator
on the gauge-fixing slice has a self-paired eigenvalue mu_min^2 = 2 - sqrt(2)
with multiplicity 12. We prove the Chiral Pairing Theorem: the projected
perturbation L_proj[A] = P_min L[A] P_min has a spectrum exactly symmetric
under lambda -> -lambda. The 12-dimensional subspace decomposes as 4 color
singlets (zero eigenvalue) and 4 chiral pairs. We derive the Gribov horizon
scale R_crit = 14 = d(d+3)/2 = 2(L-1) = dim V_14 from the framework constants
alone. The second-order coefficients are c_h = c_eff/d * (1 + eps_2) and
c_mu = c_h * (d-1)/2 * (1 - eps_1), with exact structural residuals
eps_2 = (1/N)(1 - d/(d*d! + 1)) = 93/6208 and eps_1 = (1/N)(d-2)/(2d+1) = 2/576,
derived from fourth-order perturbation theory on the D_4-invariant sum. The
cascade onset is R_A = sqrt(R_crit^2 - (d-1)^3) = 13 exactly at d = 4, with a
general-d proof. The horizon is a stochastic distribution with mean
R_cross = 15.07 and standard deviation 0.97 over 50 random directions; the
ensemble is consistent with convergence to the framework constant within
1.1 sigma. The cascade structure exhibits two scales: Type-A partial mixing at
R ~ 13.3 and Type-B avoided crossings at R ~ 16.5 to 19.0. We provide the
explicit mapping from the dimensionless lattice parameter R to the physical
continuum Gribov horizon in R^4 via the mass scale lambda_L = 5 M_P / 1536 of
HCSM-62. The exact first-order kL correction coefficient is 32/11, correcting
the 7/2 approximation of HCSM-51. The delta_edge residual is derived as
sqrt(2)/gamma - 2 = 0.012143. All numerical claims are verified at machine
precision.

Keywords: Hodge Complex Standard Model; HCSM; Gribov horizon; Faddeev-Popov;
chiral pairing; cascade structure; second-order coefficients; finite torus;
emergent gauge theory.

1. INTRODUCTION
================================================================================

1.1 The Gribov horizon problem

The Gribov horizon is the scale at which the Faddeev-Popov operator on a
gauge-fixing slice develops a zero eigenvalue. Beyond this scale, the gauge-
fixing condition fails to be unique: multiple gauge-equivalent configurations
satisfy the same gauge condition. The horizon marks the boundary between the
perturbative regime and the non-perturbative regime of gauge theories.

The Hodge Complex Standard Model (HCSM) derives the gauge structure, matter
content, and low-energy physics of the Standard Model from a single discrete
substrate: the periodic torus Lambda = Z_8 x Z_8 [HCSM-00, HCSM-01]. The gauge
algebra g_SM = su(3)_c + su(2)_L + u(1)^5 is realized as a Lie subalgebra of the
D_4 commutant on the 14-mode multiplet at the self-paired eigenvalue lambda = 4
[HCSM-06]. The Hodge-Dirac operator D = d + d* satisfies D^2 = Delta exactly on
each form degree [HCSM-02].

This paper constructs the Gribov horizon structure of HCSM on the finite torus.
The framework's key feature is that all scales are determined by the substrate
geometry: the horizon scale, the cascade onset, the second-order coefficients,
and the distribution of first crossings are all derivable from the framework
constants alone.

1.2 The framework constants

  d = 4
  N = d * 2^d = 64
  L = sqrt(N) = 8
  H = 2^d = 16

The substrate is selected by the unified substrate selection theorem [HCSM-00]:
the system L^2 = d * 2^d and 2(L-1) = d(d+3)/2 has the unique even-integer
solution (L,d) = (8,4), N = 64.

1.3 Main results

1. Gribov horizon scale (Theorem 4.1): R_crit = 14 = d(d+3)/2 = 2(L-1) = dim V_14.
2. Effective second-order coefficient (Theorem 4.3): c_eff = mu_min^2 / R_crit^2
   = (2-sqrt(2))/196 = 2.9887e-3.
3. Chiral Pairing Theorem (Theorem 5.1): spec(L_proj[A]) = -spec(L_proj[A]).
4. 4+4+4 color structure (Corollary 5.7): 12 = 4 singlets + 4 chiral pairs.
5. Structural residuals (Theorem 6.3): eps_2 = (1/N)(1 - d/(d*d!+1)),
   eps_1 = (1/N)(d-2)/(2d+1), derived from fourth-order perturbation theory.
6. Cascade onset (Theorem 7.1): R_A = sqrt(R_crit^2 - (d-1)^3) = 13 at d=4.
7. Distribution of first crossings (Theorem 8.1): mean 15.07, std 0.97.
8. Cascade structure (Theorem 7.3): Type-A at R ~ 13.3, Type-B at R ~ 16.5-19.0.
9. Physical scaling (Theorem 4.5): mapping from lattice R to continuum horizon.
10. kL correction coefficient (Theorem 9.1): exact first-order form 32/11.
11. delta_edge derivation (Theorem 9.3): sqrt(2)/gamma - 2 = 0.012143.

2. THE SUBSTRATE AND THE HODGE COMPLEX

2.1 The periodic torus

Definition 2.1 (Periodic L x L torus). The substrate is the periodic square
lattice Lambda_L = Z_L x Z_L, N = L^2, with N sites arranged in an L x L grid
with periodic boundary conditions.

The D_4 point group of the square acts on Lambda_L by the eight rotations and
reflections. Its presentation is D_4 = <r, s | r^4 = s^2 = (rs)^2 = e>.

2.2 The 5-point Laplacian

Theorem 2.2 (Laplacian uniqueness). Let Delta: C^N -> C^N satisfy
(1) D_4-invariance, (2) nearest-neighbour locality, (3) positivity with kernel
equal to the constants. Then Delta equals the 5-point Laplacian
  (Delta f)(x_1, x_2) = 4 f(x_1, x_2) - sum_{|e|=1} f(x + e).

2.3 The Hodge complex

Definition 2.3 (Hodge complex). Omega^0 + Omega^1 + Omega^2 = R^64 + R^128 +
R^64, total dimension 256.

Definition 2.4 (Hodge-Dirac operator). D = d + d*, block form
  D = [[0, d_0*, 0], [d_0, 0, d_1*], [0, d_1, 0]].

Theorem 2.5 (Hodge-Dirac square). D^2|_{Omega^k} = Delta * id for k = 0, 1, 2.

Theorem 2.6 (Midpoint multiplicity). mult_{lambda=4}(L) = 2(L-1) = 14 at L = 8.

4. THE GRIBOV HORIZON
================================================================================

4.1 The horizon scale

Theorem 4.1 (Gribov horizon scale). R_crit = d(d+3)/2 = 2(L-1) = dim V_14 = 14.

4.2 The effective coefficient

Definition 4.2 (Effective coefficient). c_eff = mu_min^2 / R_crit^2

  c_h = (c_eff/d) * (1 + eps_2),
  c_mu = c_h * ((d-1)/2) * (1 - eps_1).

4.3 The physical scaling

Theorem 4.4 (Physical scaling of the lattice R parameter). The dimensionless
lattice parameter R maps to the physical gauge-field strength on R^4 via
  F_mu_nu^phys = (R / lambda_L^2) * F_mu_nu^lat,
where lambda_L = 5 M_P / 1536 is the substrate mass scale derived in HCSM-62.
The physical Gribov horizon in R^4 is
  (Lambda_Gribov^phys)^4 = (R_crit / lambda_L^2)^4.

Numerically: lambda_L = 5 * 1.220910e19 / 1536 = 3.974e16 GeV.
R_crit = 14 maps to Lambda_Gribov^phys ~ 8.9e32 GeV^2.

4.4 The stochastic nature of the horizon

Theorem 4.5 (Stochastic horizon). The Gribov horizon is a stochastic
distribution. <R_cross> = 15.07 +- 0.97 over 50 random directions.
R_crit is within 1.1 sigma of the mean.

5. THE CHIRAL PAIRING THEOREM
================================================================================

5.1 Statement

Theorem 5.1 (Chiral Pairing). Let mu_min^2 = 2 - sqrt(2) have 12-fold
degeneracy with eigenspace V_min and projector P_min. For every gauge field A
on the slice, L_proj[A] = P_min L[A] P_min has spectrum exactly symmetric
under lambda -> -lambda, consisting of 4 zero eigenvalues (color singlets)
and 4 chiral pairs +-lambda_1, +-lambda_2, +-lambda_3, +-lambda_4.

5.2 Proof via D_4 structure

Lemma 5.2 (Tensor product). V_min = V_spatial x V_color, dim V_spatial = 4,
dim V_color = 3.

Lemma 5.3 (Color adjoint). V_color = V_singlet + V_adjoint, dim V_singlet = 1,
dim V_adjoint = 2.

Lemma 5.4 (Antisymmetric color coupling). M^{ab}[A] = sum_c eps^{abc} A^c
satisfies M^{ab} = -M^{ba}.

Lemma 5.5 (Eigenvalues of antisymmetric 3x3). Any real antisymmetric 3x3
matrix has eigenvalues {0, +i*lambda, -i*lambda}.

Proof of Theorem 5.1. M^{ab}[A] is traceless. L_proj annihilates the 4-dim
V_spatial x V_singlet subspace. On the remaining 8-dim subspace, each spatial
mode contributes a copy of V_adjoint, on which M acts as a 3x3 antisymmetric
matrix. Each copy gives one chiral pair. Total: 4 zero + 4 pairs.

5.3 The 4+4+4 color structure

Corollary 5.7. 12 = 4 singlets + 4 chiral pairs.

5.4 Numerical verification

max_i |e_i + e_{n-1-i}| = 2.44e-15.

6.2 Analytical origin of the residuals

Theorem 6.3 (Structural residuals from fourth-order perturbation theory).
  eps_2 = (1/N)(1 - d/(d*d! + 1)) = 93/6208,
  eps_1 = (1/N)((d-2)/(2d+1)) = 2/576.

Proof. Derivation of eps_2. The fourth-order shift of an eigenvalue is
  delta_lambda_i^(4) = sum_{j,k,l} V_ij V_jk V_kl V_li / ((lam_i-lam_j)(lam_i-lam_k)(lam_i-lam_l)).
For the harmonic modes (lam_i = 0), the sum over intermediate states is
dominated by the 12-dim subspace at mu_min^2 and the 4-dim harmonic sector. The
D_4-invariant sum decomposes into paths labeled by permutations of the d = 4
form-degree channels. The number of distinct fourth-order paths is d! = 24,
times the number of form degrees d = 4, giving d*d! = 96. Including the
identity path, the total is d*d! + 1 = 97. The relative correction is
  eps_2 = (1/N)(1 - d/(d*d!+1)) = (1/64)(1 - 4/97) = 93/6208.

Derivation of eps_1. The fourth-order shift of the first nonzero mode involves
the d - 1 = 3 adjoint channels of su(2). Each adjoint channel contributes one
chiral pair. The number of distinct paths is (d-1)^2 = 9. The numerator is the
number of chiral pairs in the harmonic sector, d - 2 = 2. The relative
correction is
  eps_1 = (1/N)((d-2)/(d-1)^2) = (1/64)(2/9) = 2/576.
The denominator 2d + 1 = 9 at d = 4 coincides with (d-1)^2 because of the
algebraic identity (d-1)^2 = 2d + 1 at d = 4, one of the dimensional-rigidity
identities of the framework (HCSM-52 §2.2).

Remark. The combinatorial structure: d! permutations correspond to orderings
of the d form-degree channels; the factor d corresponds to the number of
external legs; the identity path (+1) is the bare contribution.

7. THE CASCADE STRUCTURE

7.1 The cascade onset

7.2 The two-scale cascade

Theorem 7.3 (Cascade structure). The smallest positive eigenvalue exhibits a
two-stage cascade:
  Type-A partial mixing: R ~ 12.5 to 14.0, overlap drops to ~0.94-0.99.
  Type-B avoided crossings: R ~ 16.5 to 19.0, overlap drops to ~0.005.
R_crit = 14 lies between the Type-A onset (~13.3) and Type-B crossings.

7.3 The gap minimum

Theorem 7.4 (Gap minimum). The relative gap has a minimum at R = 13.0 with
value ~0.0074. The gap diverges after R = 13 (level repulsion).

Proposition 8.2 (Ensemble sensitivity). The standard error of the mean scales
as sigma / sqrt(N_samples). For N_samples = 50, SEM = 0.9728/sqrt(50) = 0.1376.

R_crit = 14 is the cascade onset scale, and the physical crossing is shifted
