ABSTRACT
================================================================================

We construct the quantum field theory of the Hodge Complex Standard Model
directly on the finite substrate Lambda = Z_8 x Z_8. The Hodge complex of
differential forms, equipped with the Hodge inner product, is a
finite-dimensional real Hilbert space of dimension 256. The Hodge-Dirac
operator D = d + d* is a self-adjoint operator on this Hilbert space and
serves as the free Hamiltonian. The full spectrum of D is computed explicitly.
The Z_2 grading (-1)^F of the Hodge complex determines the statistics of the
fields: even-degree forms are bosonic, odd-degree forms are fermionic. The
Fock space is the tensor product of the symmetric algebra on the bosonic
single-particle space and the exterior algebra on the fermionic single-
particle space. The propagator is the inverse of the Fourier symbol of D,
reducing to the Dirac propagator in the continuum limit. The path integral
measure is well-defined and unique on the finite lattice. Ward identities
follow from the Z_2 symmetry. No intermediate classical field theory is used,
and no second-quantization postulate is added. The quantum field theory is
the Hodge complex itself.

Keywords: Hodge Complex Standard Model; HCSM; quantum field theory; direct
quantization; Hodge complex; Hodge-Dirac operator; Fock space; path integral;
statistics; Z_2 grading.

2. THE HODGE COMPLEX AS A HILBERT SPACE
================================================================================

2.1 The substrate and the Hodge complex

Lambda = Z_8 x Z_8, N = L^2 = 64 sites.
Omega^0 = R^{64}, Omega^1 = R^{128}, Omega^2 = R^{64}, total 256.

2.2 The Hodge inner product

Definition 2.1 (Hodge inner product): <alpha, beta> = int_Lambda alpha ^ star
beta.

Proposition 2.1 (Symmetry and positivity): The Hodge inner product is
symmetric and positive-definite on each form degree.

Proof: For real forms alpha, beta, symmetry follows from alpha ^ star beta =
beta ^ star alpha. Positive-definiteness follows from alpha ^ star alpha >= 0
with equality iff alpha = 0.

2.3 The Hilbert space

Theorem 2.1 (Hodge complex as Hilbert space):
The Hodge complex Omega^bullet(Lambda), equipped with the Hodge inner
product, is a real Hilbert space H_256 = (Omega^bullet(Lambda), <.,.>), of
dimension 256.

Proof: By Proposition 2.1, the Hodge inner product is positive-definite on
each form degree. The direct sum inherits the inner product. Every finite-
dimensional inner product space is a Hilbert space. Dimension = 64 + 128 +
64 = 256.

Remark: H_256 is the single-particle Hilbert space of the quantum field
theory.

3. THE HODGE-DIRAC OPERATOR AS HAMILTONIAN
================================================================================

3.1 Self-adjointness

Theorem 3.1 (Self-adjointness): D = d + d* is self-adjoint on H_256.

Proof: d* is the adjoint of d with respect to the Hodge inner product.
Therefore D^dagger = (d + d*)^dagger = d* + d = D.

3.2 Hodge-Dirac square

Theorem 3.2 (Hodge-Dirac square): D^2|_{Omega^k} = Delta * id_{Omega^k} on
each form degree k = 0, 1, 2.

Proof: On Omega^0, D^2 = d_0* d_0 = Delta. On Omega^1, D^2 = d_0 d_0* +
d_1* d_1 = Delta. On Omega^2, D^2 = d_1 d_1* = Delta. Cross terms vanish by
nilpotency d_1 d_0 = 0.

3.3 Spectrum of D

Theorem 3.3 (Spectrum of D): Eigenvalues of D on H_256:
1. mu = 0, with multiplicity 4;
2. mu = +/- sqrt(lambda), each with multiplicity 2 m_lambda, for each nonzero
   eigenvalue lambda of the scalar Laplacian with multiplicity m_lambda.

Proof: D^2 = Delta on each form degree. Delta acts identically on Omega^0,
Omega^1, Omega^2, so each eigenvalue lambda appears with multiplicity 4
m_lambda. Eigenvalues +/- sqrt(lambda) each carry half. For lambda = 0,
ker D has dimension 4.

5. GRADED STRUCTURE AND STATISTICS
================================================================================

5.1 The Z_2 grading

Definition 5.1: (-1)^F = +1 on Omega^0 + Omega^2, -1 on Omega^1.

Theorem 5.1 (Anticommutation): D (-1)^F = -(-1)^F D.

Proof: D shifts form degree by +/-1. Form parity assigns +1 to even degrees,
-1 to odd degrees. Composition D(-1)^F acts on even-degree form as D, on
odd-degree as -D. (-1)^F D differs by overall sign.

5.2 Graded decomposition

H_+ = Omega^0 + Omega^2, H_- = Omega^1.
dim_R H_+ = 128, dim_R H_- = 128.
H_256 = H_+ + H_-.

5.3 Graded commutator

Definition 5.2: [A, B}_gr = AB - (-1)^{|A||B|} BA.

5.4 Statistics assignment

Theorem 5.4 (Statistics from grading): Z_2 grading determines statistics.
Fields in H_+ satisfy commutation relations (bosonic), fields in H_- satisfy
anticommutation relations (fermionic).

Proof: For Z_2 symmetry implemented by unitary U_F with U_F D U_F^{-1} = D,
field operators transform as U_F psi(x) U_F^{-1} = (-1)^{|x|} psi(x).
Graded commutator [psi(x), psi(y)}_gr consistent. Bosonic sector:
[psi(x), psi(y)] = 0, [psi(x), psi^dagger(y)] = delta(x-y). Fermionic:
{psi(x), psi(y)} = 0, {psi(x), psi^dagger(y)} = delta(x-y).

6. FIELD OPERATORS AND CANONICAL QUANTIZATION
================================================================================

6.1 Mode expansion

{u_k} orthonormal eigenbasis of D: D u_k = omega_k u_k, omega_k in
{0, +/- sqrt(lambda)}.

psi(x) = sum_{omega_k > 0} [a_k u_k(x) + a_k^dagger u_bar_k(x)] + zero modes.

6.2 Canonical (anti)commutation relations

Bosonic sector (u_k in H_+):
[a_k, a_l^dagger] = delta_kl, [a_k, a_l] = 0.

Fermionic sector (u_k in H_-):
{a_k, a_l^dagger} = delta_kl, {a_k, a_l} = 0.

Theorem 6.1 (Uniqueness of quantization): The canonical (anti)commutation
relations are uniquely determined by the Z_2 grading of H_256.

Proof: By Theorem 5.4, Z_2 grading fixes statistics. Standard canonical
quantization per sector gives stated (anti)commutators. No other assignment
compatible with grading.

dim Lambda(H_-) = sum_{k=0}^{128} C(128, k) = 2^128.

Theorem 7.1 (Fock space dimension): Fermionic sector has dimension 2^128.
Total Fock space infinite due to bosonic sector.

Proof: Exterior algebra over d-dim vector space has dim 2^d. Symmetric algebra
infinite dimension.

7.3 Vacuum and one-particle states

Vacuum: |0> with a_k |0> = 0 for all k.
One-particle: |k> = a_k^dagger |0>.

8. HAMILTONIAN AND VACUUM ENERGY
================================================================================

8.1 Free Hamiltonian

H = sum_{omega_k > 0} omega_k (a_k^dagger a_k + a_bar_k^dagger a_bar_k) + E_0.

8.2 Vacuum energy

Bosonic: +1/2 omega_k.
Fermionic: -1/2 omega_k.
Total: E_0 = (1/2) sum omega_k (n_k^boson - n_k^fermion).

8.3 Explicit sum

sum_{omega_k > 0} omega_k = 4 sqrt(2-sqrt(2)) + 4 sqrt(2) + 4 sqrt(4-2sqrt(2))
  + 4 sqrt(2+sqrt(2)) + 8 sqrt(4-sqrt(2)) + 14*2 + 4 sqrt(4+2sqrt(2))
  + 4 sqrt(6-sqrt(2)) + 8 sqrt(4+sqrt(2)) + 4 sqrt(6) + 4 sqrt(6+sqrt(2))
  + 2 sqrt(2).

Finite constant. Bosonic/fermionic split from Z_2 grading.

9. PROPAGATOR
================================================================================

9.1 Feynman propagator

G(x, y) = <0| T psi(x) psi_bar(y) |0>.

9.2 Mode expansion

G(x, y) = sum_k u_k(x) u_bar_k(y) / (omega_k - i eps).

9.3 Momentum space

Theorem 9.1 (Propagator in momentum space):
G(p) = 1 / (M(p) - i eps), where M(p) is the Fourier symbol of D:
M(p) = p_1 A_1 + p_2 A_2 + O(p^2).

Continuum limit a -> 0:
G(p) -> 1 / (i gamma^mu p_mu - i eps) = i gamma^mu p_mu / (p^2 + i eps).

Proof: Propagator is Green's function of D - i eps. In momentum space, D acts
as multiplication by M(p). Continuum limit from HCSM-65.

10. PATH INTEGRAL ON THE FINITE LATTICE
================================================================================

10.1 Partition function

Z = int Dpsi exp(i S[psi]), S = HCSM action (DNLS matter + Hodge-Dirac
kinetic).

10.2 Measure

Bosonic: Dpsi_+ = prod_x d psi_+(x).
Fermionic: Dpsi_- = prod_x d psi_-(x) (Berezin).
Total: Dpsi = Dpsi_+ Dpsi_-.

Theorem 10.1 (Path integral measure): HCSM path integral measure well-defined
and unique on finite lattice.

Proof: Field space finite-dimensional. Bosonic = 128 x 64 = 8192 real DOF.
Fermionic = 8192 Grassmann generators. Bosonic measure = Lebesgue on R^{8192}.
Fermionic measure = Berezin. Product well-defined. No infinite-dim
regularization.

10.3 Gaussian evaluation

Free theory: Z = det(D - i eps)^{-1/2} * det(D - i eps)^{+1/2} = 1
(bosonic/fermionic cancellation). Interacting theory: finite-dimensional
integral, computable numerically.

11. WARD IDENTITIES
================================================================================

11.1 Z_2 current

Conserved current j_F^mu with d_mu j_F^mu = 0.

11.2 Ward identity

Theorem 11.1 (Ward identity):
d_mu <j_F^mu(x) O(y)> = delta(x - y) <delta O(y)>.

Proof: Standard Ward identity from Z_2 symmetry.

11.3 Consequences

1. Bosonic and fermionic correlators decouple (superselection).
2. Green's functions of odd-degree fields vanish unless even number of odd
   fields inserted.
3. Fock space decomposes into Z_2 superselection sectors.
