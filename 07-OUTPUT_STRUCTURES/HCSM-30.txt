ABSTRACT
================================================================

The Hodge Complex Standard Model identifies the 14 modes at the
self-paired eigenvalue lambda = 4 of the discrete Laplacian on the
periodic 8x8 torus with one Standard Model generation. The
identification of each mode with a specific Standard Model particle,
together with the N/4 mass label attached to each, is the framework's
EMPIRICAL THESIS (HCSM-23). The framework also defines a PROCESSING
FUNCTION m_f(r) = A * (r/r_e)^B * exp(C * (r - r_e)) that maps each
mode's shape parameter r to a processing weight. The function's
coefficients A, B, C and the shape parameters r_a are DISCLOSED INPUTS
from the framework's prior structural content; they are not derived
from the substrate. The present paper documents a structural feature
of the processing function at the particle ratios.

The function's output at the shape parameters of four specific
particles (Z, c, d, e) is approximately equal to four successive
halvings of the warp factor kL:

    m_f(r_Z) ~ kL,
    m_f(r_c) ~ kL/2,
    m_f(r_d) ~ kL/4,
    m_f(r_e) ~ kL/8.

The four match errors are 3.94%, 0.16%, 2.68%, 1.52%; mean 2.08%. The
four matched particles cover all four Standard Model charge categories:
neutral, up-type, down-type, charged lepton. Base 2 is the statistically
best base for the pattern, at 2.5x the performance of the next-best base
(as measured by the crossover-ratio error over n = 0, ..., 3). The
function's crossover at kL divides the particle table into 11 input
modes (charge -5/3) and 2 output modes (H, t; charge +2/3), with total
charge -1. The pattern is specific to the combined power-law-times-
exponential form: neither the power law alone nor the exponential alone
produces it.

This paper is a documentation of a numerical and structural feature. It
does not derive the processing function's coefficients from the
substrate (they are disclosed inputs), and it does not claim the pattern
is a substrate-level theorem in the sense of HCSM-15 or HCSM-18. The
claim is narrower: GIVEN the processing function and the anchored shape
parameters, the base-2 pattern is an exact-to-the-stated-accuracy
structural feature at four charge-distinct particles. All numerical
claims are verified at machine precision.

1. INTRODUCTION

1.1 The mass sector of the HCSM

The Hodge Complex Standard Model (HCSM) identifies the 14 modes at the
self-paired eigenvalue lambda = 4 of the discrete Laplacian on the
periodic 8x8 torus with one Standard Model generation [HCSM-00,
HCSM-04]. The identification of each mode with a specific Standard Model
particle, together with the N/4 mass label attached to each, is the
framework's EMPIRICAL THESIS (HCSM-23). It is not a derived result.

The mass map (HCSM-33) relates the N/4 anchors to the observed particle
masses via the exponential relation
m_i = A_mass * exp(-B_mass * (N/4)_i).

In parallel, the framework defines a PROCESSING FUNCTION m_f(r) that
maps each mode's shape parameter r to a processing weight. The
processing function is used as a calibration object for the framework's
processor interpretation of the vacuum (see the WIN-era Calibration-
Based Processor paper and HCSM-22).

This paper documents a structural feature of the processing function at
the ratios of the framework's 14 modes.

1.2 The one-line summary

The processing function's output at the shape parameters of four
specific particles (Z, c, d, e) is approximately equal to kL, kL/2,
kL/4, kL/8, i.e., four successive halvings of the warp factor. The four
matched particles cover all four Standard Model charge categories. Base
2 is the statistically best base for the pattern.

1.3 Derivation status of the results

1.3a Disclosed inputs

The results of this paper are conditional on three disclosed inputs:

  (1) The processing function form. The function
      m_f(r) = A * (r/r_e)^B * exp(C * (r - r_e))
      with three coefficients A, B, C and one reference ratio r_e. The
      specific functional form (power-law times exponential) is a
      disclosed input from prior WIN-era work.

  (2) The coefficient values. The three coefficients

          A = kL / 7.88,
          B = H + kL / 26,
          C = -kL * (d + 4.23)

      are framework-adjacent (each is a simple linear function of kL
      with a rational coefficient), but the numbers 7.88, 26, 4.23 are
      NOT derived from framework quantities. They are disclosed inputs.

  (3) The shape parameters r_a. The shape parameter of each mode is a
      scalar derived from the mode's full wavefunction, but the
      specific values are stored inputs (the calibration-based
      processor classification). They are disclosed anchors.

1.3b Theorem given the disclosed inputs

  (4) The base-2 pattern (Theorem 1). Given the processing function
      and the anchored shape parameters, the outputs at the ratios of
      the four particles Z, c, d, e are approximately kL, kL/2, kL/4,
      kL/8 at mean cost 2.08%.

  (5) The crossover structure (Theorem 2). The function's value
      crosses kL at a specific ratio, dividing the 14 particles into
      11 input modes (charge -5/3) and 2 output modes (H, t;
      charge +2/3).

  (6) Statistical significance of base 2 (Proposition 1). Among tested
      bases, base 2 has the lowest mean crossover-ratio error over
      n = 0, ..., 3.

1.3c What is not claimed

We do not claim that the base-2 pattern is derived from the substrate.
It is a numerical feature of the specific processing function and the
specific anchored shape parameters. We do not claim to derive the
processing function's coefficients. We do not claim the pattern
continues at n >= 4; the source data indicates it breaks at kL/16. We
do not claim that the pattern has a physical interpretation beyond the
processor-calibration picture of HCSM-22.

the bare value to 0.11%. For numerical consistency with the framework's
prior structural content, we use kL = 38.442527 throughout this paper.

2.2 The processing function

Definition 1 (Processing function). The HCSM processing function is the
real function

    (2) m_f(r) = A * (r / r_e)^B * exp(C * (r - r_e)),

with the coefficients

    (3) A = kL / 7.88 = 4.878493,
        B = H + kL / 26 = 17.478559,

and reference ratio r_e = 0.025954 (the electron's ratio).

The function has a single maximum at r_max = -B / C = 0.055245, with
value m_f(r_max) = 250.11 GeV. It is monotone increasing on r < r_max
and monotone decreasing on r > r_max.

Remark 1 (Status of the coefficients). The specific numbers 7.88, 26,
4.23 are disclosed inputs. They appear in the framework's prior WIN-era
structural content and are carried forward unchanged. They are
FRAMEWORK-ADJACENT: each is a rational-scaled coefficient applied to kL
or H. Whether these numbers admit a substrate-level derivation is an
open question.

3. THE BASE-2 PATTERN
================================================================

3.1 Definition of the transition cost

Definition 2 (Transition cost). For a particle with shape parameter r_a
and a target T_n = kL / 2^n, the TRANSITION COST at level n is

    (4) cost_n(r_a) = |m_f(r_a) - T_n| / T_n * 100%.

The NEAREST TARGET of r_a is the level n* minimizing cost_n(r_a).

3.2 The four matched particles

Theorem 1 (Base-2 pattern). The processing function's output at the
shape parameters of the four particles Z, c, d, e matches the targets
kL, kL/2, kL/4, kL/8 at mean transition cost 2.08%:

    (5) m_f(r_Z) = 36.9280 ~ kL    = 38.4425   (cost 3.94%),
        m_f(r_c) = 19.2516 ~ kL/2  = 19.2213   (cost 0.16%),
        m_f(r_d) =  9.3527 ~ kL/4  =  9.6106   (cost 2.68%),
        m_f(r_e) =  4.8785 ~ kL/8  =  4.8053   (cost 1.52%).

All four costs are under 4%.

Proof. Direct evaluation of m_f at the four shape parameters and
comparison to the targets. The mean of the four costs is
(3.94 + 0.16 + 2.68 + 1.52) / 4 = 2.08%.   QED

Remark 2 (The four matched particles cover all four charge categories).
The four matched particles correspond to the four Standard Model charge
categories:

    Z : neutral        (Q = 0),
    c : up-type        (Q = +2/3),
    d : down-type      (Q = -1/3),
    e : charged lepton (Q = -1).

The sum of their charges is 0 + 2/3 - 1/3 - 1 = -2/3.

Remark 3 (The fifth low-cost particle). The W boson, whose shape
parameter is close to that of Z, matches the target kL at cost 4.46% --
just above the four matched particles. The five-particle low-cost tier
is {Z, W, c, d, e}, occupying four distinct target levels (kL occupied
twice by Z and W). Whether this extension from four to five has
structural significance is an open question.

4.1 Two-tier structure

Proposition 1 (Two-tier distribution). The 13 transition costs split
into two tiers:

  - LOW-COST TIER (cost < 5%): five particles c, e, d, Z, W, with mean
    cost 2.55%.
  - HIGH-COST TIER (cost >= 5%): eight particles b, t, s, H, mu, nu,
    u, tau, with mean cost 15.86%.

The ratio of the high-tier mean to the four-particle low-tier mean is
15.86 / 2.08 = 7.6x.

Proof. Direct from the cost table. The four-particle low-tier mean is
(0.16 + 1.52 + 2.68 + 3.94) / 4 = 2.08%; the high-tier mean is the
average of the eight costs 7.57 through 28.61, which is 15.86%.   QED

4.2 No correlation with charge, shell, or mass

We checked whether the transition cost correlates with particle charge,
framework shell assignment (HCSM-11), or observed mass. No monotone
relationship was found. The five low-cost particles span all five
charge categories (neutral, up-type, down-type, charged lepton, boson).
The highest-cost particle is tau (28.61%) at mass 1.777 GeV; the second
highest is u (27.15%) at mass 0.0022 GeV; the lowest is c (0.16%) at
mass 1.27 GeV.

5. STATISTICAL SIGNIFICANCE OF BASE 2
================================================================

The base-2 pattern uses targets kL/2^n for n = 0, 1, 2, 3. Does the
specific value 2 of the base have statistical significance, or would
any base produce a comparable pattern?

5.1 The test

For each candidate base b, compute the crossover ratio r_n(b)
satisfying m_f(r_n) = kL / b^n, for n = 0, ..., 3. For each n, find
the particle whose shape parameter is closest to r_n(b), and record
the CROSSOVER RATIO ERROR

    (6) err_n(b) = |r_a - r_n(b)| / r_n(b) * 100%.

The MEAN ERROR err_bar(b) = (1/4) sum_n err_n(b) quantifies the quality
of base b.

Remark 4 (Crossover ratio error vs transition cost). The crossover
ratio error measures the distance in r-space between the particle's
shape parameter and the crossover ratio. It is a DIFFERENT measure
from the transition cost of Definition 2, which measures the distance
in m_f-space. The two measures are consistent but not identical. This
section uses the crossover ratio error, following the framework's prior
WIN-era analysis of the base-2 significance.

5.2 Results

Proposition 2 (Statistical significance of base 2). Among the tested
candidate bases, base 2 has the lowest mean crossover ratio error over
n = 0, ..., 3:

    Base b     Mean error (%)
    2          0.269
    1.4        0.662
    3/2        0.672
    4/3        0.685
    sqrt(2)    0.732
    1.1        0.775
    1.6        0.845
    5/4        0.971
    phi        1.029
    1.2        1.068

Base 2 is best by a factor of 2.5 over the next-best base (1.4).

Proof. Numerical computation of the crossover ratio r_n(b) for each
candidate base b, followed by the closest-particle match and error
computation. The result is verified in the companion verification
script.   QED

Remark 5 (Specificity of the pattern). The base-2 pattern does not
appear with alternative functional forms. Using the power law alone,
m = A(r/r_e)^B, gives errors 21.55%, 14.30%, 6.38%, 2.28%. Using the
exponential alone, m = A exp(C(r - r_e)), gives errors 13.76%, 8.55%,
2.69%, 3.99%. Neither alternative produces the base-2 pattern; the
combined power-law-times-exponential form is required.

6. THE CROSSOVER AT kL
================================================================

6.1 The crossover ratio

The processing function's value crosses kL at

    (7) r_cross = 0.03345564,

located between the shape parameters of Z (0.033262) and H (0.041677),
closer to Z.

6.2 The two sectors

The crossover divides the 14 modes into two sectors.

Theorem 2 (Crossover structure). The crossover of m_f at kL partitions
the 14 modes as follows:

  - INPUT SECTOR (11 modes below the crossover): nu, e, u, d, s, mu,
    c, tau, b, W, Z. Total charge -5/3.
  - OUTPUT SECTOR (2 modes above the crossover): H, t. Total charge
    +2/3.

The total charge is -5/3 + 2/3 = -1, exactly the electron's charge.

Proof. Direct from the shape parameter table. Sum the charges of the
11 modes below r_cross:

    nu : 0,   e : -1,   u : +2/3,   d : -1/3,
    s : -1/3, mu : -1,  c : +2/3,   tau : -1,
    b : -1/3, W : +1,   Z : 0.

The sum is -5/3. The two modes above are H (charge 0) and t
(charge +2/3), summing to +2/3. Total charge -5/3 + 2/3 = -1.   QED

Remark 6 (Consistency with the two-mesh split). The total charge -1
matches the framework's charge invariant derived from the two-mesh
split of the substrate (see HCSM-04 and the WIN-era Torus paper).
Whether the crossover structure is a rederivation of the same
invariant or an independent numerical coincidence is an open question.

6.3 The crossover ratios form a base-0.8653 sequence

The crossover ratios r_n for n = 0, ..., 7 satisfy the targets kL/2^n
but their numerical values follow a different base:

    (8) r_n = r_inf + delta * 0.8653^n,
        r_inf = 0.012137, delta = 0.021257,

with RMS residual 5.31e-5. The crossover-ratio base (0.8653) differs
from the target base (2).

Remark 7. The base-2 structure lives in the TARGETS kL/2^n, not in the
crossover ratios themselves. The function's shape converts the base-2
target sequence into a base-0.8653 crossover-ratio sequence.

9. CONCLUSION
================================================================

This paper has documented a structural feature of the HCSM processing
function m_f(r) = A(r/r_e)^B exp(C(r - r_e)) at the shape parameters of
the framework's 14 modes.

The function's output at the shape parameters of four specific
particles -- Z, c, d, e -- is approximately equal to kL, kL/2, kL/4,
kL/8, i.e., four successive halvings of the warp factor. The match
errors are 3.94%, 0.16%, 2.68%, 1.52%; mean 2.08%. The four matched
particles cover all four Standard Model charge categories. Base 2 is
the statistically best base for this pattern, at 2.5x the performance
of the next-best base. The pattern is specific to the combined power-
law-times-exponential form.

The function's crossover at kL divides the 14 particles into 11 input
modes (charge -5/3) and 2 output modes H, t (charge +2/3), with total
charge -1, exactly the electron's charge.

The pattern is a numerical and structural fact about the specific
processing function and the specific anchored shape parameters. It is
not derived from the substrate. Whether the pattern has a substrate-
level origin, and whether the processing function's coefficients admit
a first-principles derivation, are open questions.

The framework's claim is that the Standard Model is what the substrate
looks like when you observe it. The identification of the 14 modes with
the 14 particles is the empirical thesis (HCSM-23). The processing
function and its coefficients are the framework's disclosed calibration
content. The base-2 pattern is a documented structural feature of that
calibration.
