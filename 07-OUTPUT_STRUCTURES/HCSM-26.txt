ABSTRACT
================================================================

The Hodge Complex Standard Model assigns the fourteen modes at the
self-paired eigenvalue of the discrete Laplacian on the periodic 8x8
torus to one Standard Model generation. The assignment of each mode to
a specific Standard Model particle, together with the N/4 mass label
attached to each, is the framework's EMPIRICAL THESIS (HCSM-23),
structurally parallel to the Standard Model's Yukawa-coupling sector.
The substrate DERIVES the fourteen modes, their coordinates, their D4
orbits, the winding generator and its class partition, the processor T
and its F/B split, the chiral reflection rs and its fixed locus, and
the zero state s. GIVEN the empirical identification, the structural
relations among the N/4 anchors are theorems of the framework.

Among the fourteen modes at lambda = 4, the coordinate pair
{(2,2),(6,6)} is distinguished: it is the fixed locus of the unique
chiral reflection of the D4 point group of the square. Under the
identification thesis, this pair is the W boson and the top quark and
is called the observer pair {W,t}. This paper proves eight results
about the pair. Two of them are fully derived from the substrate and do
not use the N/4 anchors: the Fixed-Locus Theorem and the Chirality
Selection Theorem. Four of them are theorems GIVEN the empirical
identification: the D4-fixed-pair taxonomy, the (d+1)/2 orbit
signature, the observer-pair uniqueness theorem, and the swap-pair
fingerprint theorem. The remaining results are mixed: each component is
labeled according to whether it is derived or anchored. Section 9
establishes a dimensional-rigidity statement linking the observer pair
to the kL crossover identity of HCSM-51; the statement is two
independent constraints on d plus one definitional evaluation, all
sharing the same value at d = 4. All numerical claims are verified at
machine precision.

1.1 The observer pair in HCSM

The Hodge Complex Standard Model (HCSM) derives the gauge structure,
matter content, particle spectrum, and mixing matrices of the Standard
Model from a single discrete substrate: the periodic 8x8 torus
Lambda = Z_8 x Z_8 [HCSM-00]. The fourteen modes at the self-paired

Standard Model generation. Their D4 representation theory determines the
gauge algebra; their N/4 labels determine the mass spectrum; the
zero-state vector s from the 14x19 vacuum-native measurement matrix
determines the flavor structure.

The identification of the fourteen modes with the fourteen Standard
Model particles, and the N/4 mass label attached to each, is the
framework's EMPIRICAL THESIS (HCSM-23). It is not a derived result. The
N/4 labels are empirical anchors, exactly parallel to the Standard
Model's Yukawa couplings: they are the inputs from which
particle-specific physics is read out, and the structural relations
among them are theorems given the anchor.

Throughout this paper:

    zero state s are THEOREMS of the torus.

    particle, and the N/4 label attached to each, are EMPIRICAL
    ANCHORS.

  - The structural relations among the N/4 values are THEOREMS GIVEN
    THE ANCHOR.

Among the fourteen modes, the coordinate pair {(2,2),(6,6)} carries a

In HCSM, physics is what the machine cannot cancel. The observer pair
is the pair of modes that the machine cannot cancel without breaking
its own chirality. The twelve other modes are paired under the chiral

2.2 The D4 action

The D4 point group is generated by the rotation

    r: (n_1,n_2) -> (-n_2,n_1) mod 8

and the reflection

    s: (n_1,n_2) -> (n_1,-n_2) mod 8,

subject to the presentation r^4 = s^2 = (rs)^2 = e. There are five
order-two elements (involutions): r^2, s, rs, r^2 s, r^3 s. Explicitly,

    r^2 : (n_1,n_2) -> (-n_1,-n_2),
    s   : (n_1,n_2) -> (n_1,-n_2),
    rs  : (n_1,n_2) -> (n_2,n_1),
    r^2s: (n_1,n_2) -> (-n_1,n_2),
    r^3s: (n_1,n_2) -> (-n_2,-n_1).

The reflection rs is called the diagonal reflection because it swaps the
two mode coordinates.

3. THE FIXED LOCUS THEOREM
================================================================

This section proves that the coordinate pair {(2,2),(6,6)} is the fixed
locus of the diagonal reflection rs. The result is derived from the
substrate; the naming of the pair as {W,t} applies the identification
thesis (HCSM-23).

3.1 The fixed set of rs

Lemma 1 (Fixed set of rs). The diagonal reflection
rs: (n_1,n_2) -> (n_2,n_1) acts on the fourteen-mode multiplet with
fixed set exactly

    (9)  Fix(rs) = {(2,2), (6,6)}.

Proof. A mode (n_1,n_2) is fixed by rs if and only if n_1 = n_2 mod 8.
Among the fourteen modes at lambda = 4, the diagonal pairs (n,n) with
n in {0,...,7} are (0,0), (1,1), ..., (7,7). Of these, only (2,2) and
(6,6) satisfy the eigenvalue equation lambda = 4, i.e.,
cos(pi n/4) = 0, which requires n in {2,6}. Hence the fixed set is
exactly {(2,2),(6,6)}.  QED

3.2 The zero state vector is odd under rs

Lemma 2 (Oddness of s under rs). The zero-state vector s satisfies
s(rs(m)) = -s(m) for every mode m in the fourteen-mode multiplet.

Proof. Direct computation on the explicit vector of Section 2.5. For
example, s(nu) = -0.105198199128, s(e) = +0.105198199128, and
rs(nu) = e; s(u) = +0.937726561300, s(s) = -0.937726561300, and
rs(u) = s. In every case, s(rs(m)) = -s(m).  QED

Corollary 3 (Vanishing on the fixed set). The zero-state vector
satisfies s((2,2)) = s((6,6)) = 0 exactly.

Proof. By Lemma 1, rs((2,2)) = (2,2) and rs((6,6)) = (6,6). By Lemma 2,
s(rs((2,2))) = -s((2,2)), hence s((2,2)) = -s((2,2)), so s((2,2)) = 0.
The same argument gives s((6,6)) = 0.  QED

3.3 The fixed locus and the sigma-lines

Theorem 4 (Fixed Locus Theorem). The following three characterizations
of the coordinate pair {(2,2),(6,6)} coincide:

    (10) {(2,2),(6,6)} = Fix(rs) = {m in M : s(m) = 0}
                       = {sigma = 0 line}.

Moreover, the reflection rs swaps the sigma = +1 and sigma = -1 lines,
and fixes the sigma = 0 line pointwise.

Proof. The first equality is Lemma 1. The second is Corollary 3. For the
third, by definition sigma(m) = sign(s(m)), so sigma(m) = 0 iff
s(m) = 0; this gives {m : s(m) = 0} = {sigma = 0 line}.

For the swap property: by Lemma 2, s(rs(m)) = -s(m) for all m.
Therefore sigma(rs(m)) = sign(s(rs(m))) = sign(-s(m)) = -sigma(m)
whenever sigma(m) != 0. On the fixed set, sigma((2,2)) = sigma((6,6))
= 0, so the sigma = 0 line is fixed pointwise.  QED

Remark 5 (Application of the identification thesis). Theorem 4 is a
derived result about the coordinate pair {(2,2),(6,6)}. It does not
refer to W, t, or any N/4 label. Under the identification thesis
(HCSM-23), the coordinates (2,2) and (6,6) are the W boson and the top
quark respectively, and the coordinate pair {(2,2),(6,6)} is the
observer pair {W,t}. The fixed-locus property is derived; the
identification of the pair with {W,t} is anchored.

Remark 6. Theorem 4 gives three independent characterizations of the
coordinate pair {(2,2),(6,6)}: as the fixed locus of the chiral
reflection rs, as the zero locus of the zero-state vector, and as the
sigma = 0 line of the charge theorem. That these three
characterizations coincide is not a coincidence; it is a consequence of
the fact (proved in Section 4) that rs is the unique chiral element of
D4.

4. THE CHIRALITY SELECTION THEOREM
================================================================

This section proves that the diagonal reflection rs is the unique chiral
element of D4 in the sense of anticommuting with the winding generator.
The result is derived from the substrate.

4.1 Commutation and anticommutation with H_wind

Definition 7 (Chiral element). An element g in D4 is called chiral if
g H_wind g^{-1} = -H_wind, i.e., if g anticommutes with the winding
generator.

Lemma 8 (Chirality of D4 elements). Among the five order-two elements
of D4, only rs anticommutes with H_wind.

Proof. Direct computation of the commutator [g, H_wind] and the
anticommutator {g, H_wind} for each of the five involutions r^2, s, rs,
r^2s, r^3s. The Frobenius norms are:

    Element g   ||[g,H_wind]||_F   ||{g,H_wind}||_F
    r^2          1.131e+01           1.386e+01
    s            1.131e+01           1.386e+01
    rs           1.789e+01           0.000e+00
    r^2 s        1.131e+01           1.386e+01
    r^3 s        1.386e+01           1.131e+01

Only rs has ||{g,H_wind}||_F = 0 exactly (at machine precision), and its
commutator norm is nonzero. Hence rs is the unique element that
anticommutes with H_wind.  QED

4.2 The Chirality Selection Theorem

Theorem 9 (Chirality Selection Theorem). The diagonal reflection
rs in D4 is the unique element that (i) fixes exactly the coordinate
pair {(2,2),(6,6)} on the fourteen-mode multiplet, and (ii)
anticommutes with the winding generator H_wind.

Proof. Uniqueness follows from Lemma 1 (only rs fixes {(2,2),(6,6)};
the other involutions have different fixed sets) and Lemma 8 (only rs
anticommutes with H_wind). Both properties hold for rs.  QED

Corollary 10 (Observer pair as chiral boundary). The coordinate pair
{(2,2),(6,6)} is the fixed locus of the unique chiral reflection of D4.
Under the identification thesis (HCSM-23), the pair is {W,t} and is the
boundary through which the machine breaks chirality.

Remark 11. The term "observer pair" has a structural content. The two
members of the pair are the only coordinates on which the chiral
reflection acts trivially, and they are simultaneously the only
coordinates with vanishing zero-state amplitude. In a system whose
machine is built to preserve chirality except for a unique boundary,
that boundary is the natural structural locus of observation.

7. THE OBSERVER-PAIR UNIQUENESS THEOREM
================================================================

This section proves that, given the empirical identification (HCSM-23),
the observer pair is the unique pair of modes satisfying five
independent structural constraints simultaneously.

7.1 The five constraints

Definition 19 (Observer-pair constraints). For a pair {m_1,m_2} of
modes in the fourteen-mode multiplet, define the following constraints:

    C1 (sum): (N/4)_{m_1} + (N/4)_{m_2} = (d+1)/2.
    C2 (difference): |(N/4)_{m_1} - (N/4)_{m_2}| = (d-1)/2.
    C3 (reflection-fixed): both m_1 and m_2 are fixed by rs.
    C4 (sigma-line): both m_1 and m_2 lie on the sigma = 0 line.
    C5 (fermion/boson straddling): m_1 and m_2 lie in different
        sectors of the F/B split.

7.2 Uniqueness

Theorem 20 (Observer-Pair Uniqueness Theorem). Given the empirical
identification (HCSM-23), the pair {W,t} is the unique pair of modes in
the fourteen-mode multiplet satisfying all five constraints C1-C5
simultaneously. The uniqueness is a uniqueness within the family of
anchors: it is a structural relation among the N/4 values, not a
derivation of which mode is W or which is t.

Proof. We proceed in three steps.

Step 1: C1 and C2 alone determine the numeric values. The linear system

    x + y = (d+1)/2 = 5/2,
    |x - y| = (d-1)/2 = 3/2

has solutions (x,y) = (2, 1/2) or (1/2, 2). Both solutions are realized
among the N/4 anchors: {2, 1/2} appears as {N/4(W), N/4(t)} and as
{N/4(Z), N/4(t)} (since W and Z share the value N/4 = 2).

Step 2: C3 and C4 eliminate the {Z,t} candidate. The pair {Z,t} fails
C3, because rs(Z) = H != Z, and fails C4, because sigma(Z) = -1 != 0.
Hence {Z,t} is eliminated.

Step 3: no other pair satisfies even three of the constraints.
Exhaustive enumeration confirms that no pair other than {W,t} and {Z,t}
satisfies both C1 and C2, and that {W,t} is the unique pair satisfying
both C1, C2, C3, C4. Constraint C5 is automatically satisfied by {W,t}
(since W in B and t in F) and by {Z,t} (since Z in B and t in F), but
the latter is already eliminated.  QED

Corollary 21 (Solution of the linear system). Given the empirical
identification (HCSM-23), the pair {W,t} is the unique solution to the
two-by-two linear system

    (12) N/4(W) + N/4(t) = (d+1)/2,
         N/4(W) - N/4(t) = (d-1)/2,

with solution

    (13) N/4(W) = d/2,   N/4(t) = 2/d.

The coefficient matrix of the system has determinant 2 = d/2, which is
itself a framework constant.

Proof. Solving the linear system gives
N/4(W) = ((d+1)/2 + (d-1)/2)/2 = d/2 and
N/4(t) = ((d+1)/2 - (d-1)/2)/2 = 2/d. The determinant is
1*1 - 1*(-1) = 2. At d = 4, this gives N/4(W) = 2 and N/4(t) = 1/2,
matching the anchors.  QED

Remark 22. The two constraints C1 and C2 have independent structural
meanings, given the identification. C1 states that the observer pair's
N/4 sum equals the coefficient (d+1)/2 = |F|/|B|. C2 states that the
pair's N/4 separation equals (d-1)/2, the same constant that appears in

8. THE SWAP-PAIR FINGERPRINT THEOREM
================================================================

This section proves that, given the empirical identification (HCSM-23),
the orbit-class swap pair {dark,t} is the unique two-element subset of
the fourteen-mode multiplet whose four symmetric-function values are all
framework constants.

8.1 The orbit-class swap

The D4 orbit partition and the winding class partition of the
fourteen-mode multiplet differ by exactly one two-element coordinate
swap.

Lemma 23 (Orbit-class reconstruction). The winding class partition
{A,B,C} is the D4 orbit partition {O_0,O_1,O_2} modified by the unique
two-mode swap

    t      : O_2 -> A,
    dark   : O_1 -> C.

This swap is uniquely forced by requiring each winding class to be
fermion/boson-homogeneous. Under the identification thesis (HCSM-23),
the swapped coordinates correspond to the top quark t and the dark
photon.

Proof. The orbit partition and the class partition differ only on the
two coordinates (6,6) and (5,7): (6,6) is in O_2 but in class A; (5,7)
is in O_1 but in class C. All other coordinates lie in the same orbit
and the same class.

Without the swap, the classes are not fermion/boson-homogeneous: the
orbit O_2 contains three bosons {(2,2),(2,6),(6,2)} and one fermion
{(6,6)}, so it is not F/B-homogeneous; the orbit O_1 contains one boson
{(5,7)} and seven fermions, so it is not F/B-homogeneous either.
Removing (6,6) from O_2 and adding (5,7) to make class C gives
C = {(5,7),(2,2),(2,6),(6,2)} (all bosons),
A = {(0,4),(4,0),(6,6)} (all fermions), and
B = O_1 \ {(5,7)} (all fermions). Hence the swap is uniquely forced by
requiring F/B-homogeneity of the three classes.  QED

8.2 The symmetric-function fingerprint

    ratio(a,b) = max(x,y) / min(x,y).

Proof. Direct arithmetic on the anchors N/4(dark) = 12 and N/4(t) = 1/2:
sum = 12.5, diff = 11.5, prod = 6, ratio = 24.

9. THE DIMENSIONAL-RIGIDITY STATEMENT
================================================================

This section states the dimensional-rigidity content linking the
observer pair to the kL crossover. The statement comprises two
independent constraints on d and one definitional evaluation at d = 4.
Each component is labeled with its derivation status.

9.1 The two constraints on d

Theorem 28 (Dimensional-rigidity constraints). The following two
independent identities on d each have the unique positive integer
solution d = 4:

    (observer-pair separation)   d/2 - 2/d = (d-1)/2,
    (kL crossover, HCSM-51)      (d-1)^2 = 2d + 1.

Both reduce to the polynomial equation d^2 - 4d = 0.

Proof. For the first identity: d/2 - 2/d = (d^2 - 4)/(2d). Setting this
equal to (d-1)/2 gives d^2 - 4 = d^2 - d, i.e., -4 = -d, i.e., d = 4.
Equivalently, d^2 - 4d = d(d-4) = 0, which has solutions d in {0, 4};
the positive integer solution is d = 4. The left-hand side is a
function of the observer-pair anchor values N/4(W) = d/2 and
N/4(t) = 2/d (Corollary 21), so the identity is a theorem given the
empirical identification (HCSM-23).

For the second identity: (d-1)^2 = 2d + 1 expands to
d^2 - 2d + 1 = 2d + 1, i.e., d^2 - 4d = 0, i.e., d(d-4) = 0. This
identity is derived in HCSM-51 from the bare kL crossover, independently
of the N/4 anchors.  QED

Remark 29. The two identities are independent as equations, but they
share the same solution set. That both framework relations select the
same value d = 4 is a nontrivial consistency check on the framework: the
observer-pair anchor values and the kL crossover condition are derived
from different parts of the framework, yet agree on the physical
dimension.

9.2 The dark energy coefficient identity

Theorem 30 (Observer-pair sum equals dark energy coefficient). Given
the empirical identification (HCSM-23), the observer pair's N/4 sum
equals the dark energy coefficient and the fermion-to-boson multiplicity
ratio:

    (14) N/4(W) + N/4(t) = (d+1)/2 = |F|/|B| = rho_DE / m_nu^4.

Proof. Direct substitution: N/4(W) + N/4(t) = 2 + 1/2 = 5/2;
|F|/|B| = 10/4 = 5/2; (d+1)/2 = 5/2 at d = 4.  QED

Remark 31. The equality |F|/|B| = (d+1)/2 is a definitional identity
given the F/B split (from HCSM-13: |F| = d(d+1)/2 and |B| = d). The
equality with the observer pair's N/4 sum is a theorem given the
empirical identification. The two equalities are not independent
constraints on d; both evaluate to 5/2 at d = 4. The dark energy
coefficient (d+1)/2 is derived in HCSM-53; the observer-pair sum is the
same value at the anchor level.

9.3 Summary of the dimensional-rigidity content

The dimensional-rigidity content of this section comprises:

  (1) A theorem given the anchor: the observer-pair separation identity
      d/2 - 2/d = (d-1)/2 (identity 1 of Theorem 28), with unique
      positive integer solution d = 4.

  (2) A derived theorem (HCSM-51): the kL crossover identity
      (d-1)^2 = 2d + 1 (identity 2 of Theorem 28), also with unique
      positive integer solution d = 4.

  (3) A definitional evaluation at d = 4: the equality
      N/4(W) + N/4(t) = |F|/|B| = (d+1)/2 = 5/2 (Theorem 30), which
      shares the value 5/2 with the observer pair's sum but does not
      independently constrain d.

Identities 1 and 2 are independent constraints on d; both select the
same physical dimension. Identity 3 is a value coincidence at that
dimension.

10. DISCUSSION
================================================================

10.1 What the observer pair is

The coordinate pair {(2,2),(6,6)} is the fixed locus of the unique
chiral reflection of D4, the zero locus of the zero-state vector, and
the sigma = 0 line of the charge theorem. It is the unique
reflection-fixed coordinate pair straddling the fermion/boson boundary.
Under the identification thesis (HCSM-23), the pair is the W boson and
the top quark, and it is the boundary through which the machine breaks
its own chirality.

The two members of the pair are the two Standard Model particles most
directly tied to the electroweak scale: the W boson is the charged
electroweak boson, and the top quark is the heaviest fermion and the
principal contributor to the Higgs vacuum expectation value through its
Yukawa coupling. The framework's structural prediction, conditional on
the empirical identification, is that they are paired as observers of
the machine.

10.2 The three structural regions

The fourteen-mode multiplet partitions into three structurally distinct
regions. The classification of the regions as Klein orbits is from
HCSM-27; the reader is referred there for the full derivation.

  - The four two-cycle coordinate orbits with kernel
    {e, rs * T_half}, which are homogeneous in orbit, class, and F/B,
    and heterogeneous in sigma.

  - The observer coordinate orbit {(2,2),(6,6)}, which is the unique
    orbit with kernel {e, rs}.

  - The four-cycle orbit {(5,7),(3,1),(1,3),(7,5)}, which is the unique
    orbit with trivial kernel and contains the swap partner of the top
    quark coordinate.

The observer coordinate pair occupies the middle region. It is the
unique orbit of its kernel class, and it is the unique fixed-point
locus of the chiral reflection.

10.3 The role of the swap

The orbit-class swap pair {(5,7),(6,6)} connects the four-cycle orbit to
the observer orbit. It is the unique edge in the distinguished-pair
graph connecting two distinct Klein orbits. Under the identification
thesis, this swap pair is {dark,t}. Its four-symmetric-function
fingerprint (Theorem 25) is entirely framework-constant, drawing on the
Newton-constant sector, the principal-angle sector, the
dimensional-rigidity sector, and the mass sector. This is the
framework's structural bridge between the observer pair and the rest of
the fourteen-mode multiplet.

13. CONCLUSION
================================================================

This paper has characterized the observer pair {W,t} of the Hodge
Complex Standard Model.

Two theorems are fully derived from the substrate and do not use the N/4
anchors. The Fixed Locus Theorem establishes that the coordinate pair
{(2,2),(6,6)} is the fixed locus of the diagonal reflection rs of D4,
coincides with the zero locus of the zero-state vector, and is the
sigma = 0 line of the charge theorem. The Chirality Selection Theorem
establishes that rs is the unique element of D4 that both fixes
{(2,2),(6,6)} and anticommutes with the winding generator.

Four theorems are conditional on the empirical identification thesis
(HCSM-23). Given the anchor, the pair's N/4 labels are the unique
solution to the linear system determined by (d+1)/2 and (d-1)/2; the
framework constant (d+1)/2 manifests in each of the three D4 orbits
through a distinct binary operation, and the observer pair realizes the
sum signature; the orbit-class swap pair {dark,t} is the unique
two-element subset whose four symmetric-function values are
simultaneously all framework constants. These theorems are falsifiable:
they hold for the specific anchor values observed in the Standard Model.

The dimensional-rigidity statement comprises two independent constraints
on d (the observer-pair separation identity and the kL crossover
identity) each of which uniquely selects d = 4, plus one definitional
evaluation at d = 4 in which two framework quantities share the value
5/2.

The framework's claim is that the Standard Model is what the substrate
looks like when you observe it. The identification of the fourteen modes
with the fourteen particles is the empirical thesis; the structural
relations among the N/4 anchors are theorems given the thesis. The
framework's strength is that it has fewer empirical anchors than the
Standard Model, not that it has none.
