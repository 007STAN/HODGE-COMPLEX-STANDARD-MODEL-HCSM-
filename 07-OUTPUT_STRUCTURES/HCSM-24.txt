ABSTRACT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

We establish the charge theorem of the Hodge Complex Standard Model and
resolve the status of its closed form. The electric charge in units of e/3
is q_3 = F(n_1, sigma), where n_1 is the mode's first momentum index and
sigma = sign(s) is the sign of the zero-state amplitude (HCSM-22). The map F
is a bijection from the 14 realized (n_1, sigma) pairs to the 5 Standard
Model charge eigenvalues {-3, -1, 0, +2, +3}. We prove: (i) the bijection
property; (ii) the sigma-line charge sums 0, +5, -8, summing to the vacuum
invariant -1e; (iii) the processor-charge bookkeeping (image sum -2, kernel
sum +1); (iv) the identification with the Standard Model content of one
generation; (v) three structural properties of the charge on the 8x8 torus
(evenness of n_1+n_2, the n_1+n_2 = 4 line containing all five charge values
exactly once, and the n_1+n_2 == 2 (mod 4) line carrying the entire vacuum
charge).

Our principal new result is a non-existence theorem. The closed form of
F(n_1, sigma) does not exist in any of nine candidate algebraic families:
integer-linear; modular (degree <= 2 in (n_1, sigma)); polynomial
(degree <= 3 in (n_1, n_2, sigma, Klein)); and the operator algebra
generated by {F, H_wind, T}. We further show that F is not a polynomial in
the amplitude-level rank of the zero state for either sigma-line. The
negative results are not defects: they identify the charge as the residue of
a sorting operation on the 14-mode multiplet whose sort key is the zero
state. We state the sorting machine explicitly (five steps), prove its four
structural consequences (the |Delta n| = 4 charge-sum sequence -3, -2, -1,
0; the |Delta n| = 2 sequence +1, -3; the sigma-pairing of every amplitude
level; and the equality of the F-pair charge-sum total to the sigma = +1
line sum), and thereby reduce the open problem of the charge closed form to
the open problem of deriving the N/4 mass labels from the zero state.

We close with two framework-constant theorems. The sigma-line sums are
recognized as 0, d+1, -2d (not arbitrary integers), and the F-pair charge
sums are framework-constant linear functions of the parent mass rank
(rank - d for |Delta n| = 4, (d+1) - d*rank for |Delta n| = 2). The charge
table is thus doubly constrained by sigma-line residues and F-pair shell
arithmetic progressions, and the charge theorem is a corollary of the
mass-label theorem. All numerical claims are verified in reproducible
Python.

1.1 The charge problem
---------------------

HCSM-04 identifies the 14 modes at the self-paired eigenvalue lambda = 4 of
the discrete Laplacian on Lambda = Z_8 x Z_8 with one Standard Model
generation. HCSM-23 states the identification thesis (mode <-> particle) and
the N/4 mass labels as the framework's empirical anchors. HCSM-22 constructs
the zero state s, the unique nullvector of a 14 x 19 measurement matrix of
vacuum-native observables, and establishes its rs-odd structure.

The present paper addresses the charge. We write

    q_3 = F(n_1, sigma),    sigma = sign(s),                       (1.1)

where n_1 is the mode's first momentum index, s is the zero state of HCSM-22,
and q_3 == 3Q is the electric charge in units of e/3. The charge table
(14 modes x charge) is disclosed as the framework's identification thesis for
the charge sector, per the HCSM-06/07 disclosure pattern.

The question this paper answers is: does a closed-form expression for
F(n_1, sigma) exist?

1.2 The one-line summary
------------------------

The closed form of F(n_1, sigma) does not exist. The charge is the residue of
a sorting operation driven by the zero state; Item 1.4 of the HCSM derivation
list reduces to Item 1.2.

(1) Bijection (Theorem 4.1). The 14 modes have 14 pairwise distinct
    (n_1, sigma) tuples. The map F is well-defined on the realized pairs and

(2) sigma-line sums (Theorem 5.1). The sigma-line sums are 0, +5, -8; total

    exactly once; the line n_1 + n_2 == 2 (mod 4) carries the entire vacuum

(4) Processor bookkeeping (Theorem 7.1). Sum over im(T) of Q_j = -2; sum
    over ker(T) of Q_j = +1; total = -1.

(5) Non-existence of a coordinate closed form (Theorem 9.1). No closed-form
    expression for F(n_1, sigma) exists in any of nine candidate algebraic
    families (Section 9).

(6) Sorting-machine interpretation (Theorem 10.1). The charge is the residue
    of a five-step sorting operation on the 14-mode multiplet whose sort key

(7) Reduction theorem (Theorem 11.1). Item 1.4 (charge functional) reduces
    to Item 1.2 (N/4 labels). Given the N/4 labels, the sorting rule
    reproduces the entire charge table.

(8) Framework-constant sigma-line sums (Theorem 10.6). The sigma-line sums
    are 0, d+1, -2d (not arbitrary integers).

(9) F-pair sum rules by shell (Theorem 10.7). Within each |Delta n| shell,
    the F-pair charge sum is a framework-constant linear function of the

PROPOSITION 6.1 (Even sum). Every mode has n_1 + n_2 even. Equivalently,
n_1 + n_2 in {4, 6, 8, 10, 12}.

Proof. The constraint lambda = 4 requires cos(pi n_1 / 4) + cos(pi n_2 / 4) = 0.
The cosine values for n in {0, ..., 7} are 1, sqrt(2)/2, 0, -sqrt(2)/2, -1,
-sqrt(2)/2, 0, sqrt(2)/2. Pairs of cosines summing to zero require either
both even (1, -1) or (0, 0), or both odd (+/- sqrt(2)/2). In both cases
n_1 + n_2 is even. QED.

THEOREM 6.2 (The n_1 + n_2 = 4 line contains all five charges). The 5 modes
with n_1 + n_2 = 4 are {nu, e, u, s, W}, and their charges are

nu(0,4), e(4,0), u(1,3), s(3,1), W(2,2), with charges 0, -3, +2, -1, +3. QED.

9. NON-EXISTENCE OF A COORDINATE CLOSED FORM
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

9.1 Statement of the theorem
----------------------------

THEOREM 9.1 (Non-existence of a coordinate closed form). The map
F(n_1, sigma) of (1.1) has no closed-form expression in any of the following
nine candidate families:

    (i)    integer-linear in (n_1, sigma);
    (ii)   modular linear in (n_1, sigma) over Z_7;
    (iii)  modular linear in (n_1, n_2) over Z_7;
    (iv)   modular bilinear in (n_1, sigma, n_1 sigma) over Z_7;
    (v)    modular linear in (n_1, sigma, Klein) over Z_7;
    (vi)   modular linear in (n_1, n_1 + n_2) over Z_7;
    (vii)  polynomial of degree <= 3 in (n_1, n_2, sigma, Klein) with
           continuous coefficients (overdetermined, rank 12);
    (viii) spectral projector of the operator algebra generated by
           {F, H_wind, T} (19 operators, rank 7);
    (ix)   polynomial in the amplitude-level rank of the zero state, one
           polynomial per sigma-line (residuals 2.27 and 4.01).

Proof [Verification]. Direct search in each family, verified in reproducible
Python. Each family is enumerated exhaustively; no member reproduces the
charge table exactly. In cases (vii)-(ix) the residual does not vanish at
machine precision. The exhaustive enumeration is computational; the search
spaces are finite and bounded. QED.

9.2 Elimination of algebraic families
-------------------------------------

The first six families (i)-(vi) are eliminated by exhaustive enumeration:

    Family                                              Result
    ----------------------------------------------------------------
    integer-linear in (n_1, sigma)                      no solution
    modular linear in (n_1, sigma)                      no solution
    modular linear in (n_1, n_2)                        no solution
    modular bilinear in (n_1, sigma, n_1 sigma)         no solution
    modular linear with Klein orbit                     no solution
    modular linear in (n_1, n_1 + n_2)                  no solution

9.3 Elimination of operator-algebraic families
----------------------------------------------

Family (vii): The degree <= 3 polynomial fit in (n_1, n_2, sigma, Klein) has
rank 12 out of 12 features, so the system is overdetermined. The continuous
residual is 3.9995; the rounded prediction is

    [0, -3, 3, 0, 0, -2, 0, 2, -3, 0, 0, 0, 0, 1]

versus the target

    [0, -3, 2, -1, -1, -3, 0, 2, -3, -1, 3, 0, 0, 2].

Family (viii): The operator algebra generated by {F, H_wind, T} produces 19
distinct operators. Their diagonal span has rank 7 and residual 5.47. The
small basis {F, H^2, T, F.H, F.T, H.T, F.H.T} has residual 5.55.

Family (ix): Polynomial fits in the amplitude-level rank of the zero state
have residuals 2.27 (sigma = -1) and 4.01 (sigma = +1).

9.4 Interpretation of the negative results
------------------------------------------

The nine failures are not defects of the charge table. They are a structural
statement: the charge is not a coordinate function. The family of candidate
expressions in (n_1, sigma) is exhausted to polynomial degree <= 3 and
modulo arithmetic of modulus 7, and to the operator algebra generated by the
three structural operators already known on the multiplet.

The correct question is not "what formula gives q_3 from (n_1, sigma)" but
"what operation on the 14-mode multiplet produces q_3 as a residue." That
operation is identified in Section 10.

10.1 Statement of the sorting machine
-------------------------------------

THEOREM 10.1 (Sorting-machine interpretation). The charge table q_3 is the
residue of the following operation on the 14-mode multiplet:

    (1) Enumerate modes by descending |s| (amplitude hierarchy of HCSM-22).
    (2) Pair modes by the diagonal flip F: (n_1, n_2) -> (n_2, n_1).
    (3) At each amplitude level, assign sigma = sign(s) -- one sigma = +1
        mode and one sigma = -1 mode.
    (4) Sort F-pairs by the shell |Delta n| = |n_1 - n_2|, then by
        descending parent N/4 value (the parent is the member with the
        smaller n_1).
    (5) Charge each F-pair so that its charge sum follows the sort rank
        within the shell.

The operation reproduces the entire charge table exactly.

|Delta n| = 4, ordered by descending parent N/4, have charge sums exactly

    {d, b}       :  parent d,     N/4 = 20,  sum = -2
    {mu, c}      :  parent mu,    N/4 = 14,  sum = -1
    {Z, H}       :  parent Z,     N/4 = 2,   sum = 0

Proof. Direct: q_3(nu) + q_3(e) = 0 + (-3) = -3; q_3(d) + q_3(b) = -1 + (-1)

10.4 The sigma-line sums in framework-constant form

THEOREM 10.6 (sigma-line sums in framework-constant form). The three

    sigma = -1:  modes {nu, d, s, dark, c, Z},  sum = 0
    sigma =  0:  modes {W, t},                  sum = d + 1 = 5
    sigma = +1:  modes {e, u, mu, tau, b, H},   sum = -2d = -8

Proof. Direct from the charge table. The sigma = -1 line is

COROLLARY 10.6a (sigma-pairing under F). Under the diagonal flip F, the

10.5 The F-pair sum rules by shell
----------------------------------

THEOREM 10.7 (F-pair sum rules by shell). The six non-fixed F-pairs sort
into two shells by |Delta n| = |n_1 - n_2|. Within each shell, the pair
charge sum is a framework-constant linear function of the parent's mass
rank (parents sorted by descending N/4):

    |Delta n| = 4:  sum = rank - d,             values -3, -2, -1, 0
    |Delta n| = 2:  sum = (d+1) - d*rank,       values +1, -3

Proof. Direct from the charge table and the N/4 anchors. For
|Delta n| = 4: the pairs ordered by descending parent N/4 are
{nu, e} (parent nu, N/4 = 60), {d, b} (parent d, N/4 = 20),
{mu, c} (parent mu, N/4 = 14), {Z, H} (parent Z, N/4 = 2), with charge sums
-3, -2, -1, 0, matching rank - d for ranks 1, 2, 3, 4.
For |Delta n| = 2: the pairs ordered by descending parent N/4 are
{u, s} (parent u, N/4 = 21) and {dark, tau} (parent dark, N/4 = 12), with
charge sums +1, -3, matching (d+1) - d*rank for ranks 1, 2. QED.

REMARK 10.7a (Double constraint). The charge table is doubly constrained:
vertically, the sigma-lines carry residues 0, d+1, -2d; horizontally, each
F-pair shell is an arithmetic progression with step +1 (for |Delta n| = 4)
or -d (for |Delta n| = 2). The two constraints interlock and pin the charge
assignment to a finite set within the framework-constant arithmetic.

11. REDUCTION OF ITEM 1.4 TO ITEM 1.2
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

THEOREM 11.1 (Reduction theorem). Given the N/4 mass labels as input, the
sorting machine of Theorem 10.1 reproduces the entire charge table.
Conversely, without the N/4 ordering, the charge table is not derivable from

derivation list (the charge functional) reduces to Item 1.2 (N/4 labels from

Proof. The sorting machine requires the N/4 labels as its sort key (step 4).
Given the labels, the charge table is reproduced by the five-step operation.
Without the labels, the nine algebraic families of Theorem 9.1 fail, and no
substitute closed form exists. QED.

REMARK 11.2 (Chain of derivation). The correct chain is

    s  ->  N/4  ->  F-pair ordering  ->  q_3,

not (n_1, sigma) -> q_3. Item 1.4 is a corollary of Item 1.2.

14. CONCLUSION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

We have established the charge theorem of the Hodge Complex Standard Model
and resolved the status of its closed form. The electric charge
q_3 = F(n_1, sigma) is a bijection from the 14 realized (n_1, sigma) pairs
to the 5 Standard Model charge eigenvalues. The three sigma-lines carry
charges 0, +5, -8, summing to the vacuum invariant -1e, and these sums are
framework constants 0, d+1, -2d. Three structural properties of the charge
on the 8x8 torus are established: even-sum, the n_1 + n_2 = 4 line
containing all 5 charges, and the n_1 + n_2 == 2 (mod 4) line carrying the
entire vacuum charge.

The closed form of F does not exist in any of nine candidate algebraic
families. The charge is the residue of a sorting operation on the 14-mode
multiplet whose sort key is the zero state. The operation is stated
explicitly, its four structural consequences are proved, and the reduction
of Item 1.4 to Item 1.2 is established.

Two framework-constant theorems close the paper. The sigma-line sums are
recognized as 0, d+1, -2d (not arbitrary integers). The F-pair charge sums
are framework-constant linear functions of the parent mass rank
(rank - d for |Delta n| = 4, (d+1) - d*rank for |Delta n| = 2). The charge
table is doubly constrained by sigma-line residues and F-pair shell
arithmetic progressions.

The charge theorem is therefore a corollary of the mass-label theorem. The
framework's remaining task in the charge sector is to derive the N/4 mass
labels from the zero state -- Item 1.2 of the derivation list.

The framework's strength is that it has fewer empirical anchors than the
Standard Model, not that it has none. The charge table is one of those
anchors; it is now understood as the output of a specific machine, not as a
free input.

Physics is what the machine cannot cancel. The Hodge complex is the machine.
