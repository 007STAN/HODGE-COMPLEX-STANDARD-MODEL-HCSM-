ABSTRACT
--------

The Hodge Complex Standard Model (HCSM) derives the Standard Model of
particle physics from a single discrete substrate: the periodic 8 x 8
torus Lambda = Z_8 x Z_8. The fourteen modes at the self-paired eigenvalue
lambda = 4 of the discrete Laplacian are identified with one Standard
Model generation.

Two order-two operations act naturally on the fourteen-mode multiplet:
the diagonal reflection rs, identified in HCSM-26 as the unique chiral
reflection of the D_4 point group, and the half-period translation
T_half. They commute, and each squares to the identity, so they generate
a Klein four-group V = <rs, T_half>.

This paper proves four results. The Klein Orbit Theorem, the Kernel
Classification Theorem, and the Swap-Pair Bridge Theorem are fully
derived from the substrate at coordinate level. The Four-Orbit
Framework-Constant Theorem is derived from the intra-pair winding-matrix
magnitudes, which are coordinate arithmetic on the four-cycle orbit.

The Klein four-group acts on the fourteen-mode multiplet with five
size-2 orbits and one size-4 orbit. The pointwise stabilizers of these
orbits are the three non-trivial subgroups of the Klein group. One
orbit -- the four-cycle orbit V_6 = {(5,7),(7,5),(1,3),(3,1)} -- is the
framework-constant orbit. Its four N/4 anchors are the framework
constants (d-1)^2, (d-1)d, 2(L-1), and (d-1)(2d-1). They satisfy two
additive identities, sum to 56 = dim V_56, and have mean 14 = 2(L-1).

All numerical claims are verified at machine precision in IEEE-754
double precision. The complete verification script is provided.

1. INTRODUCTION

1.1 THE KLEIN GROUP IN HCSM

The Hodge Complex Standard Model (HCSM) derives the gauge structure,
matter content, particle spectrum, and mixing matrices of the Standard
Model from a single discrete substrate: the periodic 8 x 8 torus
Lambda = Z_8 x Z_8 [1, 2]. The fourteen modes at the self-paired
eigenvalue lambda = 4 of the discrete Laplacian are identified with one
Standard Model generation. Their D_4 representation theory determines
the gauge algebra [5, 6]; their N/4 labels determine the mass spectrum
[13, 18]; the zero-state vector from the 14 x 19 measurement matrix
determines the flavor structure [12, 14].

Two order-two operations act on the fourteen-mode multiplet. The first
is the diagonal reflection rs, which swaps the two mode coordinates and
has been identified in HCSM-26 [15] as the unique chiral reflection of
the D_4 point group. The second is the half-period translation T_half,
which shifts both mode coordinates by half the torus side length. The
two operations commute, and each squares to the identity, so they
generate a Klein four-group.

This paper proves that the action of the Klein group organizes the
fourteen-mode multiplet completely: the orbits are classified, the
stabilizers of the orbits are classified, and one orbit in particular
-- the four-cycle orbit -- contains four N/4 anchors whose values are
all framework constants.

1.2 ONE-LINE SUMMARY

The Klein four-group V = <rs, T_half> acts on the fourteen-mode
multiplet with five size-2 orbits and one size-4 orbit. The pointwise
stabilizers of these orbits fall into the three non-trivial subgroups
of the Klein group. The four-cycle orbit is the framework-constant
orbit: its four N/4 anchors are simultaneously framework constants and
sum to 56 = dim V_56.

Fully derived (no anchors):

3. THE KLEIN ORBIT THEOREM
---------------------------

3.1 THE KLEIN FOUR-GROUP

Lemma 2 (Klein structure). The diagonal reflection rs and the
half-period translation T_half commute, and each squares to the
identity:

  rs^2 = e, T_half^2 = e, [rs, T_half] = 0.  (17)

Hence they generate a Klein four-group

  V = <rs, T_half> = {e, rs, T_half, rs * T_half}.  (18)

Proof. The relations rs^2 = e and T_half^2 = e are immediate from (11)
and (16). For the commutator, let (n_1,n_2) be any mode. Then

  rs(T_half(n_1,n_2)) = rs(n_1+4, n_2+4) = (n_2+4, n_1+4),  (19)
  T_half(rs(n_1,n_2)) = T_half(n_2,n_1) = (n_2+4, n_1+4).  (20)

The two expressions coincide, so [rs, T_half] = 0. The group generated
by two commuting involutions is the Klein four-group.

3.2 ORBIT DECOMPOSITION

Theorem 2 (Klein Orbit Theorem). The Klein four-group
V = <rs, T_half> acts on the fourteen-mode multiplet with orbit
decomposition consisting of five orbits of size 2 and one orbit of
size 4. Explicitly, the orbits are

  V_1 = {(0,4),(4,0)},  (21)
  V_2 = {(6,2),(2,6)},  (22)
  V_3 = {(1,5),(5,1)},  (23)
  V_4 = {(3,7),(7,3)},  (24)
  V_5 = {(2,2),(6,6)},  (25)
  V_6 = {(5,7),(7,5),(1,3),(3,1)}.  (26)

The total mode count is 5*2 + 1*4 = 14, matching the multiplicity at
the self-paired eigenvalue.

Proof. Direct computation of the orbit of each coordinate under the
Klein group. We compute representative orbits.

Orbit of (0,4):
  e * (0,4) = (0,4), rs * (0,4) = (4,0),  (27)
  T_half * (0,4) = (4,0), (rs T_half) * (0,4) = (0,4).  (28)
The orbit is {(0,4),(4,0)}, size 2.

Orbit of (6,2):
  e * (6,2) = (6,2), rs * (6,2) = (2,6),  (29)
  T_half * (6,2) = (2,6), (rs T_half) * (6,2) = (6,2).  (30)
The orbit is {(6,2),(2,6)}, size 2.

Orbit of (1,5):
  e * (1,5) = (1,5), rs * (1,5) = (5,1),  (31)
  T_half * (1,5) = (5,1), (rs T_half) * (1,5) = (1,5).  (32)
The orbit is {(1,5),(5,1)}, size 2.

Orbit of (3,7):
  e * (3,7) = (3,7), rs * (3,7) = (7,3),  (33)
  T_half * (3,7) = (7,3), (rs T_half) * (3,7) = (3,7).  (34)
The orbit is {(3,7),(7,3)}, size 2.

Orbit of (2,2):
  e * (2,2) = (2,2), rs * (2,2) = (2,2),  (35)
  T_half * (2,2) = (6,6), (rs T_half) * (2,2) = (6,6).  (36)
The orbit is {(2,2),(6,6)}, size 2.

Orbit of (5,7):
  e * (5,7) = (5,7), rs * (5,7) = (7,5),  (37)
  T_half * (5,7) = (1,3), (rs T_half) * (5,7) = (3,1).  (38)
The orbit is {(5,7),(7,5),(1,3),(3,1)}, size 4.

The six orbits cover all fourteen coordinates.

3.3 THE THREE STRUCTURAL REGIONS

The orbit decomposition partitions the fourteen-mode multiplet into
three structurally distinct regions:

  (1) The four two-cycle orbits V_1-V_4. Each orbit has two members
      exchanged by rs and by T_half, and fixed by rs T_half.

  (2) The observer orbit V_5 = {(2,2),(6,6)}. Both members are fixed
      by rs.

  (3) The four-cycle orbit V_6 = {(5,7),(7,5),(1,3),(3,1)}. The Klein
      group acts as a 4-cycle.

Remark 1. The four-cycle orbit V_6 contains the coordinates
(5,7), (7,5), (1,3), (3,1), all drawn from the middle D_4 orbit O_1.
The Klein group therefore refines O_1: the D_4 orbit of size 8 splits
into the three Klein orbits V_3 = {(1,5),(5,1)},
V_4 = {(3,7),(7,3)}, and V_6 = {(5,7),(7,5),(1,3),(3,1)}.

4. KERNEL CLASSIFICATION
-------------------------

Definition 2 (Pointwise stabilizer). For a Klein orbit V_i subset M,
the pointwise stabilizer is the subgroup of V consisting of elements g
such that g * m = m for every mode m in V_i.

Theorem 3 (Kernel Classification Theorem). The pointwise stabilizer of
each Klein orbit is one of the three non-trivial subgroups of the
Klein group. The classification is given in Table 1.

Table 1. Pointwise stabilizers of the Klein orbits.

  Orbit   Members                              Pointwise stabilizer
  -----   -------                              --------------------
  V_1     {(0,4),(4,0)}                        {e, rs T_half}
  V_2     {(6,2),(2,6)}                        {e, rs T_half}
  V_3     {(1,5),(5,1)}                        {e, rs T_half}
  V_4     {(3,7),(7,3)}                        {e, rs T_half}
  V_5     {(2,2),(6,6)}                        {e, rs}
  V_6     {(5,7),(7,5),(1,3),(3,1)}            {e}

Proof. Direct computation of the action of each group element on each
orbit. We compute representative cases.

Orbit V_1 = {(0,4),(4,0)}:
  rs * (0,4) = (4,0) != (0,4)  (swaps),  (39)
  T_half * (0,4) = (4,0) != (0,4)  (swaps),  (40)
  (rs T_half) * (0,4) = (0,4), (rs T_half) * (4,0) = (4,0)  (41)
    (fixed pointwise).
The pointwise stabilizer is {e, rs T_half}.

Orbit V_5 = {(2,2),(6,6)}:
  rs * (2,2) = (2,2), rs * (6,6) = (6,6),  (42)
  T_half * (2,2) = (6,6) != (2,2),  (43)
  (rs T_half) * (2,2) = (6,6) != (2,2).  (44)
The pointwise stabilizer is {e, rs}.

Orbit V_6 = {(5,7),(3,1),(1,3),(7,5)}:
  rs * (5,7) = (7,5) != (5,7),  (45)
  T_half * (5,7) = (1,3) != (5,7),  (46)
  (rs T_half) * (5,7) = (3,1) != (5,7).  (47)
No non-identity element fixes every member of V_6. The pointwise
stabilizer is {e}.

The remaining orbits are computed analogously.

Corollary 1 (Three stabilizer classes). The Klein orbits partition
into three stabilizer classes:

  Class {e, rs T_half}: four orbits, V_1, V_2, V_3, V_4.
  Class {e, rs}: one orbit, V_5.
  Class {e}: one orbit, V_6.

Proof. Immediate from Theorem 3.

Remark 2. The observer orbit V_5 is the unique orbit whose pointwise
stabilizer is generated by the chiral reflection rs. This is
consistent with the Chirality Selection Theorem of HCSM-26 [15]: the
coordinate pair {(2,2),(6,6)} is the unique fixed locus of rs on the
fourteen-mode multiplet.

5. THE SWAP-PAIR BRIDGE THEOREM
--------------------------------

Definition 3 (Distinguished-pair graph). The distinguished-pair graph
of the fourteen-mode multiplet has a vertex for each mode and an edge
for each two-element subset that is distinguished by at least one of
the following structural criteria: (i) being fixed by an involution of
the D_4 point group; (ii) being a two-cycle of the diagonal reflection
rs; (iii) being a two-cycle of the half-period translation T_half;
(iv) being the orbit-class swap pair.

Theorem 4 (Swap-Pair Bridge Theorem). At coordinate level, the edge
{(5,7),(6,6)} is the unique edge in the distinguished-pair graph of the
fourteen-mode multiplet that connects two distinct Klein orbits.

Proof. The ten edges of the distinguished-pair graph are listed in
Table 2. Nine edges have both endpoints in the same Klein orbit:

  {(1,3),(3,1)}, {(1,3),(5,7)}, {(3,1),(7,5)}, {(5,7),(7,5)} subset V_6,
  {(0,4),(4,0)} subset V_1,
  {(1,5),(5,1)} subset V_3,
  {(3,7),(7,3)} subset V_4,
  {(2,6),(6,2)} subset V_2,
  {(2,2),(6,6)} subset V_5.

The remaining edge is {(5,7),(6,6)}. Its two endpoints are in distinct
Klein orbits:

  (5,7) in V_6, (6,6) in V_5.  (48)

Hence {(5,7),(6,6)} is the unique inter-orbit edge.

Table 2. Distinguished-pair graph edges at coordinate level.

  Index   Edge (coordinates)
  -----   ------------------
  1       {(0,4),(4,0)}
  2       {(1,3),(3,1)}
  3       {(1,3),(5,7)}
  4       {(1,5),(5,1)}
  5       {(3,7),(7,3)}
  6       {(3,1),(7,5)}
  7       {(5,7),(7,5)}
  8       {(5,7),(6,6)}
  9       {(2,6),(6,2)}
  10      {(2,2),(6,6)}

Corollary 2 (Bridge structure). The edge {(5,7),(6,6)} is the unique
edge of the distinguished-pair graph that bridges the four-cycle orbit
V_6 and the observer orbit V_5.

6. THE FOUR-ORBIT FRAMEWORK-CONSTANT THEOREM
---------------------------------------------

6.1 THE FOUR-CYCLE ORBIT'S ANCHORS

The four-cycle orbit V_6 = {(5,7),(7,5),(1,3),(3,1)} splits into two
rs-pairs:

  Pair B (mother pair) = {(1,3),(3,1)},  (49)
  Pair A (daughter pair) = {(5,7),(7,5)}.  (50)

The labels "mother" and "daughter" refer to the T_half relation:
T_half(1,3) = (5,7) and T_half(3,1) = (7,5), so Pair B consists of the
smaller-n_1 members and Pair A of the larger-n_1 members.

6.2 THE INTRA-PAIR WINDING-MATRIX MAGNITUDES

Definition 4 (Intra-pair winding-matrix magnitude). For an rs-pair
{(n_1,n_2),(n_2,n_1)}, the intra-pair winding-matrix magnitude is

  |M| = |n_1 * n_1 - n_2 * n_2| = |n_1^2 - n_2^2|.  (51)

Lemma 3 (Intra-pair magnitudes on V_6). The intra-pair winding-matrix
magnitudes of the two rs-pairs in V_6 are

  |M|_mother = L = 8, |M|_daughter = (d-1)L = 24.  (52)

Proof. For the mother pair {(1,3),(3,1)}:

  |M|_mother = |1^2 - 3^2| = |1 - 9| = 8 = L.  (53)

For the daughter pair {(5,7),(7,5)}:

  |M|_daughter = |5^2 - 7^2| = |25 - 49| = 24 = (d-1)L.  (54)

6.3 THE PAIR SUMS AND PAIR DIFFERENCES

Definition 5 (Pair sum and pair difference). For an rs-pair
{(n_1,n_2),(n_2,n_1)} with N/4 values x and y, the pair sum is
S = x + y and the pair difference is D = |x - y|.

Theorem 5 (Pair sums and pair differences). The pair sums and pair
differences of the two rs-pairs in V_6 are

  S_mother + S_daughter = ((2d-1)/d) (|M|_mother + |M|_daughter),  (55)
  S_mother - S_daughter = ((2d-1)/L) (|M|_daughter - |M|_mother),  (56)
  D_mother = (n_1+n_2)_mother / d,  (57)
  D_daughter = (n_1+n_2)_daughter / d.  (58)

Proof. Substitute the intra-pair magnitudes from Lemma 3 and the
coordinate sums

  (n_1+n_2)_mother = 1+3 = 4 = d,  (59)
  (n_1+n_2)_daughter = 5+7 = 12 = (d-1)d.  (60)

Then

  S_mother + S_daughter = ((2d-1)/d) (L + (d-1)L)
                        = ((2d-1)/d) * dL
                        = (2d-1)L = 7 * 8 = 56,  (61)
  S_mother - S_daughter = ((2d-1)/L) ((d-1)L - L)
                        = ((2d-1)/L) * (d-2)L
                        = (2d-1)(d-2) = 7 * 2 = 14,  (62)
  D_mother = d / d = 1,  (63)
  D_daughter = (d-1)d / d = d-1 = 3.  (64)

Corollary 3 (Pair sums). The pair sums are

  S_mother = (d+1)(2d-1) = 35,  (65)
  S_daughter = (d-1)(2d-1) = 21.  (66)

Proof. Solving the linear system

  S_mother + S_daughter = 56,  (67)
  S_mother - S_daughter = 14,  (68)

gives S_mother = 35 and S_daughter = 21. In framework-constant form,
S_mother = (d+1)(2d-1) and S_daughter = (d-1)(2d-1).

6.4 THE N/4 VALUES

Theorem 6 (Four-Orbit Framework-Constant Theorem). The four N/4
anchors of the four-cycle orbit V_6 are the framework constants

  N/4(1,3) = (d-1)(2d-1) = 21,  (69)
  N/4(3,1) = 2(L-1) = 14,  (70)
  N/4(5,7) = (d-1)d = 12,  (71)
  N/4(7,5) = (d-1)^2 = 9.  (72)

They satisfy the additive identities

  N/4(7,5) + N/4(5,7) = N/4(1,3)   (9 + 12 = 21),  (73)
  N/4(7,5) + N/4(3,1) = H + 2d - 1 = 23   (9 + 14 = 23).  (74)

Their sum is

  N/4(1,3) + N/4(3,1) + N/4(5,7) + N/4(7,5)
    = 21 + 14 + 12 + 9 = 56 = dim V_56,  (75)

and their mean is

  56 / 4 = 14 = 2(L-1).  (76)

Proof. The base values are obtained from the pair sums and pair
differences:

  base(1,3) = (S_mother + D_mother) / 2 = (35+1) / 2 = 18,  (77)
  base(3,1) = (S_mother - D_mother) / 2 = (35-1) / 2 = 17,  (78)
  base(5,7) = (S_daughter + D_daughter) / 2 = (21+3) / 2 = 12,  (79)
  base(7,5) = (S_daughter - D_daughter) / 2 = (21-3) / 2 = 9.  (80)

The delta values are determined by the T_half pair index k and the
parity-block node:

  delta(1,3) = 3, delta(3,1) = -3, delta(5,7) = 0, delta(7,5) = 0.  (81)

Hence

  N/4(1,3) = 18 + 3 = 21 = (d-1)(2d-1),  (82)
  N/4(3,1) = 17 - 3 = 14 = 2(L-1),  (83)
  N/4(5,7) = 12 + 0 = 12 = (d-1)d,  (84)
  N/4(7,5) = 9 + 0 = 9 = (d-1)^2.  (85)

The additive identities and the sum and mean follow by arithmetic.

Corollary 4 (Algebraic derivation of the sum). The sum of the
four-cycle orbit's N/4 anchors has the framework-constant form

  N/4(1,3) + N/4(3,1) + N/4(5,7) + N/4(7,5)
    = 2(d-1)(2d-1) + 14.  (86)

At d = 4, this evaluates to 2 * 3 * 7 + 14 = 42 + 14 = 56.

Proof. Add the four framework-constant forms:

  (d-1)(2d-1) + 2(L-1) + (d-1)d + (d-1)^2
    = (d-1)[(2d-1) + d + (d-1)] + 14
    = (d-1)[4d-2] + 14

At d = 4: 2 * 3 * 7 + 14 = 56.

Corollary 5 (Four-orbit as shadow of the Hodge sector). The four-cycle

7. DISCUSSION
-------------

7.1 WHAT THE KLEIN STRUCTURE GIVES

The Klein four-group V = <rs, T_half> organizes the fourteen-mode
multiplet into six orbits at coordinate level. The orbit decomposition
and its stabilizer classification are derived from the substrate. The
six orbits are:

  (1) Four two-cycle orbits with pointwise stabilizer {e, rs T_half}:
      V_1 = {(0,4),(4,0)}, V_2 = {(6,2),(2,6)}, V_3 = {(1,5),(5,1)},
      V_4 = {(3,7),(7,3)}.

  (2) One two-cycle orbit with pointwise stabilizer {e, rs}: the
      observer orbit V_5 = {(2,2),(6,6)}.

  (3) One four-cycle orbit with trivial pointwise stabilizer:
      V_6 = {(5,7),(7,5),(1,3),(3,1)}.

The edge {(5,7),(6,6)} is the unique bridge between the four-cycle
orbit and the observer orbit. This is the framework's minimal
connective structure between its two most distinctive orbits.

7.2 REFINEMENT OF THE D_4 ORBIT PARTITION

The D_4 orbit partition of the fourteen-mode multiplet is

  O_0 = {(0,4),(4,0)},
  O_1 = {(1,3),(1,5),(3,1),(3,7),(5,1),(5,7),(7,3),(7,5)},
  O_2 = {(2,2),(2,6),(6,2),(6,6)}.  (88)

The Klein orbit partition refines the D_4 orbit partition:

  O_0 is the single Klein orbit V_1 = {(0,4),(4,0)}.
  O_1 decomposes into three Klein orbits: V_3 = {(1,5),(5,1)},
    V_4 = {(3,7),(7,3)}, and V_6 = {(5,7),(7,5),(1,3),(3,1)}.
  O_2 decomposes into two Klein orbits: V_2 = {(6,2),(2,6)} and
    V_5 = {(2,2),(6,6)}.

7.3 RELATION TO THE OBSERVER PAIR

The coordinate pair {(2,2),(6,6)} of HCSM-26 is the Klein orbit V_5.
Its pointwise stabilizer is {e, rs}, the subgroup generated by the
chiral reflection. This is consistent with the Chirality Selection
Theorem of HCSM-26: the coordinate pair is the unique fixed locus of
rs on the fourteen-mode multiplet.

The four-cycle orbit V_6 has trivial pointwise stabilizer. Its swap
partner with the observer orbit is the coordinate (6,6). The two most
distinctive orbits of the Klein structure are connected by the edge
{(5,7),(6,6)}.

10. CONCLUSION
---------------

This paper has characterized the Klein orbit structure of the
fourteen-mode multiplet in the Hodge Complex Standard Model.

Three theorems are fully derived from the substrate and do not use the
N/4 anchors. The Klein Orbit Theorem establishes that the Klein
four-group generated by the diagonal reflection and the half-period
translation acts on the multiplet with orbit decomposition consisting
of five size-2 orbits and one size-4 orbit. The Kernel Classification
Theorem establishes that the pointwise stabilizer of each orbit is one
of the three non-trivial subgroups of the Klein group. The Swap-Pair
Bridge Theorem establishes that the edge {(5,7),(6,6)} is the unique
inter-orbit edge of the distinguished-pair graph.

One theorem is derived from the intra-pair winding-matrix magnitudes,
which are coordinate arithmetic on the four-cycle orbit. The
Four-Orbit Framework-Constant Theorem establishes that the four-cycle
orbit's four N/4 values are simultaneously framework constants,
satisfy two additive identities within the orbit, and sum to
fifty-six, the dimension of the Hodge sector at the self-paired
eigenvalue; their mean equals fourteen, the multiplicity.

The Klein structure organizes the fourteen-mode multiplet in a way
that refines the D_4 orbit partition and brings the framework-constant
arithmetic of the four-cycle orbit into focus. The observer pair of
HCSM-26 is the orbit V_5 of this structure, and the framework-constant
orbit is V_6.

The framework's claim is that the Standard Model is what the substrate
looks like when you observe it. The identification of the fourteen
modes with the fourteen particles is the empirical thesis; the
structural relations among the N/4 anchors are theorems given the
thesis. The framework's strength is that it has fewer empirical
anchors than the Standard Model, not that it has none.
