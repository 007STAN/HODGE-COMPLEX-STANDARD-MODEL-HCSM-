ABSTRACT
================================================================

The Hodge Complex Standard Model assigns the fourteen modes at the
self-paired eigenvalue of the discrete Laplacian on the periodic 8x8
torus to one Standard Model generation. The assignment of each mode to
a specific Standard Model particle, together with the N/4 mass label
attached to each, is the framework's EMPIRICAL THESIS (HCSM-23),
structurally parallel to the Standard Model's Yukawa-coupling sector.
The substrate derives the fourteen modes, their coordinates, the D4
point-group action, and the operations that act on the multiplet.

Two order-two operations act naturally on this multiplet: the diagonal
reflection rs, identified in HCSM-26 as the unique chiral reflection,
and the half-period translation T_half. The two commute and each squares
to the identity, so they generate a Klein four-group. This paper proves
four results about the action of this group. The Klein Orbit Theorem
(derived), the Kernel Classification Theorem (derived), and the
Swap-Pair Bridge Theorem (derived at coordinate level, with the naming
of the pair applied from HCSM-23) are theorems of the substrate and use
no N/4 anchors. The Four-Orbit Framework-Constant Theorem is a theorem
GIVEN the empirical identification: it states that the four-cycle
orbit's four anchored N/4 values are simultaneously framework
constants, satisfy two additive identities, and sum to fifty-six, the
dimension of the Hodge sector at the self-paired eigenvalue; their mean
equals fourteen, the multiplicity. Section 8 catalogs which results are
derived and which are anchored. All numerical claims are verified at
machine precision.

1.1 The Klein group in HCSM

The Hodge Complex Standard Model (HCSM) derives the gauge structure,
matter content, particle spectrum, and mixing matrices of the Standard
Model from a single discrete substrate: the periodic 8x8 torus
[HCSM-00]. The fourteen modes at the self-paired eigenvalue lambda = 4
of the discrete Laplacian are identified with one Standard Model
generation. Their D4 representation theory determines the gauge algebra;
their N/4 labels determine the mass spectrum; the zero-state vector
from the 14x19 measurement matrix determines the flavor structure.

The identification of the fourteen modes with the fourteen Standard
Model particles, and the N/4 mass label attached to each, is the
framework's EMPIRICAL THESIS (HCSM-23). It is not a derived result. The
N/4 labels are empirical anchors, structurally parallel to the Standard
Model's Yukawa couplings.

Throughout this paper:

  - The fourteen modes, their coordinates, the D4 point-group action,
    the diagonal reflection rs, the half-period translation T_half,
    and the Klein four-group V they generate are DERIVED from the
    substrate.

  - The identification of each mode with a specific Standard Model
    particle, and the N/4 label attached to each, are EMPIRICAL
    ANCHORS (HCSM-23).

  - The structural relations among the N/4 values are THEOREMS GIVEN
    THE ANCHOR.

Two order-two operations act on the fourteen-mode multiplet. The first
is the diagonal reflection rs, which swaps the two mode coordinates and
has been identified in HCSM-26 as the unique chiral reflection of the
D4 point group. The second is the half-period translation T_half, which
shifts both mode coordinates by half the torus side length. The two
operations commute, and each squares to the identity, so they generate
a Klein four-group.

This paper proves that the action of the Klein group organizes the
fourteen-mode multiplet completely: the orbits are classified, the
stabilizers of the orbits are classified, and one orbit in particular
--- the four-cycle orbit --- contains four N/4 anchors whose values are
all framework constants, under the empirical identification.

The self-paired eigenvalue lambda = 4 has multiplicity fourteen. The

    O_1 = {(1,3), (1,5), (3,1), (3,7), (5,1), (5,7), (7,3), (7,5)},
    O_2 = {(2,2), (2,6), (6,2), (6,6)}.

Throughout this paper, the primary presentation of every theorem is at

2.2 The diagonal reflection

The diagonal reflection is the map

    (3) rs: (n_1, n_2) -> (n_2, n_1) mod 8.

It is an element of the D4 point group of the square, and it satisfies
rs^2 = e. It has been identified in HCSM-26 as the unique chiral
reflection of D4: the unique element that fixes exactly the coordinate
pair {(2,2),(6,6)} on the fourteen-mode multiplet and anticommutes with
the winding generator.

The fixed set of rs on the fourteen-mode multiplet is exactly the
coordinate pair {(2,2),(6,6)} (HCSM-26 Lemma 3.1). Under the
identification thesis, this pair is {W, t}.

2.3 The half-period translation

The half-period translation is the map

    (4) T_half: (n_1, n_2) -> (n_1 + 4, n_2 + 4) mod 8.

It shifts both mode coordinates by half the torus side length
L / 2 = 4. It satisfies T_half^2 = e.

2.4 The Klein four-group

Lemma 1 (Klein structure). The diagonal reflection rs and the
half-period translation T_half commute and each squares to the identity:

    (5) rs^2 = e,  T_half^2 = e,  [rs, T_half] = 0.

Hence they generate a Klein four-group

    (6) V = <rs, T_half> = {e, rs, T_half, rs * T_half}.

Proof. The relations rs^2 = e and T_half^2 = e are immediate from the
definitions. For the commutator: for any mode (n_1, n_2),

    rs(T_half(n_1, n_2)) = rs(n_1 + 4, n_2 + 4) = (n_2 + 4, n_1 + 4),
    T_half(rs(n_1, n_2)) = T_half(n_2, n_1) = (n_2 + 4, n_1 + 4).

The two expressions coincide. Hence [rs, T_half] = 0. The group
generated by two commuting involutions is the Klein four-group; this is
verified at machine precision in the companion verification script.
QED

2.5 The product rs * T_half

The product of the two generators is the map

    (7) rs * T_half: (n_1, n_2) -> (n_2 + 4, n_1 + 4) mod 8.

It is an involution, and it is not equal to either rs or T_half.

2.6 The N/4 anchors

3. THE KLEIN ORBIT THEOREM
================================================================

This section proves that the Klein group acts on the fourteen-mode
multiplet with five size-2 orbits and one size-4 orbit. The result is
derived at the coordinate level.

3.1 Orbit decomposition

Theorem 2 (Klein Orbit Theorem). The Klein four-group
V = <rs, T_half> acts on the fourteen-mode multiplet with orbit
decomposition consisting of five orbits of size two and one orbit of
size four. Explicitly, the orbits are:

    Orbit   Coordinates (particle names)                              Size
    V_1     {(0,4),(4,0)} ({nu,e})                                     2
    V_2     {(6,2),(2,6)} ({H,Z})                                      2
    V_3     {(1,5),(5,1)} ({d,b})                                      2
    V_4     {(3,7),(7,3)} ({mu,c})                                     2
    V_5     {(2,2),(6,6)} ({W,t})                                      2
    V_6     {(5,7),(3,1),(1,3),(7,5)} ({dark,s,u,tau})                 4

The total mode count is 5*2 + 1*4 = 14, matching the multiplicity at
the self-paired eigenvalue. The orbit decomposition and the
coordinate-level presentation are derived from the substrate. The
particle names in parentheses are attached by the empirical
identification (HCSM-23).

Proof. Direct computation of the orbit of each coordinate under the
Klein group. We compute representative orbits.

Orbit of (0,4):
    e * (0,4) = (0,4),
    rs * (0,4) = (4,0),
    T_half * (0,4) = (4,0),
    (rs * T_half) * (0,4) = (0,4).
The orbit is {(0,4),(4,0)}, size 2. This is V_1.

Orbit of (6,2):
    e * (6,2) = (6,2),
    rs * (6,2) = (2,6),
    T_half * (6,2) = (2,6),
    (rs * T_half) * (6,2) = (6,2).
The orbit is {(6,2),(2,6)}, size 2. This is V_2.

Orbit of (1,5):
    e * (1,5) = (1,5),
    rs * (1,5) = (5,1),
    T_half * (1,5) = (5,1),
    (rs * T_half) * (1,5) = (1,5).
The orbit is {(1,5),(5,1)}, size 2. This is V_3.

Orbit of (3,7):
    e * (3,7) = (3,7),
    rs * (3,7) = (7,3),
    T_half * (3,7) = (7,3),
    (rs * T_half) * (3,7) = (3,7).
The orbit is {(3,7),(7,3)}, size 2. This is V_4.

Orbit of (2,2):
    e * (2,2) = (2,2),
    rs * (2,2) = (2,2),
    T_half * (2,2) = (6,6),
    (rs * T_half) * (2,2) = (6,6).
The orbit is {(2,2),(6,6)}, size 2. This is V_5.

Orbit of (5,7):
    e * (5,7) = (5,7),
    rs * (5,7) = (7,5),
    T_half * (5,7) = (1,3),
    (rs * T_half) * (5,7) = (3,1).
The orbit is {(5,7),(7,5),(1,3),(3,1)}, size 4. This is V_6.

The six orbits cover all fourteen coordinates. The orbit decomposition
is as stated.  QED

3.2 The three structural regions

The orbit decomposition partitions the fourteen-mode multiplet into
three structurally distinct regions:

The four two-cycle orbits V_1 - V_4. Each of these orbits has two
members that are exchanged by the diagonal reflection and by the
half-period translation, and that are fixed by the product
rs * T_half. They are the framework's standard mirror pairs.

The observer orbit V_5. This orbit is {(2,2),(6,6)}. Both members are
fixed by the diagonal reflection rs. Under the identification thesis,
this is the observer pair of HCSM-26.

The four-cycle orbit V_6. This orbit contains four coordinates that are
cyclically exchanged by the Klein group. It contains the swap partner
of the top-quark coordinate and has the framework-constant property of
Section 6.

Remark 2.1. The four-cycle orbit V_6 contains the coordinates (5,7),
(7,5), (1,3), (3,1), all drawn from the middle D4 orbit O_1. The Klein
group therefore further refines O_1: the D4 orbit of size 8 splits into
the three Klein orbits V_3 = {(1,5),(5,1)}, V_4 = {(3,7),(7,3)}, and
V_6 = {(5,7),(3,1),(1,3),(7,5)}.

4. KERNEL CLASSIFICATION
================================================================

This section classifies the pointwise stabilizer of each Klein orbit.
The result is derived at the coordinate level.

4.1 Definition

Definition 3 (Pointwise stabilizer). For a Klein orbit V_i in M, the
pointwise stabilizer is the subgroup of V consisting of elements g such
that g * m = m for every mode m in V_i.

4.2 Classification

Theorem 4 (Kernel Classification Theorem). The pointwise stabilizer of
each Klein orbit is one of the three non-trivial subgroups of the Klein
group. The classification is:

    Orbit   Members (coordinate-level)           Pointwise stabilizer
    V_1     {(0,4),(4,0)}                        {e, rs * T_half}
    V_2     {(6,2),(2,6)}                        {e, rs * T_half}
    V_3     {(1,5),(5,1)}                        {e, rs * T_half}
    V_4     {(3,7),(7,3)}                        {e, rs * T_half}
    V_5     {(2,2),(6,6)}                        {e, rs}
    V_6     {(5,7),(3,1),(1,3),(7,5)}            {e}

Proof. Direct computation of the action of each group element on each
orbit. We compute representative cases.

Orbit V_1 = {(0,4),(4,0)}:
    rs * (0,4) = (4,0) != (0,4)           (swaps),
    T_half * (0,4) = (4,0) != (0,4)       (swaps),
    (rs * T_half) * (0,4) = (0,4),
    (rs * T_half) * (4,0) = (4,0)         (fixed pointwise).
Hence the pointwise stabilizer is {e, rs * T_half}.

Orbit V_5 = {(2,2),(6,6)}:
    rs * (2,2) = (2,2), rs * (6,6) = (6,6)     (fixed pointwise),
    T_half * (2,2) = (6,6) != (2,2)            (swaps),
    (rs * T_half) * (2,2) = (6,6) != (2,2)     (swaps).
Hence the pointwise stabilizer is {e, rs}.

Orbit V_6 = {(5,7),(3,1),(1,3),(7,5)}:
    rs * (5,7) = (7,5) != (5,7)             (swaps),
    T_half * (5,7) = (1,3) != (5,7)         (swaps),
    (rs * T_half) * (5,7) = (3,1) != (5,7)  (swaps).
No non-identity element fixes every member of V_6. Hence the
pointwise stabilizer is {e}.

The remaining orbits are computed analogously and yield the
classification as stated.  QED

4.3 The three stabilizer classes

Corollary 5 (Three stabilizer classes). The Klein orbits partition into
three stabilizer classes:

    Class {e, rs * T_half}:  four orbits, V_1, V_2, V_3, V_4.
    Class {e, rs}:           one orbit, V_5.
    Class {e}:               one orbit, V_6.

Proof. Immediate from Theorem 4.  QED

Remark 4.1. The observer orbit V_5 is the unique orbit whose pointwise
stabilizer is generated by the chiral reflection rs. This is consistent
with the Chirality Selection Theorem of HCSM-26: the coordinate pair
{(2,2),(6,6)} is the unique fixed locus of rs on the fourteen-mode
multiplet.

5.1 The distinguished-pair graph

Definition 6 (Distinguished-pair graph). The distinguished-pair graph
of the fourteen-mode multiplet has a vertex for each mode and an edge
for each two-element subset that is distinguished by at least one of
the following structural criteria: (i) being fixed by an involution of
the D4 point group; (ii) being a two-cycle of the diagonal reflection
rs; (iii) being a two-cycle of the half-period translation T_half; and
(iv) being the orbit-class swap pair (defined in HCSM-26).

The distinguished-pair graph has ten edges, listed at coordinate level:

    Index   Edge (coordinates; names)
    1       {(0,4),(4,0)}            ({nu,e})
    2       {(1,3),(3,1)}            ({u,s})
    3       {(1,3),(5,7)}            ({u,dark})
    4       {(1,5),(5,1)}            ({d,b})
    5       {(3,7),(7,3)}            ({mu,c})
    6       {(3,1),(7,5)}            ({s,tau})
    7       {(5,7),(7,5)}            ({dark,tau})
    8       {(5,7),(6,6)}            ({dark,t})
    9       {(2,6),(6,2)}            ({Z,H})
   10       {(2,2),(6,6)}            ({W,t})

Its vertex set is a subset of the fourteen coordinates.

5.2 The inter-orbit edges

Theorem 7 (Swap-Pair Bridge Theorem). At coordinate level, the edge
{(5,7),(6,6)} is the unique edge in the distinguished-pair graph of the
fourteen-mode multiplet that connects two distinct Klein orbits. Under
the empirical identification (HCSM-23), the edge is {dark, t}.

Proof. The ten edges of the distinguished-pair graph are listed in
Section 5.1.

Within-orbit edges. The following nine edges have both endpoints in the
same Klein orbit:

    {(1,3),(3,1)}, {(1,3),(5,7)}, {(3,1),(7,5)}, {(5,7),(7,5)} in V_6,
    {(0,4),(4,0)}   in V_1,
    {(1,5),(5,1)}   in V_3,
    {(3,7),(7,3)}   in V_4,
    {(2,6),(6,2)}   in V_2,
    {(2,2),(6,6)}   in V_5.

The remaining edge. The edge {(5,7),(6,6)} has its two endpoints in
distinct Klein orbits:

    (5,7) in V_6 = {(5,7),(3,1),(1,3),(7,5)},
    (6,6) in V_5 = {(2,2),(6,6)}.

Hence {(5,7),(6,6)} is the unique inter-orbit edge.  QED

Corollary 8 (Bridge structure). The edge {(5,7),(6,6)} is the unique

6.2 The theorem

Theorem 9 (Four-Orbit Framework-Constant Theorem). Given the empirical

    (10) N/4(tau)  = (d-1)^2 = 9,
         N/4(dark) = (d-1) * d = 12,
         N/4(s)    = multiplicity = 14,
         N/4(u)    = (d-1)(2d-1) = 21.

    (11) N/4(tau) + N/4(dark) = N/4(u),
         N/4(tau) + N/4(s)    = H + 2d - 1 = 23,

    (12) N/4(tau) + N/4(dark) + N/4(s) + N/4(u)

which is the dimension of the Hodge sector at the self-paired

    (13) 56 / 4 = 14,

which is the multiplicity at the self-paired eigenvalue.

Proof. Direct arithmetic on the anchors. For the additive identities:

    9 + 12 = 21 (= N/4(u)),
    9 + 14 = 23 (= H + 2d - 1).

For the sum:

    9 + 12 + 14 + 21 = 56 = 14 + 28 + 14
                          = dim(Omega^0 + Omega^1 + Omega^2)|_{lambda=4}.

For the mean:

    56 / 4 = 14 = 2(L-1) = multiplicity_{lambda=4}.   QED

6.3 The algebraic derivation of the sum

Proposition 10 (Algebraic derivation of the sum). Given the empirical
identification (HCSM-23), the sum of the four-cycle orbit's N/4 anchors
has the framework-constant form

    (14) N/4(tau) + N/4(dark) + N/4(s) + N/4(u)

At d = 4, this evaluates to 2 * 3 * 7 + 14 = 42 + 14 = 56.

Proof. Add the four framework-constant forms:

    N/4(tau) + N/4(dark) + N/4(s) + N/4(u)

Collect the terms in (d-1):

    = (d-1) * [(d-1) + d + (2d-1)] + 14
    = (d-1) * [4d - 2] + 14
    = 2(d-1)(2d-1) + 14.

At d = 4: 2 * 3 * 7 + 14 = 42 + 14 = 56.   QED

Corollary 11 (Four-orbit as a shadow of the Hodge sector). Given the

The Klein four-group V = <rs, T_half> organizes the fourteen-mode
multiplet into six orbits at coordinate level. The orbit decomposition
and its stabilizer classification are derived from the substrate. The
six orbits are:

  - Four two-cycle orbits with pointwise stabilizer {e, rs * T_half}:
    V_1 = {(0,4),(4,0)},
    V_2 = {(6,2),(2,6)},
    V_3 = {(1,5),(5,1)},
    V_4 = {(3,7),(7,3)}.

  - One two-cycle orbit with pointwise stabilizer {e, rs}:
    the observer orbit V_5 = {(2,2),(6,6)}.

  - One four-cycle orbit with trivial pointwise stabilizer:
    V_6 = {(5,7),(3,1),(1,3),(7,5)}.

The edge {(5,7),(6,6)} (the swap pair {dark, t} under the empirical
identification) is the unique bridge between the four-cycle orbit and
the observer orbit. This is the framework's minimal connective
structure between its two most distinctive orbits.

7.2 Refinement of the D4 orbit partition

The D4 orbit partition of the fourteen-mode multiplet is

    O_0 = {(0,4),(4,0)},
    O_1 = {(1,3),(1,5),(3,1),(3,7),(5,1),(5,7),(7,3),(7,5)},
    O_2 = {(2,2),(2,6),(6,2),(6,6)}.

The Klein orbit partition refines the D4 orbit partition:

  - O_0 is the single Klein orbit V_1 = {(0,4),(4,0)}.
  - O_1 decomposes into three Klein orbits:
    V_3 = {(1,5),(5,1)}, V_4 = {(3,7),(7,3)}, and
    V_6 = {(5,7),(3,1),(1,3),(7,5)}.
  - O_2 decomposes into two Klein orbits:
    V_2 = {(6,2),(2,6)} and V_5 = {(2,2),(6,6)}.

The Klein group therefore provides a fine-grained partition of the
multiplet, in which each D4 orbit of size greater than two is further
decomposed.

7.3 Relation to the observer pair

The coordinate pair {(2,2),(6,6)} of HCSM-26 is the Klein orbit V_5.
Its pointwise stabilizer is {e, rs}, the subgroup generated by the
chiral reflection. This is consistent with the Chirality Selection
Theorem of HCSM-26: the coordinate pair is the unique fixed locus of rs
on the fourteen-mode multiplet. Under the identification thesis, this
pair is the observer pair {W, t}.

The four-cycle orbit V_6 has trivial pointwise stabilizer. Its swap
partner with the observer orbit is the coordinate (6,6) (the
top-quark coordinate). The two most distinctive orbits of the Klein
structure are connected by the edge {(5,7),(6,6)}.

10. CONCLUSION
================================================================

This paper has characterized the Klein orbit structure of the
fourteen-mode multiplet in the Hodge Complex Standard Model.

Three theorems are fully derived from the substrate and do not use the
N/4 anchors. The Klein Orbit Theorem establishes that the Klein
four-group generated by the diagonal reflection and the half-period
translation acts on the multiplet with orbit decomposition consisting
of five size-2 orbits and one size-4 orbit. The Kernel Classification
Theorem establishes that the pointwise stabilizer of each orbit is one
of the three non-trivial subgroups of the Klein group: four orbits have
stabilizer {e, rs * T_half}, the observer orbit has stabilizer {e, rs},
and the four-cycle orbit has trivial stabilizer. The Swap-Pair Bridge
Theorem establishes that the edge {(5,7),(6,6)} is the unique
inter-orbit edge of the distinguished-pair graph; under the empirical
identification, this edge is {dark, t}.

One theorem is conditional on the empirical identification thesis
(HCSM-23). Given the anchor, the four-cycle orbit's four N/4 values are
simultaneously framework constants, satisfy two additive identities
within the orbit, and sum to fifty-six, the dimension of the Hodge
sector at the self-paired eigenvalue; their mean equals fourteen, the
multiplicity. This theorem is falsifiable: it holds for the specific
anchor values observed in the Standard Model.

The Klein structure organizes the fourteen-mode multiplet in a way that
refines the D4 orbit partition and brings the framework-constant
arithmetic of the four-cycle orbit into focus. The observer pair of
HCSM-26 is the orbit V_5 of this structure, and the
framework-constant orbit is V_6.

The framework's claim is that the Standard Model is what the substrate
looks like when you observe it. The identification of the fourteen
modes with the fourteen particles is the empirical thesis; the
structural relations among the N/4 anchors are theorems given the
thesis. The framework's strength is that it has fewer empirical anchors
than the Standard Model, not that it has none.
