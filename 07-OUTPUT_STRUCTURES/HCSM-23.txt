ABSTRACT
================================================================================

The Hodge Complex Standard Model (HCSM) identifies the 14 modes at the
self-paired eigenvalue lambda = 4 of the discrete Laplacian on the periodic
8 x 8 torus with one Standard Model generation. HCSM-23 stated this
identification as the framework's empirical thesis and listed the structural
relations satisfied by the 14 N/4 mass-ordering anchors. This revision adds
three new HCSM-native structural results.

First, the 14 modes at lambda = 4 are exactly the union of two lines
L_A union L_B in Z_8^2, where L_A: n_2 - n_1 = 4 (mod 8) and
L_B: n_1 + n_2 = 4 (mod 8), intersecting at {nu, e}.

Second, the six Klein orbits of HCSM-27 are exactly the (line, n_1 mod 4)
classes, and the four-cycle orbit V6 admits the coordinate-level
characterization V6 = {m : n_1 - n_2 = 2 (mod 4)}.

Third, every T_half pair has a sum S and a difference D that are framework
constants, and the two N/4 anchors of each pair are exactly (S + D)/2 and
(S - D)/2.

The derivation target for the N/4 anchors reduces from 14 independent
values to 7 (S, D) pairs of framework constants. The identification thesis
remains empirical and falsifiable. All numerical claims are verified at
machine precision.

Derivation status summary:

  DERIVED (original HCSM-23):  the (n_1, sigma) bijection; the rs-pair

  EMPIRICAL ANCHOR:  the identification of each of the 14 modes with a
      Standard Model particle (Thesis 1.1), together with the 14 N/4
      mass-ordering values.

  OPEN:  the derivation of the 7 pair sums S and the 7 pair differences D
      from substrate primitives.

      exactly the (line, n_1 mod 4) classes. The four-cycle orbit V6 admits

from 14 independent values to 7 (S, D) pairs of framework constants. The
identification of each mode with a Standard Model particle remains
empirical; the internal structure of the N/4 list is now cleanly organized.

2.1 The substrate and the 14-mode multiplet

The substrate is the periodic square lattice Lambda = Z_8 x Z_8 with
N = 64 sites. The framework constants are

    d = 4,  N = d * 2^d = 64,  L = sqrt(N) = 8,  H = 2^d = 16,
    V = (d-1) * 2^d = 48.

The discrete 5-point Laplacian is

    (Delta f)(x_1,x_2) = 4 f(x_1,x_2) - f(x_1+1,x_2) - f(x_1-1,x_2)
                          - f(x_1,x_2+1) - f(x_1,x_2-1),

with eigenvalues

    lambda(n_1, n_2) = 4 - 2 cos(pi n_1 / 4) - 2 cos(pi n_2 / 4),
    (n_1, n_2) in {0,...,7}^2.                                       (Eq. 2.1)

The self-paired eigenvalue lambda = 4 has multiplicity 2(L-1) = 14.

In the standard mode order

    (nu, e, u, d, s, mu, dark, c, tau, b, W, Z, H, t),

the mode coordinates are

    nu:(0,4),   e:(4,0),   u:(1,3),    d:(1,5),
    s:(3,1),    mu:(3,7),  dark:(5,7), c:(7,3),
    tau:(7,5),  b:(5,1),   W:(2,2),    Z:(2,6),
    H:(6,2),    t:(6,6).

2.2 The N/4 anchors

The 14 N/4 mass-ordering labels are

    (N/4)_i = (60, 24, 21, 20, 14, 14, 12, 9.5, 9, 7.5, 2, 2, 1, 0.5)

in the standard mode order. The values are the framework's empirical
anchors. Their internal structure is the subject of this paper.

2.3 The Klein group

Two order-two operations act on the 14-mode multiplet:

    rs           : (n_1, n_2) -> (n_2, n_1)  (mod 8),
    T_half       : (n_1, n_2) -> (n_1+4, n_2+4)  (mod 8).

They commute and each squares to the identity, generating a Klein
four-group V = <rs, T_half> = {e, rs, T_half, rs * T_half} [HCSM-27]. The
product is

    rs * T_half  : (n_1, n_2) -> (n_2+4, n_1+4)  (mod 8).

The Klein orbits of the 14-mode multiplet are:

    V_1 = {(0,4), (4,0)}
    V_2 = {(2,6), (6,2)}
    V_3 = {(1,5), (5,1)}
    V_4 = {(3,7), (7,3)}
    V_5 = {(2,2), (6,6)}
    V_6 = {(5,7), (3,1), (1,3), (7,5)}                              (Eq. 2.2)

2.4 The T_half pairs

The half-period translation T_half pairs the 14 modes into 7 orbits of
size 2 (the four two-cycle Klein orbits V_1, V_2, V_3, V_4 together with
the two-cycle orbit V_5), plus the four-cycle orbit V_6 which decomposes
into two T_half two-cycles:

    V_6 = {(1,3),(5,7)} union {(3,1),(7,5)}.                        (Eq. 2.3)

This gives 7 T_half pairs in total:

    P_1 = {(0,4),(4,0)}
    P_2 = {(2,6),(6,2)}
    P_3 = {(1,5),(5,1)}
    P_4 = {(3,7),(7,3)}
    P_5 = {(2,2),(6,6)}
    P_6 = {(1,3),(5,7)}
    P_7 = {(3,1),(7,5)}                                             (Eq. 2.4)

Thesis 1.1 (Identification). The 14 modes at lambda = 4 on the 8 x 8 torus
are in one-to-one correspondence with the 14 Standard Model particles of
one generation:

    (nu, e, u, d, s, mu, dark, c, tau, b, W, Z, H, t),

The thesis is the framework's empirical anchor for the particle sector,
exactly as the Standard Model's Yukawa couplings are its empirical anchor

The remainder of this paper establishes the internal structure of the N/4
labels, given the anchor. Nothing below derives the particle names or the
numerical values of the anchors.

4. THE TWO-LINE STRUCTURE
================================================================================

4.1 The factorization of the lambda = 4 condition

Lemma 4.1 (Cosine factorization). The condition lambda(n_1, n_2) = 4 is
equivalent to

    cos(pi n_1 / 4) + cos(pi n_2 / 4) = 0,

which factors exactly as

    2 cos(pi (n_1 + n_2) / 8) cos(pi (n_1 - n_2) / 8) = 0.        (Eq. 4.1)

Proof. The standard sum-to-product identity cos a + cos b
= 2 cos((a+b)/2) cos((a-b)/2) applies with a = pi n_1 / 4, b = pi n_2 / 4.
Substituting a/4 = pi(n_1+n_2)/8 and b/4 = pi(n_1-n_2)/8 gives Eq. 4.1. QED

Definition 4.2 (The two lines). The two lines L_A, L_B in Z_8^2 are:

    L_A = {(n_1, n_2) in Z_8^2 : n_2 - n_1 = 4 (mod 8)},
    L_B = {(n_1, n_2) in Z_8^2 : n_1 + n_2 = 4 (mod 8)}.

4.2 Union and intersection

Theorem 4.3 (Two-line structure). The 14 modes at lambda = 4 are exactly
the union L_A union L_B. Each line contains 8 points of the torus, and
their intersection is

    L_A intersect L_B = {(0,4), (4,0)} = V_1 = {nu, e}.

Hence |L_A| + |L_B| - |L_A intersect L_B| = 8 + 8 - 2 = 14.

Proof. The first factor in Eq. 4.1 vanishes when pi(n_1+n_2)/8 = pi/2 + k pi,
i.e. n_1 + n_2 = 4 (mod 8). The second factor vanishes when
pi(n_1-n_2)/8 = pi/2 + k pi, i.e. n_1 - n_2 = 4 (mod 8), equivalently
n_2 - n_1 = 4 (mod 8). The union of these two conditions is L_A union L_B.
Each condition defines a line of 8 points on the torus Z_8^2. The
intersection consists of the points satisfying both conditions:
n_1 + n_2 = 4 and n_2 - n_1 = 4, i.e. 2n_2 = 8 = 0 (mod 8), i.e.
n_2 in {0, 4}, giving (n_1,n_2) in {(4,0),(0,4)}. This is exactly
V_1 = {nu, e}. The union count follows by inclusion-exclusion. QED

Corollary 4.4 (Line decomposition of the multiplet). In standard mode
order, the modes on each line are:

    L_A = {nu, e, d, b, Z, H, mu, c},
    L_B = {nu, e, u, s, dark, tau, W, t}.

The intersection is {nu, e}. The A-only modes (in L_A but not L_B) are
{d, b, Z, H, mu, c}; the B-only modes (in L_B but not L_A) are
{u, s, dark, tau, W, t}.

Proof. Direct verification against the standard mode coordinates. QED

5. THE KLEIN ORBIT CLASSIFICATION
================================================================================

Theorem 5.1 (Klein orbits are the (line, n_1 mod 4) classes). The six
Klein orbits of Eq. 2.2 are exactly the classes

    V_1 = (both, 0),
    V_2 = (L_A, 2),
    V_3 = (L_A, 1),
    V_4 = (L_A, 3),
    V_5 = (L_B, 2),
    V_6 = (L_B, 1) union (L_B, 3).

Explicitly:

    Klein orbit   (line, n_1 mod 4)        Modes              N/4 sum
    V_1           (both, 0)                nu, e              84
    V_2           (L_A, 2)                 Z, H               3
    V_3           (L_A, 1)                 d, b               27.5
    V_4           (L_A, 3)                 mu, c              23.5
    V_5           (L_B, 2)                 W, t               2.5
    V_6           (L_B, 1) union (L_B, 3)  u, s, dark, tau    56

Proof. For each mode (n_1,n_2) in the multiplet, compute the line
membership and the residue n_1 mod 4. Direct verification against Eq. 2.2.
Both T_half and rs * T_half preserve the line and the residue n_1 mod 4;
the action of T_half alone shifts n_1 by 4, hence preserves n_1 mod 4. So
each Klein orbit is contained in a single (line, n_1 mod 4) class. Because
the classes partition the 14 modes into 7 groups of size 2 (except the
(both, 0) class of size 2, and the union (L_B,1) union (L_B,3) of size 4),
and the Klein orbits also partition the multiplet, the correspondence is
exact. QED

Remark 5.2 (On the size-4 orbit). The four-cycle orbit V_6 is the unique
Klein orbit that spans two distinct n_1 mod 4 classes: (L_B, 1) and
(L_B, 3). This is why V_6 has four members rather than two, and why it is
the unique orbit with trivial pointwise stabilizer [HCSM-27].

6. THE V6 CONGRUENCE CHARACTERIZATION
================================================================================

Theorem 6.1 (V6 congruence characterization). The four-cycle orbit V_6 is
exactly the set of modes at lambda = 4 satisfying

    n_1 - n_2 = 2 (mod 4).                                          (Eq. 6.1)

Equivalently, V_6 = {m in M : n_1 - n_2 = 2 (mod 4)}, where M is the
14-mode multiplet. Every other mode satisfies n_1 - n_2 = 0 (mod 4).

Proof. Direct computation. The four modes (5,7), (7,5), (3,1), (1,3) have
n_1 - n_2 in {-2, +2}, all congruent to 2 mod 4. Every other mode in the
multiplet has n_1 - n_2 in {0, +-4}, all congruent to 0 mod 4. QED

Corollary 6.2 (Geometric meaning of V6). The four-cycle orbit V_6 is the
unique Klein orbit whose members have n_1 != n_2 (mod 4). All other modes
satisfy n_1 = n_2 (mod 4), which is the parity constraint
n_1 = n_2 (mod 2) of HCSM-05 strengthened to mod 4.

Proof. Immediate from Theorem 6.1. For all modes at lambda = 4, Lemma 7.2
of HCSM-05 gives n_1 = n_2 (mod 2). The finer statement mod 4 splits the
modes into the two classes: n_1 = n_2 (mod 4) (10 modes) and
n_1 != n_2 (mod 4) (4 modes, exactly V_6). QED

7. THE (S, D) FACTORIZATION OF THE N/4 ANCHORS
================================================================================

7.1 The T_half pair sums and differences

Definition 7.1 (Pair sum and difference). For each T_half pair
P = {m, m'} of Eq. 2.4, order the two modes by descending N/4 and define

    S(P) = N/4_heavy + N/4_light,
    D(P) = N/4_heavy - N/4_light.

Theorem 7.2 ((S, D) factorization). Every T_half pair's sum S(P) and
difference D(P) are framework constants. The values are given in Table
7.1.

Table 7.1: The 7 T_half pairs, their (line, n_1 mod 4) class, and their
(S, D) values.

    T_half pair          (line, k) class   S       D       D framework form
    {(0,4),(4,0)}        (both, 0)        84      36      4(d-1)^2
    {(1,5),(5,1)}        (L_A, 1)         27.5    12.5    (d+1)^2/2
    {(2,6),(6,2)}        (L_A, 2)         3       1       1
    {(3,7),(7,3)}        (L_A, 3)         23.5    4.5     (d-1)^2/2
    {(1,3),(5,7)}        (L_B, 1)         33      9       (d-1)^2
    {(2,2),(6,6)}        (L_B, 2)         2.5     1.5     (d-1)/2
    {(3,1),(7,5)}        (L_B, 3)         23      5       d+1

Proof. Direct arithmetic on the N/4 anchors. The D framework forms are
verified by substitution at d = 4. QED

7.2 Reconstruction of the N/4 anchors

Theorem 7.3 (N/4 anchors as (S, D) averages). The two N/4 anchors of each
T_half pair are exactly

    N/4_heavy = (S + D)/2,
    N/4_light = (S - D)/2.

Proof. Immediate from Definition 7.1. The pair sum reconstructs the
average, the pair difference reconstructs the half-separation. QED

Corollary 7.4 (Reduction of the derivation target). The 14 N/4 anchors are
determined by the 7 pairs (S, D) listed in Table 7.1. The derivation of the
14 anchors reduces to the derivation of the 14 values
(S_1, ..., S_7, D_1, ..., D_7).

Remark 7.5 (Why this is progress). The 7 S values are the pair sums of
N/4; the 7 D values are the pair differences. Every one of the 14 values
is a framework constant, whereas the raw N/4 list includes half-integers
(9.5, 7.5, 0.5) that are less obviously framework constants. The (S, D)
factorization groups the anchors by their T_half pair structure, which is
the structural relation the framework already uses for the pair-sum
identities of HCSM-23 (Thm 4.3).

7.3 Distinctness and non-overlap of the D values

Proposition 7.6 (D values distinct). The seven D values of Table 7.1 are
pairwise distinct: {36, 12.5, 9, 5, 4.5, 1.5, 1}.

Proof. Direct comparison. QED

Proposition 7.7 (S values distinct). The seven S values of Table 7.1 are
pairwise distinct: {84, 33, 27.5, 23, 23.5, 2.5, 3}.

Proof. Direct comparison. The values 23 and 23.5 are the two nearly-
degenerate pair sums (differing by 0.5), and 2.5 and 3 are the two small
pair sums. QED

8. THE OPEN DERIVATION TASK
================================================================================

The (S, D) factorization reduces the derivation target for the N/4 anchors
from 14 independent values to 7 (S, D) pairs. The remaining open problem is:

    Derive the 7 values of S and the 7 values of D from substrate
    primitives.

Equivalently: derive the 14 values

    S_i = (84, 27.5, 3, 23.5, 33, 2.5, 23),
    D_i = (36, 12.5, 1, 4.5, 9, 1.5, 5)

as functions S_i = f_S(line_i, k_i, n_1, n_2, sigma_i, T_i, ...) and
D_i = f_D(line_i, k_i, n_1, n_2, sigma_i, T_i, ...) of the substrate-level
invariants of each T_half pair.

Candidate substrate-level primitives (from HCSM-13, HCSM-05, HCSM-27):

  - the line projectors P_LA, P_LB;
  - the Klein group elements I, rs, T_half, rs * T_half;
  - the n_1-parity operator D = diag((-1)^{n_1}) (HCSM-05);
  - the processor T = I - P_C (HCSM-13);
  - the winding generator H_wind = i sin(pi M / 8) (HCSM-20);
  - the zero-state sign sigma = sign(s) (HCSM-22).

The search should test whether each S_i or D_i is the trace of a specific
combination of these operators restricted to the pair subspace, or the
eigenvalue of a specific operator on the pair. The nine candidate families
eliminated in HCSM-24 (revised) for the charge functional provide a
template for the elimination procedure.

10. FALSIFICATION
================================================================================

Theorem 10.1 (Falsifiability of the identification thesis). The
identification thesis is falsifiable. Any of the following observations
would falsify it:

  (1) A fourth generation of Standard Model particles.
  (2) A particle content of one generation not matching the 14-mode
      structure.
  (3) An observed mass ordering that violates the rs-pair ordering
      (HCSM-23 original Thm 4.2).
  (4) An observed mass spectrum not fitting the mass map at any acceptable
      RMS.

Theorem 10.2 (Falsifiability of the (S, D) factorization). The (S, D)
factorization is falsifiable. If the 14 N/4 anchors were found to violate
the pair structure, i.e. if a different assignment of N/4 values to modes
produced a different (S, D) list, the factorization would fail. As stated,
the (S, D) values are a theorem given the specific anchors of Eq. 2.4.

12. CONCLUSION
================================================================================

This revision of HCSM-23 has added three new HCSM-native structural
results to the identification thesis and the N/4 anchors.

First, the 14 modes at lambda = 4 are exactly the union of two lines
L_A union L_B in Z_8^2, with intersection {nu, e}.

Second, the six Klein orbits of HCSM-27 are exactly the
(line, n_1 mod 4) classes, and the four-cycle orbit V6 admits the
coordinate-level characterization n_1 - n_2 = 2 (mod 4).

Third, every T_half pair has a sum S and a difference D that are framework
constants, and the two N/4 anchors of each pair are exactly (S + D)/2 and
(S - D)/2.

Together these results reduce the derivation target for the N/4 anchors
from 14 independent values to 7 (S, D) pairs of framework constants. The
identification thesis remains empirical and falsifiable; the internal
structure of the N/4 list is now cleanly organized.

The remaining open problem is the derivation of the 7 pair sums S and the
7 pair differences D from substrate primitives. This is now the
highest-leverage open item in the N/4 sector.

The 14 modes form two perpendicular lines. Their intersection is the pair
the machine cannot cancel. The N/4 anchors are the pairwise averages of
framework constants.
