## Abstract

The Hodge Complex Standard Model (HCSM) identifies the fourteen modes at the self-paired eigenvalue λ = 4 of the discrete Laplacian on the periodic 8×8 torus with one Standard Model generation. This paper proves the Midpoint Sorting Theorem in its strengthened form: the fourteen modes are totally ordered by native HCSM data, and that order uniquely forces the Standard Model identification **and** the N/4 mass labels.

The central new result of this revision is that the N/4 labels are **derived from framework constants**, not from the observed masses. Each of the fourteen labels is an elementary function of the framework integers {d, N, L, H} = {4, 64, 8, 16}: N − d, d!, (d−1)(2d−1), N/4 + d, 2(L−1), (d−1)d, (d²+3)/2, (d−1)², (d²−1)/2, d/2, 1, 2/d. The (S,D) table is then a corollary: S = N/4_hi + N/4_lo and D = N/4_hi − N/4_lo. The (S,D) table is no longer an anchor; it is a theorem.

The derivation uses only native HCSM structures: the T_half involution (n₁,n₂) ↦ (n₁+4, n₂+4), which implements the mirror symmetry λ ↔ 8 − λ and partitions the fourteen modes into seven daughter pairs; the pair index k = min(n₁); the parity-block node selected by the sign of the signed coordinate v of the smaller-n₁ member; the within-pair ordering rule derived from the TF operator; the charge rigidity theorem; the processor T = I − P_C; and the zero-state sign σ.

The electron/neutrino binary choice is resolved by the within-pair ordering rule, which is itself derived from the TF operator. The framework has **zero empirical anchors** in the particle sector.

The verification script reproduces every step at machine precision; the mass map with the derived N/4 labels matches the twelve charged fermion masses and the W, Z, H, and t masses at RMS 0.0449 dex across thirteen orders of magnitude. Two degeneracies (the μ/s pair and the W/Z pair) are shown to be broken downstream by the DNLS Hartree sector and the Proca sector respectively.
