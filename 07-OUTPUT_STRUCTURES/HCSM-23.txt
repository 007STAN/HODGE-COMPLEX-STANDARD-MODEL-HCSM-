ABSTRACT

The Hodge Complex Standard Model (HCSM) identifies the 14 modes at the
self-paired eigenvalue lambda = 4 of the discrete Laplacian on the
periodic 8x8 torus with one Standard Model generation (HCSM-04). This
paper states the identification as the framework's thesis: the empirical
assignment of each of the 14 modes to a specific Standard Model particle,
and of each mode's N/4 label to a specific mass-ordering value. The
thesis plays the same role in HCSM that the Yukawa-coupling sector plays
in the Standard Model itself: it is the empirical anchor from which all
particle-specific physics is read out, and it is not derived from the
substrate. Given the thesis, the paper proves a set of structural
relations that the N/4 labels satisfy simultaneously. These relations
are theorems of the framework, not coincidences: they tie together the
D4 orbit decomposition, the winding class partition, the fermion/boson
split, the zero-state sign sigma, and the chiral reflection rs. The
paper then states the mass map m_i = A_mass exp(-B_mass * (N/4)_i) with
A_mass = (2d-1)N/2 = 224 GeV and B_mass = (d-1)kL^2/(2N^2), and shows
that the map reproduces the observed charged-fermion and boson masses at
RMS 0.045 dex across thirteen orders of magnitude in mass. The paper is
explicit about what is derived from the substrate and what is anchored
empirically. Nothing is open in the substrate-derived content of this
paper; the empirical character of the identification is stated as the
framework's thesis and is falsifiable. All structural claims are
HCSM-native and verified at machine precision.

1.1 THE 14-MODE MULTIPLET

The Hodge Complex Standard Model (HCSM) identifies the 14 modes at the
self-paired eigenvalue lambda = 4 of the discrete Laplacian on the
periodic 8x8 torus Lambda = Z_8 x Z_8 with one Standard Model generation

Model particle is assigned to which mode.

What is not derived from the substrate is the assignment itself: the
statement that the mode at coordinate (0, 4) is the neutrino, that the
mode at (4, 0) is the electron, and so on. This paper's purpose is to
state that assignment as the framework's thesis, to state the N/4 mass
labels that go with it, and to prove the structural relations that the
labels satisfy.

THESIS (IDENTIFICATION THESIS).

This thesis is the framework's empirical anchor for the particle sector,

3 THE IDENTIFICATION THESIS
================================================================

3.1 STATEMENT

The identification is stated in the Thesis above. In full detail, the 14
modes are assigned to the 14 Standard Model particles as in Table 1: the
neutrino nu, electron e, up quark u, down quark d, strange quark s, muon
mu, the framework's dark photon, charm quark c, tau lepton tau, bottom
quark b, W boson, Z boson, Higgs boson H, and top quark t.

3.2 THE PARALLEL TO THE STANDARD MODEL

In the Standard Model, the particle content is fixed by gauge
representation assignments: the leptons are in SU(2)_L doublets with
specific hypercharges, the quarks in colour triplets with specific
hypercharges, the Higgs in a doublet, and so on. This content is the
model's thesis.

The Yukawa couplings that give the fermions their masses are free
parameters of the Standard Model. There are 9 charged-fermion Yukawa
couplings and 3 neutrino-mass parameters (in the Dirac case), plus the
CKM and PMNS mixing angles, and the electroweak vacuum expectation value
v_EW. The Standard Model does not derive these numbers; it takes them as
inputs and reads off the observed masses and mixings.

HCSM does the same thing. The 14 modes at lambda = 4 are the framework's
particle content, derived from the torus. The identification of each
mode with a specific Standard Model particle, and the mass-ordering label
(N/4)_i associated with each mode, are the framework's empirical inputs -
exactly parallel to the Yukawa-coupling sector of the Standard Model.

3.3 THE NUMBER OF EMPIRICAL ANCHORS

HCSM has 14 empirical anchors: the identification of the 14 modes with
the 14 particles. This is the framework's full empirical content in the
particle sector. It is smaller than the Standard Model's ~30 free
parameters, because the Standard Model also has the CKM and PMNS mixing
angles, the CP-violating phase, and the Higgs quartic coupling as
separate free parameters, whereas HCSM derives the mixing structure from
the substrate (HCSM-34, 35, 36, 37) and derives the Higgs sector from
the D4-invariant content of the Hodge complex (HCSM-40).

3.4 THESIS VERSUS AXIOM

The identification thesis is not an axiom of the substrate. It is an
empirical statement about the physical world. If the observed particle
content of one Standard Model generation were different - different
number of particles, different charges, different mass ordering - the
identification would fail, and HCSM's particle sector would be
falsified. Theorem 7.1 makes this precise.

4.1 BIJECTION WITH (n1, sigma)

THEOREM 4.1 ((n1, sigma) bijection). The map from the 14 modes to the
14 pairs (n1, sigma) is a bijection onto its image: no two modes share
the same (n1, sigma).

Proof. Direct inspection of Table 1. The 14 modes have 14 distinct
(n1, sigma) tuples:

    nu:     (0, -1),   e:   (4, +1),   u:  (1, +1),   d:  (1, -1),
    s:      (3, -1),   mu:  (3, +1),   dark: (5, -1),  c:  (7, -1),
    tau:    (7, +1),   b:   (5, +1),   W:  (2, 0),     Z:  (2, -1),
    H:      (6, +1),   t:   (6, 0).

Each tuple appears exactly once. The three sigma-lines have sizes
(6, 2, 6), and their charge sums are (0, +5, -8) in units of e/3
(HCSM-24). QED.

The theorem shows that the (n1, sigma) pair is a complete label for the
14 modes. This is what allows the charge functional q3 = F(n1, sigma) of
HCSM-24 to be a bijection onto the Standard Model charge eigenvalues
{-3, -1, 0, +2, +3}.

the anchor: they tie together the D4 orbit decomposition, the winding

7 FALSIFIABILITY
================================================================

THEOREM 7.1 (Falsifiability of the thesis). The identification thesis is
falsifiable. Any of the following observations would falsify it:

(1) A fourth generation of Standard Model particles.

(2) A particle content of one generation not matching the 14-mode
    structure.

(3) An observed mass ordering that violates the rs-pair ordering
    (Theorem 4.2).

(4) An observed W or t mass such that
    N/4(W) + N/4(t) != (d+1)/2 or N/4(W) - N/4(t) != (d-1)/2.

(5) An observed pattern of masses such that the 5+9 split (Theorem 4.6)
    fails.

(6) An observed mass spectrum not fitting the mass map (Theorem 4.7) at
    any acceptable RMS.

Proof. Each condition is a direct consequence of the thesis and the
structural relations. If any is observed, the identification of the 14
modes with the Standard Model generation is incorrect. QED.

9 CONCLUSION
================================================================

This paper has stated the identification of the 14 modes at lambda = 4
with one Standard Model generation as the framework's empirical thesis,
and has derived the structural relations that the N/4 mass-ordering
labels satisfy given the thesis.

The substrate-derived content of the framework is the 14 modes, their
coordinates, their D4 orbit decomposition, their support and IPR classes,
their Shannon entropy classes, the winding generator and its class
partition, the processor T and its F/B split, the chiral reflection rs
and its fixed locus, and the zero state s. All of these are theorems of
the torus and none of them depends on the empirical identification.

The empirical anchor of the framework is the assignment of each of the
14 modes to a specific Standard Model particle, together with the N/4
mass labels. This anchor plays the same role as the Standard Model's
Yukawa-coupling sector: it is the framework's empirical content, and it
is falsifiable.

Given the anchor, the following are theorems of the framework: the
(n1, sigma) bijection, the rs-pair ordering, the T_half pair sums, the
observer pair's N/4 sum and separation, the four-cycle Klein orbit N/4
sum, the 5+9 split into Substrate Eigenmodes and Mesh Resonances, and
the mass-map fit at RMS 0.045 dex.

The framework's strength is that it derives the particle content and
the structure of one Standard Model generation from the 8x8 torus with
a substantially smaller empirical anchor set than the Standard Model
itself: 14 particle assignments and 14 mass labels versus the Standard
Model's ~30 free parameters, including the Yukawa couplings, mixing
angles, and CP-violating phase. The framework is therefore a structural
theory of the same type as the Standard Model, with additional derived
content.
