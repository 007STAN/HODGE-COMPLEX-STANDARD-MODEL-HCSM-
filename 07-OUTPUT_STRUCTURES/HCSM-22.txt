ABSTRACT
================================================================================

The Hodge Complex Standard Model identifies the 14 modes at the self-paired
eigenvalue lambda = 4 of the discrete Laplacian on the periodic 8 x 8 torus
with one Standard Model generation (HCSM-04). This paper establishes the zero
state in two parts: its exact algebraic structure, and its variational
selection as the vacuum.

PART I: STRUCTURE. The 19 vacuum-native observables (support, IPR, entropy,
N/4, delta, shell, charge, three pressure components, helicity-flip
probability, five geometric labels, and three velocity components) form a
14 x 19 matrix M. We prove that rank(M) = 13 exactly and nullity(M^T) = 1:
there is exactly one 14-vector s (up to scale) satisfying M^T s = 0. This is
the zero state. Its three independent amplitudes a_2, a_4, a_6 lie in
Q(sqrt(2)) with closed forms

    a_2 = (80643 + 32544*sqrt(2)) / 135079
    a_4 = (6480 + 26856*sqrt(2)) / 135079
    a_6 = (20014 - 4104*sqrt(2)) / 135079

and exact ratios a_3 = 2*a_4, a_5 = (2 - sqrt(2))*a_4. The minimal polynomials
of a_2, a_4, a_6 are quadratic over Q. Four structural properties follow: s is
odd under the chiral reflection rs; the two rs-fixed modes W = (2,2) and
t = (6,6) have exactly zero amplitude; the |s|^2-mass distribution is 0.45%,
95.17%, 4.39% over the three D4 orbits; and the entropy column is exactly
redundant with the IPR column.

PART II: SELECTION. We prove that the vacuum of the 14-mode multiplet is the
unique state minimizing ||M^T s||^2 subject to ||s|| = 1. Two independent
derivations are given. The Landauer route uses the processor T = I - P_C at
the deterministic Landauer bound: the vacuum is the state of minimum
information, which is exactly M^T s = 0. The entropy-well route uses the
HCSM-54 entropy well S(epsilon)/S_0 = 1 - gamma*epsilon^2 + O(epsilon^4)
with the identification epsilon = ||M^T s||: the entropy is maximized exactly
when M^T s = 0. The two routes are dual descriptions of the same
minimum-information principle. Uniqueness of the maximizer follows from
rank(M) = 13 and nullity(M^T) = 1.

The stability of the zero state under the DNLS Hartree evolution is
established: it is a stationary point of the energy functional, not an
eigenstate of H_DNLS, and decays to zero under iteration of the loop operator
M_loop.

All claims are HCSM-native and verified at machine precision.

11. THE SELECTION PROBLEM

Part I establishes the zero state as the unique nullvector of M^T. It does
not explain why this nullvector is the physical vacuum. Part II supplies the
selection rule.

The physical principle is: the vacuum is the state of minimum information -

Two independent derivations are given. The Landauer route uses only the

theorem, not a coincidence.

    s = argmin_{||s||=1} ||M^T s||^2.

13. THE LANDAUER ROUTE
================================================================================

THEOREM 13.1 (Landauer selection). The processor T operates at the
deterministic Landauer bound. The vacuum is the state of minimum information.
Minimum information is exactly M^T s = 0. Hence the vacuum is the zero state.

Proof: Three steps.

(i) The machine operates at the Landauer bound. By HCSM-21 Thm 12.1, the
loop-pass duration is T_DME = 1/(2*gamma), the unique dissipative scale in
HCSM. By HCSM-13 Prop 3.4, the processor reduces the entropy of the
maximally mixed state on the 56-dimensional Hodge sector to the entropy of
the maximally mixed state on the fermion-boson product:

    Delta S = log 56 - log 40 = log(7/5).

This saturates the deterministic Landauer bound.

(ii) The machine reads the multiplet through the 19 observables. The
expectation value of observable O_a in a state s is

    <O_a>_s = sum_i O_a(m_i) s_i = (M^T s)_a.

(iii) The vacuum is the state of minimum information. A state s with
<O_a>_s != 0 for some a is a state from which the machine extracts
information. The vacuum is the state from which no information is extracted:

    <O_a>_s = 0 for all a = 1, ..., 19.

This is exactly M^T s = 0. By Corollary 5.2 of Part I, the unique solution
is the zero state. QED.

14. THE ENTROPY-WELL ROUTE
================================================================================

THEOREM 14.1 (Entropy-well selection). Identifying the dimensional deviation
epsilon = D - 4 with the observable magnitude ||M^T s||, the entropy well of
HCSM-54 becomes

    S(s)/S_0 = 1 - gamma*||M^T s||^2 + O(||M^T s||^4),

and maximizing S(s) is equivalent to M^T s = 0.

Proof: Three steps.

Step 1: Bridge identification. The dimensional deviation epsilon = D - 4 is
a scalar on the multiplet with the properties:
    (a) D4-invariant
    (b) non-negative
    (c) vanishes exactly at the vacuum.

The observable magnitude ||M^T s|| has all three properties: (a) M is built
from D4-invariant observables; (b) non-negative by definition of the
Euclidean norm; (c) vanishes exactly when M^T s = 0. The identification is
therefore unique up to a positive multiplicative constant, which is fixed to
unity by the normalization S(0)/S_0 = 1:

    epsilon = ||M^T s||.

Step 2: Substitution. The HCSM-54 entropy well is

    S(epsilon)/S_0 = 1 - gamma*epsilon^2 + O(epsilon^4),

with gamma = c_2/I(2) the stability coefficient. Substituting
epsilon = ||M^T s|| gives the state-dependent form.

Step 3: Extremization. Since gamma > 0 and ||M^T s||^2 >= 0 with equality
iff M^T s = 0, the functional S(s) is maximized exactly on the nullspace of
M^T. By Theorem 5.1, the nullspace is 1-dimensional, and the unique maximizer
is the zero state. QED.

COROLLARY 14.2 (Vacuum entropy maximum). S(s_zero)/S_0 = 1 exactly. For any
other state s != +-s_zero, S(s)/S_0 < 1.

18. FALSIFICATION TESTS
================================================================================

(1) Vacuum with nonzero observable. A confirmed vacuum with <O_a> != 0 for
    some vacuum-native observable would falsify Theorem 13.1.

(2) Non-maximizer selection. A confirmed vacuum s with S(s)/S_0 < 1 while
    another state s' has S(s')/S_0 > S(s)/S_0 would falsify Theorem 14.1.

(3) Entropy-well deviation. A measured S(epsilon)/S_0 != 1 - gamma*epsilon^2
    + O(epsilon^4) would falsify the identification epsilon = ||M^T s||.

(4) Rank-nullity violation. A measured rank(M) != 13 or nullity(M^T) != 1
    would falsify Theorem 16.1.

(5) Route disagreement. A demonstration that the Landauer route and the
    entropy-well route select different states would falsify Theorem 15.1.

       functional, not an eigenstate of H_DNLS, and decays to zero under
       iteration of the loop operator.

The zero state is selected as the physical vacuum by the variational principle

    s = argmin_{||s||=1} ||M^T s||^2.

Two independent derivations establish this selection. The Landauer route uses
the processor T at the deterministic Landauer bound; the vacuum is the state
of minimum information. The entropy-well route uses the HCSM-54 entropy well
with the identification epsilon = ||M^T s||; the entropy is maximized exactly
when M^T s = 0. The two routes are dual descriptions of the same
minimum-information principle. Uniqueness of the maximizer follows from
rank(M) = 13 and nullity(M^T) = 1.

The zero state is the physical vacuum of the 14-mode multiplet.
