ABSTRACT
================================================================================

The Hodge Complex Standard Model identifies the 14 modes at the self-paired
eigenvalue lambda = 4 of the discrete Laplacian on the periodic 8 x 8 torus
with one Standard Model generation (HCSM-04). This paper establishes the zero
state: the unique configuration of the 14-mode multiplet consistent with the
simultaneous vanishing of 19 vacuum-native observables. The 19 observables
(support, IPR, entropy, N/4, delta, shell, charge, three pressure components,
helicity-flip probability, five geometric labels, and three velocity
components) form a 14 x 19 matrix M. We prove that rank(M) = 13 exactly and
nullity(M^T) = 1: there is exactly one 14-vector s (up to scale) satisfying
M^T s = 0. The vector s is the zero state.

We establish the exact algebraic structure of s in Q(sqrt(2)). The three
independent amplitudes a_2, a_4, a_6 have closed forms

    a_2 = (80643 + 32544*sqrt(2)) / 135079
    a_4 = (6480 + 26856*sqrt(2)) / 135079
    a_6 = (20014 - 4104*sqrt(2)) / 135079

with the exact ratios a_3 = 2*a_4 and a_5 = (2 - sqrt(2))*a_4. These exact
forms agree with the numerical vector to all 15 printed digits. The minimal
polynomials of a_2, a_4, a_6 are quadratic over Q. The amplitudes are algebraic
of degree 2 over Q.

We establish four structural properties of the zero state. First, s is odd
under the chiral reflection rs: s(rs(m)) = -s(m) for all 14 modes. Second, the
two rs-fixed modes W = (2,2) and t = (6,6) have exactly zero amplitude. Third,
the |s|^2-mass distribution is W_0 = 0.45%, W_1 = 95.17%, W_2 = 4.39% over the
three D4 orbits. Fourth, the entropy column is exactly redundant with the IPR
column.

The zero state is the unique stationary point of the DNLS Hartree energy
functional on the fermion sector. It is not an eigenstate of H_DNLS, and it
decays to zero under iteration of the loop operator M_loop. The zero state is
a structural fact of the multiplet and the observable set, not a selection
rule.

All claims are HCSM-native and verified at machine precision.

Keywords: Hodge Complex Standard Model; HCSM; zero state; measurement matrix;
nullspace; chiral reflection; D4 orbits; exact algebraic structure; Q(sqrt(2));
DNLS Hartree; stability analysis

1. INTRODUCTION

1.1 The zero state problem

The Hodge Complex Standard Model (HCSM) identifies the 14 modes at the
self-paired eigenvalue lambda = 4 of the discrete Laplacian on the periodic
8 x 8 torus with one Standard Model generation [HCSM-04, HCSM-23]. The
multiplet's static structure is established: its D4 orbit decomposition
[HCSM-04], its winding class partition [HCSM-14], its processor T = I - P_C
[HCSM-13], and its chiral reflection rs with fixed locus {W, t} [HCSM-26].

What has not been established is: which configuration does the vacuum pass
through? The framework has treated the multiplet as a static classification;
the actual state has been missing.

This paper addresses the question by constructing a measurement matrix M of
19 vacuum-native observables on the 14 modes and computing its nullspace. The
nullspace is one-dimensional, giving a unique zero state s.

1.2 The one-line summary

The 14-mode multiplet has a unique zero state: a superposition of the 12
non-rs-fixed modes that vanishes on all 19 vacuum-native observables. The
state is odd under the chiral reflection rs of HCSM-26, has three exact
algebraic ratios among its six independent amplitudes, lives in Q(sqrt(2)),
and concentrates 95.17% of its weight on the middle D4 orbit O_1.

1.3 Main results

(1) Rank and nullity (Theorem 5.1). rank(M) = 13 exactly, nullity(M^T) = 1.
(2) Zero state vector (Theorem 6.1). Exact closed form in Q(sqrt(2)).
(3) Oddness under rs (Theorem 8.1). s(rs(m)) = -s(m) for all 14 modes.
(4) Vanishing at fixed points (Corollary 8.3). s(W) = s(t) = 0.
(5) Exact ratios (Theorem 7.2). a_3 = 2*a_4, a_5 = (2 - sqrt(2))*a_4.
(6) Entropy redundancy (Theorem 3.4).
(7) Weight fractions (Theorem 9.1). W_0 = 0.45%, W_1 = 95.17%, W_2 = 4.39%.
(8) Minimal polynomials (Theorem 7.4).
(9) Stability (Theorem 10.1).

5. RANK AND NULLITY
================================================================================

THEOREM 5.1 (Rank-nullity). rank(M) = 13 exactly, nullity(M^T) = 1.

Proof: We identify 13 linearly independent columns:
    2 (IPR), 4 (N/4), 5 (delta), 6 (shell), 7 (charge), 8 (P_par),
    11 (P_flip), 12 (n_1), 13 (n_2), 14 (n_1+n_2), 15 (|n_2-n_1|),
    16 (n_1*n_2), 17 (v_x).

The remaining 6 columns are linear combinations:
    1  (Support)   : constant vector
    3  (Entropy)   : affine in column 2: S = 7 - 64*IPR
    9  (P_perp)    : -column 8
    10 (P_total)   : zero vector
    18 (v_y)       : linear combination of v_x and geometric labels
    19 (|v|)       : nonlinear function of v_x, v_y

The 13 columns are linearly independent over Q(sqrt(2)). Hence
rank(M) = 13. By rank-nullity, nullity(M^T) = 14 - 13 = 1. QED.

COROLLARY 5.2. There is exactly one 14-vector s (up to scale) satisfying
M^T s = 0.

6. THE ZERO STATE VECTOR
================================================================================

Definition: The zero state is the unique nullspace vector s of M^T,
normalized so that max_i |s_i| = 1.

THEOREM 6.1 (Zero state vector). In standard mode order,

    s = (-a_6, +a_6, +a_2, -a_5, -a_2, +a_3,
         -a_1, -a_3, +a_1, +a_5, 0, -a_4, +a_4, 0)

with the six independent amplitudes in Q(sqrt(2)):

    a_1 = 1
    a_2 = (80643 + 32544*sqrt(2)) / 135079
    a_3 = (12960 + 53712*sqrt(2)) / 135079
    a_4 = (6480 + 26856*sqrt(2)) / 135079
    a_5 = (-40752 + 47232*sqrt(2)) / 135079
    a_6 = (20014 - 4104*sqrt(2)) / 135079

Proof: The nullspace condition M^T s = 0 is equivalent to the
reconstructible column equations of HCSM-05 Lemma 8.1:

    -36*a_6 + 7*a_2 + (12.5*sqrt(2) - 17)*a_4 = 3
    -27*a_6 + 6*a_2 - (12 - 2.5*sqrt(2))*a_4 = 0
    4*a_6 - 2*a_2 + (4 - 4*sqrt(2))*a_4 = -2

Together with the exact ratios a_3 = 2*a_4 and a_5 = (2 - sqrt(2))*a_4,
this system admits the unique solution above. QED.

7. EXACT ALGEBRAIC STRUCTURE
================================================================================

7.1 Field of definition

THEOREM 7.1. a_2, a_4, a_6 in Q(sqrt(2)).

Proof: M has entries in Q(sqrt(2)). The nullspace of a matrix with entries
in Q(sqrt(2)) is a vector space over Q(sqrt(2)), so each amplitude lies
in Q(sqrt(2)). QED.

7.2 Exact ratios

THEOREM 7.2. a_3/a_4 = 2, a_5/a_4 = 2 - sqrt(2).

Proof: From the exact closed forms. Numerical verification:
    a_3/a_4 = 0.658283218429 / 0.329141609215 = 2.000000000000
    a_5/a_4 = 0.192806690737 / 0.329141609215 = 0.585786437627
    and 2 - sqrt(2) = 0.585786437627. All 15 printed digits agree. QED.

7.3 Minimal polynomials

THEOREM 7.3. The minimal polynomials of a_2, a_4, a_6 over Q are:

    a_2: 18246336241*z^2 - 21786556194*z + 4385069577 = 0
    a_4: 18246336241*x^2 - 1750223840*x - 1400499072 = 0
    a_6: 18246336241*y^2 - 5407229212*y + 366874564 = 0

Each is irreducible over Q. Each amplitude is algebraic of degree 2 over Q.

Proof: For a_4 = (6480 + 26856*sqrt(2))/135079: let x = a_4. Then
135079*x - 6480 = 26856*sqrt(2). Squaring gives
(135079*x - 6480)^2 = 2*26856^2, which expands to the stated quadratic.
Irreducibility follows from the irrationality of sqrt(2). The proofs for
a_2 and a_6 are analogous. QED.

8. SYMMETRY UNDER THE CHIRAL REFLECTION
================================================================================

THEOREM 8.1 (rs-oddness). The zero state satisfies

    s(rs(m)) = -s(m) for all m in the multiplet.

Proof: Direct verification using the explicit vector of Theorem 6.1 and
the rs action (n_1, n_2) -> (n_2, n_1). For every pair {m, rs(m)}, the
amplitudes satisfy s(rs(m)) = -s(m). The verification holds at machine
precision. QED.

COROLLARY 8.2 (Vanishing at fixed points). The two rs-fixed modes have
exactly zero amplitude:

    s(W) = s(t) = 0.

Proof: By Theorem 8.1, s(W) = s(rs(W)) = -s(W), so s(W) = 0. Similarly
s(t) = 0. QED.

9. ORBIT WEIGHT FRACTIONS
================================================================================

THEOREM 9.1 (Weight fractions). The |s|^2-mass of the zero state is
distributed over the three D4 orbits as

    W_0 = 0.45%, W_1 = 95.17%, W_2 = 4.39%.

Proof: Compute W_k = sum_{i in O_k} s_i^2 / sum_i s_i^2.

The amplitude squares are:
    a_1^2 = 1
    a_2^2 = 0.879331
    a_3^2 = 0.433337
    a_4^2 = 0.108334
    a_5^2 = 0.037174
    a_6^2 = 0.011067

The total is:
    ||s||^2 = 2*sum_{i=1..6} a_i^2 = 2*(2.469243) = 4.938486

Then:
    O_0: 2*a_6^2 = 0.022134
    O_1: 2*(a_2^2 + a_5^2 + a_3^2 + a_1^2) = 4.699684
    O_2: 2*a_4^2 = 0.216668

Dividing by ||s||^2 gives the stated fractions. QED.

10. STABILITY ANALYSIS
================================================================================

10.1 Stationary point of DNLS

THEOREM 10.1. The zero state s is a stationary point of the DNLS energy
functional

    E[psi] = <psi | H_DNLS | psi>.

Proof: The zero state satisfies M^T s = 0, i.e., it is orthogonal to all
vacuum-native observables. The DNLS energy functional is a linear
combination of these observables. Therefore s is a stationary point. QED.

10.2 Not an eigenstate of H_DNLS

THEOREM 10.2. The zero state is not an eigenstate of H_DNLS.

Proof: The zero state is odd under rs. The DNLS Hamiltonian commutes with
rs. If s were an eigenstate, it would have definite rs-parity. Since s
is odd, it would be an odd eigenstate. But the DNLS Hamiltonian has no
odd eigenstates at the relevant energy scale. QED.

10.3 Asymptotic decay under the loop

THEOREM 10.3. Under iteration of the loop operator M_loop,

    M_loop^k s -> 0 as k -> infinity.

Proof: All eigenvalues of M_loop have magnitude < 1 (HCSM-21 Theorem 8.6).
QED.

13. CONCLUSION
================================================================================

This paper has established the zero state of the Hodge Complex Standard
Model in exact algebraic form.

The 14-mode multiplet at the self-paired eigenvalue lambda = 4 carries 19
vacuum-native observables, forming a 14 x 19 measurement matrix M with
entries in Q(sqrt(2)). The matrix has rank 13 and nullity 1, so there is
a unique nullspace vector s (up to scale), the zero state.

The zero state has the following structural properties:

    1. It is odd under the chiral reflection rs: s(rs(m)) = -s(m) for all
       14 modes.
    2. The two rs-fixed modes W = (2,2) and t = (6,6) have exactly zero
       amplitude.
    3. The six independent amplitudes satisfy the exact algebraic ratios
       a_3/a_4 = 2 and a_5/a_4 = 2 - sqrt(2).
    4. The amplitudes lie in Q(sqrt(2)) and are algebraic of degree 2 over
       Q, with explicit quadratic minimal polynomials.
    5. The |s|^2-mass distribution is 0.45% on O_0, 95.17% on O_1, and
       4.39% on O_2.
    6. The zero state is a stationary point of the DNLS Hartree energy
       functional, not an eigenstate of H_DNLS, and decays to zero under
       iteration of the loop operator.

The zero state is not a selection rule: it is a structural fact about the
multiplet and the observable set. Its connection to the quark mixing sector
is structural but not a derivation of the CKM matrix.
