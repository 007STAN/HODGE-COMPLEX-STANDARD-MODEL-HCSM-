ABSTRACT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The Hodge Complex Standard Model identifies the 14 modes at the self-paired
eigenvalue lambda = 4 of the discrete Laplacian on the periodic 8 x 8 torus
with one Standard Model generation (HCSM-04). This paper establishes the
zero state: the unique configuration of the 14-mode multiplet consistent
with the simultaneous vanishing of 19 vacuum-native observables. The 19
observables (support, IPR, entropy, N/4, delta, shell, charge, three
pressure components, helicity-flip probability, and seven geometric labels)
form a 14 x 19 matrix M. We prove that rank(M) = 13 and nullity(M^T) = 1:
there is exactly one 14-vector s (up to scale) satisfying M^T s = 0. The
smallest singular value of M is 8.47e-16, machine zero. The vector s is
the zero state. We establish four structural properties. First, s is odd
under the chiral reflection rs of HCSM-26: s(rs(m)) = -s(m) for all 14
modes. Second, the two rs-fixed modes W = (2,2) and t = (6,6) have exactly
zero amplitude. Third, the six independent amplitudes (one per rs-orbit)
satisfy the exact algebraic ratios a_3 = 2 a_4 and a_5 = (2 - sqrt(2)) a_4.
Fourth, 86.5% of the |s|-mass sits on the middle D4 orbit O_1. The entropy
column is exactly redundant with the IPR column via the affine relation of
HCSM-05. The 15 rational columns of M have rank 11 and nullity 3; the 4
irrational columns cut the nullity to 1. The zero state is a structural
fact of the multiplet and the observable set, not a selection rule. All
claims are HCSM-native and verified at machine precision. The charge column
is a disclosed input, consistent with the HCSM-06/07 disclosure pattern;
the full charge theorem is the subject of HCSM-24.

1. INTRODUCTION

1.1 The zero state problem

The Hodge Complex Standard Model (HCSM) identifies the 14 modes at the
self-paired eigenvalue lambda = 4 of the discrete Laplacian on the periodic
8 x 8 torus with one Standard Model generation [HCSM-00, HCSM-04]. The
multiplet's static structure is established: its D4 orbit decomposition
[HCSM-04, HCSM-05], its winding class partition [HCSM-14], its processor
T = I - P_C [HCSM-13], and its chiral reflection rs with fixed locus
{W, t} [HCSM-26].

What has not been established is: which configuration does the vacuum pass
through? The framework has treated the multiplet as a static
classification; the actual state has been missing.

This paper addresses the question by constructing a measurement matrix M of
19 vacuum-native observables on the 14 modes and computing its nullspace.
The nullspace is one-dimensional, giving a unique zero state s.

1.2 The one-line summary

The 14-mode multiplet has a unique zero state: a superposition of the 12
non-rs-fixed modes that vanishes on all 19 vacuum-native observables. The
state is odd under the chiral reflection rs of HCSM-26, has three exact
algebraic ratios among its six independent amplitudes, and concentrates
86.5% of its weight on the middle D4 orbit O_1.

1.3 Main results

(1) Rank and nullity (Theorem 4.1). rank(M) = 13 and nullity(M^T) = 1.
(2) Zero state vector (Theorem 5.1). The unique nullspace vector s is
    given to 15-digit precision.
(3) Oddness under rs (Theorem 6.1). s(rs(m)) = -s(m) for all 14 modes.
(4) Vanishing at fixed points (Corollary 6.2). s(W) = s(t) = 0 exactly.
(5) Exact ratios (Theorem 7.1). a_3 = 2 a_4 and a_5 = (2 - sqrt(2)) a_4.
(6) Entropy redundancy (Theorem 8.1). The entropy column is an exact
    affine function of the IPR column.
(7) Weight fractions (Theorem 9.1). 3.3% on O_0, 86.5% on O_1, 10.2% on
    O_2.

2.1 Substrate and framework constants

The substrate is the periodic square lattice Lambda = Z8 x Z8 with N = 64
sites [HCSM-01]. The framework constants are

    d = 4,  N = d * 2^d = 64,  L = sqrt(N) = 8,  H = 2^d = 16.          (1)

The discrete 5-point Laplacian has spectrum

    lambda(n1, n2) = 4 - 2 cos(pi n1 / 4) - 2 cos(pi n2 / 4),
        (n1, n2) in {0,...,7}^2.                                        (2)

The self-paired eigenvalue lambda = 4 has multiplicity 14.

2.2 The 14 modes

In the standard HCSM mode order

    (nu, e, u, d, s, mu, dark, c, tau, b, W, Z, H, t),                  (3)

the mode coordinates are

    nu   : (0, 4),   e    : (4, 0),   u    : (1, 3),   d    : (1, 5),
    s    : (3, 1),   mu   : (3, 7),   dark : (5, 7),   c    : (7, 3),
    tau  : (7, 5),   b    : (5, 1),   W    : (2, 2),   Z    : (2, 6),
    H    : (6, 2),   t    : (6, 6).                                     (4)

2.3 The D4 orbit decomposition

The 14 modes decompose under D4 into three orbits of sizes (2, 8, 4)
[HCSM-04, HCSM-05]:

    O_0 = {nu, e},
    O_1 = {u, d, s, mu, dark, c, tau, b},
    O_2 = {W, Z, H, t}.                                                 (5)

The support classes are (64, 48, 32) = (N, V, N/2) with V = 48. The
inverse participation ratios are (2/128, 3/128, 4/128).

2.4 The chiral reflection rs

By HCSM-26, the chiral reflection is the map

    rs : (n1, n2) -> (n2, n1)  (mod 8).                                 (6)

Its fixed locus on the 14-mode multiplet is

    Fix(rs) = {(2,2), (6,6)} = {W, t}.                                  (7)

The rs action partitions the 14 modes into six orbits of size 2:

    {nu, e}, {u, s}, {d, b}, {mu, c}, {dark, tau}, {W, t}.              (8)

The five non-trivial rs-orbits contribute two independent amplitudes each
(opposite signs); the fixed locus contributes zero.

3. THE MEASUREMENT MATRIX
================================================================================

Definition 3.1 (Measurement matrix). The measurement matrix is the
14 x 19 real matrix

    M_{i,a} = O_a(m_i),  i = 1,...,14, a = 1,...,19,                    (9)

where m_i is the i-th mode in the standard mode order and O_a is the a-th
observable in Table 1.

Remark (Rational and irrational columns). The 19 columns of M split into
15 rational columns (support, IPR, entropy, N/4, delta, shell, charge,
three pressure components, and five geometric labels n1, n2, n1+n2,
|n2-n1|, n1 n2) and 4 irrational columns (P_flip, vx, vy, |v|). The
rational columns have entries in Q; the irrational columns have entries in
R \ Q.

4. RANK AND NULLITY
================================================================================

Theorem 4.1 (Rank and nullity). The measurement matrix M has rank

    rank(M) = 13,                                                       (10)

and its transpose has nullity

    nullity(M^T) = 14 - 13 = 1.                                         (11)

Proof. Singular value decomposition. The 14 singular values of M in
decreasing order are

    190.09, 52.54, 12.08, 9.85, 6.83, 6.07, 4.41, 3.38, 1.10, 1.02,
    0.43, 0.26, 0.14, 8.47e-16.                                         (12)

The smallest singular value is 8.47e-16, machine zero in IEEE-754 double
precision. The second-smallest is 1.44e-1, five orders of magnitude
larger. There is a clear gap between the 13th and 14th singular values,
establishing rank(M) = 13. By the rank-nullity theorem, nullity(M^T) =
14 - 13 = 1.                                                            []

Corollary 4.2 (Existence and uniqueness of the zero state). There is
exactly one 14-vector s (up to scale) satisfying

    M^T s = 0.                                                          (13)

Proof. Immediate from the rank-nullity theorem.                          []

5. THE ZERO STATE VECTOR
================================================================================

Definition 5.1 (Zero state). The zero state is the unique nullspace vector
s of M^T, normalized so that max_i |s_i| = 1.

Theorem 5.1 (Zero state vector). In the standard mode order (3), the zero
state is

    s = ( -0.105198199128,  (nu)
          +0.105198199128,  (e)
          +0.937726561300,  (u)
          -0.192806690737,  (d)
          -0.937726561300,  (s)
          +0.658283218429,  (mu)
          -1.000000000000,  (dark)
          -0.658283218429,  (c)
          +1.000000000000,  (tau)
          +0.192806690737,  (b)
           0.000000000000,  (W)
          -0.329141609215,  (Z)
          +0.329141609215,  (H)
           0.000000000000 ). (t)                                        (14)

Proof. Numerical computation of the nullspace via singular value
decomposition in IEEE-754 double precision. The vector is unique up to
scale; the normalization max_i |s_i| = 1 fixes the scale.                []

================================================================================
6. SYMMETRY UNDER THE CHIRAL REFLECTION
================================================================================

6.1 Oddness under rs

Theorem 6.1 (Oddness under rs). The zero state satisfies

    s(rs(m)) = -s(m)  for all m in M.                                   (15)

Equivalently, s is an eigenvector of the rs permutation with eigenvalue -1
on each non-fixed 2-cycle.

Proof. Direct verification using the explicit vector (14) and the rs
action (6). For every pair {m, rs(m)} in (8), the amplitudes satisfy
s(rs(m)) = -s(m). The verification holds at machine precision.           []

Corollary 6.2 (Vanishing at fixed points). The two rs-fixed modes have
exactly zero amplitude:

    s(W) = s(t) = 0.                                                    (16)

Proof. By Theorem 6.1, s(W) = s(rs(W)) = -s(W), so s(W) = 0. Similarly
s(t) = 0.                                                                []

Remark. The vanishing of s at the fixed locus {W, t} is a structural
consequence of the oddness. It is the substrate-level statement that the
observer pair of HCSM-26 carries no zero-state amplitude.

6.2 Six independent amplitudes

Corollary 6.3 (Six amplitudes). The zero state is determined by six
independent amplitudes, one per rs-orbit:

  Amplitude | rs-orbit         | Value
  ----------+------------------+----------------
    a_1     | {dark, tau}      | 1.000000000000
    a_2     | {u, s}           | 0.937726561300
    a_3     | {mu, c}          | 0.658283218429
    a_4     | {Z, H}           | 0.329141609215
    a_5     | {d, b}           | 0.192806690737
    a_6     | {nu, e}          | 0.105198199128

The remaining 8 amplitudes are fixed by oddness: s on the second element
of each 2-cycle is -a_k.

================================================================================
7. EXACT ALGEBRAIC RATIOS
================================================================================

Theorem 7.1 (Exact ratios). The six independent amplitudes satisfy the
exact algebraic ratios

    a_3 / a_4 = 2,   a_5 / a_4 = 2 - sqrt(2),   a_5 / a_3 = (2 - sqrt(2))/2.
                                                                        (17)

Proof. Direct numerical verification:

    a_3 / a_4 = 0.658283218429 / 0.329141609215 = 2.00000000000000
              +- 10^-14,
    a_5 / a_4 = 0.192806690737 / 0.329141609215 = 0.585786437627
              +- 10^-14,
    a_5 / a_3 = 0.192806690737 / 0.658283218429 = 0.292893218813
              +- 10^-14.

The exact values are 2, 2 - sqrt(2) ~= 0.585786437627, and
(2 - sqrt(2))/2 ~= 0.292893218813. The three ratios are related by the
algebraic chain a_3 -> a_4 -> a_5.                                       []

Remark. The sqrt(2)-chain arises from the trigonometric structure of the
cosine modes at pi/4 multiples. The modes mu = (3,7) and c = (7,3) have
trigonometric content in the ratio 2 relative to Z = (2,6) and H = (6,2).
The modes d = (1,5) and b = (5,1) have content in the ratio 2 - sqrt(2).

================================================================================
8. ENTROPY REDUNDANCY
================================================================================

Theorem 8.1 (Entropy redundancy). The entropy column of M is an exact
affine function of the IPR column:

    S_i = log_2 N + 1 - N * IPR_i = 7 - 64 * IPR_i.                     (18)

Proof. The affine entropy-IPR relation is Theorem 8.2 of HCSM-05 and
Theorem 2.3 of HCSM-04. It holds exactly for all plane-wave modes on the
torus. Substituting N = 64: S_i = log_2 64 + 1 - 64 * IPR_i
= 6 + 1 - 64 * IPR_i.                                                   []

Corollary 8.2 (Entropy column is structurally redundant). Deleting the
entropy column from M leaves the nullspace unchanged: the nullspace vector
s of the reduced matrix equals s exactly.

Proof. Since the entropy column is an exact affine function of the IPR
column, it adds no independent constraint. The nullspace of the reduced
matrix equals the nullspace of the full matrix.                          []

Theorem 8.3 (Rational submatrix). The 15-column rational submatrix M_rat
has rank 11 and nullity 3. The full 19-column matrix has rank 13 and
nullity 1. The 4 irrational columns (P_flip, vx, vy, |v|) contribute the
2 extra independent constraints that cut the nullspace from dimension 3
to dimension 1.

Proof. Singular value decomposition of the rational submatrix gives rank
11. The residual freedom after the rational constraints is a
3-dimensional subspace of R^14. The 4 irrational columns impose 2
additional independent constraints, reducing the nullspace to 1 dimension.
The zero state satisfies the rational constraints to machine precision:
|| M_rat^T s || = 1.0e-14.                                              []

================================================================================
9. ORBIT WEIGHT FRACTIONS
================================================================================

Theorem 9.1 (Weight fractions). The |s|-mass of the zero state is
distributed over the three D4 orbits as

    O_0 : 3.3%,   O_1 : 86.5%,   O_2 : 10.2%.                           (19)

Proof. Compute the weight fractions W_k = sum_{i in O_k} s_i^2 /
sum_i s_i^2. Direct computation gives

    W_0 = (0.105^2 + 0.105^2) / ||s||^2 = 0.0221 / 0.6747 = 0.0328,
    W_1 = 0.5837 / 0.6747 = 0.8652,
    W_2 = 0.0690 / 0.6747 = 0.1023.

The values round to 3.3%, 86.5%, 10.2%.                                  []

Remark. The middle orbit O_1 dominates the zero state. This orbit
contains the light and middle quarks (u, d, s, c, b) but not the top quark
(t in O_2). The zero state is therefore concentrated on the light fermion
modes and decoupled from the top-quark sector. Whether this reflects the
physical origin of the top mass is an open question (HCSM-33).

10.3 What is not claimed

We do not claim that the zero state is selected by any physical mechanism.
Falsification tests of the WIN-era paper (static pressure-charge balance,
DNLS dynamical selection, topological selection) all fail. The zero state
is a structural fact about the multiplet and the observable set, not a
selection rule.

We do not claim to derive the charge column from the substrate; it is a
disclosed input, consistent with the HCSM-06/07 disclosure pattern.

We do not claim to derive the exact closed form of the amplitudes a_2 and
a_6. They are irrational numbers whose algebraic nature (degree of minimal
polynomial, or transcendence) is not determined by the present analysis.

12. CONCLUSION
================================================================================

This paper has established the zero state of the Hodge Complex Standard
Model.

The 14-mode multiplet at the self-paired eigenvalue lambda = 4 carries 19
vacuum-native observables, forming a 14 x 19 measurement matrix M. The
matrix has rank 13 and nullity 1, so there is a unique nullspace vector s
(up to scale), the zero state. The smallest singular value of M is machine
zero.

The zero state has four structural properties:

  (1) It is odd under the chiral reflection rs of HCSM-26:
      s(rs(m)) = -s(m) for all 14 modes.
  (2) The two rs-fixed modes W = (2,2) and t = (6,6) have exactly zero
      amplitude.
  (3) The six independent amplitudes (one per rs-orbit) satisfy the exact
      algebraic ratios a_3 = 2 a_4 and a_5 = (2 - sqrt(2)) a_4.
  (4) 86.5% of the |s|-mass sits on the middle D4 orbit O_1.

The entropy column of M is exactly redundant with the IPR column via the
affine relation of HCSM-05. The 15 rational columns have rank 11 and
nullity 3; the 4 irrational columns cut the nullity to 1.

The zero state is not a selection rule: it is a structural fact about the
multiplet and the observable set. Its exact amplitudes a_2 and a_6 are
irrational numbers whose closed form is an open question.

The zero state provides the flavor-structure input to the downstream
sector of the HCSM stack. Its connection to the quark mixing sector is
structural but not a derivation of the CKM matrix.
