ABSTRACT
================================================================================

The Hodge Complex Standard Model (HCSM) identifies the fourteen modes at the
self-paired eigenvalue λ = 4 of the discrete Laplacian on the periodic 8×8 torus
with one Standard Model generation. Three independent structures organize this
multiplet: the charge theorem (HCSM-24), which partitions the modes into three
σ-lines by the sign of the zero-state amplitude; the orbit decomposition
(HCSM-04, HCSM-05), which partitions the modes into three D₄ orbits with equally
spaced Shannon entropies; and the observer pair (HCSM-26), the two modes fixed
by the chiral reflection rs.

This paper establishes that these three structures form a triangle in a precise,
first-principles sense. We prove:

1. The three vertices are independently derived.
2. The three vertices coincide on the observer pair {W, t}.
3. The triangle closes on the framework constant d + 1 = 5.
4. The closure has a common algebraic origin as a trace identity on the kernel
   projector P_ker of the winding generator.
5. The triangle is a V₄ structure, with the three vertices corresponding to the
   three non-identity elements of the Klein four-group V₄ = ⟨rs, T_half⟩.

All claims are HCSM-native and verified at machine precision. The paper closes
the last open item in HCSM-29's scope: the structural origin of the closure
relation d + 1 = 5.

Keywords: Hodge Complex Standard Model; HCSM; charge-entropy-observer triangle;
σ-lines; D₄ orbit entropy; observer pair; framework constant d + 1; closure
relation; kernel projector; Klein four-group; first-principles derivation

    L² = d·2^d,    2(L-1) = d(d+3)/2    (2.1)

has the unique even-integer solution L = 8, d = 4, N = 64.

Proof. By the site-count relation N = d·2^d, L = sqrt(d·2^d) must be an even

RHS. For k≥3, LHS grows super-exponentially while RHS grows polynomially. Hence
k=1 is the unique solution, giving d = 4, L = 8, N = 64. □

6.1 THE CLOSURE RELATION

Theorem 6.1 (Triangle closure). The three vertices of the charge-entropy-observer

    q₃(σ = 0) = H(O₂) = 2·[N/4(W) + N/4(t)] = d + 1 = 5.    (6.1)

6.2 THE TRACE IDENTITY

Theorem 6.2 (Trace identity). The closure relation is a trace identity on the
substrate:

    d + 1 = rank(T)/Tr(I_{W, t}) = dim ker(H_wind)/Tr(I_{W, t})
          = Tr(I_{W, t})·(|F|/|B|) = 5.    (6.2)

Proof. From HCSM-13 Theorem 3.1, rank(T) = 10 = d(d+1)/2. From Theorem 2.14,
dim ker(H_wind) = 10 = d(d+1)/2. From Proposition 2.18, |F|/|B| = (d+1)/2 = 5/2.
From Proposition 5.3, Tr(I_{W, t}) = 2. Therefore:

    rank(T)/Tr(I_{W, t}) = 10/2 = 5 = d + 1,
    dim ker(H_wind)/Tr(I_{W, t}) = 10/2 = 5 = d + 1,
    Tr(I_{W, t})·(|F|/|B|) = 2·(5/2) = 5 = d + 1.    (6.3)

□

6.3 WHY THE CLOSURE WORKS

The three identities close on d + 1 = 5 because of the following structural
facts:

- Charge vertex. The σ-line at σ = 0 consists of the pair {W, t}, whose Standard
  Model charges are +3 (W) and +2 (t). Their sum is 5 = d+1 because the electric
  charge eigenvalues at d = 4 are {+2, +3} for the two modes fixed by rs.
- Entropy vertex. The orbit O₂ has entropy class 5.0 bits because 5 = d+1, and
  the entropy anchor (HCSM-01) forces the three orbit entropies to be d+2 = 6,
  d+3/2 = 5.5, and d+1 = 5.
- Observer vertex. The observer pair's N/4 sum is (d+1)/2 = 5/2, and the factor
  of 2 recovers d+1.

6.4 TWO INDEPENDENT CLOSURES

The closure in Theorem 6.1 can also be stated as two independent pairs of
equalities:

Charge-Entropy closure. The charge sum on the observer's σ-line equals the
entropy (in bits) of the observer's orbit:

    q₃(σ = 0) = H(O₂).    (6.4)

Observer-Sum closure. Twice the observer pair's N/4 sum equals the common value:

    2·[N/4(W) + N/4(t)] = q₃(σ = 0) = H(O₂).    (6.5)

Both closures are exact at machine precision. Together they make the triangle a
self-consistent structure: any one of the three quantities determines the other
two.

7. DISCUSSION
================================================================================

7.1 WHAT THE TRIANGLE IS

The charge-entropy-observer triangle is not a new partition of the fourteen-mode
multiplet. It is a coincidence theorem: three structures that each partition the
multiplet in a different way all agree on the observer pair, and all three of the
observer's three "coordinates"—its σ-line, its orbit, its N/4 pair—carry the same
framework constant d + 1.

7.2 RELATION TO THE OBSERVER-PAIR UNIQUENESS THEOREM

The Observer-Pair Uniqueness Theorem of HCSM-26 (S7.2) shows that {W, t} is the
unique pair satisfying five simultaneous numeric and structural constraints. The
triangle theorem of the present paper is a different uniqueness statement: the
pair is the unique two-element set that is simultaneously a σ-line, an
orbit-contained pair, and a chiral fixed locus. The two theorems are
complementary.

7.3 RELATION TO THE DIMENSIONAL-RIGIDITY CHAIN

The observer-pair uniqueness file records a chain of three identities that all
reduce to d² - 4d = 0:

- Observer-pair separation: d/2 - 2/d = (d-1)/2;
- kL crossover: (d-1)² = 2d + 1;
- Dark energy coefficient: (d+1)/2 = |F|/|B|.

The triangle closure is the "counting" partner of this chain: where the rigidity

7.4 WHY σ = 0

The charge vertex sits at σ = 0 because that is the unique σ-line that is
invariant under the chiral reflection rs. By the Fixed-Locus Theorem of HCSM-26,
rs swaps the σ = ±1 lines and fixes the σ = 0 line pointwise. So the observer's
charge coordinate is determined by its chiral structure—it must be σ = 0.

7.5 WHY THE ORBIT IS O₂

The entropy vertex sits at O₂ because that is the unique orbit containing the
fixed points of rs. Both W = (2,2) and t = (6,6) lie on the diagonal n₁ = n₂, so
they must lie in O₂ = {(2,2), (2,6), (6,2), (6,6)}. The other orbits—O₀ and
O₁—contain no diagonal modes.

7.6 WHY THE FACTOR OF 2

The observer vertex carries the "half" value (d+1)/2 because the observer pair is
a two-element set: the N/4 sum of one mode is 2, of the other is 1/2, and the sum
5/2 is exactly half the framework constant d + 1. Doubling is the natural
operation that recovers the common closure value.

7.7 THE V₄ STRUCTURE

The three vertices correspond to the three non-identity elements of the Klein
four-group V₄ = ⟨rs, T_half⟩:

- Observer vertex ↔ rs (fixed locus {W, t});
- Charge vertex ↔ T_half (the σ = 0 line is the T_half 2-cycle {W, t});
- Entropy vertex ↔ rs·T_half (the orbit O₂ is the union of the rs-fixed pair
  {W, t} and the rs·T_half-fixed pair {Z, H}).

The V₄ structure organizes the triangle and explains why the three vertices are
the natural structural features of the observer pair.
