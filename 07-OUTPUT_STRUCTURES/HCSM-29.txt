ABSTRACT
================================================================================

The Hodge Complex Standard Model assigns the fourteen modes at the self-paired
eigenvalue of the discrete Laplacian on the periodic 8 x 8 torus to one
Standard Model generation. Three independent structures organize this
multiplet: the charge theorem (HCSM-24), which partitions the modes into three
sigma-lines by the sign of the zero-state amplitude; the orbit decomposition
(HCSM-04, HCSM-05), which partitions the modes into three D4 orbits with
equal-spaced Shannon entropies; and the observer pair (HCSM-26), the two
modes fixed by the chiral reflection rs. This paper proves that these three
structures form a triangle in the following precise sense: the observer pair
is the unique two-element subset of the fourteen-mode multiplet that is
simultaneously (i) a sigma-line, (ii) contained in a single D4 orbit, and
(iii) the fixed locus of the chiral reflection. The triangle closes on the
framework constant d + 1 = 5, which appears at all three vertices
simultaneously: as the charge sum on the observer's sigma-line, as the
entropy (in bits) of the observer's orbit, and as twice the observer pair's
N/4 sum. The paper states the triangle theorem, derives the three vertex
identities, and gives the closure relation
q_3(sigma=0) = H(O_2) = 2 * [N/4(W) + N/4(t)] = d + 1. All claims are
HCSM-native and verified at machine precision.

1. INTRODUCTION
================================================================================

1.1 Three structures on one multiplet
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The Hodge Complex Standard Model (HCSM) derives the gauge structure, matter
content, particle spectrum, and mixing matrices of the Standard Model from a
single discrete substrate: the periodic 8 x 8 torus Lambda = Z_8 x Z_8 [1].
The fourteen modes at the self-paired eigenvalue lambda = 4 of the discrete
Laplacian are identified with one Standard Model generation. On this
multiplet, three independent structures act:

  1. The charge structure (HCSM-24 [7]). The zero-state vector s assigns to
     each mode a charge-line sign sigma = sign(s) in {-1, 0, +1}. The three
     sigma-lines partition the fourteen modes into sizes (6, 2, 6) with
     charge sums (0, +5, -8) in units of e/3.

  2. The entropy structure (HCSM-04, HCSM-05 [2, 3]). The D4 point group of
     the square decomposes the fourteen modes into three orbits O_0, O_1,
     O_2 of sizes (2, 8, 4), with Shannon entropies (6.0, 5.5, 5.0) bits and
     equal spacing 0.5 bits.

  3. The observer structure (HCSM-26 [5]). The chiral reflection rs of D4
     fixes exactly two modes on the fourteen-mode multiplet: the W boson and
     the top quark. Their pair {W, t} is the observer pair.


1.2 The one-line summary
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Three structures -- charge, entropy, and observer -- organize the same
  fourteen modes. The observer pair sits at their intersection, and the
  number d + 1 = 5 appears at all three of the observer's coordinates in the
  three structures.


1.3 Main result
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The paper proves the following triangle theorem.

Charge-Entropy-Observer Triangle Theorem (Section 3). The observer pair
{W, t} is the unique two-element subset of the fourteen-mode multiplet that
satisfies simultaneously:

  (T1) it is a sigma-line of the charge theorem;
  (T2) it is contained in a single D4 orbit;
  (T3) it is the fixed locus of the chiral reflection rs.

Moreover, the triangle closes on the constant d + 1, in the sense that three
framework quantities evaluated at the observer's three coordinates coincide:

    q_3(sigma = 0) = H(O_2) = 2 * [N/4(W) + N/4(t)] = d + 1 = 5.        (1)


1.4 Organization
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Section 2 states the three structures. Section 3 proves the triangle theorem.
Section 4 derives the three vertex identities. Section 5 derives the closure
relation. Section 6 discusses the structural interpretation. Section 7 gives
reproducibility notes. Section 8 concludes.

2. SETUP: THREE STRUCTURES ON THE FOURTEEN-MODE MULTIPLET
================================================================================

All statements in this section are HCSM-native; the reader is referred to
HCSM-00 [1] for the full foundations.


2.1 The fourteen-mode multiplet
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The substrate is the periodic eight-by-eight torus Lambda = Z_8 x Z_8 with
N = 64 sites. The discrete five-point Laplacian has self-paired eigenvalue
lambda = 4 with multiplicity 14. In the standard mode order,

    (nu, e, u, d, s, mu, dark, c, tau, b, W, Z, H, t).                   (2)

The mode coordinates in this order are:

    nu:    (0,4),   e:  (4,0),   u:  (1,3),   d:  (1,5),
    s:     (3,1),   mu: (3,7),   dark:(5,7),  c:  (7,3),
    tau:   (7,5),   b:  (5,1),   W:  (2,2),   Z:  (2,6),
    H:     (6,2),   t:  (6,6).                                           (3)


2.2 The charge structure
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The charge theorem of HCSM-24 states that the electric charge q_3 = 3Q (in
units of e/3) is a bijection

    F : (n_1, sigma) |-> q_3,    n_1 in Z_8,    sigma = sign(s) in {-1,0,+1},  (4)

from the fourteen realized pairs to the five Standard Model charge eigenvalues
{-3, -1, 0, +2, +3}. The three sigma-lines are:

    sigma = -1 : {nu, d, s, dark, c, Z},  charge sum 0,                  (5)
    sigma =  0 : {W, t},                  charge sum +5,                 (6)
    sigma = +1 : {e, mu, tau, b, H, u},   charge sum -8.                 (7)

The grand total is -3 = -1e.


2.3 The entropy structure
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Under the D4 point group of the square, the fourteen modes decompose into
three orbits:

    O_0 = {(0,4), (4,0)},                       size 2, support 64,
    O_1 = {(1,3), (1,5), (3,1), (3,7),
           (5,1), (5,7), (7,3), (7,5)},         size 8, support 48,       (8)
    O_2 = {(2,2), (2,6), (6,2), (6,6)},         size 4, support 32.

The support classes are {64, 48, 32} = {N, V, N/2} with V = (d - 1) * 2^d
= 48. The Shannon entropies are

    H(O_0) = 6.0 bits,   H(O_1) = 5.5 bits,   H(O_2) = 5.0 bits,         (9)

with equal spacing 0.5 bits.

In the standard mode order, the orbits are O_0 = {nu, e},
O_1 = {u, d, s, mu, dark, c, tau, b}, O_2 = {W, Z, H, t}.


2.4 The observer structure
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The chiral reflection rs : (n_1, n_2) |-> (n_2, n_1) (mod 8) is the unique
element of D4 that both fixes exactly the pair {(2,2), (6,6)} and
anticommutes with the winding generator H_wind [5]. Its fixed set on the
fourteen-mode multiplet is the observer pair:

    Fix(rs) = {(2,2), (6,6)} = {W, t}.                                  (10)

The observer pair's N/4 labels are N/4(W) = 2 and N/4(t) = 1/2, with sum

    N/4(W) + N/4(t) = 2 + 1/2 = 5/2 = (d + 1) / 2.                      (11)


================================================================================
3. THE TRIANGLE THEOREM
================================================================================

3.1 Statement
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Theorem 3.1 (Charge-Entropy-Observer Triangle). The observer pair {W, t} is
the unique two-element subset of the fourteen-mode multiplet satisfying all
three of:

  (T1) it is a sigma-line of the charge theorem;
  (T2) it is contained in a single D4 orbit;
  (T3) it is the fixed locus of the chiral reflection rs.


3.2 Proof
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Proof. Step 1: (T3) selects {W, t}. By the Fixed-Locus Theorem of HCSM-26
(Section 3.1), the fixed set of rs on the fourteen-mode multiplet is exactly
{(2,2), (6,6)} = {W, t}. So (T3) forces the set to be {W, t}.

Step 2: (T1) is satisfied. By (6), the sigma-line at sigma = 0 is exactly
{W, t}. So {W, t} satisfies (T1).

Step 3: (T2) is satisfied. Both W and t lie in O_2 (orbit (8)), since
W: (2,2), Z: (2,6), H: (6,2), t: (6,6) form the orbit
O_2 = {(2,2), (2,6), (6,2), (6,6)}. So {W, t} is a subset of O_2, satisfying
(T2).

Step 4: uniqueness. We must show no other two-element subset satisfies all
three conditions simultaneously.

  - Condition (T3) is the strongest: it uniquely determines the set {W, t}
    (Lemma 3.1 of HCSM-26).
  - Therefore any subset satisfying all three must equal {W, t}.
  - To confirm the conditions are compatible, we have verified (T1) and (T2)
    above.

Hence {W, t} is the unique subset satisfying (T1), (T2), and (T3).  [Box]

Remark 3.2 (Independence of the conditions). Conditions (T1) and (T2) alone
admit many candidates: any pair drawn from a single orbit and lying on a
single sigma-line. The pair {W, t} is the unique one that is simultaneously a
sigma-line (size-2), a subset of a single orbit, and the fixed locus of a
chiral involution. Condition (T3) is what forces uniqueness; conditions (T1)
and (T2) are what make the set a structurally distinguished vertex.


================================================================================
4. VERTEX IDENTITIES
================================================================================

We now evaluate three framework quantities at the observer's three
coordinates (charge, entropy, observer).


4.1 Charge vertex
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Proposition 4.1 (Charge vertex). The charge sum on the observer's sigma-line
is

    q_3(sigma = 0) := q_3(W) + q_3(t) = (+3) + (+2) = +5 = d + 1.       (12)

Proof. From HCSM-24 (Section 10.1), the charge assignments are q_3(W) = +3
and q_3(t) = +2. Sum: +5. At d = 4: d + 1 = 5.  [Box]


4.2 Entropy vertex
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Proposition 4.2 (Entropy vertex). The Shannon entropy (in bits) of the orbit
containing the observer pair is

    H(O_2) = 5.0 bits = d + 1.                                          (13)

Proof. From HCSM-04, the orbit O_2 has entropy class 5.0 bits, and the three
orbit entropies are (6.0, 5.5, 5.0) bits with equal spacing. At d = 4:
d + 1 = 5.  [Box]


4.3 Observer vertex
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Proposition 4.3 (Observer vertex). Twice the observer pair's N/4 sum is

    2 * [N/4(W) + N/4(t)] = 2 * (5/2) = 5 = d + 1.                      (14)

Proof. From HCSM-23 and HCSM-26, N/4(W) = 2 and N/4(t) = 1/2. Sum: 5/2.
Doubled: 5 = d + 1.  [Box]

Remark 4.4 (The factor of 2). The factor of 2 in (14) is not arbitrary: it
is the same factor that appears in the dimensional-rigidity chain of HCSM-26
(Section 9), where the observer-pair separation N/4(W) - N/4(t) = (d - 1)/2
doubles to d - 1 and the identity (d - 1)^2 = 2d + 1 closes at d = 4. The
observer vertex natively carries the "half" value (d + 1)/2, and doubling
recovers the framework constant d + 1 common to all three vertices.


================================================================================
5. THE CLOSURE RELATION
================================================================================

5.1 The triangle closure
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Combining Propositions 4.1, 4.2, and 4.3:

Theorem 5.1 (Triangle closure). The three vertices of the
charge-entropy-observer triangle coincide on the framework constant d + 1:

    [ q_3(sigma = 0) = H(O_2) = 2 * [N/4(W) + N/4(t)] = d + 1 = 5 ].    (15)


5.2 Why the closure works
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The three identities in (15) close on d + 1 = 5 because of the following
structural facts:

  - Charge vertex. The sigma-line at sigma = 0 consists of the pair {W, t},
    whose Standard Model charges are +3 (W) and +2 (t). Their sum is
    5 = d + 1 because the electric charge eigenvalues at d = 4 are {+2, +3}
    for the two modes fixed by rs.

  - Entropy vertex. The orbit O_2 has entropy class 5.0 bits because
    5 = d + 1, and the entropy anchor (HCSM-01) forces the three orbit
    entropies to be d + 2 = 6, d + 3/2 = 5.5, and d + 1 = 5.

  - Observer vertex. The observer pair's N/4 sum is (d + 1)/2 = 5/2, and
    doubling recovers d + 1.


5.3 Two independent closures
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The closure in (15) can also be stated as two independent pairs of
equalities:

Charge-Entropy closure. The charge sum on the observer's sigma-line equals
the entropy (in bits) of the observer's orbit:

    q_3(sigma = 0) = H(O_2).                                            (16)

Observer-Sum closure. Twice the observer pair's N/4 sum equals the common
value:

    2 * [N/4(W) + N/4(t)] = q_3(sigma = 0) = H(O_2).                    (17)

Both closures are exact at machine precision. Together they make the triangle
a self-consistent structure: any one of the three quantities determines the
other two.


================================================================================
6. DISCUSSION
================================================================================

6.1 What the triangle is
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The charge-entropy-observer triangle is not a new partition of the
fourteen-mode multiplet. It is a coincidence theorem: three structures that
each partition the multiplet in a different way all agree on the observer
pair, and all three of the observer's three "coordinates" -- its sigma-line,
its orbit, its N/4 pair -- carry the same framework constant d + 1.


6.2 Relation to the observer-pair uniqueness theorem
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The Observer-Pair Uniqueness Theorem of HCSM-26 (Section 7.2) shows that
{W, t} is the unique pair satisfying five simultaneous numeric and structural
constraints. The triangle theorem of the present paper is a different
uniqueness statement: the pair is the unique two-element set that is
simultaneously a sigma-line, an orbit-contained pair, and a chiral fixed
locus. The two theorems are complementary.


6.3 Relation to the dimensional-rigidity chain
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The observer-pair uniqueness file records a chain of three identities that
all reduce to d^2 - 4d = 0:

  - Observer-pair separation: d/2 - 2/d = (d - 1)/2;
  - kL crossover: (d - 1)^2 = 2d + 1;
  - Dark energy coefficient: (d + 1)/2 = |F| / |B|.

The triangle closure (15) is the "counting" partner of this chain: where the
rigidity chain exhibits d = 4 through three algebraic identities, the
triangle exhibits d + 1 = 5 through three structural coincidences.


6.4 Why sigma = 0
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The charge vertex sits at sigma = 0 because that is the unique sigma-line
that is invariant under the chiral reflection rs. By the Fixed-Locus Theorem
of HCSM-26, rs swaps the sigma = +/- 1 lines and fixes the sigma = 0 line
pointwise. So the observer's charge coordinate is determined by its chiral
structure -- it must be sigma = 0.


6.5 Why the orbit is O_2
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The entropy vertex sits at O_2 because that is the unique orbit containing
the fixed points of rs. Both W = (2,2) and t = (6,6) lie on the diagonal
n_1 = n_2, so they must lie in O_2 = {(2,2), (2,6), (6,2), (6,6)}. The other
orbits -- O_0 and O_1 -- contain no diagonal modes.


6.6 Why the factor of 2
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The observer vertex carries the "half" value (d + 1)/2 because the observer
pair is a two-element set: the N/4 sum of one mode is 2, of the other is
1/2, and the sum 5/2 is exactly half the framework constant d + 1. Doubling
is the natural operation that recovers the common closure value.

================================================================================
8. CONCLUSION
================================================================================

This paper has established the charge-entropy-observer triangle of the Hodge
Complex Standard Model. The three vertices are the sigma-line structure
(charge), the D4 orbit entropy structure, and the observer pair. The observer
pair {W, t} is the unique two-element subset that is simultaneously a
sigma-line, contained in a single orbit, and the fixed locus of the chiral
reflection rs. The triangle closes on the framework constant d + 1 = 5,
which appears at all three vertices: as the charge sum on the observer's
sigma-line, as the entropy (in bits) of the observer's orbit, and as twice
the observer pair's N/4 sum.

The closure relation

    q_3(sigma = 0) = H(O_2) = 2 * [N/4(W) + N/4(t)] = d + 1 = 5

is a numerical identity of the HCSM substrate, verified at machine precision.
It complements the dimensional-rigidity chain of HCSM-26 by exhibiting the
framework constant d + 1 through three structural coincidences.
