ABSTRACT
================================================================================

The Hodge Complex Standard Model identifies the 14 modes at the
self-paired eigenvalue lambda = 4 of the discrete Laplacian on the
periodic 8 x 8 torus with one Standard Model generation (HCSM-04),
and on the full 56-dimensional Hodge sector
V_56 = Omega^0 + Omega^1 + Omega^2 at lambda = 4, the processor T
selects a fermion sector of dimension |F| = 10 and a boson sector
of dimension |B_bos| = 4 (HCSM-13). This paper establishes the
entropic properties of the Hodge-HCSM interface. We prove:

  (i) the natural state on the 56-dimensional Hodge sector is the
      maximally mixed state rho_in = I_56/56, obtained as the image
      of the maximally mixed state on the full Hodge complex under
      the mirror-cancellation projection of HCSM-10; its von
      Neumann entropy is S_in = log 56;

 (ii) the processor T extends to a rank-40 orthogonal projection
      T_56 = extend(T) on V_56 (HCSM-16, Definition 1), and the
      associated processor channel is a completely positive
      trace-preserving map whose output on the maximally mixed
      input is the maximally mixed state on the 40-dimensional
      image of T_56, with entropy S_out = log 40;

(iii) the entropy reduction is
      Delta S = log 56 - log 40 = log(7/5) ~ 0.33647 nats;

 (iv) the reduction equals the exact deterministic Landauer bound
      for the processor's information-processing step, conditional
      on the HCSM measurement-irreversibility principle.

All claims are HCSM-native and verified at machine precision.

1.1 The Hodge-HCSM interface
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The Hodge Complex Standard Model (HCSM) has two structural levels.
The first is the Hodge complex: the exterior algebra of differential
forms Omega^0 + Omega^1 + Omega^2 on the periodic 8 x 8 torus
(HCSM-02 [HCSM-02]), with the Hodge-Dirac operator D = d + d* and
the self-paired eigenvalue lambda = 4 of the discrete Laplacian. The
second is the HCSM processor: the rank-10 orthogonal projection T on
the 14-mode multiplet at lambda = 4, which separates the fermion
sector F (|F| = 10) from the boson sector B_bos (|B_bos| = 4)
(HCSM-13 [HCSM-13]).

The Hodge-HCSM interface is the passage from the abstract Hodge
complex to the processed fermion/boson structure. This paper
establishes the information-theoretic properties of that interface:
the entropy of the state the Hodge complex delivers, and the entropy
of the state the processor produces.

2. SETUP
================================================================================

2.1 The 56-dimensional Hodge sector
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The substrate is the periodic square lattice Lambda = Z_8 x Z_8 with
N = 64 sites (HCSM-01 [HCSM-01]). The Hodge complex at the self-paired
eigenvalue lambda = 4 is

    V_56 = Omega^0|_{lambda=4} + Omega^1|_{lambda=4}
           + Omega^2|_{lambda=4},                                    (3)

of total dimension 14 + 28 + 14 = 56 (HCSM-02 [HCSM-02]). Its D4
isotypic decomposition is

    V_56 = 9A_1 + 5A_2 + 7B_1 + 7B_2 + 14E                            (4)

with dimension check 9 + 5 + 7 + 7 + 28 = 56
(HCSM-02 [HCSM-02], HCSM-05 [HCSM-05]).

2.2 The HCSM processor
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

On the 14-mode multiplet at lambda = 4, the processor T is the
rank-10 orthogonal projection (HCSM-13 [HCSM-13]):

    T = P_mesh + P_tau = I_14 - P_C,                                 (5)

with image the fermion sector F and kernel the boson sector B_bos:

    F = {nu, e, u, d, s, mu, c, tau, b, t}, |F| = 10,                (6)

    B_bos = {dark, W, Z, H},                |B_bos| = 4.             (7)

The classes A, B_wind, C are the winding classes of HCSM-14; the
boson sector is the winding class C.

2.3 Extension of the processor to V_56
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

By HCSM-16 (Definition 1, Lemma 2), the block-diagonal extension map
extend : C^{14 x 14} -> C^{56 x 56},

    extend(G) = diag(G, G (x) I_2, G),                               (8)

in the block ordering C^14 + (C^14 (x) C^2) + C^14 is a Lie algebra
homomorphism. We denote by

    T_56 = extend(T) = T (+) (T (x) I_2) (+) T                        (9)

the 56-dimensional processor.

Lemma 2.1 (Processor rank on V_56). The operator T_56 is an
orthogonal projection with

    rank(T_56) = 10 + 20 + 10 = 40,
    dim ker(T_56) = 16.                                              (10)

Proof. T is a rank-10 projection on C^14 with T^2 = T and
T^dagger = T. The block-diagonal structure (9) inherits idempotence
and Hermiticity. The rank is rank(T) + rank(T (x) I_2) + rank(T)
= 10 + 20 + 10 = 40. The kernel has dimension 56 - 40 = 16. [Box]

Lemma 2.2 (Fermion/boson structural decomposition). The processor
T_56 distinguishes a fermion sector of dimension |F| = 10 and a
boson sector of dimension |B_bos| = 4 per form degree; on V_56 the
processor image has dimension

    rank(T_56) = |F| * (1 + 2 + 1) = 10 * 4 = 40.                   (11)

Proof. By Lemma 2.1, rank(T_56) = 40. The factor 1 + 2 + 1 counts
the contributions to Omega^0, Omega^1, and Omega^2 respectively.
[Box]

3. ENTROPIC FLATNESS OF THE HODGE OUTPUT
================================================================================

3.1 The full Hodge complex and its maximally mixed state
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The full Hodge complex on Lambda has dimension
dim(Omega^0 + Omega^1 + Omega^2) = 64 + 128 + 64 = 256 (HCSM-02
[HCSM-02]). We take as the natural state on the full Hodge complex
the maximally mixed state

    rho_full = I_256 / 256,                                         (12)

the unique unit-trace state invariant under the full unitary group
U(256) preserving the Hodge inner product. This is a maximum-entropy
principle, not a D4-uniqueness claim: on a reducible representation,
the D4 commutant is strictly larger than the scalars, so D4
invariance alone does not single out I/dim.

3.2 Mirror cancellation and the projection onto V_56
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

By HCSM-10 [HCSM-10], the mirror symmetry lambda <-> 8 - lambda of
the 5-point Laplacian spectrum pairs 50 of the 64 scalar modes into
cancelling partners; only the 14 self-paired modes at lambda = 4
survive. On the full Hodge complex this is the orthogonal projection
P_mir = P_{lambda=4} onto the 56-dimensional self-paired eigenspace
V_56 (3).

The mirror-cancellation projection defines a completely positive
trace-preserving (CPTP) map

    Phi_mir(rho) = P_mir rho P_mir
                 + Tr((I_256 - P_mir) rho) |0><0|_bath,             (13)

where |0>_bath is a fixed reference state on the discarded
200-dimensional bath.

Theorem 3.1 (Entropic flatness). The natural state on the
56-dimensional Hodge sector V_56 is the maximally mixed state

    rho_in = Phi_mir(rho_full) = I_56 / 56,                         (14)

the image of the maximally mixed state on the full Hodge complex
under the mirror-cancellation projection. Its von Neumann entropy is

    S_in = -Tr(rho_in log rho_in)
         = log 56 ~ 4.0253516907 nats.                             (15)

Proof. Since P_mir is a projection and rho_full = I_256 / 256,

    P_mir rho_full P_mir = P_mir / 256 = (I_56 + 0_200) / 256.

Taking the trace and normalizing yields rho_in = I_56 / 56. The
entropy follows: S_in = -sum_{i=1}^{56} (1/56) log(1/56) = log 56.
[Box]

Remark 3.1 (Why maximally mixed). The state rho_in = I_56 / 56 is
the unique unit-trace state invariant under the full unitary group
U(56) acting on V_56. The derivation via the mirror-cancellation
projection is HCSM-native: the projection onto the self-paired
eigenspace is the structural content of the mirror-cancellation
theorem (HCSM-10).

Remark 3.2 (Entropic flatness). The term "entropic flatness" refers
to the fact that rho_in is uniform over the 56 dimensions of V_56:
every dimension carries equal weight 1/56. This is the maximum-entropy
state on V_56.

4. THE PROCESSOR CHANNEL
================================================================================

4.1 The CPTP map
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Definition 4.1 (Processor channel). The processor channel is the
CPTP map

    Phi_T(rho) = T_56 rho T_56
               + Tr((I_56 - T_56) rho) |0><0|_bos,                 (16)

with T_56 = extend(T) from (9) and |0>_bos a fixed reference state
on the 16-dimensional bosonic kernel.

The map Phi_T is completely positive because it is a sum of two
completely positive maps (rho -> T_56 rho T_56 and the trace-scaling
channel onto |0><0|_bos), and it is trace-preserving because
Tr(T_56 rho T_56) + Tr((I - T_56) rho) = Tr(rho).

4.2 The output state
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Theorem 4.1 (Processor channel output). The processor channel Phi_T
maps the maximally mixed input rho_in = I_56 / 56 to the maximally
mixed state on the 40-dimensional image of T_56:

    rho_out proportional to Phi_T(rho_in)
          = T_56 / 56
          = (I_{im(T_56)} + 0_16) / 56.                             (17)

Restricted to the image of T_56 and renormalized,
rho_out|_{im(T_56)} = I_40 / 40, with von Neumann entropy

    S_out = log 40 ~ 3.6888794541 nats.                            (18)

Proof. Since T_56 is a projection (T_56^2 = T_56) and
rho_in = I_56 / 56,

    T_56 rho_in T_56 = T_56 / 56.

The trace of this term is rank(T_56) / 56 = 40 / 56 = 5/7. The
complementary term Tr((I - T_56) rho_in) |0><0|_bos contributes a
pure state on the 16-dimensional kernel with weight 16/56 = 2/7.
The marginal on im(T_56) is therefore I_40 / 40, with entropy
log 40. [Box]

Remark 4.1 (Image-of-T_56 language). The output state is naturally
a state on the image of T_56, of dimension 40. The state is not,
strictly speaking, a state on the product space F (x) B_bos; the
language of "fermion-boson product space" of the first revision
should be replaced by the language of the image of T_56.

5. THE ENTROPY REDUCTION
================================================================================

Corollary 5.1 (Entropy reduction). The entropy reduction effected
by the processor is

    Delta S = S_in - S_out = log 56 - log 40
            = log(56/40) = log(7/5) ~ 0.3364722366 nats.             (19)

Proof. By Theorems 3.1 and 4.1, S_in = log 56 and S_out = log 40.
Subtract. [Box]

Corollary 5.2 (Framework-constant form). The entropy reduction has
the clean HCSM-native form

    Delta S = log(dim V_56 / rank(T_56))
            = log(56/40) = log(7/5).                                (20)

The numerator dim V_56 = 56 is the dimension of the Hodge sector;
the denominator rank(T_56) = 40 is the rank of the processor. Both
are framework constants at d = 4. [Box]

Remark 5.1 (Ratio in bits). The entropy reduction in bits is

    Delta S / log 2 = log_2(7/5) ~ 0.4854268272 bits.               (21)

6. LANDAUER SATURATION
================================================================================

The entropy reduction Delta S = log(7/5) of Corollary 5.1 is a
structural identity of the HCSM state space. Whether it equals the
thermodynamic Landauer bound is a physical statement that requires
an additional principle: the identification of the entropy reduction
with heat dissipated into the DME bath at the rate 1/T_DME.

Principle 6.1 (Measurement irreversibility). The processor channel
Phi_T is a non-unitary projection. The entropy reduction
Delta S = log(7/5) is transferred to the DME bath at the rate
1/T_DME, where T_DME = 1/(2 gamma) is the unique dissipative time
scale of the HCSM five-layer loop (HCSM-21 [HCSM-21],
HCSM-54 [HCSM-54]).

Theorem 6.1 (Landauer saturation, conditional). Conditional on
Principle 6.1, the entropy reduction Delta S = log(7/5) equals the
exact deterministic Landauer bound for the processor's information-
processing step. The processor erases exactly log(7/5) nats of
entropy per pass, at the exact deterministic limit. The loop-pass
duration is T_pass = T_DME = 1/(2 gamma).

Proof. The processor channel Phi_T is a deterministic rank-40
projection with well-defined image and kernel; no randomness enters
its action. By Principle 6.1, the entropy reduction is transferred
to the DME bath at the rate 1/T_DME. The deterministic Landauer
bound is therefore saturated exactly, and the loop-pass duration
equals the dissipative time T_DME. [Box]

Remark 6.1 (Determinism of the processor). The processor T_56 is a
fixed linear map with a well-defined image (the fermion sector on
each form degree) and a well-defined kernel (the boson sector on
each form degree). No randomness enters its action. The Landauer
bound is therefore saturated exactly, conditional on Principle 6.1.

Remark 6.2 (Status of the conditional). The Landauer saturation is
a conditional theorem: the structural identity Delta S = log(7/5)
is unconditional, but the identification with the thermodynamic
Landauer bound requires Principle 6.1. This principle is HCSM-native
(it is the content of HCSM-21, Theorem 12.1) but is not derived from
the substrate alone.

7. INTERPRETATION
================================================================================

7.1 The Hodge-HCSM interface as an information-theoretic transition
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The Hodge complex provides the substrate's raw information content:
a flat (maximally mixed) state on the 56-dimensional Hodge sector,
of entropy log 56 nats. The processor T_56 reads out the
fermion/boson structure, reducing the entropy to log 40 nats.

The interface therefore performs an information-theoretic transition
from a flat state on 56 dimensions to a flat state on 40 dimensions.
The reduction is exactly log(7/5) ~ 0.33647 nats. This is the
information the processor extracts, and it is the amount of entropy
the substrate's raw state must release for the fermion/boson
structure to be observable.

7.2 The interface preserves a specific structure
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The reduction preserves the fermion/boson factorization. The state
before processing is flat on all 56 dimensions; the state after
processing is flat on the 40-dimensional image of T_56. The
processor's information content is the fermion/boson label
structure; the finer structure of the Hodge complex (the individual
D4 multiplicities, the class partition) is not directly observable
at the level of the interface.

7.3 Relation to the Landauer bound
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The processor is a Maxwell demon in the exact deterministic limit,
conditional on Principle 6.1. The Landauer bound is the
thermodynamic constraint on the amount of entropy that can be
erased per bit of information processing. The processor saturates
this bound exactly:
Delta S = log(7/5) = log(dim V_56 / rank(T_56)).

The fact that the reduction saturates the bound, rather than being
strictly larger, is a statement about the optimality of the
processor: it erases exactly the entropy needed to convert the raw
Hodge state into the fermion/boson output state, and no more.

7.4 What the interface does not claim
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The interface does not claim to derive any particle properties
(masses, charges, mixings) from the entropic reduction. Those are
the subject of downstream papers (HCSM-23, HCSM-30, etc.). The
interface establishes only the information-theoretic transition:
what state the Hodge complex delivers, what state the processor
produces, and how much entropy is reduced.

The interface also does not claim that the entropy reduction is a
fundamental physical process. It is a structural identity: the
difference between two specific entropies computed from the HCSM
state space.

10. CONCLUSION
================================================================================

This paper has established the entropic properties of the
Hodge-HCSM interface of the Hodge Complex Standard Model.

The natural state on the 56-dimensional Hodge sector at lambda = 4
is the maximally mixed state rho_in = I_56 / 56, obtained as the
image of the maximally mixed state on the full Hodge complex under
the mirror-cancellation projection of HCSM-10. Its von Neumann
entropy is S_in = log 56 ~ 4.02535 nats.

The processor T extends to a rank-40 orthogonal projection
T_56 = extend(T) on V_56 (HCSM-16), and the associated processor
channel Phi_T maps rho_in to the maximally mixed state on im(T_56)
of dimension 40, with entropy S_out = log 40 ~ 3.68888 nats. The
entropy reduction is

    Delta S = log 56 - log 40 = log(7/5) ~ 0.33647 nats.            (22)

The reduction saturates the deterministic Landauer bound,
conditional on the HCSM measurement-irreversibility principle
(Principle 6.1).

The Hodge-HCSM interface is therefore an information-theoretic
transition from a flat state on 56 dimensions to a flat state on
40 dimensions, with entropy reduction log(7/5). The transition is
structural, exact, and HCSM-native. It is the natural starting
point for downstream information-theoretic analyses of the processor
and its outputs.

Physics is what the machine cannot cancel. The Hodge complex is the
machine. The log(7/5) is what the machine cannot cancel.
