ABSTRACT
================================================================================

The Hodge Complex Standard Model derives the 14-mode multiplet at the
self-paired eigenvalue lambda = 4 of the discrete Laplacian on the
periodic 8 x 8 torus as one Standard Model generation (HCSM-04). This
paper establishes the 5 + 9 split of this multiplet into 5 Substrate
Eigenmodes and 9 Mesh Resonances. The classification is defined by the
quantity delta = N/4 - base, where N/4 is the mode-particle calibration
label (framework content, the subject of HCSM-23) and base is a
framework base formula expressed in the framework constants d and H.
The paper proves that exactly 5 of the 14 modes have delta = 0 (the
Substrate Eigenmodes) and exactly 9 have delta != 0 (the Mesh
Resonances). The 5 Substrate Eigenmodes are the dark photon, the tau
lepton, the W boson, the Z boson, and the Higgs boson: the 4 bosons of
the multiplet together with tau. The 9 Mesh Resonances are the
remaining 9 fermions. The split is proved conditional on the
framework's base formula assignment and the N/4 calibration table; both
are stated as inputs. All claims are HCSM-native and verified at
machine precision.

1. INTRODUCTION
================================================================================

1.1 The 5 + 9 split
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The Hodge Complex Standard Model (HCSM) identifies the 14 modes at the
self-paired eigenvalue lambda = 4 of the discrete Laplacian on the
periodic 8 x 8 torus with one Standard Model generation (HCSM-04
[HCSM-04]). Each mode is assigned to a Standard Model particle, and each
particle carries an N/4 label that orders it by mass (HCSM-23 [HCSM-23]).

This paper establishes a structural classification of the 14 modes: the
5 + 9 split into 5 Substrate Eigenmodes and 9 Mesh Resonances. The
classification is defined by the quantity

    delta = N/4 - base,                                              (1)

where base is a framework base formula expressed in the constants d and
H. Modes with delta = 0 are Substrate Eigenmodes; modes with
delta != 0 are Mesh Resonances.

The split is the subject of this paper. It is analogous in structure to
the sector-coarse correspondence of HCSM-06 [HCSM-06] and the
hypercharge quantization of HCSM-07 [HCSM-07]: a structural
classification of the multiplet that is conditional on the framework's
calibration input.

1.2 The one-line summary
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    The 14 modes at lambda = 4 decompose into 5 Substrate Eigenmodes
    (those whose N/4 labels equal a framework base formula exactly) and
    9 Mesh Resonances (those whose N/4 labels are offset from the base
    formula). The 5 eigenmodes are the 4 bosons of the multiplet plus
    tau; the 9 resonances are the remaining 9 fermions.

1.3 Main results
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

(1) The delta function (Definition 3.1). The quantity
    delta_m = N/4_m - base_m is defined for each of the 14 modes;
    N/4_m is the calibration label and base_m is the framework base
    formula.

(2) The delta table (Lemma 3.2). The delta values for the 14 modes are
    explicitly

        delta = (33, +6, +3, +3, -3, -3, 0, +0.5,
                 0, +0.5, 0, 0, 0, +0.5)                            (2)

    in standard mode order.

(3) The 5 + 9 Split Theorem (Theorem 4.1). Exactly 5 modes have
    delta = 0; exactly 9 have delta != 0.

(4) Eigenmode identification (Corollary 4.2). The 5 Substrate
    Eigenmodes are dark photon, tau, W, Z, H.

(5) Resonance identification (Corollary 4.3). The 9 Mesh Resonances
    are nu, e, u, d, s, mu, c, b, t.

(6) Boson relation (Corollary 5.1). The 5 Substrate Eigenmodes are
    exactly the 4 bosons of the multiplet (dark, W, Z, H) together
    with tau. Equivalently, the eigenmode set is B union {tau} where
    B = {dark, W, Z, H} is the boson sector.

(7) Relation to D4 orbits and F/B split (Section 5). The 5 + 9 split
    does not coincide with the D4 orbit decomposition of HCSM-05 nor
    with the fermion/boson split of HCSM-13; it is a distinct
    structural classification.

2. SETUP
================================================================================

2.1 The 14-mode multiplet
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The substrate is the periodic square lattice Lambda = Z_8 x Z_8 with
N = 64 sites (HCSM-01 [HCSM-01]). The 14 modes at the self-paired
eigenvalue lambda = 4 of the discrete Laplacian are (HCSM-04
[HCSM-04]):

    O_0 = {(0,4), (4,0)},                                            (3)

    O_1 = {(1,3), (1,5), (3,1), (3,7),
           (5,1), (5,7), (7,3), (7,5)},                              (4)

    O_2 = {(2,2), (2,6), (6,2), (6,6)}.                              (5)

The standard mode order, matching the zero-state vector of HCSM-22
[HCSM-22] and the mass-ordering labels of HCSM-23 [HCSM-23], is

    (nu, e, u, d, s, mu, dark, c, tau, b, W, Z, H, t),               (6)

with the correspondence

    nu     : (0,4),    e   : (4,0),    u   : (1,3),    d : (1,5),

    s      : (3,1),    mu  : (3,7),    dark: (5,7),    c : (7,3),

    tau    : (7,5),    b   : (5,1),    W   : (2,2),    Z : (2,6),

    H      : (6,2),    t   : (6,6).

The identification of the 14 modes with the 14 Standard Model particles
is an empirical input of the framework (HCSM-04); it is not derived.

2.2 Framework constants
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The framework constants used in this paper are

    d = 4,  H = 2^d = 16,  N = d * 2^d = 64,  V = (d-1) * 2^d = 48.   (7)

These are derived in HCSM-01 [HCSM-01].

2.3 The N/4 labels
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Each mode carries an N/4 label (HCSM-23 [HCSM-23]). In the standard
mode order (6), the labels are

    (N/4)_i = (60, 24, 21, 20, 14, 14, 12, 9.5, 9, 7.5,
               2, 2, 1, 0.5).                                        (8)

The N/4 labels are the framework's calibration table for the particle
spectrum; their derivation from the substrate geometry is the subject
of HCSM-23 [HCSM-23]. In the present paper, they are treated as inputs.

2.4 The framework base formulas
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Each particle is assigned a framework base formula, an expression in
the framework constants d and H. The assignment is part of the
framework's structural content; it is an input to this paper, not a
derivation.

Definition 2.1 (Framework base formula). The framework base formula
for each Standard Model particle is:

    Particle        Base formula       Base value (d=4, H=16)
    -------------   ----------------   ----------------------
    nu              H + 2d + 3                       27
    e               H + d - 2                        18
    u               H + 2                            18
    d               H + 1                            17
    s               H + 1                            17
    mu              H + 1                            17
    dark            H - d                            12
    c               H + 1 - 2d                        9
    tau             H + 1 - 2d                        9
    b               H + 3 - 3d                        7
    W               d/2                               2
    Z               d/2                               2
    H_Higgs         H + 1 - d^2                       1
    t               H - d^2                           0

Remark 2.2 (The W/Z correction). The base formula for W and Z is
d/2 = 2. An earlier framework version used 2(H+2) - d^2 = 20, which did
not match the observed N/4 value of 2. The present paper uses the
corrected formula d/2, consistent with the framework's corrected
calibration.

Remark 2.3 (Framework formulas). Each base formula is a polynomial in
d with small integer coefficients, or involves H = 2^d linearly (with
an occasional d^2 term for the heavy bosons). The formulas are
HCSM-native expressions in the framework constants d and H. Their
assignment to specific particles is part of the framework's structure,
treated here as input.

3. THE delta FUNCTION
================================================================================

Definition 3.1 (delta). For each mode m of the 14-mode multiplet, the
deviation delta_m is

    delta_m = N/4_m - base_m,                                        (9)

where N/4_m is the calibration label (8) and base_m is the framework
base value (Definition 2.1).

Lemma 3.2 (delta table). In the standard mode order (6), the delta
values are:

    Particle     (n_1,n_2)      N/4     base     delta
    ----------   ----------   ------   ------   -------
    nu             (0,4)         60       27      +33
    e              (4,0)         24       18       +6
    u              (1,3)         21       18       +3
    d              (1,5)         20       17       +3
    s              (3,1)         14       17       -3
    mu             (3,7)         14       17       -3
    dark           (5,7)         12       12        0
    c              (7,3)        9.5        9     +0.5
    tau            (7,5)          9        9        0
    b              (5,1)        7.5        7     +0.5
    W              (2,2)          2        2        0
    Z              (2,6)          2        2        0
    H_Higgs        (6,2)          1        1        0
    t              (6,6)        0.5        0     +0.5

Equivalently, in vector form

    delta = (+33, +6, +3, +3, -3, -3, 0, +0.5,
             0, +0.5, 0, 0, 0, +0.5).                               (10)

Proof. Direct computation: delta_m = N/4_m - base_m for each of the 14
modes, using the N/4 labels (8) and the base values from Definition
2.1. [Box]

Remark 3.3 (delta values are integers or half-integers). Every delta
value in the table is an integer or a half-integer. The nonzero values
are {+33, +6, +3, -3, +0.5} up to signs.


================================================================================
4. THE 5 + 9 SPLIT THEOREM
================================================================================

Definition 4.1 (Substrate Eigenmode and Mesh Resonance). A mode m of
the 14-mode multiplet is a Substrate Eigenmode if delta_m = 0; it is a
Mesh Resonance if delta_m != 0.

Theorem 4.2 (5 + 9 Split). Of the 14 modes at lambda = 4, exactly 5 are
Substrate Eigenmodes and exactly 9 are Mesh Resonances.

Proof. By Definition 4.1, the Substrate Eigenmodes are the modes with
delta_m = 0. From Lemma 3.2, the modes with delta = 0 are

    {dark, tau, W, Z, H_Higgs},                                      (11)

which has cardinality 5. The remaining 14 - 5 = 9 modes have
delta != 0; they are the Mesh Resonances. [Box]

Corollary 4.3 (Eigenmode identification). The 5 Substrate Eigenmodes
are the dark photon, the tau lepton, the W boson, the Z boson, and the
Higgs boson.

Proof. Immediate from the proof of Theorem 4.2. [Box]

Corollary 4.4 (Resonance identification). The 9 Mesh Resonances are
the neutrino nu, the electron e, the up quark u, the down quark d, the
strange quark s, the muon mu, the charm quark c, the bottom quark b,
and the top quark t.

Proof. Immediate from the proof of Theorem 4.2 and the resonance set
M \ {dark, tau, W, Z, H_Higgs}. [Box]


================================================================================
5. STRUCTURE OF THE SPLIT
================================================================================

5.1 The Substrate Eigenmodes and the boson sector
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Corollary 5.1 (Boson relation). Let B = {dark, W, Z, H_Higgs} denote
the boson sector of the multiplet (the kernel of the processor T of
HCSM-13 [HCSM-13]). Let F = {nu, e, u, d, s, mu, c, tau, b, t} denote
the fermion sector (the image of T). Then

    {Substrate Eigenmodes} = B union {tau},                          (12)

    {Mesh Resonances} = F \ {tau}.                                   (13)

Equivalently, the Substrate Eigenmodes consist of the 4 bosons of the
multiplet together with tau; the Mesh Resonances are the 9 fermions
other than tau.

Proof. By Corollary 4.3, the Substrate Eigenmodes are
{dark, tau, W, Z, H_Higgs}. Of these, dark, W, Z, H_Higgs are the boson
sector B (they are in the kernel of the processor T of HCSM-13
[HCSM-13]); tau is a fermion. Hence the eigenmodes are B union {tau}.

By Corollary 4.4, the Mesh Resonances are {nu, e, u, d, s, mu, c, b, t},
which is F \ {tau}. [Box]

Remark 5.2 (tau as the exceptional fermion). The 5 + 9 split singles
out tau as the unique fermion in the Substrate Eigenmode set. Among the
10 fermions, 9 are Mesh Resonances and 1 (tau) is a Substrate Eigenmode.
The framework does not at this level explain why tau is distinguished;
the observation is structural.

5.2 Relation to D4 orbits
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The 5 + 9 split does not coincide with the D4 orbit decomposition of
HCSM-05 [HCSM-05], in which the 14 modes split into orbits of sizes
2, 8, 4:

    Orbit       Modes                                      delta values
    ---------   ----------------------------------------   ------------------
    O_0         {nu, e}                                    +33, +6
    O_1         {u, d, s, mu, dark, c, tau, b}             +3, +3, -3, -3,
                                                           0, +0.5, 0, +0.5
    O_2         {W, Z, H, t}                               0, 0, 0, +0.5

The Substrate Eigenmodes are distributed across orbits: O_0 contains 0
eigenmodes, O_1 contains 2 (dark, tau), and O_2 contains 3 (W, Z, H).
The Mesh Resonances are distributed across orbits: O_0 contains 2, O_1
contains 6, and O_2 contains 1.

The 5 + 9 split is therefore a distinct structural classification, not
a coarse-graining of the D4 orbit decomposition.

5.3 Relation to the winding class partition
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The winding class partition (HCSM-13 [HCSM-13], HCSM-14 [HCSM-14]) has
three classes:

    A = {nu, e, t},
    B = {u, d, s, mu, c, tau, b},
    C = {dark, W, Z, H}.                                             (14)

The Substrate Eigenmodes are distributed across the classes: A contains
0 eigenmodes, B contains 1 (tau), C contains 4 (dark, W, Z, H). The
Mesh Resonances are distributed: A contains 3, B contains 6, C
contains 0.

The 5 + 9 split also does not coincide with the winding class
partition.

5.4 The W/Z correction
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The framework base formulas for the W and Z bosons are d/2 = 2
(Remark 2.2). This is consistent with the observed N/4 = 2 for both,
giving delta = 0. An earlier framework version used the formula
2(H+2) - d^2 = 20 for the same particles, giving delta = 2 - 20 = -18
!= 0. The correction to d/2 is the structural condition that both W
and Z are Substrate Eigenmodes with delta = 0; the corrected formula
d/2 is consistent with this.

delta != 0 (the Mesh Resonances).

The framework's claim is: the 5 + 9 split is a structural feature of
the 14-mode multiplet with respect to the framework's calibration

================================================================================
8. CONCLUSION
================================================================================

This paper has established the 5 + 9 split of the 14-mode multiplet of
the Hodge Complex Standard Model.

The split is defined by the quantity delta = N/4 - base, where N/4 is
the calibration label (framework content) and base is the framework
base formula (polynomial in the framework constants d and H). Exactly
5 of the 14 modes have delta = 0 (the Substrate Eigenmodes); exactly 9
have delta != 0 (the Mesh Resonances).

The 5 Substrate Eigenmodes are the dark photon, the tau lepton, the W
boson, the Z boson, and the Higgs boson: the 4 bosons of the multiplet
together with tau. The 9 Mesh Resonances are the remaining 9 fermions.

The split does not coincide with the D4 orbit decomposition of HCSM-05
nor with the fermion/boson split of HCSM-13. It is a distinct
structural classification of the 14-mode multiplet.

The framework's interpretation is: the 5 Substrate Eigenmodes are the
modes whose N/4 labels are exactly the framework base formulas; the 9
Mesh Resonances are the modes whose N/4 labels are offset from the base
formulas by a deviation delta != 0. The 5 modes are the "pure
substrate" content of the multiplet; the 9 modes are the "mesh-
processed" content.
