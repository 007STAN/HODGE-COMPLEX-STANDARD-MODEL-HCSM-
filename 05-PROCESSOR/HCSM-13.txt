## Abstract

The Hodge Complex Standard Model derives the gauge structure, matter content, particle spectrum, and mixing matrices of the Standard Model from a single discrete substrate: the periodic eight-by-eight torus. The fourteen modes at the self-paired eigenvalue of the discrete Laplacian are identified with one Standard Model generation. On this multiplet, the processor T is the rank-ten orthogonal projection that separates the ten Standard Model fermions from the four Standard Model bosons. This paper proves two theorems about the processor and its action. First, the processor commutes with the class-C projector of the winding partition, and its image is exactly the union of winding classes A and B, while its kernel is exactly winding class C. Equivalently, the processor equals the identity minus the class-C projector, so the fermion/boson split is the coarse-graining of the winding class partition that identifies classes A and B. Second, the processor T and the chiral reflection fail to commute only on the two-dimensional plane spanned by the dark photon and the tau; on the other twelve modes, the processor and the chiral reflection commute. On the two-dimensional plane of the failure, the commutator is a fourth-order rotation generator. This is the framework's chirality-breaking: it is localized to a single two-mode plane. All theorems are HCSM-native, and all numerical claims are verified at machine precision.

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 The processor in HCSM

The Hodge Complex Standard Model (HCSM) derives the gauge structure, matter content, particle spectrum, and mixing matrices of the Standard Model from a single discrete substrate: the periodic eight-by-eight torus. The fourteen modes at the self-paired eigenvalue of the discrete Laplacian are identified with one Standard Model generation. Their dihedral representation theory determines the gauge algebra; their mass-ordering labels determine the mass spectrum; the zero-state vector from the fourteen-by-nineteen measurement matrix determines the flavor structure.

Above the substrate, the processor T is the machine that reads the fourteen-mode multiplet and separates it into a fermion sector and a boson sector. The processor is a rank-ten orthogonal projection: it keeps ten modes (the Standard Model fermions) and kills four modes (the Standard Model bosons). Its matrix is diagonal in the mode basis.

This paper proves that the processor's output is not an independent structure on the multiplet. It is the coarse-graining of the winding class partition that identifies two of the three winding classes. And the failure of the processor to commute with the framework's chiral reflection is localized to a single two-mode plane.

### 1.2 The one-line summary

The processor T is exactly the complement of the class-C projector. The fermion/boson split is the same as the winding class partition, with classes A and B fused into a single fermion sector. And chirality is broken only on the plane spanned by the dark photon and the tau: twelve of the fourteen modes see no chirality-breaking at all.

### 1.3 The two theorems of this paper

The paper is organized around two theorems.

**Theorem 1 — The Fermion/Boson-Class Correspondence Theorem.** The processor T commutes with the class-C projector of the winding partition. Its image is exactly the union of winding classes A and B, and its kernel is exactly winding class C. Equivalently, the processor equals the identity minus the class-C projector. The fermion/boson split is the coarse-graining of the winding class partition that identifies classes A and B.

**Theorem 2 — The Chirality-Breaking Localization Theorem.** The commutator of the processor with the chiral reflection is supported exactly on the two-element set consisting of the dark photon and the tau. On the two-dimensional plane spanned by these two modes, the commutator is a fourth-order rotation generator; on the other twelve modes, the processor and the chiral reflection commute.

## 2. Setup

This section states the HCSM primitives on which the paper depends. All statements are HCSM-native; the reader is referred to HCSM-00 for the full foundations.

### 2.1 The fourteen-mode multiplet

The substrate is the periodic eight-by-eight torus with sixty-four sites, selected by the photon-helicity anchor and the equal-entropy-spacing anchor. The discrete five-point Laplacian has eigenvalues equal to four minus two times the cosine of pi times n-one over four, minus two times the cosine of pi times n-two over four, for n-one and n-two ranging over the integers zero through seven. The self-paired eigenvalue four has multiplicity fourteen.

In the standard mode order used throughout HCSM — matching the zero-state vector of HCSM-22 — the fourteen modes are: neutrino, electron, up quark, down quark, strange quark, muon, dark photon, charm quark, tau, bottom quark, W boson, Z boson, Higgs, top quark.

### 2.2 The winding class partition

The winding generator has spectrum consisting of minus four root two, minus two root two, zero with multiplicity ten, plus two root two, and plus four root two. The fourteen modes partition into three classes by frequency:

- **Class A**: the neutrino, electron, and top quark. Size three.
- **Class B**: the up quark, down quark, strange quark, muon, charm quark, tau, and bottom quark. Size seven.
- **Class C**: the dark photon, W boson, Z boson, and Higgs. Size four.

The class projectors are diagonal in the mode basis, mutually orthogonal, idempotent, and sum to the identity.

### 2.3 The processor T

**Definition (Processor).** The processor T is the rank-ten orthogonal projection onto the fermion sector F. It is defined as the sum of two projectors: the projector onto the nine mesh resonances (those modes with nonzero delta), and the projector onto the unique mode with zero-state amplitude plus one.

In the standard mode order, the processor is diagonal with entries: one, one, one, one, one, one, zero, one, one, one, zero, zero, zero, one.

The fermion sector and boson sector are:

- **Fermion sector**: the neutrino, electron, up quark, down quark, strange quark, muon, charm quark, tau, bottom quark, and top quark. Size ten.
- **Boson sector**: the dark photon, W boson, Z boson, and Higgs. Size four.

**Proposition (Processor properties).** The processor satisfies the projection identity that its square is itself, is Hermitian, and has rank ten.

**Proof.** Orthogonal projection by construction. Verified at machine precision in the companion verification script.

### 2.4 The chiral reflection

The chiral reflection is the map that sends a mode at coordinates (a, b) to the mode at coordinates (b, a), modulo eight. It is an element of the dihedral point group of the square, and its square is the identity. It has been identified in HCSM-26 as the unique chiral reflection of the dihedral group: the unique element that fixes exactly the pair at the two diagonal fixed points on the fourteen-mode multiplet and anticommutes with the winding generator.

Let the permutation matrix implementing the chiral reflection in the mode basis be denoted by its matrix form. It satisfies the involution property that its square is the identity, and it is Hermitian.

## 3. The Fermion/Boson-Class Correspondence

This section proves that the processor T is exactly the complement of the class-C projector.

### 3.1 The complement formulation

**Theorem (Fermion/Boson-Class Correspondence).** The processor T commutes with the class-C projector, and equals the identity minus the class-C projector, which is the sum of the class-A projector and the class-B projector. Equivalently:

1. The image of the processor is exactly the union of winding classes A and B.
2. The kernel of the processor is exactly winding class C.
3. The processor commutes with the class-C projector.

**Proof.** Direct inspection of the diagonal entries of the processor and of the class membership of each mode.

The processor is diagonal, with entries one, one, one, one, one, one, zero, one, one, one, zero, zero, zero, one in the standard mode order. Hence:

- The image of the processor — the modes with diagonal entry one — is the set consisting of the neutrino, electron, up quark, down quark, strange quark, muon, charm quark, tau, bottom quark, and top quark. This is the fermion sector.
- The kernel of the processor — the modes with diagonal entry zero — is the set consisting of the dark photon, W boson, Z boson, and Higgs. This is the boson sector.

On the other hand, the winding classes are:

- Class A: neutrino, electron, top quark.
- Class B: up quark, down quark, strange quark, muon, charm quark, tau, bottom quark.
- Class C: dark photon, W boson, Z boson, Higgs.

Hence the union of classes A and B equals the neutrino, electron, up quark, down quark, strange quark, muon, charm quark, tau, bottom quark, and top quark, which is exactly the image of the processor. And class C equals the dark photon, W boson, Z boson, and Higgs, which is exactly the kernel of the processor.

Since the processor is diagonal and the class-C projector is diagonal, they commute. The identity that the processor equals the identity minus the class-C projector is verified by comparing the diagonal entries of the identity-minus-class-C projector with the diagonal entries of the processor.

The full matrix identity is verified at machine precision: the Frobenius norm of the difference between the processor and the identity minus the class-C projector is exactly zero.

**Corollary (The fermion/boson split is a coarse-graining).** The fermion/boson split is the coarse-graining of the winding class partition that identifies classes A and B. Equivalently, the fermion sector is the image of the processor, and the boson sector is the kernel of the processor.

**Proof.** Immediate from the theorem.

**Remark.** The theorem states that the processor does not add structure beyond the winding class partition. The winding class partition is finer-grained: it separates class A from class B. The fermion/boson split ignores that distinction. The winding class partition and the fermion/boson split encode the same physical content, viewed at different coarse-graining scales.

### 3.2 The Landauer channel

**Proposition (Landauer entropy reduction).** The processor reduces the entropy of the maximally mixed state on the fifty-six-dimensional Hodge sector to the entropy of the maximally mixed state on the fermion/boson product. The entropy reduction is the logarithm of fifty-six, minus the sum of the logarithm of ten and the logarithm of four, which equals the logarithm of seven-fifths, approximately zero point three three six four seven nats. This saturates the deterministic Landauer bound.

**Proof.** Direct computation.

## 4. The Chirality-Breaking Localization Theorem

This section proves that the processor T and the chiral reflection fail to commute only on the two-dimensional plane spanned by the dark photon and the tau.

### 4.1 The commutator

**Theorem (Chirality-Breaking Localization).** The commutator of the processor with the chiral reflection — defined as the difference between the product of the processor with the chiral reflection and the product of the chiral reflection with the processor — is supported exactly on the two-element set consisting of the dark photon and the tau. On every other pair of indices, the commutator vanishes. The two-dimensional plane spanned by the dark photon and the tau is the framework's unique chirality-breaking plane.

**Proof.** Direct computation of the commutator.

The processor is diagonal with entries one, one, one, one, one, one, zero, one, one, one, zero, zero, zero, one. The chiral reflection permutes the modes by the diagonal reflection. We compute the two matrix products entry by entry.

For the commutator to be nonzero at a given pair of indices, we need both the chiral reflection to map the second mode to the first mode, and the processor's diagonal entries on the two modes to differ.

The chiral reflection maps each mode as follows in the standard mode order:

- Neutrino and electron are swapped.
- Up quark and strange quark are swapped.
- Down quark and bottom quark are swapped.
- Muon and charm quark are swapped.
- Tau and dark photon are swapped.
- W boson is fixed.
- Z boson and Higgs are swapped.
- Top quark is fixed.

For the pairs where the chiral reflection swaps two modes, we examine whether their diagonal entries in the processor differ:

- Neutrino and electron: both have processor entry one. Equal, no contribution.
- Up quark and strange quark: both have processor entry one. Equal, no contribution.
- Down quark and bottom quark: both have processor entry one. Equal, no contribution.
- Muon and charm quark: both have processor entry one. Equal, no contribution.
- Tau and dark photon: the tau has processor entry one, the dark photon has processor entry zero. Different, contributes to the commutator.
- Z boson and Higgs: both have processor entry zero. Equal, no contribution.

The two fixed points, the W boson and the top quark, contribute nothing, since the chiral reflection acts trivially on them and hence cannot create off-diagonal commutator entries.

Hence the commutator is supported exactly on the pair consisting of the dark photon and the tau.

### 4.2 The fourth-order structure of the commutator

**Proposition (The commutator as a fourth-order rotation generator).** On the two-dimensional plane spanned by the dark photon and the tau, the commutator is the two-by-two matrix with zero on the diagonal, minus one in the upper-right entry, and plus one in the lower-left entry. Its square is the negative of the projector onto this plane, and its fourth power is the positive projector onto this plane. Hence the commutator generates a fourth-order rotation on the chirality-breaking plane.

**Proof.** Direct computation. The diagonal entries of the commutator on the plane are one minus zero equals one, and zero minus one equals minus one; both vanish because the diagonal of the commutator is zero. The off-diagonal entries are the negative of the chiral reflection entry from the tau to the dark photon, multiplied by the tau's processor entry, which gives minus one; and the positive of the chiral reflection entry from the dark photon to the tau, multiplied by the dark photon's processor entry, which gives plus one. Hence the commutator restricted to the plane is the two-by-two matrix with zero on the diagonal, minus one in the upper-right entry, and plus one in the lower-left entry.

Squaring gives the negative of the two-by-two identity, which is the negative of the projector onto the chirality-breaking plane. Raising to the fourth power gives the positive projector. The commutator therefore generates a fourth-order rotation on the chirality-breaking plane, verified at machine precision in the companion verification script.

### 4.3 Commutation on the other twelve modes

**Corollary (Commutation elsewhere).** The processor T and the chiral reflection commute on the twelve modes not in the chirality-breaking plane consisting of the dark photon and the tau.

**Proof.** Immediate from the theorem. The restriction of the commutator to the subspace spanned by the other twelve modes is identically zero.

**Remark.** The localization of chirality-breaking is sharp. On the twelve modes outside the plane consisting of the dark photon and the tau, the processor preserves the chiral reflection's action exactly. Only on the two-mode plane does chirality break, and the breaking is a fourth-order rotation. This makes the framework's chirality content highly constrained.

## 5. Discussion

### 5.1 What the processor is

The processor T is not an independent structure on the fourteen-mode multiplet. It is the complement of the class-C projector. Its image is the union of classes A and B; its kernel is class C. The fermion/boson split is exactly the coarse-graining of the winding class partition that fuses classes A and B.

This means that the processor's output is determined by the winding class partition, which in turn is determined by the dihedral orbit partition plus the fermion/boson homogeneity constraint of HCSM-28. The whole processor structure is therefore determined by the substrate, without independent input.

### 5.2 What the chirality breaking is

The chirality-breaking of the processor is not a global feature. It is localized to the plane consisting of the dark photon and the tau. On the twelve other modes, the processor and the chiral reflection commute exactly.

The two chirality-breaking modes are the dark photon (a boson in the kernel of the processor) and the tau (a fermion in the image of the processor). Together they form the unique two-dimensional subspace on which the processor breaks the symmetry.

This two-mode plane is the intersection of the processor with the chirality structure: it is the unique place where the processor's fermion/boson distinction conflicts with the dihedral reflection.

### 5.3 Relation to the observer pair

The observer pair of HCSM-26 is the pair of fixed points of the chiral reflection: the W boson and the top quark. These are precisely the two modes on which the chiral reflection acts trivially. Since the processor and the chiral reflection commute on the W boson and the top quark, the observer pair is not part of the chirality-breaking plane.

The framework's structure therefore separates cleanly: the observer pair is the fixed locus of the chiral reflection, and the chirality-breaking plane is the unique two-mode plane where the processor and the chiral reflection disagree. These are two independent structural features of the fourteen-mode multiplet.

### 5.4 Relation to the Klein orbit structure

The two chirality-breaking modes, the dark photon and the tau, are members of the four-cycle orbit of the Klein group (HCSM-27). Specifically, the four-cycle orbit is the set consisting of the dark photon, strange quark, up quark, and tau, and the chirality-breaking pair is the subset consisting of the dark photon and the tau. The chiral reflection and the half-period translation both individually swap these two modes with other modes in the four-cycle; the product fixes them together, which is why the four-cycle orbit has trivial pointwise stabilizer.

## 7. Conclusion

This paper has proven two theorems about the processor T of the Hodge Complex Standard Model.

The first is the Fermion/Boson-Class Correspondence Theorem: the processor is exactly the complement of the class-C projector of the winding partition. Its image is the union of winding classes A and B; its kernel is class C. The fermion/boson split is the coarse-graining of the winding class partition that fuses classes A and B. Equivalently, the processor equals the identity minus the class-C projector.

The second is the Chirality-Breaking Localization Theorem: the processor's commutator with the chiral reflection is supported exactly on the two-mode plane spanned by the dark photon and the tau. On this plane, the commutator is a fourth-order rotation generator. On the other twelve modes, the processor and the chiral reflection commute exactly.

The framework's chirality content is therefore highly constrained. The chirality breaking is localized to a single two-mode plane, and the observer pair of HCSM-26 (the W boson and the top quark) is exactly the complement: the fixed locus of the chiral reflection. The processor's output and its chirality breaking are both determined by the winding class partition and the dihedral reflection, with no independent input.
