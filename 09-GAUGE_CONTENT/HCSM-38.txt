--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The Hodge Complex Standard Model (HCSM) identifies the 14 scalar modes at the
self-paired eigenvalue lambda = 4 of the discrete Laplacian on the periodic
8x8 torus with one Standard Model generation (HCSM-04, HCSM-23). This paper
establishes the hidden sector content of the framework: the 42 modes at
lambda = 4 that appear in the Hodge lift Omega^1|_{lambda=4} + Omega^2|_{lambda=4}
but are not identified with the 14 visible scalar modes. We compute the full
D4 decomposition of the Hodge complex Omega^0 + Omega^1 + Omega^2 from
character theory, derive the D4 content of the hidden sector as

    6A1 + 4A2 + 5B1 + 5B2 + 11E,

and show that under the sector-coarse correspondence of HCSM-06 this content
maps to the physical hidden sector

    (d+1) weak doublets + (d+2) color triplets + (N/2 - 1) singlets
    = 5 + 6 + 31.

The hidden sector content is derived from the D4 representation theory of the
Hodge complex on Lambda. Three physical choices are disclosed: the
identification of D4 irreps with SU(3) x SU(2) x U(1) representations, the
U(1)_Y neutrality of the hidden singlets, and the fermionic statistics of the
hidden B1 doublets. These three choices are consistent with the HCSM-06/07
disclosure pattern. The result sets up the Weinberg angle derivation of
companion paper HCSM-39. Every numerical claim is verified in reproducible
Python.

2. SETUP: THE HODGE COMPLEX AND THE D4 ACTION

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.1 The 8x8 torus
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The substrate is the periodic square lattice Lambda = Z_8 x Z_8 with N = 64
sites (HCSM-01). The framework constants are

    d = 4, N = d*2^d = 64, L = sqrt(N) = 8, H = 2^d = 16,

and the discrete 5-point Laplacian has eigenvalues

    lambda(n_1, n_2) = 4 - 2 cos(pi n_1 / 4) - 2 cos(pi n_2 / 4).

The self-paired eigenvalue is lambda = 4 with multiplicity 2(L-1) = 14.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.2 The Hodge complex
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

On the two-dimensional torus Lambda, the Hodge complex has three graded pieces
(HCSM-02):

    - Omega^0: 0-forms on the 64 sites, dim Omega^0 = 64, bosonic.
    - Omega^1: 1-forms on the 128 directed edges, dim Omega^1 = 128,
      fermionic.
    - Omega^2: 2-forms on the 64 plaquettes, dim Omega^2 = 64, bosonic.

The total dimension is

    dim(Omega^0 + Omega^1 + Omega^2) = 64 + 128 + 64 = 256.

The Hodge-Dirac operator D = d + d* satisfies D^2|Omega^k = Delta exactly on
each form degree, where Delta is the scalar 5-point Laplacian. The
Z2-grading (-1)^F distinguishes bosonic (Omega^0 + Omega^2) from fermionic
(Omega^1) sectors.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.3 The D4 action
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The dihedral group D4 of order 8 acts on Lambda by the point group of the
square, generated by the rotation

    r: (x_1, x_2) -> (-x_2, x_1) mod 8

and the reflection

    s: (x_1, x_2) -> (x_1, -x_2) mod 8,

subject to the presentation r^4 = s^2 = (rs)^2 = e. The group has eight
elements:

    {e, r, r^2, r^3, s, rs, r^2 s, r^3 s}.

The D4 action on the Hodge complex is:

    - On Omega^0: by permutation of the 64 sites.
    - On Omega^1: by the vector-twisted action. If omega = (omega_1, omega_2),
      then r: (omega_1, omega_2) -> (-omega_2, omega_1) and
      s: (omega_1, omega_2) -> (omega_1, -omega_2). As a D4 representation,
      Omega^1 = Omega^0 tensor E, where E is the 2-dimensional irreducible
      representation of D4.
    - On Omega^2: by permutation of the 64 plaquettes, dual to Omega^0.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.4 The D4 character table

The D4 group has five irreducible representations with dimensions 1, 1, 1, 1, 2,
denoted A1, A2, B1, B2, E. The character table is:

    -------------------------------------------------------------------------

    A2        1     1     1     1    -1    -1    -1      -1

    B2        1    -1     1    -1    -1     1    -1       1
    E         2     0    -2     0     0     0     0       0

3. THE FULL D4 DECOMPOSITION OF THE HODGE COMPLEX
================================================================================

This section derives the D4 decomposition of the full Hodge complex
Omega^0 + Omega^1 + Omega^2 from character theory. Although the lambda = 4
content is known from HCSM-02, the full decomposition is needed to establish

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.1 Character of Omega^0
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The character of the position representation Omega^0 is the number of sites
fixed by each D4 element.

    Table 2: Fixed-point counts for the D4 action on Lambda = Z_8 x Z_8.
    -------------------------------------------------------------------------
    Element    Action            Fixed points
    -------------------------------------------------------------------------
    e          (x, y)             64
    r          (-y, x)            2
    r^2        (-x, -y)           4
    r^3        (y, -x)            2
    s          (x, -y)            16
    rs         (y, x)             8
    r^2 s      (-x, y)            16
    r^3 s      (-y, -x)           8
    -------------------------------------------------------------------------

THEOREM 3.1 (Omega^0 decomposition). As a D4 representation, Omega^0
decomposes as

    Omega^0 = 15A1 + 3A2 + 10B1 + 6B2 + 15E.

Proof. Apply orthogonality relations with the character table (Table 1) and
the character values (Table 2). The multiplicities are:

    a1 = (1/8)(64 + 2 + 4 + 2 + 16 + 8 + 16 + 8) = 120/8 = 15,
    a2 = (1/8)(64 + 2 + 4 + 2 - 16 - 8 - 16 - 8) = 24/8 = 3,
    b1 = (1/8)(64 - 2 + 4 - 2 + 16 - 8 + 16 - 8) = 80/8 = 10,
    b2 = (1/8)(64 - 2 + 4 - 2 - 16 + 8 - 16 + 8) = 48/8 = 6,
    e  = (1/8)(64*2 + 4*(-2)) = 120/8 = 15.

Check: 15 + 3 + 10 + 6 + 30 = 64.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.2 Character of Omega^1
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Since Omega^1 = Omega^0 tensor E as D4 representations, its character is

    chi_{Omega^1}(g) = chi_{Omega^0}(g) * chi_E(g).

The character chi_E is 2 at e, 0 at r, r^3, s, rs, r^2 s, r^3 s, and -2 at r^2.

Hence the Omega^1 character is:

    chi_{Omega^1}(e)  = 128,
    chi_{Omega^1}(r)  = 0,
    chi_{Omega^1}(r^2) = -8,
    chi_{Omega^1}(r^3) = 0,
    chi_{Omega^1}(s)  = 0,
    chi_{Omega^1}(rs) = 0,
    chi_{Omega^1}(r^2 s) = 0,
    chi_{Omega^1}(r^3 s) = 0.

THEOREM 3.2 (Omega^1 decomposition). As a D4 representation,

    Omega^1 = 15A1 + 15A2 + 15B1 + 15B2 + 34E.

Proof. The tensor product of Omega^0 with E is computed using
E tensor E = A1 + A2 + B1 + B2:

    Omega^1 = Omega^0 tensor E
            = (15A1 + 3A2 + 10B1 + 6B2 + 15E) tensor E
            = 15E + 3E + 10E + 6E + 15(A1 + A2 + B1 + B2)
            = 15A1 + 15A2 + 15B1 + 15B2 + 34E.

Check: 15 + 15 + 15 + 15 + 68 = 128.

On a two-dimensional torus, the 2-form sector is Hodge-dual to the 0-form
sector: Omega^2 = Omega^0 as D4 representations.

THEOREM 3.3 (Omega^2 decomposition). As a D4 representation,

    Omega^2 = 15A1 + 3A2 + 10B1 + 6B2 + 15E.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.4 The full Hodge complex
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Combining Theorems 3.1-3.3:

THEOREM 3.4 (Full D4 decomposition). The Hodge complex on Lambda decomposes
as

    Omega^0 = 15A1 + 3A2 + 10B1 + 6B2 + 15E,
    Omega^1 = 15A1 + 15A2 + 15B1 + 15B2 + 34E,
    Omega^2 = 15A1 + 3A2 + 10B1 + 6B2 + 15E.

Proof. Add the multiplicities form-degree by form-degree. Check:
45 + 21 + 35 + 27 + 128 = 256.

REMARK 4.1 (Why the hidden sector is the Hodge lift). The visible sector uses

Dimension check: 6 + 4 + 5 + 5 + 22 = 42.

================================================================================

The hidden sector content derived in Section 5 contributes to the running of
the SM gauge couplings. Below the HCSM threshold, each hidden-sector field
contributes to the one-loop beta-function coefficients.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6.1 Beta function coefficients
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

For a gauge group G with coupling g, the one-loop beta-function coefficient is

    b_G = (11/3) C_2(G) - (2/3) sum_{Weyl} T(R) - (1/6) sum_{scalar} T(R),

where C_2(G) is the quadratic Casimir of the adjoint, T(R) is the Dynkin
index of representation R, and the sums run over Weyl fermions and complex
scalars.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6.2 Contributions from the hidden sector
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The hidden sector contributes to b_3 (SU(3)), b_2 (SU(2)), and b_1 (U(1))
according to its SM representations. Given the content
(5 doublets) + (6 triplets) + (31 singlets) with the disclosed U(1)_Y
neutrality:

    - SU(3) triplet contribution: 6 triplets, each with T(R) = 1/2.
    - SU(2) doublet contribution: 5 doublets, each with T(R) = 1/2.
    - SM singlet contribution: 31 singlets, each with T(R) = 0.

The net contributions to the beta-function coefficients (with the
statistics-factors of 2/3 for fermions and 1/6 for scalars) are the inputs to
the HCSM-39 Weinberg angle derivation. The precise accounting depends on the
fermionic/bosonic split of the hidden sector, which is determined by the form
degree:

    - Hidden sector modes in Omega^1 are fermionic.
    - Hidden sector modes in Omega^2 are bosonic.

We have derived the hidden sector content of the Hodge Complex Standard Model.
