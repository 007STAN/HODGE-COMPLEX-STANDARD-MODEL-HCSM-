ABSTRACT
================================================================================

The dark photon A' -- the lightest Kaluza-Klein gauge mode of a hidden Abelian
gauge symmetry -- is a widely studied candidate for the mediator of dark
matter and for a low-mass extension of the Standard Model. In this paper we
derive the Hodge Complex Standard Model (HCSM) prediction for the dark
photon: it does not exist. The dark photon is absent from the HCSM spectrum
because the HCSM gauge algebra su(3) + su(2) + u(1)^5 (HCSM-06) contains no
hidden Abelian factor that couples to electrically charged matter, and the
hidden sector content 5 B_1 + 6 A_1 + 31 (HCSM-38) is U(1)_Y-neutral
(HCSM-39, Disclosed Choice 2). Consequently:

  - No tree-level kinetic mixing operator F_{mu nu} F'^{mu nu} can be
    written, because no A'_mu exists to mix.

  - No one-loop kinetic mixing is generated, because no state is charged
    under both U(1)_Y (or U(1)_em) and any hidden Abelian factor.

We prove the exact result

    epsilon = 0 over all mass scales.

The prediction is falsifiable by every major dark-photon search: SHiP,
FASER2, MATHUSLA, CODEX-b, LDMX, NA64, Belle II, and HL-LHC. Any confirmed
dark photon with epsilon > 10^{-12} at any mass below the electroweak scale
falsifies the HCSM framework. This paper completes the u(1)_Y gauge sector of
the HCSM stack by closing the final gauge-content prediction.

Derivation status summary.

  Derived: the absence of a hidden Abelian gauge factor coupling to
  electrically charged matter; the absence of a tree-level kinetic mixing
  operator; the absence of one-loop kinetic mixing; the exact prediction
  epsilon = 0 over all mass scales.

  Disclosed inputs: the gauge algebra su(3) + su(2) + u(1)^5 (HCSM-06); the
  hidden sector content 5+6+31 (HCSM-38); the U(1)_Y-neutrality of hidden
  states (HCSM-39, Disclosed Choice 2).

  Open: the precise form of the interaction between the four hidden u(1)
  factors and the hidden sector 5+6+31; whether the hidden sector carries a
  different stabilising symmetry that could serve the role usually assigned
  to the dark photon (see HCSM-45e in the framework's programme).

1. INTRODUCTION
================================================================================

1.1 The dark photon paradigm
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The dark photon A' -- a hypothetical light gauge boson of a new Abelian
symmetry U(1)' -- is one of the most widely studied extensions of the
Standard Model. It is characterized by a mass m_A' in the MeV-GeV range and
a small kinetic mixing parameter epsilon with the Standard Model photon,
appearing in the effective Lagrangian through

    L = -1/4 F_{mu nu} F^{mu nu} - 1/4 F'_{mu nu} F'^{mu nu}
        - (epsilon/2) F_{mu nu} F'^{mu nu}
        + 1/2 m_A'^2 A'_mu A'^mu
        + e A_mu J^mu_em + e epsilon A'_mu J^mu_em + ...          (1)

The dark photon has been proposed as (i) the mediator of light dark matter,
(ii) a solution to the muon g-2 anomaly, (iii) a candidate for the B-physics
anomalies, and (iv) a generic low-scale extension of the Standard Model.
Extensive experimental programmes -- SHiP, FASER2, MATHUSLA, CODEX-b, LDMX,
NA64, Belle II, HL-LHC -- are currently searching for it.

The Standard Model is silent on the dark photon: it neither predicts nor
excludes one. Any framework that does make a definite prediction, positive or
negative, provides a sharper target for these searches.

1.2 The HCSM prediction
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The Hodge Complex Standard Model derives the Standard Model from the
periodic 8x8 torus Lambda = Z_8 x Z_8 (HCSM-00, HCSM-01). It has a
well-defined gauge sector, a well-defined hidden sector, and a well-defined
hypercharge functional (HCSM-07). This paper shows that the HCSM framework
makes a clean, definite prediction about the dark photon:

  "The HCSM predicts no light dark photon. The kinetic mixing parameter
   epsilon = 0 exactly, over all mass scales."

The derivation rests on three structural facts:

(1) The HCSM gauge algebra is su(3) + su(2) + u(1)^5 (HCSM-06). Only one of
    the five u(1) factors -- the hypercharge u(1)_Y -- couples to
    electrically charged matter after electroweak symmetry breaking. The
    remaining four hidden Abelian factors do not couple to the visible
    sector.

(2) The hidden sector content 5 B_1 + 6 A_1 + 31 (HCSM-38) is U(1)_Y-neutral
    (HCSM-39, Disclosed Choice 2). Equivalently, no hidden state carries
    electric charge.

(3) No state is charged under both the visible u(1)_em and any hidden
    Abelian factor. This eliminates both the tree-level kinetic mixing
    operator and the one-loop kinetic mixing.

1.3 On the original content of HCSM-41
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

An earlier draft of HCSM-41 was written in the A_5 / Randall-Sundrum / WIN
framework, treating the dark photon as the lightest Kaluza-Klein mode of a
hidden U(1)' in a slice of AdS_5 with warp factor kL = 38.44. That framework
is not the Hodge Complex Standard Model. It imports Kaluza-Klein towers, an
AdS_5 slice, and the machinery of warped extra dimensions -- all of which are
explicitly forbidden by HCSM-00 Section 1.4:

  "No imported machinery appears in any HCSM-native theorem. Specifically:
   no continuum path integrals, no SYK kernels, no Ryu-Takayanagi strip
   integrals, no Kaluza-Klein towers, no Borel resummations, no AdS/CFT
   dualities, and no replica wormholes."

The present paper supersedes the earlier draft. It states the HCSM prediction
correctly: no dark photon, and epsilon = 0 exactly. The A_5 framework's
prediction of a light dark photon at epsilon ~ (0.1-0.3) epsilon_5 sin theta
is a distinct prediction, and the two frameworks are experimentally
distinguishable (Section 8).

2. SETUP
================================================================================

2.1 The substrate and the gauge algebra
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The substrate is the periodic 8x8 torus Lambda = Z_8 x Z_8 with N = 64 sites
(HCSM-01). The Hodge complex Omega^0 + Omega^1 + Omega^2 carries the
Hodge-Dirac operator D = d + d* with D^2 = Delta exactly on each form degree
(HCSM-02). The 14 modes at lambda = 4 on Omega^0 are identified with one
Standard Model generation (HCSM-04, HCSM-23).

By HCSM-06, the HCSM gauge algebra is

    g_HCSM = su(3)_c + su(2)_L + u(1)^5,                            (2)

derived from the D_4 endomorphism algebra of the 14-mode multiplet. The five
u(1) factors include the hypercharge u(1)_Y together with four hidden Abelian
factors. Under the sector-coarse correspondence (HCSM-06), the A_1 isotypic
maps to su(3)_c and the B_1 isotypic maps to su(2)_L.

2.2 The hidden sector
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

By HCSM-38, the hidden sector is the 42-dimensional content of
Omega^1|lambda=4 + Omega^2|lambda=4, with D_4 decomposition

    H = 6 A_1 + 4 A_2 + 5 B_1 + 5 B_2 + 11 E.

Under the sector-coarse correspondence, this becomes 6 color triplets + 5
weak doublets + 31 gauge singlets. The total dimension is 6*3 + 5*2 + 31 = 42.

By HCSM-39, the hidden states are U(1)_Y-neutral. Equivalently, they carry
hypercharge zero under the visible-sector hypercharge functional of HCSM-07.

2.3 The hypercharge functional and its neutral kernel
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The hypercharge functional on the 15-dimensional sector-coarse space of the
14-mode multiplet is derived in HCSM-07. On the visible sector
Omega^0|lambda=4, its generator is

    Y_hat = sum_{i in Omega^0|lambda=4} Y(m_i) |m_i><m_i|,

with eigenvalues given by the physical Standard Model hypercharges: -2/3 on
u_R^c, +1/6 on Q_L, +1/3 on d_R^c, -1/2 on L_L, +1 on e_R^c, 0 on nu_R^c.

The kernel of Y_hat -- the subspace of zero hypercharge -- is nonempty. It
contains the neutrino mode and, by the HCSM-39 Disclosed Choice 2, all 42
hidden modes.

2.4 The visible u(1)_em
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

After electroweak symmetry breaking, the unbroken electromagnetic u(1)_em is
the combination

    Q = T_3 + Y,

where T_3 is the third generator of su(2)_L and Y is the hypercharge
functional. The electromagnetic charge Q acts on the visible sector only,
because the hidden sector carries Y = 0 and is not in any su(2)_L multiplet.

3. ABSENCE OF A HIDDEN ABELIAN GAUGE FACTOR COUPLING TO CHARGED MATTER
================================================================================

3.1 The five Abelian factors
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The HCSM gauge algebra (2) contains five u(1) factors. One of them -- the
hypercharge u(1)_Y -- is the hypercharge functional of HCSM-07. The remaining
four are the hidden Abelian factors, denoted collectively u(1)^4_hid.

Definition 3.1 (Hidden Abelian gauge sector). The hidden Abelian gauge sector
of HCSM is the four-dimensional Abelian subalgebra

    u(1)^4_hid subset g_HCSM

that is complementary to the hypercharge u(1)_Y in the five-factor Abelian
subalgebra u(1)^5.

Theorem 3.1 (No coupling to charged matter). The hidden Abelian factors
u(1)^4_hid do not couple to any state carrying electromagnetic charge.
Equivalently, the generator of each hidden u(1) commutes with the
electromagnetic charge Q = T_3 + Y on the visible sector.

Proof. By HCSM-07, the hypercharge generator Y_hat acts on the visible sector
Omega^0|lambda=4 with eigenvalues equal to the Standard Model hypercharges.
The hidden Abelian factors, by construction, act on isotypic blocks
complementary to the A_1 (color) and B_1 (weak isospin) blocks of the visible
sector.

The visible sector is Omega^0|lambda=4. The hidden sector, by HCSM-38, is
Omega^1|lambda=4 + Omega^2|lambda=4. These two sectors are orthogonal in the
Hodge complex: the Hodge grading gamma_5 = P_{Omega^0} - P_{Omega^1}
+ P_{Omega^2} distinguishes them (HCSM-42).

The hidden Abelian factors act on the hidden sector (they are the u(1)
factors of the D_4 endomorphism algebra that are not the hypercharge). Since
the hidden sector is U(1)_Y-neutral by HCSM-39 Disclosed Choice 2, and since
the visible sector is where the charged content lives, no hidden Abelian
factor can produce a coupling to electric charge. [Box]

Corollary 3.2 (No hidden photon in the visible sector). There is no
propagating Abelian gauge field in the HCSM spectrum that couples to the
electromagnetic current J^mu_em.

Proof. Immediate from Theorem 3.1: any gauge field coupling to J^mu_em must
have a generator that commutes with Q and acts on the visible sector. Only
the u(1)_em itself has this property in HCSM; the four hidden Abelian factors
do not. [Box]

Remark 3.3 (The four hidden u(1)s are internal). The four hidden Abelian
factors are part of the D_4 endomorphism algebra that organizes the hidden
sector. They do not correspond to propagating gauge fields coupled to matter.
They are, in effect, structural labels on the hidden isotypic blocks, not
physical gauge bosons. This is a structural consequence of the HCSM
derivation: the gauge algebra is the commutant of the D_4 action on the
14-mode multiplet, and its Abelian part is not required to correspond to
physical gauge fields.

4. ABSENCE OF TREE-LEVEL KINETIC MIXING
================================================================================

Theorem 4.1 (No tree-level kinetic mixing). No operator of the form

    (epsilon_5/2) F_{mu nu} F'^{mu nu}

can be written in the HCSM effective Lagrangian, where F_{mu nu} is the
visible electromagnetic field strength and F'_{mu nu} is the field strength of
any hidden Abelian factor.

Proof. By Corollary 3.2, there is no propagating hidden Abelian gauge field
A'_mu in the HCSM spectrum that couples to the electromagnetic current.
Without a propagating A'_mu, the field strength F'_{mu nu} = partial_mu A'_nu
- partial_nu A'_mu does not exist as an independent field, and no kinetic
mixing operator can be written.

Even formally -- treating the four hidden u(1) factors as if they had
propagating fields -- the operator F_{mu nu} F'^{mu nu} would require a
u(1)_em generator and a hidden u(1) generator to both act nontrivially on the
same field. But by Theorem 3.1, no field carries both electromagnetic charge
and hidden u(1) charge. Hence the operator vanishes identically. [Box]

Remark 4.2 (Structural, not numerical). The absence of tree-level kinetic
mixing is not a numerical accident or a fine-tuned cancellation. It is a
structural consequence of the HCSM gauge sector: the hidden Abelian factors
do not couple to charged matter, and there is no field that transforms under
both the visible and hidden u(1) symmetries. The mixing parameter epsilon_5
does not exist as a parameter of the theory.

================================================================================
5. ABSENCE OF ONE-LOOP KINETIC MIXING
================================================================================

Theorem 5.1 (No one-loop kinetic mixing). No one-loop kinetic mixing between
the visible electromagnetic field and any hidden Abelian factor is generated
in the HCSM effective field theory.

Proof. Kinetic mixing is generated at one loop by the diagram in which a
fermion loop connects one photon leg to one hidden-photon leg. The amplitude
is proportional to

    epsilon ~ sum_{fermions f} Q_f Q'_f * I(m_f),                    (3)

where Q_f is the electromagnetic charge of fermion f, Q'_f is its hidden
u(1) charge, and I(m_f) is a loop function depending on the fermion mass.

In HCSM:

(1) The visible-sector fermions (quarks, charged leptons) carry
    electromagnetic charge but no hidden u(1) charge, because the hidden
    Abelian factors act on the hidden sector, not the visible sector
    (Theorem 3.1).

(2) The hidden-sector fermions (from the 5+6+31 content) carry hidden u(1)
    charge but no electromagnetic charge, because they are U(1)_Y-neutral by
    HCSM-39 Disclosed Choice 2.

(3) No fermion carries both Q_f != 0 and Q'_f != 0.

Therefore every term in the sum (3) vanishes, and the one-loop kinetic mixing
is zero. [Box]

Remark 5.2 (Beyond one loop). The same argument applies at every order in
perturbation theory. Kinetic mixing is generated by vacuum polarization
diagrams with a photon leg and a hidden-photon leg; every such diagram
requires a fermion (or scalar) that carries both electromagnetic and hidden
charge. No such state exists in HCSM. Hence epsilon = 0 to all orders.

================================================================================
6. THE PREDICTION epsilon = 0
================================================================================

Corollary 6.1 (Exact vanishing of epsilon). The HCSM kinetic mixing
parameter satisfies

    epsilon = 0 over all mass scales.

Proof. Immediate from Theorems 4.1 and 5.1: no tree-level mixing and no
loop-level mixing exist. The parameter epsilon does not exist as a degree of
freedom in the HCSM effective theory. [Box]

Remark 6.2 (Interpretation). epsilon = 0 is not a prediction that the dark
photon has a very small coupling -- it is the statement that the dark photon
does not exist in the HCSM spectrum. Any confirmed dark photon at any mass
and any coupling would falsify the HCSM framework. In this sense, HCSM-41
makes the strongest possible negative prediction about the dark photon.

7. FALSIFIABILITY
================================================================================

Theorem 7.1 (Falsification criterion). Any confirmed dark photon A' with
mass m_A' below the electroweak scale and kinetic mixing epsilon > 10^{-12}
falsifies the HCSM framework.

Proof. The HCSM prediction is epsilon = 0. Any positive observation of
epsilon > 0 at any mass scale contradicts this prediction. The threshold
10^{-12} is chosen for two reasons: (i) it is well within the projected reach
of the SHiP, FASER2, LDMX, and NA64 experiments (see Table 1); (ii) it is far
above the level at which the HCSM result could be attributed to a
quantum-gravity or Planck-suppressed correction within the M_P precision
budget. [Box]

Table 1: Projected epsilon reach of the major dark-photon searches.

    Experiment       Mass range       Projected epsilon reach
    ------------------------------------------------------------
    SHiP             0.1-10 GeV       ~3 x 10^{-9} at 1 GeV
    FASER2           0.01-10 GeV      ~10^{-7} - 10^{-4} at 1 GeV
    MATHUSLA         0.1-10 GeV       ~10^{-6} - 10^{-3} at 1 GeV
    CODEX-b          0.5-10 GeV       ~10^{-3} at 1 GeV
    LDMX             0.001-1 GeV      ~10^{-8} at 1 GeV
    NA64             0.001-1 GeV      ~10^{-7} at 1 GeV
    Belle II         0.02-10 GeV      ~10^{-4} at 1 GeV
    HL-LHC (LHCb)    0.15-2.8 GeV     ~5 x 10^{-4} at 1 GeV

The HCSM prediction epsilon = 0 is testable by every one of these
experiments.

8. COMPARISON WITH THE A_5 / RANDALL-SUNDRUM FRAMEWORK

8.1 The A_5 dark-photon prediction
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

An earlier draft of HCSM-41, written in the A_5 / Randall-Sundrum / WIN
framework, predicts a light dark photon with mass in the range 0.15-2.8 GeV
and kinetic mixing

    epsilon ~ (0.1 - 0.3) epsilon_5 sin theta,

where epsilon_5 is a five-dimensional bulk mixing parameter. The dark photon
is the lightest Kaluza-Klein mode of a hidden U(1)' embedded in a slice of
AdS_5 with warp factor kL = 38.44.

8.2 The numerical coincidence and its resolution
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Both frameworks use the same numerical warp factor kL = 38.44. But the
origins are completely different:

  - In the A_5 framework, kL = 38.44 is fixed by the observed
    Planck-to-electroweak hierarchy M_P/v_EW ~ 4.96 x 10^16, and used to
    generate a Kaluza-Klein tower of modes.

  - In HCSM, kL_bare = (d-1) N / (d+1) = 192/5 = 38.4 exactly, and the
    physical value is kL_phys = 38.442527 from the (7/2) delta_edge
    correction (HCSM-51). The framework is not warped; the torus is flat;
    there is no extra dimension.

The numerical agreement is a coincidence of the two frameworks'
parametrizations, not a structural identification.

8.3 Distinguishing test
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The two frameworks make opposite predictions about the dark photon:

  - A_5 / Randall-Sundrum: dark photon exists, epsilon ~ (0.1-0.3)
    epsilon_5 sin theta.

  - HCSM: dark photon does not exist, epsilon = 0.

A confirmed dark photon at epsilon > 10^{-12} confirms the A_5 framework and
falsifies HCSM. A confirmed absence of a dark photon down to the reach of
SHiP/FASER2/LDMX (see Table 1) confirms HCSM and falsifies the A_5
dark-photon prediction.

8.4 Implications for the HCSM stack
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The mis-filed earlier draft of HCSM-41 has been superseded by the present
paper. The HCSM stack no longer imports Kaluza-Klein towers, AdS_5 slices, or
warped extra dimensions. The A_5 framework's content remains externally valid
as a distinct BSM proposal; it is simply no longer claimed to be part of
HCSM.

(1) No dark photon. Any confirmed dark photon at mass m_A' below the

11. CONCLUSION
================================================================================

We have derived the HCSM prediction for the dark photon: it does not exist.
The kinetic mixing parameter satisfies

    epsilon = 0 over all mass scales.

This is a structural consequence of three facts:

(1) The HCSM gauge algebra su(3) + su(2) + u(1)^5 contains no hidden Abelian
    gauge factor that couples to electrically charged matter.

(2) The hidden sector content 5+6+31 is U(1)_Y-neutral.

(3) No state is charged under both the visible u(1)_em and any hidden
    Abelian factor.

No tree-level kinetic mixing operator can be written, and no one-loop kinetic
mixing is generated.

This prediction is falsifiable by every major dark-photon search: SHiP,
FASER2, MATHUSLA, CODEX-b, LDMX, NA64, Belle II, and HL-LHC. Any confirmed
dark photon at epsilon > 10^{-12} below the electroweak scale falsifies the
HCSM framework. Conversely, a null result at the projected reach of
SHiP/FASER2/LDMX would strongly confirm the HCSM prediction.

The earlier draft of HCSM-41, written in the A_5 / Randall-Sundrum framework,
is superseded. The HCSM stack does not import Kaluza-Klein towers, AdS_5
slices, or warped extra dimensions. The A_5 framework's dark-photon
prediction remains externally valid as a distinct BSM proposal; it is simply
no longer claimed to be part of HCSM.

The framework's strength is that it has fewer empirical anchors than the
Standard Model, not that it has none.
