1.1 The Proca problem
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The Proca Lagrangian describes a massive vector field A_mu with a kinetic
term -1/4 F_{mu nu} F^{mu nu} and a mass term +1/2 m^2 A_mu A^mu. At the
level of the Lagrangian, it is not gauge invariant -- the mass term violates
the A_mu -> A_mu + partial_mu Lambda transformation rule. The Proca field
describes three propagating polarizations (two transverse, one longitudinal)
rather than the two of a massless gauge field.

In the Standard Model, the Proca sector describes the W+/- and Z bosons after
electroweak symmetry breaking. The masses are

    m_W = g v_EW / 2,
    m_Z = sqrt(g^2 + g'^2) v_EW / 2,
    m_W/m_Z = cos theta_W,                                           (1)

where g is the su(2)_L coupling, g' is the u(1)_Y coupling, v_EW = 246.21965
GeV is the electroweak vacuum expectation value, and sin theta_W = g' /
sqrt(g^2 + g'^2) is the weak mixing angle.

The Standard Model does not derive v_EW, g, or g'. In the Hodge Complex
Standard Model (HCSM), all three are derivable from the substrate, and the
Proca sector becomes a theorem.

    Y_A1 = alpha_A1 I + beta_A1 F + gamma_A1 O,

With the hypercharge functional in hand, the u(1)_Y gauge symmetry is the

3.2 The u(1)_Y gauge symmetry

mode basis). Any two diagonal operators commute, so [Y_hat, T] = 0.

For the orientation Z_2: F permutes the modes via F: (n_1, n_2) ->
(n_2, n_1). To check [Y_hat, F] = 0, it suffices to verify that Y(F(m)) =
Y(m) for every mode m. This follows from the HCSM-07 hypercharge functional:
the coefficient triples (0, -1/6, -1/2) and (0, +1/2, +1/2) are invariant
under the exchange of the two sectors Omega^0 <-> Omega^2, because the sector
operators satisfy

    I|sectors = (1, 1, 1),
    F|sectors = (1, -1, 1),
    O|sectors = (1, 0, -1),

which are all invariant under the sector swap Omega^0 <-> Omega^2. And F
preserves the D_4 orbits O_0, O_1, O_2 individually (HCSM-43, Theorem 3.1),
so the isotypic labels are preserved.

Direct computation on the 14-mode multiplet confirms [Y_hat, F] = 0 at
machine precision (see Section A, verification item 5). [Box]

4.1 The gauge sector before symmetry breaking
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Before electroweak symmetry breaking, the gauge sector Lagrangian is

    L_gauge = -1/4 W^a_{mu nu} W^{a mu nu} - 1/4 B_{mu nu} B^{mu nu},  (4)

where W^a_{mu nu} is the field strength of the su(2)_L gauge field W^a_mu
(a = 1, 2, 3) and B_{mu nu} is the field strength of the u(1)_Y gauge field
B_mu.

Both W^a_mu and B_mu are massless in (4). The su(2)_L gauge field has three
polarizations (one for each generator T^a); the u(1)_Y gauge field has one.

4.2 Electroweak symmetry breaking
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The HCSM Higgs doublet H is the complexified B_1 isotypic of
Omega^0|lambda=4 (HCSM-40). Its potential is

    V(H) = -mu^2 H^dagger H + lambda_tree (H^dagger H)^2,

with mu^2 = v_EW^2 lambda_tree and lambda_tree = ln(kL)/[(d-1) pi^2]
(HCSM-40, Theorem 17).

The Higgs field acquires a vacuum expectation value. In the unitary gauge,

    H = (1/sqrt(2)) (0, v_EW + h(x))^T,

where h(x) is the physical Higgs boson.

The kinetic term of the Higgs field generates the mass terms for the gauge
bosons through the covariant derivative

    D_mu H = (partial_mu - i g T^a W^a_mu - i (g'/2) Y B_mu) H,

where T^a are the su(2)_L generators and Y is the hypercharge generator of
Definition 3.1.

4.3 The Proca Lagrangian
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Theorem 4.1 (Proca Lagrangian). After electroweak symmetry breaking, the
effective Lagrangian for the massive W+/- and Z fields takes the Proca form

    L_Proca = -1/4 W^a_{mu nu} W^{a mu nu} - 1/4 B_{mu nu} B^{mu nu}
              + 1/2 m_W^2 W^+_mu W^{-mu} + 1/2 m_Z^2 Z_mu Z^mu,       (5)

with the mass eigenstates

    W^+/-_mu = (W^1_mu -/+ i W^2_mu) / sqrt(2),
    Z_mu = cos theta_W W^3_mu - sin theta_W B_mu,
    A_mu = sin theta_W W^3_mu + cos theta_W B_mu.                    (6)

The photon A_mu remains massless.

Proof. The mass terms arise from |D_mu H|^2 evaluated at the Higgs vacuum
expectation value. Substituting H = (0, v_EW/sqrt(2))^T into the covariant
derivative and expanding, the mass matrix for the gauge fields
(W^1, W^2, W^3, B) takes the block form

    M^2 = (v_EW^2/4) ( g^2  0    0    0
                       0    g^2  0    0
                       0    0    g^2  -g g'
                       0    0   -g g'  g'^2 ).

The upper 2x2 block gives mass g v_EW/2 to W^1 and W^2, which combine into
the charged W+/- with mass m_W = g v_EW/2. The lower 2x2 block has
eigenvalues 0 and (g^2 + g'^2) v_EW^2/4, corresponding to the massless photon
A_mu and the massive Z boson with mass m_Z = sqrt(g^2 + g'^2) v_EW/2. The
mixing angle is tan theta_W = g'/g. [Box]

The Proca Lagrangian (5) is the standard description of the massive vector
bosons after electroweak symmetry breaking. In the HCSM derivation, it
follows from the HCSM Higgs mechanism (HCSM-40) and the HCSM hypercharge
functional (HCSM-07).

5. THE MASS RATIO
================================================================================

Theorem 5.1 (Mass ratio). The Proca mass ratio is

    m_W/m_Z = cos theta_W = sqrt(1 - sin^2 theta_W).

With the HCSM-39 value sin^2 theta_W(M_Z) = 0.2312,

    m_W/m_Z = sqrt(1 - 0.2312) = 0.8769.

Proof. From Theorem 4.1, m_W = g v_EW/2 and m_Z = sqrt(g^2 + g'^2) v_EW/2.
Their ratio is

    m_W/m_Z = g / sqrt(g^2 + g'^2) = cos theta_W,

where tan theta_W = g'/g. Using the standard relation
sin^2 theta_W = g'^2/(g^2 + g'^2), we have cos^2 theta_W = 1 - sin^2 theta_W.
Substituting the HCSM-39 value gives the numerical result. [Box]

Corollary 5.2 (Tree-level masses). With the HCSM-40 value v_EW = 246.21965
GeV, the value g fixed by alpha_em(M_Z) = 1/127.9 and the HCSM-39 value
sin^2 theta_W = 0.2312, the tree-level Proca masses are

    m_W = 80.35 GeV, m_Z = 91.63 GeV.

These match the PDG values m_W = 80.377 GeV and m_Z = 91.1876 GeV to 0.03%
and 0.5% respectively.

Proof. From the electromagnetic relation alpha_em = e^2/4 pi with
e = g sin theta_W and the HCSM-39 value sin^2 theta_W = 0.2312, the su(2)_L
coupling is g = e/sin theta_W. Substituting into m_W = g v_EW/2 and

m_Z) is consistent with the tree-level approximation; two-loop corrections
and threshold effects from the hidden sector are expected to shift the
prediction at the sub-percent level. [Box]

6. TRANSVERSALITY
================================================================================

Theorem 6.1 (Transversality). The Proca field satisfies the equation of
motion

    partial^mu A_mu = 0

for each massive vector field A_mu in {W^+/-_mu, Z_mu}. The longitudinal mode
is the Goldstone boson eaten by the Higgs mechanism.

Proof. The equation of motion from the Proca Lagrangian (5) is

    partial^mu F_{mu nu} + m^2 A_nu = 0.

Taking the divergence,

    partial^nu partial^mu F_{mu nu} + m^2 partial^nu A_nu = 0.

The first term vanishes by antisymmetry of F_{mu nu}. Hence
partial^nu A_nu = 0 for m^2 != 0.

The Goldstone boson is eaten in the HCSM Higgs mechanism: in the unitary
gauge, the three would-be Nambu-Goldstone modes of the Higgs doublet are
absorbed as the longitudinal polarizations of W+/- and Z. The HCSM Higgs
doublet is the complexified B_1 isotypic of Omega^0|lambda=4 (HCSM-40,
Theorem 3.2), which provides exactly the three would-be Goldstone modes for
the three massive vector bosons. [Box]

Remark 6.2 (Goldstone count). The Higgs doublet has four real components.
After electroweak symmetry breaking, three are eaten by W+/- and Z (giving
each its longitudinal polarization), and one remains as the physical Higgs
boson h(x). This matches the physical Higgs content.
