ABSTRACT
================================================================================

The Hodge Complex Standard Model identifies the graviton as the self-paired
curvature mode of the Hodge complex on the periodic 8 x 8 torus: the
symmetrized bilinear h = omega (x) omega in the substrate 1-form, projected
onto the B_1 + B_2 isotypic piece of Sym^2(Omega^1|lambda=4), with
polarization space V = B_1 + B_2 (HCSM-45). This paper establishes the
self-coupling structure of the graviton by computing the D4-invariant
content of the graviton vertex spaces. We prove: (i) the pure-polynomial
A_1 counts of Sym^n(V) for n = 1, 2, 3, 4 are 0, 2, 0, 3, forbidding pure-
polynomial kinetic and cubic graviton self-couplings; (ii) the derivative-
included A_1 counts with two derivatives are 5, 12, 11 at orders 2, 3, 4,
establishing the representation-theoretic content required for the kinetic,
cubic, and quartic vertices; (iii) the odd-derivative selection rule: the
A_1 content vanishes for odd numbers of derivatives, as a consequence of
the faithful action of D4 on the derivative irrep E; (iv) the D4 action on
ker D ~ 2A_1 + E embeds as a finite subgroup of O(3,1), so D4-invariant
structures are a subset of Lorentz-invariant structures. The results
reproduce the derivative structure of the Einstein-Hilbert expansion: the
pure-polynomial kinetic and cubic vertices vanish, and the physical
vertices carry exactly two derivatives. The graviton is a composite
bilinear, and its nonlinearity arises from the composite structure of
h = omega (x) omega, not from the Faddeev-Popov measure, which is
field-independent on the discrete torus. All claims are HCSM-native and
verified at machine precision.

1.1 The nonlinear sector of gravity
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

General relativity is nonlinear. Expanding the Einstein-Hilbert action
around a flat background produces a tower of self-couplings at every order
in the metric fluctuation h_mu_nu, and every vertex carries derivatives:
the kinetic term has two derivatives, the cubic vertex has two derivatives,
and the quartic vertex has either two or four. No vertex is a pure
polynomial in h.

In the Hodge Complex Standard Model (HCSM), the graviton is the self-paired
curvature mode of the substrate: the symmetrized bilinear
h = omega (x) omega in the substrate 1-form omega, projected onto the
B_1 + B_2 isotypic piece of Sym^2(Omega^1|lambda=4) (HCSM-45 [HCSM-45]).
The polarization space is V = B_1 + B_2, of dimension 2, with D4 character
(2, 2, -2, 0, 0) on the five conjugacy classes.

This paper establishes the self-coupling structure of the graviton. It
computes the D4-invariant content of the graviton vertex spaces, both pure
polynomial and derivative included, and shows that the framework reproduces
the derivative structure of the Einstein-Hilbert expansion: pure-polynomial
kinetic and cubic vertices vanish, and the physical vertices carry exactly
two derivatives.

    The pure-polynomial D4-invariant content of the graviton vertex spaces

2.3 The cycle-index formula for symmetric powers
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

For a finite-dimensional representation V of a finite group and any group
element g, the character of Sym^n(V) on g is given by the cycle-index
formula

    chi_{Sym^n(V)}(g) = (1/n!) sum_{sigma in S_n}
                          prod_{cycles kappa} chi_V(g^{|kappa|}),   (3)

where the product runs over the cycles kappa of the permutation sigma and
|kappa| is the length of the cycle. For small n, the formula reduces to

    chi_{Sym^1(V)}(g) = chi_V(g),

    chi_{Sym^2(V)}(g) = (1/2) [chi_V(g)^2 + chi_V(g^2)],

    chi_{Sym^3(V)}(g) = (1/6) [chi_V(g)^3
                        + 3 chi_V(g) chi_V(g^2)
                        + 2 chi_V(g^3)],

    chi_{Sym^4(V)}(g) = (1/24) [chi_V(g)^4
                        + 6 chi_V(g)^2 chi_V(g^2)
                        + 3 chi_V(g^2)^2
                        + 8 chi_V(g) chi_V(g^3)
                        + 6 chi_V(g^4)].

The multiplicities of the irreps in Sym^n(V) follow from the orthogonality
relation

    m_rho = (1/|D4|) sum_C |C| chi_{Sym^n(V)}(C) chi_rho(C).         (4)

3. PURE-POLYNOMIAL SELF-COUPLING
================================================================================

Definition 3.1 (Pure-polynomial vertex space). The pure-polynomial vertex
space at order n is Sym^n(V).

Theorem 3.2 (Pure-polynomial A_1 counts). The D4 isotypic decompositions
of Sym^n(V) for n = 1, 2, 3, 4 are:

    Sym^1(V) = B_1 + B_2,       dim = 2, A_1 count = 0,              (5)

    Sym^2(V) = 2A_1 + A_2,      dim = 3, A_1 count = 2,              (6)

    Sym^3(V) = 2B_1 + 2B_2,     dim = 4, A_1 count = 0,              (7)

    Sym^4(V) = 3A_1 + 2A_2,     dim = 5, A_1 count = 3.              (8)

The dimensions match (n+1 choose n) = n + 1, as required for symmetric
powers of a 2-dimensional representation.

Proof. We compute chi_{Sym^n(V)} for each n using (3), then apply (4) to
extract multiplicities.

n = 1: chi_{Sym^1(V)} = chi_V = (2, 2, -2, 0, 0). Multiplicities:
m_{A_1} = (1/8)(2 + 2 - 4 + 0 + 0) = 0; m_{B_1} = (1/8)(2 + 2 + 4 + 0 + 0)
= 1; m_{B_2} = 1. So Sym^1(V) = B_1 + B_2.

n = 2: Using chi_{Sym^2(V)}(g) = (chi_V(g)^2 + chi_V(g^2))/2, and the
values chi_V(g^2):

  g = e:   chi_V(e) = 2, chi_V(e) = 2, so chi_{Sym^2(V)}(e) = 3,
  g = r^2: chi_V(r^2) = 2, chi_V(e) = 2, so chi_{Sym^2(V)}(r^2) = 3,
  g = r:   chi_V(r) = -2, chi_V(r^2) = 2, so chi_{Sym^2(V)}(r) = 3,
  g = s:   chi_V(s) = 0, chi_V(e) = 2, so chi_{Sym^2(V)}(s) = 1,
  g = rs:  chi_V(rs) = 0, chi_V(e) = 2, so chi_{Sym^2(V)}(rs) = 1.

So chi_{Sym^2(V)} = (3, 3, 3, 1, 1). Multiplicities:
m_{A_1} = (1/8)(3 + 3 + 6 + 2 + 2) = 2; m_{A_2} = (1/8)(3 + 3 + 6 - 2 - 2) = 1.
So Sym^2(V) = 2A_1 + A_2.

n = 3: Using chi_{Sym^3(V)}(g) = (chi_V(g)^3 + 3 chi_V(g) chi_V(g^2)
+ 2 chi_V(g^3))/6:

  g = e:   (8 + 12 + 4)/6 = 4,
  g = r^2: (8 + 12 + 4)/6 = 4,
  g = r:   (-8 - 12 - 4)/6 = -4,
  g = s:   0,
  g = rs:  0.

So chi_{Sym^3(V)} = (4, 4, -4, 0, 0). Multiplicities:
m_{A_1} = (1/8)(4 + 4 - 8 + 0 + 0) = 0; m_{B_1} = (1/8)(4 + 4 + 8 + 0 + 0)
= 2; m_{B_2} = 2. So Sym^3(V) = 2B_1 + 2B_2.

n = 4: Using the cycle-index formula for Sym^4:

  g = e:   (16 + 48 + 12 + 32 + 12)/24 = 5,
  g = r^2: (16 + 48 + 12 + 32 + 12)/24 = 5,
  g = r:   (16 + 48 + 12 + 32 + 12)/24 = 5,
  g = s:   (0 + 0 + 12 + 0 + 12)/24 = 1,
  g = rs:  1.

So chi_{Sym^4(V)} = (5, 5, 5, 1, 1). Multiplicities:
m_{A_1} = (1/8)(5 + 5 + 10 + 2 + 2) = 3; m_{A_2} = (1/8)(5 + 5 + 10 - 2 - 2)
= 2. So Sym^4(V) = 3A_1 + 2A_2. [Box]

Remark 3.3 (Interpretation). The vanishing A_1 content at order 1 forbids
a pure-polynomial linear graviton coupling. The vanishing A_1 content at
order 3 forbids a pure-polynomial cubic graviton self-coupling. This is
the discrete analogue of the standard statement that gauge invariance
forbids pure-polynomial self-couplings of the graviton. The order-2
invariants correspond to mass terms, forbidden as physical masses by Schur
orthogonality of the A_1 trace sector and the B_1 + B_2 graviton sector
(HCSM-46 [HCSM-46]). The order-4 invariants provide the pure-quartic
potential structure.

4.1 Derivatives as E insertions
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

On the discrete torus, the nearest-neighbor difference of a B_1 + B_2
field transforms in the two-dimensional vector irrep E of D4. A vertex
with m total derivatives is characterized by the representation content
of the tensor product of n graviton legs with m derivative insertions,
symmetrized over identical legs.

Definition 4.1 (Derivative-included vertex space). The n-point vertex
space with m derivatives is

    Vc_{n,m} = sum_{k_1 + ... + k_n = m, k_i >= 0}
                 (x)_k Sym^{a_k}(V (x) Sym^k(E)),                   (9)

where the outer tensor product runs over distinct values of k, and a_k
is the number of legs with derivative count k.

    Vc_{4,2}: dim = 54, A_1 count = 11.                              (12)

Proof. We enumerate the partitions of m = 2 into n non-negative parts and
apply the vertex-space formula (9).

n = 2: Partitions of 2 into 2 parts: (2, 0) and (1, 1).

Partition (2, 0): distinct values k in {0, 2} with a_0 = 1, a_2 = 1.
Contribution: Sym^1(V (x) Sym^0(E)) (x) Sym^1(V (x) Sym^2(E)) =
V (x) V (x) Sym^2(E). Since Sym^0(E) = C.

Total for n = 2: 2 + 3 = 5. Dimension: 12 + 10 = 22. Check.

5. THE ODD-DERIVATIVE SELECTION RULE
================================================================================

Theorem 5.1 (Odd-derivative vanishing). For all n, the A_1-invariant
content of Vc_{n,m} vanishes when m is odd:

    dim Hom_{D4}(A_1, Vc_{n,m}) = 0 for m odd.                      (13)

Proof. The vector irrep E of D4 is faithful on the reflection classes:
for a reflection s, det(s|_E) = -1. A tensor product E^{(x) m} transforms
under s by det(s|_E)^m = (-1)^m. The trivial irrep A_1 transforms trivially
under s. Therefore the projection of E^{(x) m} onto A_1 vanishes for odd
m. [Box]

Corollary 5.2 (Even-derivative self-coupling). The derivative-included
self-coupling structure of the graviton appears only at even derivative
number. The leading nontrivial derivative structures are at m = 2, 4,
6, ...

Remark 5.3 (Consistency with the Einstein-Hilbert expansion). The
Einstein-Hilbert expansion has vertices with an even number of
derivatives: the kinetic term has two, the cubic vertex has two, and the
quartic vertex has two or four. The odd-derivative selection rule is the
discrete analogue of this even-derivative structure.

6. EMBEDDING IN O(3,1)
================================================================================

Theorem 6.1 (D4 action on ker D). The D4 action on ker D decomposes as

    ker D = 2A_1 + E,                                                (14)

where the two A_1 components correspond to the constant 0-form and the
constant 2-form, and the E component corresponds to the two harmonic
1-forms.

Proof. Established in HCSM-03 [HCSM-03] for the Lorentzian signature
derivation. The kernel dimension is
dim ker D = b_0 + b_1 + b_2 = 1 + 2 + 1 = 4, matching
dim (2A_1 + E) = 2 + 2 = 4. [Box]

Theorem 6.2 (O(3,1) embedding). The D4 action on ker D embeds as a finite
subgroup of O(3,1).

Proof. The E component of ker D carries a real two-dimensional
representation of D4 on which the generators r and s act as rotations by
90 degrees and reflections respectively, giving a finite subgroup of
O(2). The two A_1 components of ker D are scalars under D4. The canonical
Lorentzian metric on ker D, constructed in HCSM-03 [HCSM-03], assigns
signature (3,1) with the timelike direction in the A_1 sector.

The combination of the E action on a spacelike 2-plane with the two A_1
directions embeds D4 as a finite subgroup of O(3,1):

    D4 -> O(2) -> O(3,1),                                            (15)

where O(2) acts as rotations of a spacelike plane, and the two A_1
directions correspond to the timelike and longitudinal directions. [Box]

Remark 6.3 (From D4 to Lorentz invariance). Every O(3,1)-invariant
structure is automatically D4-invariant, because D4 is a subgroup of
O(3,1). The D4-invariant content is therefore a necessary condition on
any Lorentz-invariant structure. In particular, the vanishing of pure-
polynomial A_1 content at orders 1 and 3 is a statement about the full
Lorentz-invariant structure, not only about the discrete subgroup.

7.1 The substrate action is quadratic
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The HCSM substrate action on the Hodge complex at lambda = 4 is

    S[omega] = (1/2) <omega, D^2 omega>,                             (16)

purely quadratic in the substrate 1-form omega in Omega^1. The path
integral over omega is Gaussian.

7.2 The graviton is a composite operator
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Proposition 7.1 (Graviton as bilinear). The graviton is the self-paired
bilinear of the substrate field,

    h = omega (x) omega,                                             (17)

projected onto the B_1 + B_2 isotypic sector of
Sym^2(Omega^1|lambda=4) (HCSM-45 [HCSM-45]).

Proof. Direct consequence of the graviton identification in HCSM-45
[HCSM-45]. The graviton multiplet is 54B_1 + 54B_2 in
Sym^2(Omega^1|lambda=4), which is the image of the bilinear map
omega (x) omega -> Sym^2(Omega^1) restricted to the B_1 + B_2 sector.
[Box]

The nonlinear self-coupling of the graviton is generated by the composite
structure of the bilinear h = omega (x) omega, combined with the Gaussian
path integral over omega. The effective action for h is obtained by

    exp(-S_eff[h]) = integral D omega delta(h - omega (x) omega)
                       exp(-S[omega]),                              (18)

and its expansion in powers of h generates the graviton self-couplings.
The D4 structure of the effective vertices is determined by the D4
structure of the composite map omega (x) omega -> h, which is a
D4-equivariant bilinear. The combination produces the derivative-included
vertices counted in Theorem 4.2.

7.3 Faddeev-Popov field-independence
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Proposition 7.2 (Field-independent FP measure). The Faddeev-Popov
measure on the discrete torus does not contribute to nonlinear vertices.

Proof. In the Faddeev-Popov derivation of the Newton constant (HCSM-48
[HCSM-48]), the gauge-fixing condition on the discrete torus is
d* omega = f, linear in omega. The Faddeev-Popov operator is the scalar
Laplacian on Omega^0, whose determinant is field-independent. Therefore
the Faddeev-Popov measure does not contribute to the nonlinear vertices
on the discrete torus. The nonlinearity of the effective gravitational
action arises entirely from the composite structure (18). [Box]

Remark 7.3 (Consequence for the composite-operator effective action).
The composite-operator effective action — the omega-integral (18) and its
expansion in powers of h — is the subject of HCSM-50 [HCSM-50]. The
present paper establishes the D4-invariant content of the vertex spaces
on which that effective action acts.

   These are standard representation-theoretic facts.
5. The Faddeev-Popov gauge-fixing condition d* omega = f (HCSM-48

  - Compute the full composite-operator effective action. This is the

embedding.

================================================================================
10. CONCLUSION
================================================================================

This paper has established the self-coupling structure of the graviton in
the Hodge Complex Standard Model.

The pure-polynomial D4-invariant content of the graviton vertex spaces is
(0, 2, 0, 3) at orders 1 through 4. Pure-polynomial kinetic and cubic
graviton self-couplings are forbidden by D4 symmetry — the discrete
analogue of the standard statement that gauge invariance forbids pure-
polynomial self-couplings of the graviton.

The derivative-included content with two derivatives is (5, 12, 11) at
orders 2, 3, 4. These establish the representation-theoretic content
required for the kinetic, cubic, and quartic vertices. The pattern
reproduces the derivative structure of the Einstein-Hilbert expansion.

The odd-derivative selection rule forbids self-couplings with an odd
number of derivatives, as a consequence of the faithful action of D4 on
the derivative irrep E.

The D4 action on ker D ~ 2A_1 + E embeds as a finite subgroup of O(3,1),
so D4-invariant structures are a subset of Lorentz-invariant structures.
The vanishing of pure-polynomial kinetic and cubic content is therefore a
statement about the full Lorentz-invariant structure, not only about the
discrete subgroup.

The graviton is a composite bilinear h = omega (x) omega in the substrate
1-form omega, and its nonlinear self-coupling is generated by the
composite structure through the effective action

    exp(-S_eff[h]) = integral D omega delta(h - omega (x) omega)
                       exp(-S[omega]).                              (19)

The Faddeev-Popov measure does not contribute nonlinear vertices on the
discrete torus because the gauge-fixing condition is linear and the
determinant is field-independent.

The explicit computation of the composite-operator effective action, the
reduction of the raw representation-theoretic counts to physical vertex
counts, and the matching to the Einstein-Hilbert expansion are stated as
the remaining open steps toward the full nonlinear Einstein equations
(HCSM-50 [HCSM-50]).
