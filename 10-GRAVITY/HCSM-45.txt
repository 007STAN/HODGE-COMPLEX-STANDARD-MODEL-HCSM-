ABSTRACT
================================================================================

The Hodge Complex Standard Model realizes one Standard Model generation on
the 14 modes at the self-paired eigenvalue lambda = 4 of the discrete
Laplacian on the periodic 8 x 8 torus (HCSM-04), and its gauge structure on
the full 56-dimensional Hodge sector Omega^0 + Omega^1 + Omega^2 at
lambda = 4 (HCSM-02, HCSM-06). This paper identifies the graviton as the
self-paired curvature mode of the substrate: the symmetric bilinear
h = omega (x) omega in the substrate 1-form omega in Omega^1, projected
onto the B_1 + B_2 isotypic sector of the symmetric tensor square
Sym^2(Omega^1|lambda=4). We prove: (i) the D4 isotypic decomposition of
Sym^2(Omega^1|lambda=4) is

    Sym^2(Omega^1|lambda=4)
        = 60 A_1 + 46 A_2 + 54 B_1 + 54 B_2 + 96 E,

of total dimension 60 + 46 + 54 + 54 + 192 = 406 = 28 * 29 / 2; (ii) the
graviton sector is the B_1 + B_2 isotypic piece, comprising
54 B_1 + 54 B_2 = 108 modes; (iii) the graviton polarization space is
V = B_1 + B_2, of dimension 2, with D4 character (2, 2, -2, 0, 0) on the
five conjugacy classes; (iv) the B_1 component corresponds to the
h_xx - h_yy polarization and the B_2 component to the h_xy polarization.
The result is HCSM-native: it uses only the Hodge complex of HCSM-02, the
D4 representation theory of HCSM-05 and HCSM-06, and the standard tensor-
square character formula. No imported gravitational machinery appears. All
claims are verified at machine precision.

1.1 The graviton problem
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The Hodge Complex Standard Model (HCSM) derives the gauge algebra
su(3) + su(2) + u(1)^5 as a Lie subalgebra of the D4 commutant on the
14-mode multiplet at lambda = 4 (HCSM-06 [HCSM-06]) and identifies the
physical hypercharge functional on the sector-coarse space (HCSM-07
[HCSM-07]). The full 56-dimensional Hodge sector at lambda = 4 carries
the D4 content

    Omega^0|lambda=4 + Omega^1|lambda=4 + Omega^2|lambda=4
        = 9 A_1 + 5 A_2 + 7 B_1 + 7 B_2 + 14 E                       (1)

(HCSM-02 [HCSM-02], HCSM-05 [HCSM-05]).

The graviton in general relativity is a massless spin-2 field h_mu_nu,
identified with the metric fluctuation g_mu_nu = eta_mu_nu + h_mu_nu.
In the HCSM framework, the natural home of the graviton is the symmetric
tensor square Sym^2(Omega^1) of the 1-form sector: this is the discrete
analogue of the space of symmetric 2-tensors on the torus.

This paper establishes the graviton as the self-paired curvature mode of
the substrate: the bilinear h = omega (x) omega in the substrate 1-form
omega in Omega^1, symmetrized and projected onto the B_1 + B_2 isotypic
sector of Sym^2(Omega^1|lambda=4).

2.1 The Hodge complex at lambda = 4
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The substrate is the periodic square lattice Lambda = Z_8 x Z_8 with
N = 64 sites (HCSM-01 [HCSM-01]). The Hodge complex of differential
forms is

    Omega^0 = R^64,  Omega^1 = R^128,  Omega^2 = R^64,               (4)

of total dimension 256 (HCSM-02 [HCSM-02]). The Hodge-Dirac operator
D = d + d* satisfies D^2|Omega^k = Delta exactly on each form degree.

At the self-paired eigenvalue lambda = 4, the Hodge complex decomposes
under D4 as (HCSM-02 [HCSM-02], HCSM-05 [HCSM-05]):

    Omega^0|lambda=4 = 3 A_1 + A_2 + 2 B_1 + 2 B_2 + 3 E,            (5)

    Omega^1|lambda=4 = 3 A_1 + 3 A_2 + 3 B_1 + 3 B_2 + 8 E,          (6)

    Omega^2|lambda=4 = 3 A_1 + A_2 + 2 B_1 + 2 B_2 + 3 E.            (7)

The sector of interest in this paper is

    V_1 = Omega^1|lambda=4,  dim V_1 = 3 + 3 + 3 + 3 + 16 = 28.      (8)

2.2 The D4 character table
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The D4 irreps are A_1, A_2, B_1, B_2, E (four 1-dimensional, one
2-dimensional). The conjugacy classes are {e}, {r^2}, {r, r^3},
{s, r^2 s}, {rs, r^3 s} with class sizes (1, 1, 2, 2, 2):

    irrep    e    r^2   r     s     rs
    -----   ---   ---   ---   ---   ---
    A_1      1     1     1     1     1
    A_2      1     1     1    -1    -1
    B_1      1     1    -1     1    -1
    B_2      1     1    -1    -1     1
    E        2    -2     0     0     0

(Columns are labeled by a representative of the class.)

2.3 The D4 tensor-product rules
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The tensor product of D4 irreps follows the standard rules. The relevant
ones for this paper are:

    A_1 (x) rho = rho  for any irrep rho,

    A_2 (x) A_2 = A_1,

    A_2 (x) B_1 = B_2,  A_2 (x) B_2 = B_1,

    A_2 (x) E = E,

    B_1 (x) B_1 = A_1,  B_2 (x) B_2 = A_1,

    B_1 (x) B_2 = A_2,

    B_i (x) E = E  for i = 1, 2,

    E (x) E = A_1 + A_2 + B_1 + B_2.

In particular, the symmetric and antisymmetric parts of E (x) E are

    Sym^2(E) = A_1 + B_1,  Lam^2(E) = A_2 + B_2.                    (9)

2.4 The symmetric-square character formula
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

For a finite group G acting on a finite-dimensional representation V, the
character of Sym^2(V) on a group element g is

    chi_{Sym^2(V)}(g) = (1/2) [chi_V(g)^2 + chi_V(g^2)].             (10)

The multiplicities of the irreps in Sym^2(V) follow from the orthogonality
relation

    m_rho = (1/|G|) sum_C |C| chi_{Sym^2(V)}(C) chi_rho(C),          (11)

where the sum runs over conjugacy classes C and |C| is the class size.

3.1 Sym^2 of the individual isotypic pieces
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Let V_1 = sum_i V_i with V_i = m_i rho_i the isotypic pieces. Then

    Sym^2(V_1) = sum_i Sym^2(V_i) + sum_{i<j} (V_i (x) V_j).         (12)

We compute each piece for V_1 = 3 A_1 + 3 A_2 + 3 B_1 + 3 B_2 + 8 E.

Lemma 3.1 (Sym^2 of the 1-dimensional pieces). For V_i = 3 rho_i with
rho_i in {A_1, A_2, B_1, B_2},

    Sym^2(3 rho_i) = 6 A_1.                                          (13)

Proof. Take rho_i = B_1 (the other cases are identical). Then V = 3 B_1
is a 3-dimensional space with D4 action via the character chi_{B_1} on
each basis vector. So chi_V(g) = 3 chi_{B_1}(g), giving character
(3, 3, -3, 3, -3) on the five classes.

Using (10):

    chi_{Sym^2(V)}(e)  = (3^2 + 3)/2 = 6,
    chi_{Sym^2(V)}(r^2) = (3^2 + 3)/2 = 6,
    chi_{Sym^2(V)}(r)   = ((-3)^2 + 3)/2 = 6,
    chi_{Sym^2(V)}(s)   = (3^2 + 3)/2 = 6,
    chi_{Sym^2(V)}(rs)  = ((-3)^2 + 3)/2 = 6.

Hence chi_{Sym^2(V)} = (6, 6, 6, 6, 6), which by (11) decomposes as
6 A_1. Dimension check: 6 = dim Sym^2(3-dim) = 3 * 4 / 2 = 6. Check.
[Box]

Lemma 3.2 (Sym^2 of 8 E).

    Sym^2(8 E) = 36 A_1 + 28 A_2 + 36 B_1 + 36 B_2.                  (14)

Proof. V = 8 E has dimension 16 and character
chi_V = (16, -16, 0, 0, 0). Using (10):

    chi_{Sym^2(V)}(e)  = (16^2 + 16)/2 = 136,
    chi_{Sym^2(V)}(r^2) = ((-16)^2 + 16)/2 = 136,
    chi_{Sym^2(V)}(r)   = (0^2 + (-16))/2 = -8,
    chi_{Sym^2(V)}(s)   = (0^2 + 16)/2 = 8,
    chi_{Sym^2(V)}(rs)  = (0^2 + 16)/2 = 8.

So chi_{Sym^2(V)} = (136, 136, -8, 8, 8).

Applying (11):

    m_{A_1} = (1/8)[136 + 136 + 2(-8) + 2(8) + 2(8)] = 36,
    m_{A_2} = (1/8)[136 + 136 + 2(-8) + 2(-8) + 2(-8)] = 28,
    m_{B_1} = (1/8)[136 + 136 + 2(8) + 2(8) + 2(-8)] = 36,
    m_{B_2} = (1/8)[136 + 136 + 2(8) + 2(-8) + 2(8)] = 36,
    m_E     = (1/8)[136*2 + 136*(-2)] = 0.

Dimension check: 36 + 28 + 36 + 36 + 0 = 136 = dim Sym^2(16-dim). Check.
[Box]

Lemma 3.3 (Cross terms). The pairwise cross terms of V_1 are:

    3 A_1 (x) 3 A_2 = 9 A_2,
    3 A_1 (x) 3 B_1 = 9 B_1,
    3 A_1 (x) 3 B_2 = 9 B_2,
    3 A_1 (x) 8 E  = 24 E,
    3 A_2 (x) 3 B_1 = 9 B_2,
    3 A_2 (x) 3 B_2 = 9 B_1,
    3 A_2 (x) 8 E  = 24 E,
    3 B_1 (x) 3 B_2 = 9 A_2,
    3 B_1 (x) 8 E  = 24 E,
    3 B_2 (x) 8 E  = 24 E.

Proof. Direct application of the tensor-product rules of Section 2.3.
For example, 3 A_1 (x) 3 A_2 = 9 * (A_1 (x) A_2) = 9 A_2, and
3 A_2 (x) 3 B_1 = 9 * (A_2 (x) B_1) = 9 B_2. [Box]

3.2 The full decomposition
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Theorem 3.4 (Symmetric-square decomposition). The D4 isotypic
decomposition of Sym^2(V_1) is

    Sym^2(V_1) = 60 A_1 + 46 A_2 + 54 B_1 + 54 B_2 + 96 E.           (15)

The total dimension is 406, matching dim Sym^2(C^28) = 28 * 29 / 2.

Proof. We sum the contributions from the individual pieces (Lemma 3.1,
Lemma 3.2) and the cross terms (Lemma 3.3).

Sym^2 of the individual isotypic pieces:

    Sym^2(3 A_1) = 6 A_1,
    Sym^2(3 A_2) = 6 A_1,
    Sym^2(3 B_1) = 6 A_1,
    Sym^2(3 B_2) = 6 A_1,
    Sym^2(8 E)   = 36 A_1 + 28 A_2 + 36 B_1 + 36 B_2.

Sum: 24 A_1 + 0 A_2 + 0 B_1 + 0 B_2 + 0 E plus
36 A_1 + 28 A_2 + 36 B_1 + 36 B_2 = 60 A_1 + 28 A_2 + 36 B_1 + 36 B_2.

Cross terms: From Lemma 3.3, the cross terms contribute

    A_2:  9 (from 3 A_1 (x) 3 A_2) + 9 (from 3 B_1 (x) 3 B_2) = 18,
    B_1:  9 (from 3 A_1 (x) 3 B_1) + 9 (from 3 A_2 (x) 3 B_2) = 18,
    B_2:  9 (from 3 A_1 (x) 3 B_2) + 9 (from 3 A_2 (x) 3 B_1) = 18,
    E:    24 * 4 = 96.

Total:

    A_1:  60,
    A_2:  28 + 18 = 46,
    B_1:  36 + 18 = 54,
    B_2:  36 + 18 = 54,
    E:    96.

Dimension check: 60 + 46 + 54 + 54 + 2 * 96 = 406 = 28 * 29 / 2. Check.
[Box]

Remark 3.5 (The 54 in context). The 54 appears as 36 + 18: 36 from
Sym^2(8 E) and 18 from cross terms of the 1-dimensional pieces. This is
the source of the 54 B_1 + 54 B_2 count that the handoff records. The
number 54 = 36 + 9 + 9 has no obviously simpler algebraic form in
framework constants; it is the exact result of the D4 representation-
theoretic computation.

4. THE GRAVITON SECTOR
================================================================================

Definition 4.1 (Graviton sector). The graviton sector of the Hodge
complex is the B_1 + B_2 isotypic piece of Sym^2(V_1).

Corollary 4.2 (Graviton mode count). The graviton sector has dimension
108 and decomposes as

    V_grav = 54 B_1 + 54 B_2.                                        (16)

Proof. Immediate from Theorem 3.4: the B_1 and B_2 multiplicities are
both 54. Dimension: 54 + 54 = 108. [Box]

Remark 4.3 (Why B_1 + B_2?). The B_1 and B_2 irreps are the two
1-dimensional nontrivial representations of D4 that transform as the two
physical polarizations of the graviton in the discrete setting:
h_xx - h_yy (B_1) and h_xy (B_2). The A_1 and A_2 pieces correspond to
the trace and the antisymmetric part of the tensor; the E piece
corresponds to vector content that is not the graviton.

5. POLARIZATION SPACE
================================================================================

Theorem 5.1 (Polarization space). The graviton polarization space is

    V = B_1 + B_2,                                                   (17)

of dimension 2, with D4 character

    chi_V = (2, 2, -2, 0, 0)                                         (18)

on the five conjugacy classes (e, r^2, r, s, rs).

Proof. By definition, the graviton sector is the B_1 + B_2 isotypic
piece. As a D4 representation, it is V = B_1 + B_2 (the polarization
space), tensored with the multiplicity space of dimension 54 (in each
isotypic component).

The character of V = B_1 + B_2 is

    chi_V(e)  = 1 + 1 = 2,
    chi_V(r^2) = 1 + 1 = 2,
    chi_V(r)   = (-1) + (-1) = -2,
    chi_V(s)   = 1 + (-1) = 0,
    chi_V(rs)
