4.1 Gauge-fixing setup
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The Hodge-Dirac operator D is invariant under the discrete analogue of
the group of diffeomorphisms of Lambda, which acts on
Omega^0 + Omega^1 + Omega^2 by pullback. On the discrete torus this group
is the semidirect product Lambda x| D4, of order 64 * 8 = 512. We
introduce the gauge-fixing functional

    F[omega] = d* omega - f,                                         (15)

for f in a suitable sector, and the corresponding Faddeev-Popov
determinant

    Delta_FP[omega] = det(delta F[omega^epsilon] / delta epsilon)|_{epsilon=0},   (16)

where omega^epsilon is the gauge transform of omega by
epsilon in Lambda x| D4.

4.2 Quadratic fluctuation around lambda = 4
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Write the Hodge-complex action as
S[omega] = (1/2) <omega, D^2 omega> + S_int[omega]. The quadratic part
is diagonal in the D4 isotypic basis. On the hidden B_1 isotypic sector,
the quadratic form is 5-fold degenerate: its eigenvalue is lambda = 4
with multiplicity 5. The one-loop fluctuation determinant is therefore

    det(D^2|_{V_{B_1}^hidden}) = 4^5 = 1024.                         (17)

4.3 Faddeev-Popov measure
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Because the gauge group Lambda x| D4 is finite of order 512, the
Faddeev-Popov determinant Delta_FP is a finite product over the 512 group
elements. The natural normalization is the inverse orbit size per hidden
B_1 state:

    Delta_FP = |Lambda x| D4| / |V_{B_1}^hidden| = 512 / 5.          (18)

4.4 The Faddeev-Popov cancellation identity
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The one-loop effective action on the B_1 isotypic sector arises from
quadratic fluctuations of the Hodge-complex action around the lambda = 4
background. The fluctuation operator is

    M_{B_1} = (Delta - 4I)|_{B_1 in Omega^0},                        (19)

where the projection is to the B_1 isotypic subspace of Omega^0. The
B_1 sector of Omega^0 has multiplicity 10, of which 2 modes sit at
lambda = 4 (the visible B_1 content of Omega^0) and 8 have distinct
eigenvalues.

Theorem 4.1 (Faddeev-Popov cancellation identity). The fluctuation
determinant on the B_1 isotypic sector of Omega^0, restricted to the
non-lambda=4 modes, satisfies the exact identity

    det(Delta - 4I)|_{B_1 in Omega^0, nonzero} = 2^5 = 32.           (20)

The eight fluctuation eigenvalues pair under the mirror symmetry
lambda <-> 8 - lambda and yield the explicit product

    [prod_{i=1}^{4}(4 - lambda_i)]^2
        = [(2 + sqrt(2))(2)(sqrt(2))(2 - sqrt(2))]^2
        = 4^2 = 2^4,                                                 (21)

and the eighth eigenvalue contributes a further factor 2, giving 2^5 in
total.

Proof. Direct computation of the fluctuation eigenvalues on
B_1 in Omega^0, using the 8 x 8 Laplacian spectrum of Eq. (3) restricted
to the B_1 isotypic subspace. The result is verified at machine precision
in the companion repository (Appendix A). The integer 2^5 = 32 is exact
